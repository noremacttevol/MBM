## Video 1 — "What is Milk Before Meat?" (~75 seconds)

**Purpose:** the channel's front-door video. Someone who just watched a story and
clicked the channel wants to know what this is. Honest answer, no pitch.

**Narration (Brian, the narrator voice from the stories):**

> Every video on this channel is one story about Jesus, told from
> the King James Bible. No music. No commentary. No arguing. Just the story,
> the way it reads.
>
> We made them for an app called Milk Before Meat.
>
> It works like this: the app tells you one story at a time — the woman who
> touched his cloak, the son who came home, the shepherd who went after the one.
> After each story it asks you one honest question, and it listens to your answer.
> No account. No feed full of noise. And whenever you want a real person instead
> of an app, a real person is one tap away.
>
> It's free, and it's quiet, and it's not trying to trap you into anything.
> If the stories on this channel have meant something to you, the app is where
> they come to you one at a time — milkb4meat.org.

**Visuals:** stills from the approved videos (already-approved art only), a few
slow screen recordings of the app — the cold-open story, a question card, the
Talk-to-a-real-person button. End card: app icon + `milkb4meat.org`.

## Video 2 — "Why only stories?" (~90 seconds)

**Purpose:** answers the question thoughtful viewers actually ask — why there's no
preaching, no debate, no music on this channel. States the method plainly. This is
also the honest-about-what-we-are video.

**Narration (Brian):**

> You might notice what's missing on this channel. Nobody preaches. Nobody argues.
> There's no music telling you what to feel.
>
> That's on purpose — because it's how Jesus taught. He told stories. A man had
> two sons. A shepherd had a hundred sheep. A woman lost a coin. He let the story
> do the work, and he let people walk away if they wanted to. He asked questions
> more often than he answered them.
>
> The stories here are told from the King James Bible, with nothing
> added — because they don't need help. They've carried themselves for two
> thousand years.
>
> And when a story is over, it ends with a question. Not a trick question — a real
> one. The kind you carry into your day. If you want somewhere to sit with those
> questions one at a time, that's what the Milk Before Meat app is for. It's free.
> It learns what you're carrying, it never pressures you, and there's always a
> real person one tap away.
>
> One story, one verse, one honest question. That's the whole thing.
> milkb4meat.org.

**Visuals:** stills from the parable videos as each is named (two sons, lost
sheep, lost coin), then the app's question card, then the end card.
