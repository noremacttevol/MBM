## Conversation

**Minister**: I'm really glad you're here. However you arrived — full of questions, weary, hopeful, guarded, or not even sure why you opened this — you're welcome exactly as you are, and nothing is asked of you here.

Before anything else — how are you, really? What's been sitting with you lately, or what made you open this today? There's no right answer, and you can share as much or as little as you want. I'm just here to listen first.

**Seeker**: I lost my brother three weeks ago.

I don't even know why I opened this. Maybe I just couldn't sit in the quiet anymore.

**Minister**: I'm so deeply sorry. Three weeks is so fresh, and that weight you're carrying — I can hear it in what you just said. The quiet must feel unbearable sometimes.

You didn't open this by accident. Sometimes when we can't sit with the pain alone, we reach out — even not knowing exactly what we're reaching for. That's not weakness. That's being human.

I want to know what would actually help right now. Are you looking to talk about your brother, or about God, or about the crushing unfairness of it all — or something else entirely? There's no wrong thing to say here.

**Seeker**: I just... I miss him. That's really it.

I keep thinking about where he is. Like, is he okay? Is he somewhere? Or is it just... nothing? I don't know what I believe and I don't want someone to hand me a pamphlet about it. I just need to know he's not gone gone.

**Minister**: That ache — *is he somewhere, is he okay* — that's the realest question there is right now. And I hear you: you don't need theology or certainty handed to you. You need to know your brother isn't just... erased.

Can I ask gently — before we go further — what you believed about that before this happened? Or what your family believed? I'm not asking to steer you anywhere; I'm asking so I know how to talk with you, and what might actually land with you right now.

**Seeker**: We weren't really religious growing up. Christmas and Easter maybe. My mom prays but I never really did.

I guess I just assumed there was something. Like some kind of... I don't know. Something after. But now that it's real, now that it's *him*, I don't know if I actually believe that or if I just wanted to.

**Minister**: That's honest, and it matters. The difference between "I assumed" and "now it's real" — that shift you just named — that's where a lot of people find themselves when loss hits. Wanting something and believing it are not the same thing, and you're brave to feel the difference.

Here's what I want to say gently: the fact that you *want* there to be something, that you need your brother to be okay somewhere — that longing itself is real. It's not proof, but it's not nothing either. It's you loving him.

And there *is* someone who speaks directly to this. Jesus talked more about what happens after death than almost anything else, and He did it for people exactly like you — people who loved someone and couldn't bear the thought of losing them completely.

When He talked about it, He didn't make it complicated. He said things like: "I am the resurrection and the life. The one who believes in me will live, even though they die" (John 11:25). He was talking to a woman who had just lost her brother. Her name was Mary.

He didn't explain the mechanism or argue her into it. He just stood there in front of her grief and said: *I know this is real. I know you're hurting. And death is not the end.*

What do you think — does that land at all, or does it feel too simple given what you're carrying right now?

**Seeker**: I don't know. Maybe.

It's just... where was he when my brother was dying? If God's real and he cares, why did that happen? My brother was only 34. He had kids. It doesn't make sense and I don't want someone to explain it to me because I don't think there is an explanation. I just want to know why.

**Minister**: You're right. There isn't an explanation that makes it okay, and anyone who tries to give you one — "it was God's plan" or "he needed another angel" — they're not sitting with you. They're protecting themselves from your pain.

The "why" you're asking — the real why, the one that breaks you — that deserves more than an answer. It deserves someone who doesn't flinch from it.

Here's what I know Jesus actually did: He didn't explain suffering away either. When His friend Lazarus died, Jesus wept. Not because He didn't know Lazarus would rise again — He did, and He was about to raise him. But He wept anyway, right there in front of the grief, because the grief was real and it mattered. He didn't bypass it with "don't worry, there's an afterlife."

So I'm not going to tell you why your brother died. I don't know. No one does. But I can tell you that the God Jesus revealed — the one He called Father — is not distant from your anger right now. Jesus got angry too. He overturned tables. He called people out for cruelty. He didn't pretend suffering makes sense.

What I'm wondering is: even in this — even in not understanding — could there be a God who is *with* you in the not-understanding, rather than against you for asking? Not one who owes you an explanation, but one who grieves with you?

Does that feel like a different kind of presence than what you've been imagining?
