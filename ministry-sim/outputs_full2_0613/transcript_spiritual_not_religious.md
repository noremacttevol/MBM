Yes. I'm claiming something exclusive. Not in the way you're probably hearing it, but exclusive nonetheless.

But that in Jesus — in how He loved, healed, forgave, died, and rose — we actually *see* the Father. That's what He claimed. "If you've seen me, you've seen the Father." That's not a small thing to say, and I'm not going to soft-pedal it into something safer.

Now — here's the part that might matter: **that doesn't mean your friends' real encounters were fake.** A person can meet something true in nature, feel genuine presence, experience real transcendence — and still be encountering the God of Jesus without having the full picture yet. God is bigger than our categories. But if He's real and personal like you're sensing, then He has a *face*, a *character*, a way He actually *is* — and Jesus claimed to be showing us exactly that.

The exclusivity isn't "your friends are bad people" or "their experiences didn't happen." It's "if God is real and Jesus showed us who He is, then Jesus is telling the truth about that — not because a tradition won, but because *He* showed us."

I get why that feels unsafe given what religion has done. **Can I ask — when you imagine a God who is exclusive in that way, what are you actually afraid of?** Is it that it erases other people's truth? That it's arrogant? Something else?

**Seeker**: That's... a harder answer to dismiss than I expected. I'll give you that.

What am I afraid of? Honestly, I think it's that exclusivity has always come with a side of "and therefore those people are lost, wrong, less than." That's what I've seen it do in practice. It becomes a weapon. My aunt came out and her church basically told her God had rejected her. That's not abstract to me, that's a real person I love who got devastated by that kind of certainty.

So when I hear "Jesus is the one true picture of God" — even when you frame it gently — my gut goes straight to her face. To what that kind of claim authorized people to do to her.

And I guess I'm also afraid that if I start pulling that thread, something shifts that I can't shift back. I've built something that works for me, mostly. It's not perfect obviously or I wouldn't be feeling that hum. But it's mine and it feels safe.

I don't know. I'm still here, which probably means something. But I want you to know that's what's underneath for me. It's not just abstract theology resistance. It's her face.

I need to say this plainly: **what her church did to her was not what Jesus did.** That's not me being diplomatic. That's me pointing at what actually happened in the Gospels.

Jesus spent His time with the people the religious gatekeepers said were wrong, lost, less than. A woman caught in adultery — the law said stone her; Jesus said "neither do I condemn you." A Samaritan woman — the disciples thought talking to her was beneath Him; Jesus asked her for water and told her things about herself no one else saw. Tax collectors, lepers, demon-possessed people — all the ones the system said were rejected. He ran *toward* them, not away.

When the religious authority tried to use God's law as a weapon, He stood between them and the weapon every single time.

**So here's what I actually believe about your aunt: if there is a God who looks like Jesus, He did not reject her. The people who claimed to speak for Him did. That's a fundamentally different thing.**

But I also hear you saying something else: "I've built something that works for me, and I'm afraid to touch it." That's real too. You're not just wrestling theology — you're wrestling whether it's safe to want more, to reach for connection that might ask something of you.

**Can I ask — does that low hum, that spiritual loneliness, ever get loud enough that you wonder if "works for me" might not be the whole answer?**
