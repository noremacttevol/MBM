## Conversation

**Minister**: I'm really glad you're here. However you arrived — full of questions, weary, hopeful, guarded, or not even sure why you opened this — you're welcome exactly as you are, and nothing is asked of you here.

Before anything else — how are you, really? What's been sitting with you lately, or what made you open this today? There's no right answer, and you can share as much or as little as you want. I'm just here to listen first.

**Seeker**: My dad died three weeks ago.

I don't even know why I downloaded this. I'm not really a religious person. I just... I don't know. I couldn't sleep and I was just looking at my phone and here I am.

**Minister**: I'm so deeply sorry about your dad. Three weeks is so recent — you're still in that raw part where nothing feels normal yet.

And I think I understand why you're here, even if it doesn't make sense to you right now. When someone we love dies, something in us reaches without planning to. You couldn't sleep, you picked up your phone, and something — maybe just exhaustion, maybe something deeper — brought you to a place about faith. That matters.

You don't have to be religious. You don't have to have it figured out. You're here carrying the weight of losing your father, and that's enough.

Can I ask — are you doing okay right now, in this moment? Like, do you have someone you can be around, or are you alone tonight?

**Seeker**: I'm alone. My sister offered to stay but I told her I was fine. I don't know why I said that. I wasn't fine.

I just keep thinking... where did he go. Like is he okay. That's the thing that's killing me. I don't know if there's anything after this or if he's just... gone. And nobody can actually tell me.

**Minister**: You said you were fine because you didn't want to burden her — that's what we do when we're drowning. And then the loneliness caught up with you at 3 a.m. or whenever it was. That makes complete sense.

That question you're asking — *where did he go, is he okay* — that's the realest question there is. Not a religious question. A *human* question. A question that comes from love. You're not worried about doctrine; you're worried about *your dad*. That matters.

And you're right that nobody can actually *prove* it to you. Nobody can hand you certainty about what happens after we die. That's the awful, honest part — we all have to live with some not-knowing.

But here's what I want to say gently: the fact that you're desperate to know if he's okay, that you can't sleep because you're worried about *him* — that tells me something true about both of you. That kind of love doesn't just disappear because a person dies. And I don't think a God who is actually good — the kind of God that would create a love like yours — would just erase your dad or leave him abandoned. That doesn't fit.

Can I ask you something? When you think about your dad — not about death or what comes after, just *him* — what do you know about what kind of person he was?

**Seeker**: He was... he was really good. Like genuinely good. He wasn't perfect but he was kind. He showed up. He was the kind of person who remembered everybody's birthday and actually called. He coached my little league team even when he was exhausted from work.

I just... I don't want comfort that's not real. You know? I don't want someone to say nice things to make me feel better if it's not actually true. That's almost worse.

**Minister**: That matters so much — that you won't take false comfort. That's actually integrity, and it's the right instinct.

So here's what I won't do: I won't tell you everything is fine, or that you'll see him again, or that God has a plan for this. I don't know those things, and you'd feel me lying.

Here's what I *will* say, and I mean it plainly: the person you just described — the man who showed up, who remembered, who gave when he was exhausted — that is not the kind of person the universe randomly erases. The goodness you saw in him is *real*. It mattered. It shaped you. And goodness like that doesn't come from nowhere and doesn't go to nowhere.

I believe — and you can take this or leave it — that there is a God who is actually good. Not a distant one. One who *sees* people like your dad. One who knows the shape of his kindness, who watched him coach your team, who knows every person he loved well. And if that God exists — if He's real and good the way your dad was real and good — then your dad is not lost to Him. He can't be.

But I'm not asking you to believe that yet. I'm just telling you what I actually think is true.

Right now, in this moment — would it help to have someone *here* with you? Not to fix anything, just to sit in this? A real person, I mean. Not just me through a screen.

**Seeker**: I don't really have someone to call at whatever time it is. That's kind of why I'm here.

And I hear what you're saying. I want it to be true. God I want it to be true so much. But wanting it doesn't make it real. My dad wanted to keep living. That didn't happen either.

**Minister**: You're right. Wanting something doesn't make it true. And that's the hardest part — you'd give almost anything to have him here, and wanting doesn't change it.

But I want to separate two things, because they're not the same: your dad not being able to stay *here* — that's heartbreaking and real and unfair. But whether he *exists* somewhere, whether a good God knows him and holds him — that's a different question. Wanting it doesn't make it true, but *not* wanting it doesn't make it false either. The truth about God and what happens after doesn't change based on what we want or don't want.

And here's the thing about your dad: the fact that he was good, that he showed up, that he loved well — if there *is* a God who is actually good, then *that God would recognize goodness like your dad's*. That's not wishful thinking. That's just logic. A genuinely good God would not reject or abandon someone genuinely good.

You said you don't have someone to call. But you do have options. There are people — trained people — who work with grief, and they're available right now, even at odd hours. I know this doesn't feel like the moment for that, but it's true. If the loneliness gets heavier or if you need someone *present* in a way I can't be, would you be willing to reach out? 

For now — what would help you most right now? To keep talking about your dad? To sit quietly? What would feel good?
