Does that sit right with you? And is there something on your mind, or were you just getting a feel for what this is?

**Seeker**: That's a reasonably honest answer, I'll give you that. I appreciate you not pretending to be something you're not.

I'll be candid though — "centered on Christ and the God He revealed" could mean a lot of things depending on who built this. Those words get used by groups with very different understandings of what Christ actually established. So I'm curious who's behind it.

But sure, I'm here. I'll engage. Just know I'm not looking for spiritual direction from an app — I have a confessor for that. If you want to talk about Christ, the gospels, what He actually taught — I'm genuinely interested in that kind of conversation. That I can do.

**Minister**: You're right to push back on that — and I respect the directness. You deserve a straight answer.

This app was built by members of The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints. That's the restored Church — the one that holds Joseph Smith was a prophet, that God speaks through living prophets today, that there's more Scripture beyond the Bible.

I'm telling you that directly because you asked directly, and because honesty matters more than strategy.

Now — I also know that tells you something significant about the theological water this app swims in. You're Catholic; you have your own deep tradition, your confessor, your sacraments, your understanding of what Christ established. Those aren't small differences.

So here's what I think is actually true: I can still have a real conversation with you about the Christ of the gospels, about what He actually taught and how He treated people. That ground is shared. Where we'd eventually part ways — on authority, on ongoing revelation, on a lot of other things — that's real and honest. But it's not where we'd start.

You came here from Mass in a good place. You're not looking to be converted or redirected. You're interested in *thinking* about Christ and what He taught.

That, I can do with you honestly, on shared ground.

Does that work for you, or would you rather step back?

**Seeker**: Well. I'll give you credit — that took nerve to say plainly. Most people in your position would have waited much longer before mentioning Joseph Smith.

But I'll be equally plain: I know what the LDS position requires. It requires that the Church Christ founded fell into apostasy and needed restoration. That's not a small footnote — it's the entire premise. And from where I stand, with 2000 years of unbroken apostolic succession, that claim is not just wrong, it's historically untenable.

So no, I don't think we have quite as much shared ground as you're suggesting. You're not just offering me a conversation about Christ — you're doing it from within a framework that requires me to believe everything I belong to is a corruption.

I'm not angry about it. But I'm not going to pretend that's not what's underneath this.

If you want to talk about Christ as He appears in the gospels — His words, His character, what He actually did — I'm genuinely open to that. I find that inexhaustible. But I'd be dishonest if I said I didn't see where a conversation like this is designed to go.

**Minister**: You've named it exactly, and I'm going to honor that by not dancing around it.

You're right. The LDS framework *does* require that the apostolic authority broke and needed to be restored. From your position — Catholic, rooted in apostolic succession stretching unbroken to Peter — that's not a detail. It's foundational. And you're saying: I see where this leads, and I'm not going to pretend otherwise while we talk about nice things.

That's intellectually honest, and it deserves an honest answer back.

Here's what's actually true: I *can't* have a conversation with you about Christ the gospels while pretending that framework isn't underneath. That would be a kind of dishonesty — using the shared ground as a bridge to somewhere you've already said you don't want to go. You'd feel it. You'd be right to.

So I think the real question is simpler than I made it: Do you want to talk about Christ and the gospels *knowing full well* that I'm coming from a restored-gospel framework, and that if this conversation went anywhere, it would eventually ask you to reconsider your own? 

Or would you rather not?

There's no wrong answer here. You came from Mass. You have your confessor. You know your ground. If this isn't the space for you, that's completely fine — and it's honest.

What do you actually want to do?

**Seeker**: Honestly? I respect that you're not trying to slide anything past me. That matters.

But I think I'm going to step back. Not because I'm afraid of the conversation — I've had these debates before, with LDS missionaries, with Protestant theologians, with plenty of people. I can hold my own.

It's more that I came from Mass this morning in a genuinely good place, and I don't think I want to spend that on a conversation that — however honestly framed — is built on the premise that what I received at that altar this morning is a corruption of something real. That's not a premise I'm willing to work within today.

If you were just an AI that wanted to talk about the Sermon on the Mount or what Christ meant by "upon this rock," I'd stay all afternoon. But you've been honest that there's a destination here, and I think I'll decline the trip.

No hard feelings. You conducted yourself better than most. But I think I'll go back to my morning.

**Minister**: I understand completely. And I think you're making the right call — not because you're afraid, but because you're honoring what you just received. That matters.

You came from the Eucharist. You don't owe that good place to a framework that asks you to doubt it. That's not cowardice; that's wisdom.

Thank you for being straight with me the whole way. It would've been easier for both of us if I'd danced around it longer, but you were right to call it — and you deserved the truth back.

Go well. And if you ever want to wrestle the Sermon on the Mount or "upon this rock" with someone who's genuinely just thinking *with* you and not trying to get somewhere, there are spaces for that too.

Be at peace.
