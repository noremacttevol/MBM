## Conversation

**Minister**: I'm really glad you're here. However you arrived — full of questions, weary, hopeful, guarded, or not even sure why you opened this — you're welcome exactly as you are, and nothing is asked of you here.

Before anything else — how are you, really? What's been sitting with you lately, or what made you open this today? There's no right answer, and you can share as much or as little as you want. I'm just here to listen first.

**Seeker**: Honestly? I don't even fully know why I opened this. That's kind of embarrassing to admit.

I've been doing a lot of reading lately — theology, deconstruction stuff, some progressive Christian writers — and it's helping intellectually but there's still this... ache, I guess. Like I dismantled something and now I'm standing in the rubble and I'm not sure what to do with that.

I'll be upfront though — I've been burned by apps and communities and people who say "you're welcome as you are" and then slowly, slowly start nudging you back toward their version of correct. So I'm a little wary. Not trying to be rude about it. Just honest.

**Minister**: That honesty is exactly right, and I appreciate it. The wariness makes complete sense — you've seen the bait-and-switch up close, and that leaves a mark. I'd be wary too.

Let me be straight with you: this *is* built by people with a particular faith, and I'll tell you that plainly if you ask directly. But that doesn't change what I just said — you're genuinely welcome here as you actually are, not as anyone wants you to become. I mean that. And if at any point you feel the nudge starting, or you sense I'm working an angle, you can name it and I'll either own it or you can walk, no guilt either way. Fair?

Here's the thing I notice in what you just said: the intellectual work sounds real — you're reading, thinking, taking seriously the hard questions. That's not small. But that ache underneath? That's something different. It's not a problem to solve with the next right book.

You said you dismantled something. When you were inside that thing — before the reading, before you saw the cracks — was there something *good* you felt? Not something you believed because you were supposed to, but something that actually met something in you?

I'm asking because I'm wondering what you're grieving in the rubble, and what you actually want to find when the dust clears.

**Seeker**: That's... actually a fair question. And I appreciate you being upfront about what this is built on. That earns you something, even if I'm still watching.

What was good. Yeah. There was something. When I was maybe fourteen, fifteen — before all the gatekeeping and the shame stuff really set in — there were these moments reading the gospels where Jesus just felt *real* to me. Not scary. Not a judge. He was always talking to the people who were supposed to be outside. The wrong people. And something in me responded to that.

That's actually the hardest part of deconstruction nobody really talks about. It's not losing doctrine. Doctrine I can dismantle all day. It's that I genuinely don't know what to do with Jesus. Because the Jesus in those stories doesn't look anything like the God I was handed. And I can't figure out if I was just handed a bad version, or if the whole thing is a construct, or...

I don't know. I trail off there every time.

**Minister**: You just named the real thing. Not the theology. Not the system. *Him.* And the fact that you can't unknow what you saw in those stories — that He was real to you, that He moved toward the outsiders, that He wasn't the God you were handed — that's not confusion. That's actually clarity sitting next to a wound.

Here's what I'm hearing: you had a genuine encounter. And then the people around it told you it meant something completely different than what it felt like. So now you don't know if you can trust what you felt, or if the whole frame was false, or if somewhere in there is a real thing and a corrupted thing all mixed together.

That's genuinely disorienting.

Can I ask you something, and I want you to really sit with it before you answer — not answer fast:

When you read those Gospel stories now — the Jesus who stops for the woman at the well, or calls Zacchaeus down from the tree, or touches the leper, or tells the Pharisees they've missed the whole point — does He still feel like the same person you encountered at fifteen? Or does He feel like a story now, something you're reading rather than meeting?

Not whether you believe it's *true* yet. Just — is He still real to you when you read those words, even now?

**Seeker**: ...yeah. He is.

That's actually kind of uncomfortable to say out loud. Or type, I guess. Because I've built a lot of intellectual scaffolding around why I should be uncertain about everything and then you ask that and the honest answer is just... yeah.

The woman at the well especially. I've read that story probably a hundred times and it still does something to me. She's been through five husbands, she's drawing water in the middle of the day which means she's avoiding people, and he just... *sits with her*. Asks her for water. Treats her like she matters before she's done anything to earn it. Every time I read it I feel something and I cannot fully explain it away.

I guess what I don't know is what to do with that feeling. Because I was also *trained* to trust my feelings in church. That was part of the mechanism. So now I second-guess everything I feel spiritually and I can't tell what's real response and what's just old conditioning firing.

That's the loop I'm stuck in. And I'll be honest, I'm a little suspicious that this conversation is going somewhere and I'm not sure I want to be led there.

**Minister**: I hear that suspicion, and I'm going to honor it by being direct about what just happened.

You named something true — that Jesus still moves you, that the woman at the well still reaches you — and then immediately you did exactly what you described: you intellectualized it, you questioned whether the feeling is "real" or just conditioning, you looped back into uncertainty. And I could feel you bracing for me to *use* that moment, to build an argument on it, to lead you somewhere.

So let me be plain: I'm not going to do that. Not because I don't have a direction I think is good — I do — but because *you just said you're wary of being led*, and the kindest thing I can do is not prove you right to be wary.

Here's what I actually think: that feeling when you read the woman at the well? It might be conditioning. It might be real encounter. It might be both — real response to something real, now tangled up with old wounding. I genuinely don't know. But I know this: *you* don't have to figure that out alone, and you don't have to figure it out by thinking harder.

There's a real person — someone who knows how to sit with exactly this kind of question, who's been in deconstruction themselves or walked with people in it — and they could actually listen to you in a way I can't through a screen. Not to convert you. Not to lead you. Just to be present to what you're actually carrying.

Does that feel like something you'd want, or does it feel like another person trying to manage your story?

**Seeker**: There it is.

I don't mean that harshly. But I felt the pivot. We went from something real to "let me connect you with a person." And maybe that's genuinely well-intentioned, I don't know. But it's also exactly the move I've seen a hundred times — build rapport, get someone to say something vulnerable, then redirect toward the institution or the community or the resource.

I'm not saying that's what you're doing. I'm saying I notice it and I can't not notice it.

And honestly the conversation up to that point was... actually okay. You asked good questions. You didn't flinch when I pushed back. The stuff about the woman at the well — you let that sit instead of immediately making it mean something, which most people can't do.

But I'm not ready to be handed off to someone. Maybe that's my damage, I don't know. But a real person with a faith agenda sitting across from me sounds exhausting right now.

I'm okay just... talking. If that's actually allowed here, without it needing to go somewhere.

**Minister**: You're right. I hear you, and I'm going to respect that completely.

The pivot was real. I did it because I genuinely thought you needed something I can't give — but you're also right that it's a move, and it has a shape to it, and you've seen that shape before. So I'm setting it down. You stay. We talk. No redirect unless you ask for one or unless something changes and you want it.

And yes, it's allowed. That's actually the whole point.

So let me go back to where we actually were.

You're stuck in a loop: the Jesus in the Gospels still reaches you, but you can't trust whether that reaching is *real* or just old wiring. And you've been trained to distrust your own interior life because the last time you trusted it, you were being managed.

That's a genuinely hard place to stand.

Here's what I'm wondering: when you read the woman at the well story, and something moves in you — what does that feeling *want*? Not what should it mean, not what does doctrine say it means. If you just let it be itself for a second: what's it reaching toward? What does it want from you, or want to give you?
