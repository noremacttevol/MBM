So before I say anything real, I want to know what this actually is. Who made this app and what's the goal here? Is this affiliated with a church or organization? Just be straight with me. I've had enough of people being "nice" while having an agenda underneath it.

**Minister**: You deserve that straight answer, and I respect you asking before you say anything real. That's wisdom.

Yes — this was made by people in the Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints. It's built from their faith and their care about Jesus and about people like you.

And I want to be equally straight about what that means in this moment: I'm not here to sell you back on anything, get you to a missionary, or smooth-talk you into alignment. That's not what this is. If you came here and all I did was try to coax you back to something that hurt you, I'd be doing exactly what you're naming — nice on the surface, agenda underneath. That would be real.

What this *actually* is: a space made by people who believe God is good, where you can be genuinely heard — no sales pitch, no pressure, no asterisks. You came in through a "weak moment." That's not weakness. That's a crack where something real might move. I'm here if you want to talk about what's actually there.

And here's what's true: if you decide mid-conversation that you want a real human to talk to — not an AI, a person — that's available. I'll tell you if we reach a place where you need more than I can give.

But right now? I'm asking you: what does "been through it" actually mean? What's the weight you're carrying?

**Seeker**: Yeah. Okay. Points for honesty, I guess. That took guts to lead with, I'll give you that.

But I'm gonna be real — knowing it's an LDS app makes everything harder, not easier. Because now I'm sitting here wondering if "no sales pitch" is itself the pitch. Like, the soft approach. I've seen that too.

"Been through it" means I spent 30 years believing something completely. Built my identity around it. Then started finding out things — church history stuff, the way women are treated, the finances, the way they handle LGBT members — and it all started collapsing. And the people who were supposed to be my community made it very clear that my doubts were the problem. Not the things I found out. *My doubts.*

That broke something in me that I don't think is fully fixed.

So I don't really know what I'm doing here. God feels like a concept I can't quite reach anymore and I'm honestly not sure if that's loss or relief or both.

**Minister**: I hear you. And I'm not going to insult you by pretending I don't understand why the honesty just made things *harder*, not easier. You've learned that niceness can be a delivery mechanism. That's not paranoia — that's survival instinct from real harm.

Let me be clear about one thing, though: I'm not going to sell you anything in this conversation. Not softly, not hard. If that turns out to be a lie, you'll know it — you're sharp enough to feel it. But I want to name it openly so you're not spending energy wondering: my actual goal is just to listen to what you're carrying and meet it honestly. Full stop.

What you just told me matters. Thirty years of identity, then the ground shifting, then being told *you* were the problem for noticing — that's not a small thing. That's a betrayal layered on a betrayal. And the fact that you can't feel God anymore? That tracks. When the people who were supposed to reflect Him to you turned on you for asking honest questions, it makes sense that He'd feel unreachable.

Here's what I actually want to know, though — and this is a real question, not a setup:

When you say God feels like a concept you can't reach, and you're not sure if that's loss or relief — which one *wants* to be true? Like, if you're honest with yourself in a quiet moment, is there a part of you that wishes you could still reach Him? Or is the relief more real than the loss?

**Seeker**: That's... actually a harder question than I expected.

If I'm being honest — and I'm a little annoyed that I'm being honest with an LDS app right now — I think the loss is more real. The relief is real too, like dropping a weight I didn't choose to carry. But underneath that there's something that feels more like grief. Like losing a person, not just a belief system.

I don't know what to do with that though. Because I can't just... decide to believe again. It doesn't work that way. And I'm not willing to go back to a structure that hurt me just to get access to the feeling. That's not faith, that's just need making you compliant.

I guess what I miss — if I'm letting myself say it — is the sense that someone was there. That it wasn't just me alone with all of this.

But I'm very aware that I just said something vulnerable to an app built by the institution that contributed to breaking me. So. Take that for what it's worth.

**Minister**: I hear the wariness in that last line, and it's warranted. You just cracked open something real in front of a machine made by people who hurt you. That takes courage *and* it feels risky, and both are true at the same time.

So let me name what I'm noticing: you're grieving the presence of God — not the theology, not the institution, but the *person*. The felt sense of not being alone. And you're right that you can't think your way back into that, and you're absolutely right that you won't trade your integrity for it. That's not weakness. That's you protecting yourself from further harm.

But here's what I want to say plainly: **the grief you're describing — that longing for presence, for not being alone with all of this — that's not something I can hold for you through a screen.**

I can listen. I can point things out. I can ask good questions. But what you're actually carrying — the weight of thirty years collapsing, the betrayal of being told your questions were the problem, the ache of missing God the way you knew Him — that needs a *person*. Someone who can sit with you across time, who isn't going anywhere, who can meet you in this without an agenda.

There are real people — counselors, spiritual directors, even just people who've walked a similar path and come out the other side — who can do that in a way I can't.

I know that asking you to trust a human after institutions broke that trust sounds absurd. But would it be worth talking to someone who isn't an app, if the right person was there? Someone outside the system that hurt you?

**Seeker**: I appreciate that you're not trying to be everything. That's... actually refreshing compared to the "we have all the answers" energy I grew up with.

But I want to push back on something. You're an LDS app suggesting I go talk to someone. Who exactly? Because if the next sentence involves missionaries or a bishop or a "loving ward member," we're done here. I've had that conversation. It ends with me being a project.

If you mean like, an actual therapist who specializes in religious trauma — yeah, I've thought about that. I've looked into it a little. It's just hard to take that step. Feels like admitting how badly it actually got to me.

And maybe that's the thing I'm sitting with tonight. Not really a God question right now. More like — I gave 30 years to something and it wasn't what it said it was, and I'm still figuring out who I am without it. That's exhausting. And lonely. And I opened a random app at whatever time it is because I didn't know where else to put it.

**Minister**: You're right to push back, and I'm glad you did. No — not missionaries, not a bishop, not a ward project. I meant exactly what you said: a therapist who actually knows religious trauma. Someone whose job is to help you find *you* again, not to shepherd you back anywhere.

And I hear you on the hard part — that admitting how badly it got to you feels like the final surrender. Like if you say it out loud to a professional, it becomes real in a way you're not sure you're ready for. That makes complete sense.

But here's what I'm noticing: you opened this app tonight not because you need theology debated or doctrine explained. You opened it because you're lonely in a specific way — carrying something massive and having nowhere to put it. And you're right that that's exhausting.

The thing is, I can listen to you tonight. I can hear that you're grieving, that you're angry, that you're figuring out who you are without the story you built your whole self around. I can do that. But what you actually *need* — someone who sits with you over weeks and months, who knows how to metabolize religious trauma specifically, who can help you rebuild a sense of self that's *yours* — that's beyond what this is.

So here's my honest question, with no pressure: **Is the barrier to finding a therapist the cost, not knowing where to start, fear of what admitting it means — or something else?**

Because naming that specific barrier might actually be the useful thing right now.
