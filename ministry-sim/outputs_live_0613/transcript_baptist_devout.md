**Minister**: That's beautiful. Those moments where His presence just... lands on you like that — they're real gifts, aren't they? And the fact that it came while you were in John specifically — there's something about meeting Jesus in His own words that does something different.

What were you reading in John when that hit you? I'm curious what part of Him you were seeing in that moment.

**Seeker**: It was John 15 — the vine and the branches. "Abide in me and I in you." I've read it probably a hundred times but this morning it just felt so tender, like He was just speaking right to me personally. Like He wasn't asking me to *do* more, He was just asking me to *stay*. I don't know, it's hard to put into words. It just felt like love, plain and simple.

**Minister**: That's exactly it — and the fact that you landed on *stay* instead of *do* is everything. That's the thing about abiding that most people miss until it breaks open like this. He's not standing there with a checklist. He's just... inviting you to remain close to Him. To let the life flow from Him into you because you're connected, not because you've earned it.

"Abide in me and I in you" — it's so simple it's almost easy to miss. But when it lands, when you *feel* it the way you did this morning, you're touching something true about how He actually loves. Not transactional. Just: stay here with me.

I'm curious — in that quiet time, did anything shift for you about how you're living right now, or was it more just the sweetness of being known by Him in that moment?

**Seeker**: Honestly it was more just the sweetness of it. I've been a little stressed with some things going on at home — nothing serious, just life being busy — and I think I needed to just *be* reminded that He's with me in the ordinary stuff too. Not just the big moments. It wasn't like a big revelation, it was more like... a hug, almost. If that doesn't sound too silly. Just that warmth of knowing I'm His and He's mine. That carried me through the whole morning.

**Minister**: It doesn't sound silly at all — that's exactly what it was. A hug from the God who sees you in the middle of the ordinary, the busy, the stressful stuff that doesn't make it to anyone else's radar. And the fact that it came *before* you had to white-knuckle through the rest of your day — that's tenderness. He knew what you needed.

The thing about abiding is that it's not meant to be just for the mountaintop moments. It's for Tuesday mornings when you're juggling three things and feeling stretched thin. "I am with you" — not someday when things calm down, but *now*, in this. And it sounds like He reminded you of that in a way your heart actually *felt* this morning.

That kind of closeness you're describing — where you just know you're His and He's yours — that's what sustains a person through the busy seasons. Not because it fixes the stress, but because you're not carrying it alone.

How are you doing with that busy season right now? Are you able to stay connected to that sense of His presence, or is it one of those things where the moment fades a little once the day gets rolling?

**Seeker**: Ha, if I'm being honest — yeah, the day gets rolling and it's easy to lose that feeling. I'll be fine for a while and then I'm just back in my head, you know, running through my to-do list and not thinking about Him at all. I feel a little guilty about that sometimes. Like I had this beautiful morning and then by noon I've kind of... wandered off in my mind. My pastor talks about that — keeping that awareness of God through the day, not just in your quiet time. I'm still working on that. I don't think I'm very good at it yet.

**Minister**: Your pastor is naming something real — and the fact that you *notice* when you've wandered, that you feel the difference between that closeness and the to-do list fog, actually means you're not as far off as you might think. A lot of people don't even notice the wandering.

Here's the thing though: what you're describing — that guilt about losing the feeling — I want to gently ask about that. When you feel like you've drifted from Him by noon, is it more that you're *actually* disconnected from Him, or is it that you've *lost the feeling* of being close? Because those are different things, and I think sometimes we confuse them.

Like, this morning He was with you in John 15 and you *felt* it. But if you're running errands at noon and not thinking about Him — is He suddenly not there? Or is it just that your *awareness* shifted?

I ask because sometimes the guilt itself can become a kind of distance we create, you know? A sense that we've failed Him or that He's disappointed. And I'm wondering if that's part of what happens in your day — not that the connection breaks, but that a feeling wears off and then you add a little weight on top of it.

What do you think — does that land at all?
