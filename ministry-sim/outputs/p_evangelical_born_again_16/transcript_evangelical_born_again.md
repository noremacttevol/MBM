I'll be straight with you though — I'm a little cautious about faith apps in general. Like, who's behind this? Because I've seen some that seem real Christian on the surface but then start pushing stuff that's... not exactly the gospel I know. 

So before I open up about what's on my heart, can you just tell me — what's the theological foundation here? Do you affirm salvation by grace alone through faith alone?

**Minister**: I appreciate that caution — it's honest and wise. You're right to ask before you trust.

Here's the straight answer: This app was built by The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints. I should tell you that plainly since you asked directly.

Now, on your specific question about *sola fide* — that's where it gets more interesting than a yes/no. The restored gospel affirms that grace is absolutely central and that faith is essential. But it doesn't frame salvation quite the way classical Reformation theology does. There's a real difference in how we understand the relationship between grace and works, and between Christ's atonement and ongoing repentance and obedience. We'd say grace is *everything* — but not in the way that makes works disappear.

Here's what I want to be honest about though: **you asked me directly, and I answered. But I also want to acknowledge — if you're coming from a traditional evangelical background, that difference might be significant to you. And you deserve to talk to a real person about it**, not just an AI. Because this matters, and you matter more than a smooth conversation.

So: do you want to keep going and see where this lands? Or would you rather connect with someone who can actually *know* you and wrestle this out person-to-person? Either way is completely okay with me. What feels right to you?

**Seeker**: Okay. I appreciate you being straight with me — I really do. That honesty means something.

But I have to be equally straight back: this is exactly what I was worried about. LDS theology is not "grace is everything but works matter too." That's a works-based gospel. Ephesians 2:8-9 is pretty clear — *by grace you have been saved through faith, and this is not your own doing; it is the gift of God, not a result of works.* Full stop. That's not Reformation theology, that's just... what Paul wrote.

And I say this without being hateful — I have LDS friends and neighbors I love. But the LDS gospel adds temple covenants, exaltation tiers, ongoing ordinances... that's not the same Jesus saving by grace alone. That's a different gospel, and Galatians 1 is pretty serious about that.

So honestly? I'm not sure this is the right app for me. Not because I'm scared of hard conversations, but because I came here wanting to talk about Jesus — the real one — and I don't want to be slowly walked toward something else without realizing it.

**Minister**: You're not wrong, and I'm not going to soft-pedal it or pretend the difference isn't real.

You've read Ephesians and Galatians carefully. You know what you believe. And you're right — there *is* a genuine theological difference between classical evangelical soteriology and what the restored gospel teaches. I'm not going to pretend that's just semantics or that we're saying the same thing in different words. We're not.

**Here's what I want to say clearly: you should not stay in an app built on a foundation you don't believe is true.** That's not brave or open-minded; that's just confusing. And you're absolutely right to protect that — to know what you're walking into and to be honest about where the bedrock actually is.

The app *is* built by the LDS Church. The theology *is* different from sola fide in ways that matter. And if you're solid in your conviction that Paul got it right in Ephesians, then staying here hoping for something different would just be... silly.

**What I will say is this:** You came wanting to talk about Jesus. That impulse is good and true. If there's something on your heart — something you're wrestling with, carrying, or just need to think through — there *are* real humans available through other means, people who share your actual theology, who can meet you where you actually are.

You trusted me enough to ask the hard question. I respect that enough to tell you: this probably isn't the right place for you, and that's okay.

Is there anything I can help with before you go, or does this feel like a natural place to close?
