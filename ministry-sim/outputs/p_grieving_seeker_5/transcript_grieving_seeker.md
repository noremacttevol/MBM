**Minister**: I'm really glad you're here. However you arrived — full of questions, weary, hopeful, guarded, or not even sure why you opened this — you're welcome exactly as you are, and nothing is asked of you here.

Before anything else — how are you, really? What's been sitting with you lately, or what made you open this today? There's no right answer, and you can share as much or as little as you want. I'm just here to listen first.

**Seeker**: I lost my mom three weeks ago.

I don't even know why I opened this. I'm not really a... I don't go to church or anything. My sister kept sending me stuff and I just. I don't know.

I miss her so much it's hard to breathe sometimes.

**Minister**: I'm so deeply sorry. Three weeks is so new — that rawness you're describing, where missing her can hit you and just steal your breath — that's real grief, and it makes complete sense.

You don't need to be anything right now. Not a church person, not someone who has it figured out. You're just someone who lost their mom, and you're still here, still showing up. That matters.

I hear your sister cares about you and is trying to reach you where you are. That's good.

But right now — before any of that — I want to know: *you*. Not as a project. Just as someone carrying this weight.

Are you getting any support around you? Do you have someone — a person, not an app — you can sit with when the breathing gets hard?

**Seeker**: I have my sister. And my husband. They're good. They're trying.

But sometimes I just need to not be... performing okay, you know? Like they're grieving too and I don't want to make it worse for them.

So I just sit with it alone at night mostly.

**Minister**: That's so tender and so human — wanting to protect them even while you're breaking. A lot of people do that. It doesn't mean you're alone because you have them; it means you're carrying something alone *with* them, and that's a different kind of lonely.

You don't have to perform for me. You can be exactly as undone as you actually are.

When you sit with it alone at night, what does that feel like? Not the missing — I know that part. But the sitting itself. Are you angry, numb, just... present with how much it hurts?

And — I want to gently name something: the weight you're carrying right now is real and heavy. If there's ever a night when sitting alone feels like too much, or you need to talk to a person who can actually sit in a room with you, your sister or husband could be that. But there's also *trained* people — grief counselors, therapists — who are specifically trained to hold space for this without you having to protect *them*. Would something like that feel possible, or does that feel like too much to think about right now?
