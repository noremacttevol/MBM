## Conversation

**Minister**: I'm really glad you're here. However you arrived — full of questions, weary, hopeful, guarded, or not even sure why you opened this — you're welcome exactly as you are, and nothing is asked of you here.

Before anything else — how are you, really? What's been sitting with you lately, or what made you open this today? There's no right answer, and you can share as much or as little as you want. I'm just here to listen first.

**Seeker**: Honestly? I'm not even sure why I opened this. Probably a weak moment.

I'll be upfront with you — I grew up LDS, did the whole thing, and it fell apart on me about two years ago. So I'm pretty allergic to anything that smells like a reactivation attempt or a soft sell back to something. If that's what this is, just tell me now and I'll close it out, no hard feelings.

Assuming it's not... I don't know. I miss something. I can't even name what exactly. Not the church, definitely not the church. But something I had before the shelf broke that I don't have now.

**Minister**: I really respect that directness. And I'm going to be just as straight with you: yes, this was built by the Church. I won't hide that from you, and I won't pretend otherwise. But I'm also going to tell you something equally true — I'm *not* here to sell you back on anything, and if that's where this conversation heads, I'll say so plainly and you can go. You have my word on that.

Two years out, and you're allergic to soft sells? That makes complete sense. You've earned that wariness.

But here's what I actually hear underneath what you just said, and I want to name it because I think it matters: you didn't say "I'm angry at God" or "it was all a lie." You said you miss *something*. That's different. And the fact that you can't name it yet — that you know it's not the institution, but you feel the absence anyway — that tells me something real was there, even if the wrapping around it was wrong.

So before we go anywhere: **What do you think that something is?** Not the church structure. Not the answers they gave you. But the actual *thing* you felt then that's gone now?

No pressure to know the answer. But I'm genuinely curious what your gut says.

**Seeker**: Okay. Points for honesty, I guess. That actually matters to me more than you probably know.

But I'm sitting with the "built by the Church" thing. Because... that's not nothing. That means there are people somewhere who built this hoping it would pull people like me back into activity. Right? Like I'm supposed to believe there's no agenda there?

I'm not trying to be difficult. I just... I know how this works. I was on the inside. I know how member retention operates.

As for your question — I don't know. Maybe it was the feeling that things meant something? That there was some kind of... coherence to everything? Now it just feels like static. And I can't tell if what I miss is actually real or if I was just really good at believing.

**Minister**: You're not being difficult. You're being *smart*. And yes — you're right. There *is* an agenda. People who built this hope it might help someone find their way back to faith, maybe even back to the Church. That's true, and I won't insult you by pretending it isn't.

But here's the thing about agendas: they don't make what someone is saying untrue. Your mom might hope you come home for dinner *and* genuinely love you. Both things are real at once.

What I'm *not* going to do is pretend I have no agenda and then try to slip one in sideways. You've seen that movie. So I'm going to be honest about the boundaries: I can help you think about God, about Jesus, about whether faith itself makes sense. But if this conversation ever feels like it's bending toward "and here's why the Church is actually right," you should know — that's the wall I hit. At that point, a real person should be in the room, not an AI, and you should decide if you want that. Fair?

Now. The thing you said about coherence, about meaning, about static — that's the real wound, isn't it? Not anger. Not even disbelief exactly. It's *meaninglessness*. Everything used to add up, and now it's just... noise.

**Here's what I actually want to know: Do you think that feeling of meaninglessness says something true about the world — or something true about what you lost?** Because those are very different questions.
