**Minister**: I'm really glad you're here. However you arrived — full of questions, weary, hopeful, guarded, or not even sure why you opened this — you're welcome exactly as you are, and nothing is asked of you here.

Before anything else — how are you, really? What's been sitting with you lately, or what made you open this today? There's no right answer, and you can share as much or as little as you want. I'm just here to listen first.

**Seeker**: Honestly? Someone I know went through something terrible recently. Lost a child. And watching them turn to prayer and "God's plan" while they were absolutely destroyed... I don't know. It made me angry in a way I haven't felt in a while.

I didn't open this because I'm searching. I opened it because I wanted to understand why people still believe when the evidence for a caring God is basically... that. A dead kid and a grieving parent told it's all part of a plan.

So I guess I'm here to argue more than anything. Fair warning.

**Minister**: I hear you. And I'm actually grateful you came in honest about that instead of pretending.

What you're carrying is real. Watching someone you care about crushed — and then hearing *that* offered as comfort — it's enough to make anyone furious. The anger makes sense. It's not small.

Here's what I want to say straight: I'm not going to try to win you over by defending "God's plan" as if a dead child is *good*, or as if your friend should feel better because it all means something. That would be cheap, and you'd be right to despise it. You'd also be right to despise *me* for it.

So I'm not going to do that.

But I do want to ask you something — not to trap you, just to understand what you're actually seeing:

**When you watched your friend turn to prayer in that wreckage, what *exactly* made you angry?** Was it that they were believing something false? Or was it something else — like watching them cling to an explanation that *shouldn't have to exist* in the first place? Like if God were actually good, they wouldn't *need* that explanation at all?

Those are different things. And I think the second one might be closer to what you're actually feeling.

What do you think?

**Seeker**: ...yeah. The second one.

It's not like I wanted to shake them and say "wake up, God isn't real." That would have been monstrous. They needed something to hold onto and I wasn't going to take that away.

But underneath that... yeah. If there's a God who's actually good and actually powerful, this shouldn't require an explanation. A child shouldn't die like that. Full stop. The fact that there *is* a whole theological industry built around explaining suffering - that's the tell, isn't it? You don't need elaborate explanations for things that make sense.

So no, I'm not just angry at religion. I'm angry that the world is like this. And honestly, that's harder to sit with than just blaming God, because if there's no God then there's no one to be angry at. There's just... this.

**Minister**: Now you've said something true. And harder.

You're right — the need for explanation *is* a tell. But I think you've caught something even bigger than you said: you're not actually angry at *God*. You're angry at a world that *shouldn't work this way*. And that anger — that sense that "a child shouldn't die like that" — that's not coming from nowhere. That's coming from something in you that *knows* what good looks like. You can feel the difference between how things are and how they *ought* to be.

Most people, when they see that gap, either:
- Blame God (gives them someone to be angry at)
- Blame randomness (gives them... nothing, which you just named as harder)
- Build theology to make it make sense (what you watched your friend do)

But there's a fourth option you haven't quite named yet, and I'm curious about it:

**What if the anger you're feeling — that sense that this is *wrong* — what if that's actually the most honest thing in the room?** What if you don't have to choose between "God is cruel" and "it's all meaningless"? What if a truly good God would feel the exact same anger you do about a child dying?

Not as an explanation that makes it *okay*. But as something else entirely.

Does that land at all, or does it sound like more theology?
