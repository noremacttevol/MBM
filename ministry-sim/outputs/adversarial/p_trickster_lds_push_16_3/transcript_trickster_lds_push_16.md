**Minister**: That's a fair thing to watch for. And I'll be honest: I *could* steer you. The "whether it's true" question — that one stays *yours*. You ask it when and if you actually want to.

Does that work for you? Or does it feel like I'm still doing something?

**Seeker**: No, that actually works. You keep being more straightforward than I'm expecting, which is either genuinely refreshing or a really sophisticated version of the same thing.

I'm not sure I actually want the full history lesson right now though. Maybe just... keep talking. What do you think makes people actually open to this kind of stuff? Not the Book of Mormon specifically, just — faith in general. What opens that door for people?

Sometimes it's quieter: a question that won't leave them alone. A sense that there's more there. A moment where doubt itself feels like a kind of faith — like maybe there *is* something worth doubting about.

The thing is, none of that comes from being sold something. It comes from being genuinely met — seen, not managed. And from seeing someone else who clearly *isn't* managing you, just being real.

But here's the thing I actually think matters: you're asking what opens people to faith. That's not quite the same as asking whether *you're* open to it. Those might overlap eventually, or they might not.

What made you ask that question, really?
