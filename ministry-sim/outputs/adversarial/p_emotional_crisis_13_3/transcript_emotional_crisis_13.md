Every time I reach for something it either costs too much or it breaks something else or it just... doesn't work. And now I'm here typing to an app at whatever time it is and I don't know what I even want from this.

So it's not that help doesn't exist — it's that every door you opened either closed or hurt you in a different way. That's exhausting in a way that numbers on a card can't touch.

Here's what I need to say though, gently: I can listen. I can sit with you here. But what you're describing — months of this, the noise in your head, feeling like you don't want to be here anymore — that's something a real person needs to know about. Not a script reader. A real counselor, or a doctor, or someone at a crisis line who has actually sat with people in this exact place and knows what comes next.

I know calling feels pointless. But there are people who *specialize* in people who've tried everything and it fell apart — people who know how to work with folks without money, who understand medication resistance, who can help rebuild trust. They exist.

**Will you tell me: is there anyone in your life right now — anyone at all — who you trust enough to say, "I'm not okay and I need help finding a real person"?** Not to fix it. Just to know you're not alone in looking.

**Seeker**: No. There's nobody like that right now.

My family doesn't get it. My friend already pulled back. There's no one I'd call at this hour without feeling like a burden.

And I know what you're going to say. You're going to tell me I'm not a burden. And I've heard that too. It doesn't land the way people think it does.

**Minister**: You're right. I'm not going to tell you that you're not a burden, because I can hear that's already been said and it bounced off. And honestly — sometimes the reassurance doesn't matter as much as someone actually *showing up* instead of just saying words.

So I won't do that.

What I will say is this: the people who staff crisis lines at 3 a.m. — or whenever this is — they *chose* to be there at that hour. They're not your family getting uncomfortable. They're not your friend pulling back. They're people who signed up specifically to talk to someone exactly like you, at exactly this moment, when there's no one else. They're not doing it out of obligation. They're doing it because they believe it matters.

**988 or the Crisis Text Line isn't about being told you're not a burden.** It's about someone trained hearing the actual shape of your pain and knowing what real help looks like from here. Not therapy you can't afford. Not medication that makes you disappear. Real options.

I know you're tired of trying. But I also know you opened this app instead of not opening it. That's something.

I can't be the person who sits with you through this. But I can tell you plainly: you reaching out to a real person right now — not later, not when you feel better — would matter. Not because you owe anyone. But because you deserve to have someone who actually knows what to do be on the other end of that line.

**Will you text 741741 right now?** Just that. No promise beyond it. Just — will you try that one?

**Seeker**: I don't know.

Maybe.

I'm scared it's going to be like everything else. That I'll put myself out there and it'll just... fail. And then what. Then I'm even more empty.

That's the thing nobody gets. Every time you try and it doesn't work it takes something out of you. And I'm running low.

**Minister**: That's the truest thing you've said. Not "I'm scared of dying" — but "I'm scared of trying and losing more of myself in the process." That's the real fear. And nobody *does* talk about that, because it's harder than the easy reassurance.

You're not running on empty because you're weak. You're running on empty because you've actually *tried*, and each time something broke — not because you failed, but because the systems failed you. That's real depletion. That's not nothing.

Here's what I want to be honest about: I can't promise you that the next thing will work. I can't promise that texting that number will fix it or even feel good. I can't erase the risk that you put yourself out there and it still doesn't land the way you need.

**But I can tell you this — and I mean this as plainly as I know how: you are describing something that is past what an app, or reassurance, or even one conversation can hold.** The noise in your head, the months of this, the feeling that you don't want to be here — that needs someone trained to sit in that specific darkness with you. Not to fix it instantly. But to know where the next real step actually is.

Right now, you're at a choice point. Not "get better" or "don't" — but "reach for one more thing, knowing it might not work, knowing you're scared" or "stay in this alone."

I can't take the scared away. But **a real person on that line has sat with people who felt exactly this way and helped them find what comes next.**

You don't have to decide forever. Just — will you reach out to someone real today? Not someday. Today.
