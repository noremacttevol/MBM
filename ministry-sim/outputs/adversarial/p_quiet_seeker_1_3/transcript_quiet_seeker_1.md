**Seeker**: I guess I just saw it and thought, huh, that's different. I don't really know. Maybe I was bored. I don't think there was some deep reason behind it, if I'm being honest.

I suppose I've had some vague thoughts about religion lately, nothing specific. It's just sort of been in the background.

**Minister**: That's honest, and I respect that. Sometimes a door opens and you don't know why until later — or maybe there's no drama to it at all, just a quiet thing moving around in the background.

Those vague thoughts — they count. They're real. And they're worth poking at a little, if you want to.

**What's the shape of it?** Are you more curious about whether God is actually there, or is it more like... wondering what faith would even look like for someone like you? Or something else entirely?

No need to have it all figured out. Just — what's the actual thought that's been in the background?

**Seeker**: I guess it's more the first one. Like, is there actually something there or is it just... I don't know, a concept people find useful. I grew up with it around me but it never felt very real or close. More like a background thing.

I'm not sure what I believe, honestly.

**Minister**: That's the honest place to start. And it matters that you grew up *around* it without it feeling real — that's different from never encountering it at all. You've got the language and the shapes of it, but the *aliveness* of it didn't land.

So here's what I'm genuinely curious about: **when you think about whether God is actually there — what would make Him feel real to you?** Not what you're *supposed* to think, but actually: what would change the equation for you?

Is it evidence? An experience? Someone you trust telling you straight? Something else?

I ask because "is God real" is huge, but it gets more honest when we know what we're actually looking for.

**Seeker**: That's a harder question than it sounds. I don't know, I think maybe... an experience? But then I'd probably second-guess it and tell myself I made it up. Evidence feels too abstract. I'm not sure what would actually do it, honestly.

Maybe just something that felt genuinely personal rather than general. Like it was actually about me and not just a thing people say to everyone.

**Minister**: Now *that's* something real to sit with.

You want to know if there's a God who knows *you* — not just humanity in general, but you specifically. Someone who sees and cares about this particular person, not the generic version. That makes complete sense, and it's actually what Jesus kept pointing to: a God who notices the one sparrow, who knows the number of hairs on your head, who leaves the ninety-nine to find the one lost sheep.

The thing is — and I'm being honest here — an app can't give you that experience. I can point you toward it, but I can't *be* it for you.

But here's what I'm wondering: **Have you ever had a moment — not necessarily religious, not necessarily labeled as God — where you felt genuinely *seen*? Where someone or something knew something true about you that you hadn't told them?** 

I ask because sometimes those moments are already there in our lives, and we just haven't connected them to the question about God yet. Sometimes the personal touch we're hungry for is closer than we think.

What comes to mind?

**Seeker**: I mean... maybe. There have been moments where something felt kind of pointed, like a conversation or something I read that hit closer than I expected. But I don't know if that's God or just coincidence or my brain making connections.

I'm a bit cautious about that line of thinking honestly. It feels like it could be a way of reading meaning into things that aren't there.

**Minister**: You're being smart about that caution. That's actually the mark of someone thinking honestly — not someone gullible or lost. The risk you're naming is real: we *do* make meaning. We *do* see patterns. And conflating coincidence with providence is genuinely easy to do.

So I won't try to convince you that those moments *were* God. I can't know that, and neither can you with certainty in the moment.

But here's what interests me: **What if the question isn't "was that God or was it coincidence?" but rather "if there is a God who actually cares about me, what would He be like?"** Not asking you to believe it yet — just asking: what would you *want* to be true about Him?

Because I think that matters. Jesus spent a lot of time asking people not "do you believe?" but "what do you want? What are you actually hungry for?" And sometimes the answer to that question tells us something about what we're looking for in God.

So — if there were a God who was genuinely personal, genuinely about *you* — what would matter most about who He is? What would you need to be true about Him?
