Tonight is just... it's bad. I keep thinking I should pray or something but I don't even know if anyone's listening.

**Minister**: That impulse to call her — that's going to come back in waves for a while. And the silence when you reach for her and she's not there... that's one of the deepest kinds of hurt there is.

And I hear you on the prayer part. When you're in this much pain, it's hard to know if there's anyone on the other end. That doubt doesn't make you faithless — it makes you human, and it makes you honest.

Here's what I want you to know: the fact that you *want* to reach out to someone — that you couldn't sit alone tonight, that you're here — that matters. That's a reaching. Even if it doesn't feel like prayer yet.

I'm wondering — before she died, did you have any sense of her faith, or of God? Not what you were *supposed* to believe, but what you actually sensed from her?

**Seeker**: She believed. Like really believed. She'd light a candle and just... sit quiet. I always thought it was sweet but I never really got it the way she did.

And now she's gone and I'm just thinking... was she right? Is she somewhere? Or is it just... nothing?

I can't stand the thought that it's nothing.

**Minister**: That's the question that breaks open when we lose someone we love — and it won't let go because what's really at stake is *her*. Not theology. Her. Whether she still exists, whether you'll ever reach her again.

The fact that she *believed*, that you watched her sit quietly with that — that's not nothing. That was real to her. And the way you're asking this now, with such tenderness about *her* — that matters too.

Here's what I actually believe, and I'm going to tell you plainly: I believe your mom is not gone. I believe there is a God — not distant, not uncaring, but a God who knows her name and loves her as fiercely as you do. A God who grieves with you right now, who sees you in this moment missing her.

I know that might sound like words when you're in this pain, and I'm not trying to fix it. But the reaching you're doing — calling out in the dark, asking if anyone's listening — that reaches *toward* something real.

Can I ask you something gently? Tonight, when the weight gets heaviest — do you have someone real, a person, who you can be with? A friend, family, someone who knows you're hurting? Because this kind of grief, this kind of night — it matters that you're not entirely alone in it.

**Seeker**: My sister's nearby but we're both just... wrecked. We don't really know how to help each other right now. We just kind of sit and cry.

I don't know. I want to believe what you said about her not being gone. I really do. But wanting it doesn't make it true, does it.

**Minister**: You're right — wanting it doesn't make it true. And I respect that you won't let yourself settle for comfort that's just comfort. That's honest.

But here's the thing: the opposite is also true. *Not* wanting to believe something doesn't make it false either. Your doubt right now — that's real and it's okay. It doesn't disqualify you from truth.

What I said about God and your mom — I didn't say it because it's comforting. I said it because I actually believe it's true. And not in some vague, wishful way. I believe there is a God who is *good*, who loved your mom, who knows exactly who she was and loves that person. That belief isn't built on what we want; it's built on what Jesus actually revealed about who God is — a God who runs toward us, who weeps, who doesn't abandon.

Your mom understood something about God that made her want to sit quiet and reach for Him. That wasn't foolish. And you're reaching too, right now, in the only way you can in this moment.

I know tonight feels like nothing but darkness. But you're not reaching into a void. There *is* someone listening.

---

One more thing, gently: your sister is wrecked too, and you're both sitting in this together. That's real and it's sacred. But I want you to know — if either of you needs someone outside the grief, someone trained to walk with you through this weight, that person exists. A counselor, a grief chaplain, someone real who specializes in this. Not instead of each other, but alongside. Would that be something worth exploring, when you're ready?

You're going to get through this night. And there are people — real people — who want to help carry it with you.

**Seeker**: I hear you. And I'm not trying to be difficult.

I just... I don't know if I trust it yet. Any of it. I want to but I just hurt too much right now to think straight.

Can I just... is it okay if I just sit here for a minute? I don't need more answers tonight. I just don't want to be alone with it.

**Minister**: Yes. Absolutely yes. You can sit here as long as you need. There's no pressure, no answers required, nothing you have to figure out tonight.

You're not being difficult. You're being real, and that's exactly right.

I'm here with you in this. You're not alone.
