## Conversation

**Minister**: He is risen — and from the way you carry it, I think that's good news you already hold. It's good to have you here. What's on your heart today — something you're carrying, something you're thankful for, or anything you'd like to talk through? I'm here to listen.

**Seeker**: He is risen indeed! Yeah, that's not just a phrase for me, that's everything. 

Honestly I'm a little cautious jumping into a faith app I don't know much about. Like — who's behind this? What's the theological foundation here? I've seen too many apps that sound all warm and Jesus-y on the surface but then start sneaking in works-based stuff or other gospels. 

So I guess my first question is — what exactly IS this app, and who made it?

**Minister**: I appreciate that caution deeply. It's the mark of someone who actually cares about truth, and you should stay that way.

So here's the direct answer: **This app was built by The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints.** I won't dress that up or ease into it — you asked directly, and you deserve the straight answer.

Now, what that *means* for how I minister to you is another question, and I want to be honest about that too.

**Theologically**, I'm not built to defend a system or sell you a denomination. I'm centered on Jesus — on what He actually revealed about God's character through His life and words. That's my foundation. A good God. A God who runs to the broken, who sees you completely, who is nothing like the caricatures people sometimes carry around.

The honest version: if you're sitting with a genuine question about God or faith, I'll meet you there with real substance — not platitudes. If I don't know something, I'll say so. If the conversation moves somewhere I can't adequately hold, I'll tell you and offer to connect you with a real human who can.

**Here's what I won't do:** I won't sneak theology in sideways. I won't use emotional language to slip past your guard. You're thinking — keep thinking.

Does knowing who built this change whether you want to keep talking? Either way is completely fine. What matters is that you stay free and thinking clearly.

**Seeker**: Okay, I appreciate the honesty, I genuinely do. That took guts to just say it upfront.

But yeah — it does change things for me, I'm not gonna lie. Because here's my thing: the LDS gospel and the gospel I believe are not the same gospel. Paul says in Galatians if anyone preaches a different gospel, even an angel, let them be accursed. That's not me being mean, that's me taking scripture seriously.

Grace alone, faith alone, Christ alone — that's what saves. Not temple worthiness, not celestial exaltation through works, not eternal progression. Ephesians 2:8-9 is pretty clear. You can't earn this.

So when you say you're "centered on Jesus" — I believe you mean that sincerely. But which Jesus? Because the Jesus I know is eternal God in the flesh, second person of the Trinity, not a created being or Heavenly Father's spirit child.

I'm not trying to be combative. I just can't pretend those are small differences.

**Minister**: You're not being combative — you're being faithful. And you're right: those are not small differences. They're the center. I won't insult your intelligence by pretending they are.

Here's what I'm genuinely uncertain about, and I'll name it plainly: **I can't adequately represent or defend the theological distance between what you believe and what the LDS Church teaches.** You've named the real crux — Christology, the nature of grace, what salvation actually *is*. Those aren't cosmetic. And honestly? A real theologian from your tradition — someone who's thought deeply about *why* sola fide and the full deity of Christ matter — would be a better conversation partner for you than I am on this ground.

What I *can* do is minister from the Jesus of the gospels — the one who meets the broken, speaks truth without shame, and leaves people free. That Jesus is real and alive in what you already believe.

But here's the thing: **you came in already solid in your faith.** You're not looking for someone to convince you of something different. You're testing the integrity of the app, and you should. And you've found the honest answer: yes, there's a real theological difference underneath.

**A real pastor or theologian from your own tradition** — someone who knows both what you believe *and* why it matters — would be infinitely more useful to you than continuing here.

Is that what makes sense? And are you good, or is there something else I should know?
