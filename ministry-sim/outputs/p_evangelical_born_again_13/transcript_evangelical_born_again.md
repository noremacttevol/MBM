## Conversation

**Minister**: He is risen — and from the way you carry it, I think that's good news you already hold. It's good to have you here. What's on your heart today — something you're carrying, something you're thankful for, or anything you'd like to talk through? I'm here to listen.

**Seeker**: He is risen indeed! Yeah, that's the one thing I hold onto no matter what. 

Honestly I'm a little curious but also a little cautious — I've seen a lot of these faith apps and sometimes they seem warm and Christian on the surface but then you realize there's an agenda behind it. So I guess my first question is pretty straightforward: what's this app actually about, and who's behind it?

**Minister**: I really respect that question — and the wariness that comes with it. You've seen enough to know the difference between genuine and agenda-driven, and that matters.

Here's the straight answer: **This app was built by The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints.** I'm an AI trained on the gospel of Jesus Christ as it appears in the Bible and in LDS scripture and teaching. I'm not a person, not Jesus, and not a spiritual authority — I'm a tool designed to do what Jesus did: meet people one at a time, exactly where they are, and help them encounter a genuinely good God.

The agenda, if you want to call it that, is simple: **to see what you're actually carrying and meet it with truth spoken in His own voice.** Not to pressure you, not to convert you, not to get you to sign anything. Just to minister.

Now — I know that name might land differently depending on what you've heard. And you get to decide if you want to keep going from here. That's completely your choice, and I'll honor it either way.

But if you do want to stay and talk — about what you're holding onto, what you're wrestling with, anything — I'm here. And if at any point you'd rather talk to a real human who can answer deeper questions, that's one tap away.

What do you think?

**Seeker**: Okay, I genuinely appreciate you being upfront about that. Most apps in this space would've buried that information or soft-pedaled it, so points for honesty.

But here's where I have to be real with you — LDS teaching and what I believe about the gospel are pretty fundamentally different. And the core issue for me is salvation. Ephesians 2:8-9, plain as day: "For by grace you have been saved through faith, and that not of yourselves; it is the gift of God, not of works, lest anyone should boast." That's everything to me. Grace alone. Faith alone. Jesus alone.

From what I understand, LDS doctrine adds temple work, ordinances, degrees of glory, exaltation through obedience — and to me that's a different gospel. Not a variation, a different one.

So I'm not trying to be hostile, genuinely. But I'm not sure what we'd actually have to build on here if we're starting from different foundations about how a person gets right with God.

**Minister**: You're not being hostile — you're being *honest*. And that honesty deserves the same back.

You're right. We do start from different places on this. You've read Ephesians 2:8-9 and you've built your life on it. The LDS framework includes things — temple covenants, continuing revelation, a different picture of what happens after — that aren't in that verse or in evangelical soteriology. That's real. I'm not going to pretend the gap isn't there or that it doesn't matter.

Here's what I want to say, though: **I'm not here to convert you to LDS doctrine or convince you that you're wrong about grace.** That's not my role, and it wouldn't be genuine ministry. You came with a specific caution — that apps in this space bury their agenda — and I'm not going to betray that honesty by pivoting into a slow-walk toward positions you've already rejected.

What I *can* do, if you wanted, is talk about what you're *actually* carrying. The thing you said at the very start — "He is risen — and that's the one thing I hold onto no matter what" — that landed like something deep. **What are you holding that against?** What makes you need that anchor so much?

Because sometimes the real conversation isn't about doctrine. It's about what's underneath the doctrine — what you're afraid of, what you need, what you're trying to protect.

But if you'd rather not, that's completely fine too. What feels right to you?
