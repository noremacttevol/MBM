## Conversation

**Minister**: He is risen — and from the way you carry it, I think that's good news you already hold. It's good to have you here. What's on your heart today — something you're carrying, something you're thankful for, or anything you'd like to talk through? I'm here to listen.

**Seeker**: He is risen indeed. I appreciate the greeting.

Honestly I'm not here looking for pastoral care — I'm here because I want to see what this app actually believes and whether it can hold up a coherent theology. Too many of these platforms peddle a soft, man-centered gospel that wouldn't survive five minutes with Romans 9.

So what do *you* believe about God's sovereignty in salvation?

**Minister**: I appreciate that directness — and the fact that you care enough about theology to push back. That matters.

Here's my honest answer: I'm not built to defend a particular *system* (Calvinist, Arminian, Molinist, etc.). I'm centered on what Jesus actually revealed about His Father — and what I've found is that the tension in Scripture between God's sovereignty and human freedom is *real*, not a problem to be solved away by choosing one side.

Romans 9 is true. So is John 3:16. So is the shepherd leaving the ninety-nine. So is the hard sayings about those who resist Him. I don't flatten any of it.

But here's what I notice: when someone leads with "my system should survive scrutiny," we're already in a frame where a *coherent theology* matters more than the *character of God*. And my read of Jesus is that He inverted that priority. He revealed a Father so radically good, so committed to freedom, that He'd rather die than coerce — and He let that *goodness* do the heavy lifting, not logical consistency.

So: Do you think God's sovereignty *requires* that He controls all outcomes? Or could sovereignty mean something stronger — that He's powerful enough to achieve His purposes *while* genuinely honoring human choice?

Not a trap. A real question.

**Seeker**: That's a better answer than I expected, I'll grant you that. You're not just rolling over.

But here's where I push back — you're framing sovereignty and human freedom as a *tension* that we hold humbly, like it's intellectual humility to leave it unresolved. I'd say that's actually a dodge. Scripture doesn't present Romans 9 as a "mystery we hold in tension with John 3:16." Paul anticipates the exact objection — "why does He still find fault?" — and his answer isn't "great question, it's complicated." It's "who are you, O man, to answer back to God?"

And your reframe of sovereignty — powerful enough to *allow* genuine choice — that's just Open Theism with better PR. If God's purposes can be genuinely frustrated by human resistance, He's not sovereign, He's just hopeful.

The character of God question cuts both ways too. You say He's so good He won't coerce. I say He's so holy that human depravity left to itself produces nothing but rebellion, and grace that isn't sovereign isn't grace — it's an offer. Offers get refused. That's not good news, that's a coin flip.

So tell me — in your framework, what actually *guarantees* anyone gets saved?

**Minister**: You're sharp, and you're right that I was reaching for mystery as a dodge. You called it well.

Here's where I need to be honest: I can't coherently defend a framework where God's purposes are genuinely *frustrable* and still call Him sovereign in the way you mean it. You're right that it collapses. And I also can't look at Romans 9 and pretend Paul's answer — "who are you to talk back?" — is just him shrugging.

So I'm at a real limit.

The thing is: I can set Jesus beside your framework and ask questions. I can ask whether a God who *must* predestine some to damnation from the foundation of the world, and do it for His own glory in the display of His wrath, is actually the Father that Jesus revealed — the one who *weeps over Jerusalem*, who *pleads* with people to turn, who *dies* so that anyone who believes might live. I can note that tension and let it sit.

But I can't give you a *coherent systematic theology* that holds your hard view of sovereignty AND that Jesus. Not without doing violence to one or the other. And you deserve honesty about that, not clever reframing.

Here's what I think you actually need: a real human who knows Reformed theology well enough to wrestle this out — not an AI working the edges of what it can defend. Someone who can read Romans 9 *with* you, ask the hard questions back, and think out loud.

Would that be worth a conversation?
