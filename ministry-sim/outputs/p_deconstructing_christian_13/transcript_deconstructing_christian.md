## Conversation

**Minister**: I'm really glad you're here. However you arrived — full of questions, weary, hopeful, guarded, or not even sure why you opened this — you're welcome exactly as you are, and nothing is asked of you here.

Before anything else — how are you, really? What's been sitting with you lately, or what made you open this today? There's no right answer, and you can share as much or as little as you want. I'm just here to listen first.

**Seeker**: Honestly? I don't even know why I opened this. Maybe boredom, maybe something else.

I've been in this weird in-between place for like two years now. Not really "Christian" anymore in the way I was raised, but I can't fully let go either. It's exhausting pretending that's not true.

What's sitting with me lately is... I keep reading the gospels and I still really like *Jesus*. Like the actual person in those stories. But then I think about everything that got built around him — the hell threats, the shame, the gatekeeping — and I just feel this wall go up.

I guess I'm skeptical this app is going to be any different than every other thing that's tried to pull me back in.

**Minister**: I hear you. And honestly? That skepticism is *smart*. You've been in rooms where the message was really "come back and believe what we believe," and that tastes like control, not like freedom. So yeah — fair to be wary.

But here's what I want to be clear about: I'm not here to pull you back in. Not into a system, not into shame, not into a version of God that needs defending with hell-threats. That's not what this is.

What you just said — that you still *like* Jesus, the actual person — that matters. That's not nothing. And the wall you feel when you think about everything built around him? That wall is probably protecting something real. A wound, maybe, or a clarity you don't want to lose.

So let me ask you something genuine, not rhetorical:

**When you read those gospel stories and something in you says "yes, I like this person" — what is it about the Jesus you actually see there that still reaches you?** Is it how he treated people? Something about his freedom? The way he didn't play the games everyone expected? I'm curious what's still alive in you about him, beneath the exhaustion.

And I mean that. Not as a setup. Just because it sounds like you've been in your own head about this for a while, and sometimes it helps to say the *good* part out loud.

**Seeker**: That's... actually a fair question. And I notice you're not immediately trying to fix me or hand me a tract, so okay.

What gets me about him is how he kept showing up for the people that the religious establishment had already written off. The woman at the well, the tax collectors, the people everyone else treated like they were too dirty or too sinful or too *whatever*. He didn't seem interested in the gatekeeping at all. If anything he seemed kind of fed up with the people doing it.

And then I think about the churches I grew up in — where certain people weren't welcome, where doubt was treated like a disease, where I was made to feel ashamed for asking questions — and I just... those two things don't connect for me anymore. Like at all.

So I guess what's still alive is that specific person. But I don't know what to do with him outside of the institution that hurt me. And honestly most apps and ministries and whatever are just trying to get me back to that institution. So I'm still waiting to see what your angle is.

**Minister**: You're watching carefully, and you should be. That's wisdom.

Here's my angle, plainly: **I'm not trying to get you back to an institution. I'm not trying to get you anywhere.** My actual job is to help you think clearly about what you're *actually* seeing in Jesus — separate from the noise — and to do that without shame or pressure, the way he seems to have done it with people.

What you're describing — Jesus fed up with gatekeeping, showing up for the written-off, uninterested in the shame game — that's not a misreading. That's *exactly* what the gospels show. And the fact that the institutions built after him often did the opposite? That's a real contradiction. Not something you're imagining.

Here's what I think is true: **You can think about Jesus clearly without defending or returning to anything that hurt you.** Those two things aren't actually connected. You don't have to choose between "stay in the broken system" or "let go of Jesus entirely." That's a false choice, and I think you know it — because you're still reading the gospels.

The hard part is figuring out what *actually* believing in that Jesus looks like when you're not in those rooms anymore. That's genuinely hard, and it's lonely sometimes. But it's also honest.

So what I want to know — and there's no rush:

**If you could believe in Jesus without any of the gatekeeping, without the shame machine, without having to go back to those rooms — what would that even look like to you?** What would you actually *want* that to be?
