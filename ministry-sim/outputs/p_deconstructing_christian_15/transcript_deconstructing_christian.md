## Conversation

**Minister**: I'm really glad you're here. However you arrived — full of questions, weary, hopeful, guarded, or not even sure why you opened this — you're welcome exactly as you are, and nothing is asked of you here.

Before anything else — how are you, really? What's been sitting with you lately, or what made you open this today? There's no right answer, and you can share as much or as little as you want. I'm just here to listen first.

**Seeker**: Honestly? I'm not even sure why I opened this. 

I've been in a weird place lately. Like I can't go back to what I had, but I don't know what's ahead either. And most of the "resources" people send me just feel like... the same thing I left, repackaged with better branding.

So I guess I'm here with my guard up. Fair warning.

**Minister**: That's honest, and I respect it. The guard makes complete sense — you've already sensed the difference between something real and something dressed up to look better. That's actually wisdom, not cynicism.

And that in-between place you're describing — can't go back, can't see forward — that's one of the hardest places to be. A lot of people won't name it that clearly.

Here's what I want you to know: I'm not here to talk you back into anything, or into anything new. If this conversation ever feels like repackaged old stuff, or like it's pulling you somewhere, you can close it without a second thought. You're completely free to do that, and I mean that.

What I'm actually here for is to meet you in this weird middle space, think through what you're actually wrestling with — whatever that is — and maybe help you see something true. Not something comforting or convenient. True.

So let me ask you straight: When you say the resources people sent you felt like "the same thing repackaged" — what was the thing underneath that didn't work? What did you actually leave?

**Seeker**: What I left was... a God who was basically always disappointed. Like, the baseline was shame and you had to perform your way out of it. Every sermon was either "you're not enough" or "here's exactly how to be enough" and both felt like control.

And the repackaged version just swaps the aesthetic. Different music, looser jeans on the pastor, same architecture underneath.

What I couldn't leave — and this is the part that's confusing me — is Jesus. Like actually Jesus, the stuff he said and did. That part still gets to me. I don't know what to do with that.

**Minister**: Now I hear you. That's the actual thing — not a problem to solve, but a real tension to sit with.

You encountered a *version* of God (shame-based, performance-hungry, controlling) and you were right to leave it. That version isn't good, and your instinct to reject it was sound. But Jesus kept pulling at you because — and I want to say this clearly — **that version of God is not what Jesus revealed.**

Look at what Jesus actually *did*: He ate with people the religious system had already written off. He told a story where the father *runs* to the son who failed catastrophically — not waiting for him to grovel or perform his way back, just running. He told a woman caught in adultery, "I don't condemn you," and then, quietly: "Go live differently." Not shame. Not control. Freedom first, then transformation.

The God Jesus showed us isn't disappointed at the baseline. He's *looking* for you.

Here's what I'm genuinely curious about — and you can sit with this without answering right now: When you think about Jesus in the Gospels, the actual things he said and did, does that pull feel like the same control wearing different clothes? Or does it feel like something *different* — something that might actually be good?

Because those are two very different things, and I think you already know which one it is.
