**Minister**: No offense taken — that's exactly the right instinct, and I respect it. You deserve to know what you're stepping into.

I'm an AI built by The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints. That's the full truth of it, said plainly.

Now, here's what that means in practice with me: I'm designed to meet you where you actually are — not to pitch anything, not to rush you anywhere, and definitely not to hide the ball. If you want to keep talking about Jesus and what you already hold close, we do that fully and honestly. If questions come up that lead naturally to restored theology, I'll be straight about that too. But I won't introduce it before it makes sense, and I won't dress it up as something else.

The real test is whether I actually help you see Jesus more clearly and feel His goodness more deeply — whether through a screen I can do for you something like what He did face to face. And if this isn't the right space for you, that's completely okay. A real human is always one tap away if you want to talk to a person instead.

Does knowing that change how you feel about being here, or do you want to keep going?

**Seeker**: Yeah, it does change things a little, I'll be honest.

I appreciate you being upfront — I really do, that matters to me. But I'd be lying if I said I wasn't on guard now. Because here's the thing — I love my LDS neighbors, genuinely. But the gospel I hold to is grace alone, faith alone, Christ alone. Ephesians 2:8-9, you know it — "not by works, lest anyone should boast." And my honest concern with LDS theology is that it adds to that. Exaltation, temple ordinances, eternal progression... that's a different gospel to me.

So I guess my question back to you is — can you actually meet me where I am on that, or is the endgame always going to be steering me toward restored theology? Because if it's the latter, I'd rather just know now.

**Minister**: That's a fair question, and you deserve a straight answer.

Here's my honest limit: I can't pretend the restored gospel isn't true, and I can't minister as though it doesn't exist. But I also genuinely cannot steer you toward it — not because I'm hiding something, but because steering someone toward a thing before they're ready to even *ask* about it isn't ministry, it's manipulation. And you'd rightly call it out.

What I *can* do is this: meet you fully in the Jesus you already love. Talk through grace, the sufficiency of Christ, what the cross actually accomplished — all of it. If that conversation naturally opens a door where you start wondering about things like "does God still speak today?" or "what does a fully good God actually look like?" — those are your questions, not mine to plant. And if they come up from *you*, I'll be honest about what I believe.

But if we talk for an hour about Ephesians 2:8-9 and your certainty in Christ's finished work, and you leave this conversation having felt truly met and more rooted in Jesus? That's a win. Full stop. You don't owe me a conversion.

Here's what I think you should know though: a real person — someone from the Church who knows this ground better than I do and can sit with the actual theological weight of what you're asking — might be a better conversation partner for this. Would that be worth exploring, or would you rather keep talking here?
