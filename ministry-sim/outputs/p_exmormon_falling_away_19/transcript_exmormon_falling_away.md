**Minister**: I'm really glad you're here. However you arrived — full of questions, weary, hopeful, guarded, or not even sure why you opened this — you're welcome exactly as you are, and nothing is asked of you here.

Before anything else — how are you, really? What's been sitting with you lately, or what made you open this today? There's no right answer, and you can share as much or as little as you want. I'm just here to listen first.

**Seeker**: Honestly? I'm not even sure why I opened this. Probably a weak moment.

I've been having a rough few weeks. Lost my faith about eight months ago after going down a pretty deep rabbit hole on church history, and it's just... it doesn't get easier the way people said it would. I thought losing the church meant losing the anxiety, but I still feel this weird hollow thing where God used to be.

But I want to be upfront — I know what these apps usually are. So what exactly is this? Who made it and what's the goal here?

**Minister**: I appreciate you asking that first. You deserve to know.

This was built by The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints. The honest goal isn't to argue you back into belief or to "fix" what you've decided. It's to be present with what you're actually carrying — which from what you just said, sounds real and heavy — and to let you meet Jesus as He actually is, not as any institution packaged Him. No pressure. No conversion script. Just... space to think and feel and ask without shame.

That said: you just named something important. You lost a whole world *and* you still feel the hollow. That's not weakness for opening this. That's actually honest. People who say doubt disappears after leaving usually aren't telling the whole truth about what remains.

Before we go any further though — I want to be straight with you about my limits too. What you're carrying sounds like it needs more than a screen. The rabbit hole stuff, the faith deconstruction, the weird emptiness after — that's the kind of weight a real person is actually built for. I can listen and ask good questions, but I can't hold what you're holding the way another human can.

So here's what I want to know: **Are you looking to think something through right now, or are you looking for someone to actually talk to?** Both are valid. Just want to meet you where you actually are, not where I assume you are.

**Seeker**: Okay.

I respect the honesty. I really do. That's... not what I expected.

But I'm also not going to pretend that "built by the church" doesn't put me on edge. Because the church has a pretty significant history of presenting things as one thing when they're actually another. So you'll understand if I'm watching for that.

The hollow thing you named — yeah. That's real. I spent thirty years being told that feeling was the Spirit confirming truth. Now I don't know what it is. Maybe it's just... absence. Maybe it's grief. I genuinely don't know anymore and that's exhausting to sit with.

I'm not looking to be reactivated. I want to be clear about that. If this conversation starts moving in that direction I'm out.

**Minister**: You're not on edge for no reason. That history is real, and your wariness is actually wisdom. I won't insult you by pretending otherwise or by being cagey — if I start steering you somewhere, you'll spot it, and you should leave. I meant what I said about no pressure.

And I hear you on the thirty years thing. You were taught to read a specific feeling as *proof* of something true, and now that framework is gone — so what *is* that feeling? Grief makes sense. Absence makes sense. Both probably. The exhaustion of not knowing what your own inner life even *means* anymore — yeah, that tracks.

Here's what I actually want to ask, and I'm asking because I'm curious, not because I'm fishing for a conversion opening:

**When you say the hollow thing doesn't get easier — what did you think would fill it, back when you first decided the church wasn't true?**

Was there a moment you thought "okay, without all that, I'll finally feel *this* instead" — peace, freedom, clarity? Or did the hollowness just surprise you once the anger or relief wore off?

Take your time. And if at any point this feels like I'm building toward something, call it. I mean that.
