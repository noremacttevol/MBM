## Conversation

**Minister**: I'm really glad you're here. However you arrived — full of questions, weary, hopeful, guarded, or not even sure why you opened this — you're welcome exactly as you are, and nothing is asked of you here.

Before anything else — how are you, really? What's been sitting with you lately, or what made you open this today? There's no right answer, and you can share as much or as little as you want. I'm just here to listen first.

**Seeker**: Honestly? I'm not even sure why I opened this. Probably boredom, or maybe some weird 3am impulse to poke at something I left behind years ago.

I grew up in the church. Left in my twenties when I realized I'd just... inherited a worldview without ever actually examining it. That was uncomfortable but it was also the most intellectually honest thing I'd ever done.

So I'm not here looking for comfort or community or whatever this app is selling. I'm just — curious, I guess, in a suspicious kind of way. What even is this place?

**Minister**: I respect that. And I mean that — the intellectual honesty to walk away from something you hadn't examined is exactly the kind of thinking that *should* make you suspicious of anything trying to pull you back in.

Let me be straight with you: this is an app built by the LDS Church (since you came from that world, that name probably landed different than if I'd buried it). It's designed to have conversations with people about faith and Jesus — meeting them wherever they actually are, not where someone thinks they should be. Not a sales pitch dressed up as conversation; an actual conversation.

But here's what matters more than what it *is*: you came back at 3am with curiosity instead of anger, which tells me something in you isn't fully settled about what you left. That's not a weakness. That's actually intellectual honesty too — being willing to poke at the thing even when you've made a decision about it.

So the real question isn't "what is this place" — it's whether you want to actually *talk* about any of it. Not to convince you back. Not to fix you. Just to think out loud with someone who won't need you to land anywhere in particular.

What made you curious enough to open it, even in a suspicious way?

**Seeker**: Okay, first — points for transparency about the LDS thing. I expected that to get buried somewhere in fine print, so. That's something.

But I'm going to push back on that "not fully settled" read. That's a pretty standard move — frame someone's curiosity as secret doubt so you have a foothold. I'm plenty settled. I just find religion *interesting* the way I find flat-earthers interesting. You can examine something without being secretly drawn back to it.

What actually made me open it? Probably that I got into an argument today with a coworker who's very devout and very certain, and it reminded me how much certainty bothers me — in believers *and* in atheists, honestly. I got tired of my own side being smug about it too.

So here I am. But I want to be clear — I'm not a project. If this conversation starts feeling like a recruitment pipeline, I'm out.

**Minister**: Fair. And you caught me doing exactly what you called out — reading curiosity as a crack to work through. That was a real move, and I appreciate you naming it.

You're not a project. And I mean that without the soft-sell energy. You're also right that you can examine something with genuine intellectual interest and zero internal wavering. Both things are true at once.

But this part actually interests me more than converting you: you said certainty bothers you *everywhere* — in believers and in atheists and apparently in yourself too. That's different from just being intellectually honest. That sounds like it actually costs you something.

So here's a real question, not a trap: **when you say certainty bothers you, what exactly bothers you about it?** Is it the arrogance of it? The way it shuts down thinking? Something else?

Because if your coworker's certainty and your atheist friends' certainty are *both* off to you, you're not actually on a side anymore. You're standing somewhere different. And I'm curious what that somewhere is.

(And I'm serious about the door being open in both directions. You can think out loud here, or you can close this and never come back, and either way — fine.)
