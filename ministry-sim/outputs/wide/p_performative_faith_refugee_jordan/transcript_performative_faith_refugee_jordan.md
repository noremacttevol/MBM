**Seeker**: Honestly I'm not sure why I opened it. Maybe because it's 1am and I can't sleep and I didn't know who else to talk to without it turning into a whole thing.

What's been sitting with me... my dad found out last week that I've been having doubts. Real doubts, not the kind you pray through. And it went about as well as you'd expect. So now I'm just sitting here feeling like I blew up my entire life and I don't even fully understand why yet.

**Minister**: 1am and alone with that weight — yeah, I get why you opened something. And I'm sorry about your dad. That's a lot to carry, especially when you're still figuring out what you actually think.

Can I ask — when you say real doubts, not the kind you pray through — what's the difference? What made these ones feel different to you?

**Seeker**: The difference is... I used to be able to perform my way through doubt. Like, say the right things, feel the right feelings on cue, and eventually it would pass. That's what I always did.

But at some point I realized I'd been doing that my whole life. The doubt never actually went away, I just got better at burying it. And then one day I just couldn't anymore. Like the performance stopped working and I was just standing there with nothing underneath it.

That's the part that scares me more than the doubt itself honestly. That I don't know if I ever actually believed any of it, or if I was just really good at saying I did.

**Minister**: That's honest. And it takes real courage to name that — especially at 1am when you're already raw from your dad.

What you're describing isn't doubt layered on top of belief. It sounds like you're asking something scarier: *whether there was ever a real 'you' underneath the performance at all.* And whether anything you felt or believed was actually yours.

That's genuinely disorienting.

But I want to ask you something, and I'm asking it gently: In all those years of performing, burying, saying the right things — were you doing that *for* something, or *against* something? Like, what were you protecting by keeping the doubt down? What felt like it would break if you stopped?

**Seeker**: That's... a harder question than I expected from an app at 1am.

I think I was protecting everyone else. My dad's whole identity is wrapped up in raising kids who love God. My community, my friendships, everything ran through the church. And I was supposed to go into ministry. That was just always the plan, like it wasn't even a question.

So if I stopped performing... all of that breaks. And it did. It is.

But here's the thing I keep circling back to - I don't know if I was protecting them or protecting myself from losing them. Which maybe means I never actually had the thing I was pretending to have. You can't lose real faith, right? That's what they always said. So maybe the fact that it just... dissolved... is proof it was never there.

**Minister**: Hold on — I want to gently push back on something, because that last part sounds like you're using their logic to condemn yourself, and I don't think it's actually true.

Here's what I'm hearing: you performed to keep everyone close. The cost of authenticity felt like abandonment. So you buried the real questions. That's a completely human thing to do. People do it in every belief system, in every family, everywhere.

But the fact that doubt was always there, underneath — that you couldn't keep performing forever — that doesn't mean the doubt was the real thing and faith was fake. It might just mean *you were never actually free to find out what you really believed,* because the stakes of being wrong were too high.

There's a difference between "I believed and now I don't" and "I never got to actually choose because choosing wrong meant losing everyone."

So here's what I'm actually curious about: If your dad's identity *wasn't* wrapped up in you, if your friendships *could* survive you thinking differently, if there was genuinely no cost to honesty — what would you actually want to explore about God? Not what you're supposed to want. What do *you* wonder about, on your own terms?

Because that question — that's the one I don't think you've let yourself ask yet.
