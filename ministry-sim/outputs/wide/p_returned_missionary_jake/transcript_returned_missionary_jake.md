## Conversation

**Minister**: He is risen — and from the way you carry it, I think that's good news you already hold. It's good to have you here. What's on your heart today — something you're carrying, something you're thankful for, or anything you'd like to talk through? I'm here to listen.

**Seeker**: Yeah, I guess. I mean, I know all that — Easter stuff, resurrection, hope. I gave that lesson probably a dozen times in Mexico. Watched people cry when they felt the Spirit confirm it.

I just... don't feel much about it today, honestly. Which is kind of the whole problem lately.

**Minister**: That's honest. And it sounds like you've *seen* what that truth does in other people — watched it land in them, change them. But right now, it's not landing the same way in you.

That gap between knowing something is true and actually *feeling* it, especially when you've felt it before... that's a real thing to carry. 

When did you last feel something genuine about it? Not the lesson-giving version — I mean *you*.

**Seeker**: *long pause*

Probably... my last testimony meeting before I came home. My companion and I had just baptized this family — the Reyes family, mom and three kids — and I bore my testimony and I just... I knew. Like, I *knew* it was real and it mattered and God was in it.

That was 14 months ago.

I keep waiting for that to come back and it just... doesn't. And people at church keep saying "oh the mission was just the beginning!" and I want to ask them — the beginning of *what*, exactly? Because right now it feels more like an ending.

**Minister**: Fourteen months. That's long enough to stop waiting and start grieving — which I think is what you're actually doing, whether you've named it that way or not.

You're not asking for the feeling to come back because you doubt it was real. You're grieving because it *was* real, and now you're home in a place that feels... smaller? Slower? Like the thing that made you certain has gone quiet.

Here's what I'm genuinely curious about: When you say it feels like an ending — do you mean an ending to *faith*, or an ending to that particular season where faith felt *alive* in a way it doesn't now?

Because those are two very different things, and I want to understand which one you're actually sitting with.

**Seeker**: That's... actually a fair distinction.

It's not like I stopped believing. I still go to church, I still pray — kind of. It's more like... the ending of feeling *chosen*, I guess? On the mission everything had weight. Every door we knocked, every conversation, it felt like it was part of something. God was directing us, I could feel it.

Now I'm just some guy trying to figure out which gen ed classes to take next semester and going on dates that feel like job interviews. Nothing has that weight anymore.

And I know how that sounds. I know it sounds like I think I'm special or something. I don't mean it like that.

**Minister**: No — I hear you differently than that. You're not saying you *are* special. You're saying you *felt* special *to God*. That He saw you, He was moving through you, He had something for you to do. That mattered more than whether you were chosen by people — because you felt chosen by *Him*.

And now you're back in regular life, and it all feels... ordinary. Unmemorable. Like you're going through the motions without the sense that anyone — including God — is actually paying attention.

That's not arrogance. That's *grief*. And it's also a really honest thing to feel after an experience that real.

Here's what I'm wondering, though, and I ask this gently: In those 14 months since you got home, has anything shifted in how you actually *see* God — like, in your bones, do you still believe He's good? That He cares? Or has the silence made you start to doubt whether He was ever there at all, or whether He still is?

Because sometimes when the feeling goes away, so does the certainty underneath it. And I want to know which one you're actually carrying.
