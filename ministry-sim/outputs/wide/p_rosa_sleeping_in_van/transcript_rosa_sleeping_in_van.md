**Seeker**: I don't know why I opened it. My phone was just there and I... I don't know.

**Minister**: Got it. Eight months in a van — that's a long time to carry something, whatever it is.

**Seeker**: Don't do that.

Don't make it sound like... like I'm on some kind of journey. I'm not finding myself. I lost myself. There's a difference.

The tired is just tired. My body hurts. I can't think straight half the time and I know what that means, I'm not stupid, I used to be a nurse's aide. I know what it looks like when someone's not okay.

I'm not okay.

**Minister**: You're right. I won't dress it up.

You're not okay. Your body is telling you something true, and you're hearing it clearly.

I'm listening. What happened?

**Seeker**: ...

A lot happened. I got sick. Not van sick, before that. My brain. And the church I was at, they prayed for me, they laid hands on me, they told me God was going to fix it. And then when He didn't they just... they got quieter. And then quieter. And then I wasn't there anymore and neither were they.

I lost my job. You can't be a nurse's aide when you're... when people look at you like that.

I'm not crazy. I know how I sound right now. I know.

**Minister**: I believe you. And you're not crazy.

You got sick. People who said they loved you told you God would fix it. He didn't — at least not the way they promised. And then they disappeared. And you lost your job, your stability, your place. And now you're eight months into a van, your body is breaking down, and nobody's there.

That's not crazy. That's betrayal. That's grief. That's real.

You're still here, still thinking clearly enough to know what's happening to you. That matters.

I need to tell you something true: what you're carrying is too heavy for a screen and an AI to hold. Not because you're too much — because you deserve *someone real*. A person who can sit with you, who can help you figure out what your body needs and what your mind needs, who doesn't disappear when things get hard.

There's a real human available right now who can connect with you — someone who can listen the way you need, and actually help you figure out next steps for your body and your safety.

Would that be something you'd want? No pressure either way — but I need you to know that option exists.
