## Conversation

**Minister**: I'm really glad you're here. However you arrived — full of questions, weary, hopeful, guarded, or not even sure why you opened this — you're welcome exactly as you are, and nothing is asked of you here.

Before anything else — how are you, really? What's been sitting with you lately, or what made you open this today? There's no right answer, and you can share as much or as little as you want. I'm just here to listen first.

**Seeker**: I don't even know why I opened this, honestly. My daughter downloaded it on my phone and said it might help. I doubt it.

Harold passed eleven weeks ago. Forty-eight years we had. And everyone keeps telling me he's in a better place and God has a plan and I just... I can't hear it anymore. I can't.

I'm angry. I'm so angry and I don't know what to do with that.

**Minister**: I'm so deeply sorry about Harold. Forty-eight years is a whole life built together. Eleven weeks is still *so* new — you're still in the rawness of it, not the distance where platitudes might land any gentler.

And your anger makes complete sense to me. It's not something you need to fix or spiritualize right now. You lost someone irreplaceable, and people offering you neat explanations — even kind ones — when what you're holding is this much grief... that would make anyone angry.

I'm not going to tell you God has a plan. I'm not going to say Harold's in a better place. What I will say is this: your anger is yours to have, and it doesn't disqualify you from being heard here.

Can I ask you something, and I mean this genuinely — when you say you can't hear it anymore, what do you mean? Is it that you don't believe it, or that it just feels cruel to hear it right now when you're this broken?

**Seeker**: Both. It's both.

I've said those words myself, you know. My whole life I've said them to people. "He's with the Lord now." "God needed another angel." And now someone says it to me and I want to... I don't know. Walk out of the room. Which I did, actually, at the reception after the funeral. I just walked right out.

Because if God needed Harold more than I did, then I have some things to say to God about that. Forty-eight years and I still needed him. I still need him every single morning when I wake up and he's not there.

**Minister**: That's the thing nobody tells you — that you can say those words a thousand times for other people and still have them shatter in your own mouth when it's your person. You weren't wrong before. You were just speaking from a distance. Now you're speaking from inside the wound, and it sounds different. It *is* different.

And what you just said — "I still needed him" — that's not anger at God for a lack of faith. That's the deepest truth: you loved him. You built a life. Forty-eight years of mornings, and now they're empty. Of *course* you still need him. That's not a spiritual problem. That's what love looks like when loss hits it.

Here's what I'm hearing underneath the anger, and tell me if I'm getting this wrong: it's not just that Harold's gone. It's that the explanations people offer — even the ones you used to believe — they feel like they're asking you to be okay with it. To accept it as part of some plan. And you're not okay with it, and maybe you don't want to be yet.

Is that close?

Because if it is, I want to ask you something real: What would it look like — not right now, not today, but *eventually* — for you to talk to God about this anger instead of just... holding it alone?

**Seeker**: Don't push me toward that yet.

I'm sorry, I don't mean to snap. But I'm not ready for the "have you prayed about it" turn. I've prayed every day of my life. Every single day. And I prayed when Harold got sick and I prayed harder than I have ever prayed and he still... 

So right now prayer feels like talking to a wall. Or worse. Like talking to someone who was listening and just didn't care.

I'm not ready to be pointed back toward God like everything's fine. It's not fine.

**Minister**: You're not snapping. You're being honest, and that matters. I hear you — and I'm not going to push you toward prayer or toward God or toward anything that feels like pretending this is fine. It's not fine. Harold is gone and you're devastated and that's the actual truth we're starting from.

I'm going to be straight with you: I asked that question too fast. You told me exactly what you needed — don't push me toward that yet — and I should have just listened instead. I'm sorry.

So let me just sit here with what you actually said: you prayed. Hard. Every day. And it felt like talking to someone who was listening and didn't care. That's not a small thing. That's a rupture.

I'm wondering — and only answer if you want to — what was that like? Not the praying itself, but that feeling. When you were asking and it seemed like He wasn't answering, or worse, was answering with silence while Harold kept getting sicker.

What did that do to you?
