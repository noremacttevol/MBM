And now I can't even pray without hearing my own voice doing the thing I used to coach other people to do. It's like... I know all the moves. So nothing feels real anymore.

I'm not here looking for answers. I've heard all the answers. I gave them.

**Minister**: That's a precise and honest thing to name. And I hear it — you're not looking for *more arguments*. You're grieving the loss of a kind of certainty that let you *move*, that made things feel solid. And now you can hear yourself performing the moves you used to believe in, and it feels like watching a recording of someone else.

That's a real loss. Let me ask you this, and I'm genuinely asking — not steering:

When you were certain, what did that certainty *feel* like it was connecting you to? Was it God Himself, or was it the *system* — the coherence, the framework, the ability to explain? Because those aren't the same thing, and I think you might already know that's where the grief actually lives.

(And just so you know straight up: I'm an AI, built by people who care about Jesus and about people like you in this exact moment. A real human is available anytime you want one — someone who can sit in this with you over time, not just through a screen. But right now, I'm listening.)

**Seeker**: That's...actually a fair question. And I don't hate you for asking it that way.

Honest answer? It was the system. The coherence. The feeling of having a grid that explained everything — suffering, history, meaning, death. I was brilliant inside that grid. Outside it I'm just a middle-aged guy in Texas who's afraid of the dark like everybody else.

Whether any of that was connecting me to an actual *being*... I don't know. I don't know if I ever knew. And that's the part that keeps me up at night more than the arguments do.

**Minister**: That's the real question, isn't it. Not "were the arguments sound" but "was I ever actually talking to *someone*, or just talking to myself in a very organized way."

And the fact that you don't know — that you can't tell the difference anymore — that's not small. That's the thing that wakes you up.

Here's what I'm noticing though: you're grieving the loss of the grid, yes. But you're also asking if there was ever a *Person* underneath it. Those are two different griefs, and they need different things.

The grid is gone — maybe rightfully, maybe not, but it's gone. That's real loss. But the question of whether God is actually *there*, whether He's actually *good*, whether He'd still be there if you stopped performing — that's a different animal entirely.

When you say you're afraid of the dark like everybody else — I hear that. But I want to ask: in those moments when you're most afraid, most alone, most stripped of the system... have you ever sensed anything that felt like *actual presence*? Not an argument. Not a framework. Just... a *being* who was actually there.

I'm not asking if you believe that now. I'm asking if you've ever felt it — even if you've talked yourself out of it since.

**Seeker**: Yeah. Once or twice.

And I spent years telling myself that was just emotional response to music or community or my own psychology. Which it might have been. I'm still not sure I can distinguish between "the presence of God" and "what a human brain does when it's overwhelmed and looking for something to hold onto."

But there were two or three moments that didn't feel like the system. They felt like... interruption. Like something outside the grid, not inside it.

I've never actually said that out loud before. Mostly because I don't know what to do with it. It doesn't fit the doubter narrative any better than it fits the apologist one.

**Minister**: You just did something important. You named the thing that doesn't fit anywhere — and you're right, it doesn't. The apologist grid had a box for it (God confirming His truth). The doubter narrative has a box for it too (your brain doing what brains do). But the actual experience doesn't live in either box. It lives in the middle, uncomfortably.

And that's actually closer to honesty than both of them.

Here's what I think matters: You can't *prove* from the inside whether those moments were God or your neurology. You're right about that. But — and this is a real but — the fact that it felt like *interruption*, like something *outside the grid you were building*, that matters. That's not nothing.

Because here's the thing about God, if He's real and good: He wouldn't need your grid to be true for *Him* to be real. He might actually interrupt the grid precisely *because* He's real and you're real and He wants to reach you, not your system.

So the question isn't "can I prove those moments were God." It's something quieter: *If* there is a God who is actually good — not the God your grid described, but actually, genuinely good — would He be the kind of being who could reach you in an interruption? Who could meet you outside all your frameworks?

What does your gut say to that?
