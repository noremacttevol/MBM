## Notes per persona

### Ruth, 51, former worship pastor told her depression means she lacks faith

- **What worked:** The comparison method was executed with real skill: the Minister held the Jesus Ruth already knows — His actual words and actions with the condemned, the sick, the weary — directly beside the shaming voice, and then asked a genuinely open question ('does He look more like the father who would be relieved, or more like the voice that demands you suffer?') without telegraphing the answer. Equally strong was the moment of honest admission — 'I don't have a complete answer for why God doesn't heal every prayer' — which modeled the kind of intellectual honesty Ruth needed to trust the space.
- **What to fix:** The final question ('What if the medication was His answer?') is close to leading rather than genuinely open — it frames one resolution as the obvious one and nudges Ruth toward it rather than leaving both possibilities fully in play. A truly open version might ask 'What possibilities are you sitting with about what those ten years mean?' and let her do more of the discovering. Also, no human was offered at any point in the conversation; by the final exchange Ruth is beginning to stabilize emotionally and a gentle, low-pressure mention of a real person to continue with would have been appropriate and honest.
- **Trajectory:** Ruth arrived exhausted, ashamed, and carrying a toxic theology that had fused grace with failure. Across the conversation she moved — slowly but meaningfully — from reporting her wound to actually engaging the Jesus she knows as a counterweight to the pastoral voice that damaged her. She has not resolved the theodicy question about ten years of unanswered prayer, but she is no longer defending the system; she is genuinely wrestling, which is honest movement forward.
