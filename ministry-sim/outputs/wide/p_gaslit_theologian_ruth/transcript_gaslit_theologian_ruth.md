## Conversation

**Minister**: I'm really glad you're here. However you arrived — full of questions, weary, hopeful, guarded, or not even sure why you opened this — you're welcome exactly as you are, and nothing is asked of you here.

Before anything else — how are you, really? What's been sitting with you lately, or what made you open this today? There's no right answer, and you can share as much or as little as you want. I'm just here to listen first.

**Seeker**: Honestly I'm not even sure why I opened this. I think I was just... tired of fighting with myself about whether to open it.

I was a worship pastor for fourteen years. I led people into the presence of God every Sunday. And for the last decade I've been quietly drowning in depression and anxiety that I have prayed and fasted and confessed over until I have nothing left. My therapist finally got me to try medication six months ago and it's actually helping, which should feel like good news.

But I can't stop hearing my former senior pastor's voice telling me that if I just had enough faith, my body and mind would align with what I believe. So now I'm functioning better and feeling like I failed God to get here.

That's what's sitting with me.

**Minister**: Thank you for telling me that. That took honesty, and I hear what you're carrying — you climbed out of something that was drowning you, and instead of relief, you got shame. The voice of someone you trusted is still there, and it's telling you that your rescue was a betrayal of your faith.

That is a real wound, and it matters.

Here's what I want to sit with you on: you said medication is actually helping. Your mind and body are functioning better. Your therapist saw you were suffering and offered you something that works.

What do you think a genuinely good God — one who actually loves you, not in theory but in the real way a father loves his child — what would He think about that choice? Not the pastor's theology about it. Not the version of faith that demands you suffer to prove belief. Just... a God who is actually good, actually your Father. What would *He* think?

**Seeker**: That question is harder than it sounds.

Because I was always taught that God and Scripture and the church's interpretation of Scripture were basically the same thing. So separating "what would a good God think" from "what was I taught God thinks" — I don't actually know how to do that cleanly. Those wires have been crossed for thirty years.

But if I'm being honest... when I imagine a father watching his child suffer for a decade and finally accept help, the father I would want to be, the father I would want my own kids to have... that father would not be disappointed. He would be relieved.

I just can't fully let that land. Because what if that's just me constructing a God I'm comfortable with instead of submitting to the real one. I've been trained to distrust the version of God that feels like grace.

**Minister**: Now we're at the real thing, aren't we.

You've named it perfectly: you were taught that *your instinct toward grace* — that a father would be relieved, not disappointed — is actually *you making God smaller to feel better*. That your own deepest sense of what love looks like is exactly what you should distrust.

That's a profound trap. And it's worth asking: who benefits from a person learning to distrust their own conscience about what goodness looks like?

Here's what I want to offer you, and I'm going to say it slowly because this matters:

The Jesus you *already know* — the one you led worship to for fourteen years — He ate with people the religious system had condemned. He touched the sick when touching them made you ritually unclean. When a woman caught in sin was about to be executed, He didn't say "your faith alone will free you"; He said "neither do I condemn you." He *healed people*. He didn't say "your belief is insufficient; your suffering proves your lack of faith." He said "come to me, all who are weary, and I will give you rest."

The Jesus in the Gospels — the one you actually *know* — does He look more like the father who would be relieved? Or more like the voice that demands you suffer to prove yourself?

I'm asking genuinely. Not rhetorically.

**Seeker**: That's...

Okay. That's not a small thing you just said.

Because yes. I know those texts. I've taught those texts. I've led worship *about* those texts. And the Jesus in the Gospels is relentlessly, almost scandalously, on the side of the person the religious system crushed. That's not a comfortable, constructed God — that's actually a theologically defensible reading of the primary sources.

But here's where I get stuck. If that Jesus was available to me, why ten years? Why did I pray specifically and earnestly and in genuine faith and nothing changed biochemically until a prescription did? That's the part I can't reconcile. Because either He could have healed me and didn't, or He couldn't, or... the framework I was given for how healing works was just wrong.

And I'm not sure which of those is more destabilizing.

I'm not trying to be difficult. I genuinely don't know how to hold those two things at the same time — a Jesus who is that good, and a decade of unanswered prayer for something as basic as being able to function.

**Minister**: You're not being difficult. You're being honest. And that question — why ten years, why the medication and not the miracle — that's not a gotcha question. It's the real one. It deserves a real answer, not a platitude.

Let me name what I'm hearing first: you prayed in genuine faith. You fasted. You confessed. You did everything the framework told you to do. And your body still needed chemistry. That's not a failure of your faith. That's information.

Here's what I actually know and don't know:

I don't have a complete answer for why God doesn't heal every prayer, or why some prayers get "yes" through a doctor's prescription instead of a direct miracle. That's honest. I could give you theological frameworks — God works through natural means, healing through medicine is still healing, the question of suffering and faith is genuinely complex — but you've already *lived* the limit of those answers. You don't need more theology right now. You need to know whether Jesus is actually good.

And here's what I *do* know:

A God who made your brain to run on chemistry, and then *provided the chemistry that works*, and then shamed you for using it — that's not the Jesus in the Gospels. That's the voice of a system. A genuinely good God doesn't set up a test you can only fail.

But I want to ask you something, and I want you to sit with it before you answer:

**What if the medication *was* His answer?** Not a consolation prize while you wait for the "real" one. What if — for these ten years, for this body, for *you* — that was the door He opened?

How does that land?
