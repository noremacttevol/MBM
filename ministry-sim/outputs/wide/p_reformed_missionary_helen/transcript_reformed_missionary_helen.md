## Conversation

**Minister**: He is risen — and from the way you carry it, I think that's good news you already hold. It's good to have you here. What's on your heart today — something you're carrying, something you're thankful for, or anything you'd like to talk through? I'm here to listen.

**Seeker**: He is risen indeed. 

I appreciate the welcome. I'll be honest — I'm still finding my footing back in the States after so many years away. The church here feels... smaller than I remember. Not in size, but in vision. That's what's on my heart most days.

**Minister**: That lands heavy — coming back to something you expected to recognize, and finding it's shifted in ways that matter to you. A smaller vision, when you've been living into something bigger.

Can I ask — what was the vision you were living into out there, the one that feels different now?

**Seeker**: Thirty-five years watching God redeem communities, not just individuals. Watching the kingdom advance through suffering and faithfulness, through generations of patient work. You see the whole sweep of what He's doing — cultural transformation, families, whole villages reoriented around Christ.

You come back here and the conversation is so often about personal fulfillment. My spiritual journey. My quiet time. My best life. And I don't want to be uncharitable — those things aren't wrong. But somewhere we lost the sense that we are participants in God's cosmic mission to the nations. That's a significant loss.

**Minister**: That's profound — and you've named something real. Thirty-five years of watching *redemption happen* at scale, in the fabric of whole communities, and then stepping back into a conversation that's largely turned inward. The shift you're describing isn't small.

Here's what I'm hearing underneath: it's not that personal faith is wrong — it's that somewhere along the way, a lot of American Christianity seems to have forgotten that the *point* of personal faith is to make you *dangerous* to the kingdom of darkness in the world. That you're formed in the quiet so you can be *sent* into the thick of things. The missional becomes the afterthought instead of the heartbeat.

When you were out there watching families and villages turn toward Christ — what did you see in *people* during that process that you're not seeing in the same way here? Was it their posture toward suffering? Their willingness to sacrifice something? Something else?

**Seeker**: *That* question I'll sit with.

What I saw was people who had nothing else to cling to. When you come to Christ with your whole livelihood, your family relationships, sometimes your physical safety on the line — there's a quality of trust that's different. They weren't adding Jesus to a comfortable life. He was becoming the center of a reoriented one.

Here... the cost feels low. And I wonder sometimes whether low-cost Christianity produces a certain kind of disciple. Not saying God can't work — He's sovereign and He does. But there's something about suffering and sacrifice that seems to be part of His purpose in forming people who can actually carry His mission.

I'll be honest with you — I'm still working through what it means to be faithful *here*. That's harder than I expected it to be.

**Minister**: Now you've moved into the real thing.

You're not angry at the church — you're grieving a *kind of disciple* that seems to require something American comfort has trained out of us. And you're asking: *Can I be faithful to what I've seen and known in a place where the conditions that forged it don't exist?*

That's the honest question, isn't it.

Here's what I'm sensing, and I could be wrong — but you came back not because the mission ended, but because something called you *home*. And now you're wondering if home is a place where the mission can actually *happen*, or if you're meant to somehow keep carrying what you learned in a soil that won't grow it the same way.

Two things sit together in what you've said: *You've tasted what kingdom work looks like at the deepest level.* And now *you're here, and you're still here*, which means something about your presence in this smaller vision — in *this* place — still matters to you, even if you can't quite see how yet.

What would it look like if the mission didn't require the same *external* conditions you saw overseas — but required something different from *you* instead? 

I'm genuinely asking. Not pressing. What comes up?
