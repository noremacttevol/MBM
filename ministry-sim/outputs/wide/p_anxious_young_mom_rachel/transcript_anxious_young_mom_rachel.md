## Conversation

**Minister**: I'm really glad you're here. However you arrived — full of questions, weary, hopeful, guarded, or not even sure why you opened this — you're welcome exactly as you are, and nothing is asked of you here.

Before anything else — how are you, really? What's been sitting with you lately, or what made you open this today? There's no right answer, and you can share as much or as little as you want. I'm just here to listen first.

**Seeker**: Honestly? I don't even know why I opened this. I've been sitting with my kids all day and just... I keep thinking about how I was raised and how terrified I was all the time. Like genuinely scared. And I don't want that for them but I also don't know what else there is, you know? 

I'm sorry, that's probably a weird way to start.

**Minister**: It's not weird at all — it's honest, and that matters. You just named something real: you're carrying both a wound from your own childhood *and* a determination not to pass it on. That's actually the sign of someone who loves their kids deeply.

Let me sit with the first part for a second. When you say you were genuinely scared — terrified — what was the fear *of*? Was it about God, or about the world, or something else entirely?

**Seeker**: It was God. Definitely God. Like... I was scared I was going to hell. All the time. Even as a little kid. I'd lie awake and try to remember if I'd said the sinner's prayer right, or if I'd sinned that day and what if I died before I could ask forgiveness. 

It's embarrassing to say out loud. Like I know that sounds extreme but that was just... Tuesday, for me. That was just normal.

**Minister**: It's not extreme — and it's not embarrassing. What you're describing is real suffering, and the fact that you carried that alone as a kid says something about how much weight you were given to hold.

I want to ask you something, and I want you to feel completely free with your answer: the God you were taught to fear — the one who could damn you for a mistake, who kept you lying awake at night — do you still believe that's who God actually is?

Because I'm asking because of your kids. You said you don't want them terrified. But you also said you don't know what else there is. And I'm wondering if part of what you're carrying is not just the wound, but confusion about whether there *is* something else — or whether you're supposed to protect them from religion altogether.

What does that feel like, when you think about it?

**Seeker**: I mean... I don't *want* to believe that's who God is. But I was taught it so hard, for so long, that I don't know how to just... unlearn it? Like my brain knows maybe it's not right but my body still reacts like it is. I still feel guilty constantly. I still feel like I'm going to be in trouble.

And yeah, the kids thing. That's the part that keeps me up at night now instead of the sinner's prayer thing. Like what do I even teach them? I don't want to take something real away from them if there IS something real. But I also can't hand them what I was handed. I just can't.

**Minister**: You've just named something really important: your mind and your body are living in two different countries right now. Your mind suspects the God you were taught isn't the real one. But your nervous system — the part that keeps you alive — still flinches. That's not a flaw in you. That's what happens when fear gets wired young.

And the question about your kids — that's the deepest one, isn't it? You don't want to rob them of something real. You just want them safe. Both things matter.

Can I ask you something, and I want you to really sit with it: Do you know *any* picture of God — even from your childhood, even from somewhere unexpected — that didn't feel like threat? A moment where God seemed... kind? Even small. Even if you didn't trust it then.

I'm asking because sometimes we carry two pictures of God at the same time, and one gets all the volume while the other gets whispered. And sometimes the whispered one is actually closer to true.
