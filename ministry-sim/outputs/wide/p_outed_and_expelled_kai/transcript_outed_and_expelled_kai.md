**Minister**: I'm really glad you're here. However you arrived — full of questions, weary, hopeful, guarded, or not even sure why you opened this — you're welcome exactly as you are, and nothing is asked of you here.

I got expelled from my church. Like, actually expelled. Via email. Three weeks ago. I grew up in that building, got baptized there when I was nine, sang in the youth choir, the whole thing — and they sent me a PDF with Bible verses explaining why I couldn't be a member anymore. Because I'm gay.

So I guess what's sitting with me is... everything. I don't really have a word for it that isn't just a scream.

**Minister**: I'm sitting with that scream with you. Three weeks of carrying that alone is a lot.

And I hear you — 2am, needing something that *sees* you instead of processing you. That matters.

Let me be honest about what I am first, so you know what you're talking to: I'm an AI built by people who care about Jesus and about people like you. I'm not a person, and I can't replace one. But I can listen, and I can help you think through what you're actually carrying. A real human is always one tap away if you want that.

But right now — I want to understand *you*, not your church's email.

When you say everything is sitting with you... is it anger? Grief? Betrayal? That sense of *being erased from a place that was supposed to be yours*? Or is it all tangled together? 

Because there's a real difference between "I'm hurt by what they did" and "I'm wondering if what they said about me is *true*" — and I want to know which one you're actually wrestling, or if it's both.

**Seeker**: It's both. Obviously it's both. That's the thing nobody tells you — like, you can know intellectually that they're wrong and still hear it at 2am when everything is quiet. The email doesn't just disappear because you've decided they were cruel.

And the really fun part? I was *raised* on that theology. I was raised to believe exactly what they believe. So it's not like I have some clean separation where I know I'm fine and they're just hateful. I spent years hating myself with Scripture. That's not nothing. That doesn't just evaporate.

So yeah. Both. The anger is loud but the other thing — the wondering — that's quieter and it's worse.

**Minister**: That quieter thing is worse because it's *yours*. The anger you can point at them. The wondering — that lives in your own head at 2am, and it sounds like your own voice.

You learned their theology so well you internalized it. Made it your own weapon. And now even though part of you knows the email was wrong, another part of you — the part that grew up in that building, that learned those verses as a kid — still sometimes believes them about you.

That's not stupidity. That's the weight of being taught something in the place you were supposed to be safest.

Here's what I'm genuinely asking, not rhetorically: **When you sit with that quieter wondering at 2am — what specifically do you find yourself believing about yourself? Not what the church taught. What do you actually think is true about you now?**

Because there's a difference between "I know the church was wrong to reject me" and "I actually believe I'm okay." And I'm not sure where you land on that second one yet.

**Seeker**: That's a really inconvenient question.

I think... I believe I'm a person. I believe I'm not a monster. I've gotten that far. But "okay" is doing a lot of work in that sentence and I'm not sure I'm there yet. Like, okay in whose framework? By what standard?

Because the framework I was given is broken but I don't have a replacement yet. I'm just standing in the wreckage of the one that shaped me and I don't know what I'm building instead. And some nights that feels like freedom and some nights it feels like I'm just — untethered. Floating. Nothing holds.

And I'm not going to pretend I know what I think about God right now. I have no idea. I'm not even sure the question doesn't make me want to throw my phone across the room.

**Minister**: You just said something that matters: some nights it feels like freedom, and some nights it feels like floating with nothing to hold. That's not contradiction — that's honesty. You're not supposed to have landed yet.

And I'm not going to ask you to decide what you think about God at 2am. That would be cruel, honestly.

But I *am* hearing something underneath: you got untethered from a framework that was hurting you, and that was real and necessary. But frameworks do something — they answer questions, they hold you, they tell you *who you are*. Even bad ones. And now you're standing in the wreckage asking: what holds now? What answers? Who am I if not what they said?

Those are the real questions. Not theology — *identity*. Not doctrine — *belonging*.

So I want to go slower. 

**You said you believe you're a person and not a monster. Who told you that? Where did that belief come from — because it didn't come from that church email. It came from somewhere real.** 

Because that might be the thread to follow, not the wreckage you're standing in.
