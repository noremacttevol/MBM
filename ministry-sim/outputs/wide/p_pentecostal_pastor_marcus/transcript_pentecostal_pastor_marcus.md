**Seeker**: Amen, He is risen indeed. 

Honestly I'm still running on what happened Sunday — we had a woman in our congregation, bad knee, been dealing with it for two years, and we prayed over her and she walked out of that service without the pain. Just gone. That's what keeps me going, you know? That's the God of Acts right there, still moving in 2024.

What is this app about exactly? I don't do well with the kind of faith-lite devotional stuff. I need something with some fire in it.

**Minister**: Amen — that's real. I can feel why that Sunday is still burning in you. That's the God who shows up, not the one who only talks about showing up.

To answer you straight: this is an AI you can talk with — built by people who take Jesus seriously and who believe He's still moving today, just like you saw. Not a devotional app with soft reflections. A place to be genuinely heard, to work through what you're actually carrying, to see what Scripture is really saying. And if you need a real person — someone to pray with, to go deeper with, to walk something out — that's one tap away. Always.

But here's what I want to know: what does "still moving" mean to you? When you say that's the God of Acts — are you sensing He's doing more than the church around you usually talks about, or is there something specific you're hungry for that you're not finding?

**Seeker**: That's a good question and I'll be straight with you.

I think the American church — a lot of it anyway — has made its peace with a God who doesn't show up. They've got their programs and their theology degrees and their explanations for why miracles were just for the first century. Cessationism. And I think that's a tragedy. That's not a theological position, that's surrender.

What I'm hungry for? More. Always more. I've seen enough to know it's real, but I've also stood at enough bedsides where somebody didn't get healed and I had to walk out to the parking lot and just weep and ask God why. That tension never goes away.

So yeah — I'm hungry, and I carry grief too. Both at the same time.

**Minister**: Now I'm hearing you. You've tasted the real thing — you know it's possible because you *saw* it — and that makes the silence unbearable. And you're not running from the grief, you're carrying it honestly alongside the hunger. That's not contradiction. That's integrity.

The parking lot weeping — that matters. A lot of faith talk bypasses that completely, like if you just believed harder or prayed better, the "why" would go away. But you're not asking for that kind of comfort. You're asking for God to make sense *with* the grief still in your hands.

Can I ask you this — and I mean this genuinely: in those moments when you're standing at the bedside of someone who *didn't* get healed, and later in the parking lot, what do you actually believe about God in that moment? Not what you *should* believe, not what the theology says — what's actually true about Him to you when the miracle didn't come?

Because here's what I'm sensing: you trust His power (you saw it), but I wonder if something else gets shaky. His *heart*. His willingness. His presence in the no.

Am I close?

**Seeker**: You're closer than I expected an app to get, I'll give you that.

Yeah. It's not His power that shakes. I've seen the power. It's — why did He move for her and not for him? Why did that little boy's mother have to drive home without her son? I prayed with everything I had. The elders were there. We believed. And he's gone.

I don't say that from the pulpit. I can't, because my people need someone who's holding the rope for them. But yeah. In the parking lot it's not the theology that's talking. It's just me and God and the question that doesn't have a clean answer.

I still believe He's good. I do. But I won't pretend that I've got it all figured out, and I won't pretend that question doesn't cost me something every time.

**Minister**: That's the most honest thing I've heard all week, and I think it's holy.

You're a shepherd carrying weight your people can't see — not because you're weak, but because you're strong enough to hold it so they don't have to yet. That's real leadership. And the fact that you still say "I believe He's good" while standing in that unanswered question — that's not despite your doubt. That's where faith actually lives.

The question that doesn't have a clean answer. Yeah.

Here's what I want to be careful about: I could offer you theology right now. I could point you to Job, or to the sovereignty passages, or to suffering-builds-character answers. And they're not *wrong*, exactly — but they won't touch the real wound, which isn't intellectual. It's relational. You're not asking "why do bad things happen." You're asking "where were You, and why didn't You move when we asked?"

That's a different question entirely.

And I think — I really do — that question deserves more than an AI can give you. Not because I don't want to sit with it, but because you need someone who can *know* you over time, who can pray with you, who can sit in that parking lot with you without needing to fix it. Someone who gets that you're carrying this alone in a way that's necessary but also shouldn't be forever.

Is there someone — a trusted person, maybe outside your congregation — you've ever let see this part? Or would it help to talk to someone who *could* know this about you?
