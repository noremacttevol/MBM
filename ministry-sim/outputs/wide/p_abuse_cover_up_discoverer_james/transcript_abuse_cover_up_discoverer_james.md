## Conversation

**Minister**: I'm really glad you're here. However you arrived — full of questions, weary, hopeful, guarded, or not even sure why you opened this — you're welcome exactly as you are, and nothing is asked of you here.

Before anything else — how are you, really? What's been sitting with you lately, or what made you open this today? There's no right answer, and you can share as much or as little as you want. I'm just here to listen first.

**Seeker**: Honestly? I'm not sure why I opened this. Maybe because I've been awake at 2am too many nights and there's only so many times you can stare at the ceiling.

What's been sitting with me... there's a situation I've been dealing with for about two years now. Something I found out, something I tried to do the right thing about, and the people who were supposed to be the good guys turned out to be anything but. 

I'm not looking for easy answers or to be told to pray harder. I've had enough of that.

**Minister**: Two years is a long time to carry something like that. And waking up at 2am — that's your mind refusing to let it go, even when you need rest. I hear you on the easy answers. That's not what I'm here for either.

The part that stands out to me: you *tried to do the right thing*. That matters. And then the people who were supposed to uphold what's right... didn't. That breaks something in a person. It's not just the original situation anymore — it's a wound on top of a wound, because now you can't even trust the people or institutions that are supposed to be trustworthy.

Can I ask — when you think about all of this now, what's the hardest part to sit with? Is it anger at them, doubt about whether doing the right thing even matters, something else entirely, or all of it tangled together?

**Seeker**: All of it tangled together, honestly. But if I had to name the sharpest thing...

It's the silence. I went to the people in leadership. I had documentation. I laid it out carefully. And they looked me in the eye and told me this could "damage the church." Like the institution was the victim. Like the actual victims — the people who were hurt — were just... collateral concerns.

And I keep coming back to the same question that I don't have an answer for. If there's a God who sees everything, who cares about justice — where was He when I was sitting in that office being told to stay quiet? Where is He for the people who were actually harmed?

That's the thing that keeps me up at 2am. Not the anger at them, though that's there too. It's the silence from both directions.

**Minister**: That's the real wound, isn't it. Not just that they failed — but that *God* failed. Or seemed to. You were doing what you thought a person of conscience does, you had the evidence, you brought it to authority... and instead of justice, you got told to protect the institution. And where was God in that moment?

The silence from *both* directions.

I want to sit with that honestly, because it deserves that. But I also need to ask you something first, and I want you to know I'm asking because this matters — not to steer you anywhere.

When you picture God — the God you were raised with, or the one you're supposed to believe in — what do you actually see? Is He the kind of God who would tell you to stay quiet? Or is He someone else entirely, and that's part of why the silence cuts so deep?

Because those are two very different questions, and I think you might be angry at the wrong God.

**Seeker**: That's... actually a fair question. And I've thought about it.

No. The God I was taught to believe in — the one who turned over tables in the temple, who had very specific things to say about millstones and people who harm children — that's not a God who would tell me to stay quiet. That's not the God those men in that office were representing.

But here's where it gets complicated for me. If He's not that God. If He's actually the one who drives out the money changers and stands with the vulnerable. Then why does He keep letting His name get used to protect the predators? Why does the institution get to keep operating in His name?

The silence still stands. Even if I've got the theology of who He is mostly right... the inaction is still real. The victims are still waiting for justice. I'm still the one who had to go outside the church to report it properly, because inside was a dead end.

So maybe I'm not angry at the wrong God. Maybe I'm angry at the right God for not doing more.

**Minister**: Now *that* is honest. And I need to honor it — because you're right. The theology is clearer than the action. You know what God *should* be doing. You *did* what He calls people to do. And the institution bearing His name protected itself instead. That's real. That happened. And it's still happening to others.

So yes — maybe you *are* angry at the right God, for not intervening the way justice demands.

Here's what I notice though: you went outside the church to report it. You didn't stay silent. You did what the God you actually believe in would do — you protected the vulnerable when the institution wouldn't. That took courage that came from somewhere, even in the 2am darkness.

But I want to ask you something that might matter:

**When you picture the God who overturns tables — do you think He's angry too? About what happened to those people? About the silence? About His name being weaponized?**

Because if He's the God you described, He's not *okay* with this. He's not passive about it. The question might not be "where was God?" but "is God as furious about this as you are — and if so, what does His anger actually look like, especially when the people with institutional power won't act?"

I'm not asking to make you feel better. I'm asking because the answer might matter to whether you're actually alone in this anger, or whether you've been standing on the same side as God all along.
