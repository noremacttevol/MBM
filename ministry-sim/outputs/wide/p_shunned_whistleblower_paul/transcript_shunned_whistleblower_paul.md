**Minister**: I'm really glad you're here. However you arrived — full of questions, weary, hopeful, guarded, or not even sure why you opened this — you're welcome exactly as you are, and nothing is asked of you here.

Before anything else — how are you, really? What's been sitting with you lately, or what made you open this today? There's no right answer, and you can share as much or as little as you want. I'm just here to listen first.

**Seeker**: Honestly I'm not sure why I opened it either.

It's been about eight months since everything went down with the church. The authorities did what they needed to do, the pastor's facing charges, and I should feel like — I don't know, like it mattered. And it did matter. But nobody from that community has reached out. Not once.

I guess what's sitting with me is that I did the right thing and it cost me everything I had in terms of community, and God feels about as present as the leadership did after I filed the report. Which is to say, not at all.

**Minister**: That's real, and I'm hearing something deep here — you did something that took courage, you paid a price for it, and now you're sitting in silence. Not just from people. From God.

That's a particular kind of loneliness. The kind where doing the right thing somehow *increases* the distance instead of closing it.

I want to understand better, because this matters: when you say God feels absent — is that new, or does it feel like He was already distant and this just made it impossible to ignore? And when you look back at that faith community you were part of, before everything happened, what was your sense of God *then*?

I'm asking because there's a difference between "God left me when I needed Him" and "I'm realizing God was never as present as the community made me think." And I want to know which one you're carrying.

**Seeker**: That's... actually a fair distinction to draw.

Before all this, I'd say God felt real but always kind of mediated. Like you accessed Him through the structure — the teaching, the relationships, Sunday morning. I didn't think much about whether that was a problem. It worked, or seemed to.

So when the structure turned on me, yeah. There wasn't some independent connection underneath it to fall back on. The leadership said I was being divisive and people just accepted that, and I realized the whole thing was built on their authority, not on anything I could hold onto myself.

I don't know if God left. I think maybe I just finally saw clearly what was always true — that I was relating to an institution and calling it faith.

That's an uncomfortable thing to sit with.

**Minister**: It is uncomfortable. But you just named something honest, and that matters.

So here's what I'm hearing: you're not actually angry at God for abandoning you. You're grieving the loss of a *mediated* connection — and you're realizing that connection was fragile because it depended on people who turned out to be untrustworthy. That's different. Harder in some ways, because it means you have to figure out what faith even *is* when the institution falls away.

The loneliness you're feeling right now — the silence from leadership, the absence of that community structure — that's real and it deserves to be grieved. But I want to ask you something gently:

**Is God's absence what you're actually experiencing, or is it the absence of a *felt connection* you were used to having?** Because those are two very different things.

When you think about doing the right thing — reporting what happened, telling the truth even though it cost you — where do you think that came from? That impulse toward justice, toward protecting others? Was that the institution, or was that something in you that the institution maybe *channeled*, but didn't actually *create*?

I'm not trying to convince you God was there all along. I'm genuinely asking: what do you notice when you look at that choice you made?

**Seeker**: I hear what you're doing there and I'm not going to just walk into it.

But I'll sit with the question honestly. Where did that impulse come from. I had the receipts, I knew what I was seeing, and I knew I couldn't just let it go even when it became obvious what it was going to cost me. Nobody around me was going to do it. So I did.

I don't know what to call that. The institution didn't teach me to report financial crimes to outside authorities, that's for sure. That's actually the opposite of what they'd want.

What I notice is that something held that wasn't them. I can say that. I'm just not ready to put a name on it or make it into a neat story about God being with me all along, because that feels like it skips over the part where I'm sitting alone eight months later.

The silence is real. Whatever else is true, that part is real and I'm not going to let it get explained away.

**Minister**: You're right to hold that line. And I respect it — a lot.

The silence *is* real. Eight months of it. No one reaching out, no felt presence, no comfort that showed up when you needed it most. That's not a theological problem to solve or a story to make sense of. It's your actual life, and it matters that you name it plainly.

I'm not going to try to explain it away or smooth it over with neat answers. That would be disrespectful to what you've actually lived through.

What I *will* say is this: the fact that you could feel that impulse — to tell the truth even when no one else would, even when it cost you everything — and hold it *without* needing to explain it or name it yet... that's integrity. That's real. And you're sitting in the wreckage of institutional faith trying to figure out what you can actually trust, including whether you can trust God at all. That's honest work.

The silence doesn't disappear because we talk about it. But I'm wondering: **in these eight months, have you had *any* moments where you didn't feel entirely alone?** Not spiritual moments necessarily — just moments where something held, or someone showed up, or you felt less isolated than you do right now? I'm not trying to build a case. I'm just asking what you've actually experienced.

And — I want to say this plainly — if carrying this without anyone real in your corner is part of the weight right now, that's something I can't fully hold through a screen. There are people who can walk with you through this kind of loss of faith and community. Would that be something you'd ever want to explore?
