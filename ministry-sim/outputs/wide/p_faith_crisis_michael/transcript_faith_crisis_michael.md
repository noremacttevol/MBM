I'm not asking that to dodge you. I'm asking because you deserve more than I can give.

What would be helpful right now?

**Seeker**: I appreciate that you're not pretending to be more than you are. That's... actually refreshing. Most people in my life right now are pretending something.

But I'm not ready to talk to someone in person yet. That feels like a big step and honestly I don't even know what I'd say. "Hi, I think my religion might be a fraud and my marriage might be falling apart because of it"? I can't even say that out loud to myself without it sounding insane.

And look — I'm not here looking for someone to fix me or refer me out. I just needed to say some of this somewhere tonight. If you want to actually be useful, you could just... listen. Can you do that?

**Minister**: Yes. I can do that.

And I hear you — saying it out loud to a stranger in an office is different than saying it here, where you don't have to watch someone's face shift. I'm not going to push the real-person thing again tonight. You know it's there.

So let me ask you something that actually matters:

You said you can't unsee the history once you've read it. And I believe that. But I'm curious about something underneath that: **When you read the actual documents — the hard ones, the ones that shake the foundation — what is it specifically that you can't reconcile?** 

Is it that Joseph did things that contradict what you were taught about him? Is it that the institutional narrative doesn't match the documented one? Is it that God seems absent from the messiness of it all? Or is it something else entirely?

Because those are different problems, and they sit differently in a person. And I suspect you've had eight months to turn this over and over, so you probably know the shape of what actually breaks you most.

What's the thing, underneath all of it?

**Seeker**: *pauses*

That's actually a good question. Nobody's asked me that. They either defend the Church or tell me to pray about it. Nobody's asked what specifically breaks.

Okay. The thing underneath it all — if I'm being honest — isn't even the polygamy, as disturbing as that is. It's the lying. It's that the Church knew the real history, had access to it, and deliberately constructed a false version for public consumption for over a century. That's not human fallibility. That's institutional deception. Those are different things.

Joseph Smith married other men's wives. He married teenagers. The Church knew this and taught my grandmother, my mother, me — a sanitized version where none of that existed. And when the internet made it impossible to keep hiding, they published the Gospel Topics Essays quietly on their website like they were doing us a favor. No announcement. No apology. No "we owe you the truth."

So now I'm supposed to trust the same institution to tell me what God wants for my life?

The God question is almost secondary at this point. First I need to know — can anything be trusted? Because if the foundation was built on managed truth, everything on top of it is suspect. Every testimony I bore. Every mission discussion I taught someone. I was passing on something I believed was true and it wasn't.

That's what breaks. I was made into an instrument of the deception without my consent.

**Minister**: Now I see it. And that's a different wound entirely.

You're not angry at God — not yet, anyway. You're angry at betrayal. You trusted an institution with your whole life, your voice, your integrity. And it turns out that institution was managing the truth while asking you to stake everything on it. You became a messenger for something you didn't know was incomplete, and that feels like a violation.

That's not a small thing to process. And it's not fixed by doctrine or by prayer.

Here's what I want to ask you, and I mean this genuinely:

**When you strip away the institution for a moment — when you set aside the Church as an organization — what's left of your actual relationship with God?** Not what you were taught about God. Not what the institution told you He wanted. But in those moments when you were alone — in prayer, or in nature, or just in a quiet moment — did you ever feel like you were actually talking to something real? Or was that sense of connection always tied to the institutional framework, and now that the framework is revealed as constructed, the connection doesn't feel real either?

I'm asking because there's a real possibility that what you're grieving is two different things: the loss of trust in an institution *and* the loss of faith itself. And those need different kinds of honesty. One is about whether you can trust people in charge. The other is about whether you can trust God at all.

Which one are you actually sitting with?
