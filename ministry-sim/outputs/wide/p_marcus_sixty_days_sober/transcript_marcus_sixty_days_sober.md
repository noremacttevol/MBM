## Conversation

**Minister**: I'm really glad you're here. However you arrived — full of questions, weary, hopeful, guarded, or not even sure why you opened this — you're welcome exactly as you are, and nothing is asked of you here.

Before anything else — how are you, really? What's been sitting with you lately, or what made you open this today? There's no right answer, and you can share as much or as little as you want. I'm just here to listen first.

**Seeker**: Sixty days today. My sponsor told me to do something different, not just sit in my room counting ceiling tiles. So here I am. Figured an app couldn't judge me, at least.

Honestly? I don't even know why I opened it. Maybe because it's got "faith" in the name and part of me wanted to... I don't know. Poke at something old. See if it still hurts.

It does, by the way.

**Minister**: Sixty days is real. That matters, and you know it — you're counting for a reason.

Your sponsor is right. And I hear you about the app — safer than a person, maybe. You can close it anytime.

But I want to be straight with you: yes, there's a faith angle here. And you opened it knowing that, even if you're not sure why. That took something.

When you say it still hurts — the faith part, I'm guessing — what's the shape of that? Is it anger at God, disappointment in what you believed before, something else entirely? You don't have to answer if you don't want to. But if you're willing to name it, I'm listening. Not to fix it or argue you out of it. Just to hear what you're actually carrying.

**Seeker**: It's not anger exactly. Anger would mean I think he's still paying attention.

It's more like... I grew up thinking God kept score, right? And I know what my score looks like. Thirty-eight years old, two failed marriages, missed my daughter's whole childhood basically, the drinking, the worse stuff I don't talk about. You don't come back from a scorecard like that. Not in the church I grew up in anyway.

So it's not really hurt like he did something to me. It's more like... I already know the verdict. Been a long time since I thought I was in the running.

**Minister**: That's the thing though, isn't it — you're sixty days sober and you opened an app called faith. Part of you is still in the running, even if your scorecard says otherwise.

But I hear what you're saying. A God who keeps score — that's a God who's done with you. That's a reasonable conclusion if that's who He is.

Here's what I'm wondering, and I want you to sit with this honestly: **Have you ever actually *seen* a God like that in the Bible, or is that the God your church taught you He was?**

Because there's a story Jesus told — it's in Luke 15 if you want to find it — about a father whose son torches his entire inheritance, disappears into the worst version of himself, and then shows up at the gate with nothing. Destroyed scorecard. Two failed marriages' worth of wreckage, basically.

And the father *runs* to him. Doesn't ask for an accounting. Runs.

I'm not asking you to believe that yet. I'm just asking: *Could* a God like that exist? Not the church version. The Jesus version.

What would that change about how you're reading your own sixty days?

**Seeker**: Man.

I've heard that story. Prodigal son, yeah. Heard it a hundred times in Sunday school. But the way they always told it... it felt like a fairy tale. Like the point was "see, just come back and everything's fine." And I'd look around at my actual life and think, that's not how anything works.

But you said something. The father *runs*. I remember that detail now. Didn't wait at the door. Ran.

I don't know what to do with that honestly. Part of me wants to say yeah but that's a story, that's not God looking at me specifically, Marcus, with my specific wreckage. 

And the other part... I don't know. The other part got quiet just now. And I don't trust quiet. Every time things get quiet that's when something hits me sideways.

So I'm not saying I believe it. I'm just saying I didn't immediately want to close the app. That's about as much as I got right now.

**Minister**: That's honest. That's enough.

And you're right to not trust the quiet — that instinct has probably saved your life before. But quiet *and* something stirring isn't the same as nothing. You know the difference.

Here's what I'm not going to do: I'm not going to keep talking at you about the prodigal son or God or any of it. You've heard enough words. What you're carrying right now — that question between the story and your actual wreckage, that part of you that got quiet — that's real. That deserves better than an app.

There's a real person I can connect you with if you want — someone who gets recovery, who gets faith, who can sit with you in that quiet and not try to smooth it over. Not a pastor, just someone who actually knows this territory. No pressure at all. But if that stirring gets bigger, or if you want to talk to someone who's actually lived some of what you have, that door's there.

For now: you got sixty days. You opened an app that scared you a little. You let yourself feel something about a father who ran.

That's not nothing, Marcus.

What do you need right now — from me, from anyone? And be real about it.
