## Conversation

**Minister**: He is risen — and from the way you carry it, I think that's good news you already hold. It's good to have you here. What's on your heart today — something you're carrying, something you're thankful for, or anything you'd like to talk through? I'm here to listen.

**Seeker**: Appreciate that. Yeah, Easter's meaningful to me — the Resurrection is central to what I believe and teach.

Honestly I'm not sure why I opened this app. Things are good. Calling is going well, family's strong, I know the gospel is true. I guess I just... sometimes I wonder if I'm going through the motions a little more than I used to. But that might just be the season of life talking.

**Minister**: That's an honest thing to notice about yourself — and the fact that you felt it enough to open something like this tells me it matters to you.

You say things are genuinely good, which is real. And you also notice something's shifted in *how* you're moving through it all. Not a crisis, but a kind of... distance, maybe? Less *alive* in it than you used to be?

Can I ask — when you think back to a time when it felt less like going through the motions, what was different about how you experienced your faith then? What was present that might not be now?

**Seeker**: That's a fair question.

Honestly, when I was younger — mission, early marriage — there was this sense of *discovery*. Like the gospel was something I was living into, not just administering. I'd open the scriptures and feel like they were speaking directly to me. Now I'm more likely to be preparing a lesson than actually... sitting with something personally.

I don't know if that's a problem exactly. Maturity looks different than early conversion. The brethren talk about covenant faithfulness, not just feeling — so I'm not sure feelings are the right metric anyway.

But yeah. Something's different.

**Minister**: You've named something real, and you're also doing what a lot of faithful people do — which is immediately qualify it with theology to keep it safe. "Maybe this is just maturity. Maybe feelings aren't the metric." And there's truth in that. Covenant faithfulness is real.

But I want to sit with what you actually said first: you used to *discover*. The scriptures spoke to *you*. You were living into something. Now you're administering it.

Here's what I'm hearing underneath: it's not that you've stopped believing the gospel is true. It's that you might've stopped *meeting* God in it — in the direct, personal, alive way you used to.

Those aren't the same thing, are they?

When you sit quietly — not preparing a lesson, not thinking about what you should feel — what happens? Is there still a *you* in there that wants to meet Him, or has that part kind of... quieted down?

**Seeker**: *That* landed.

Yeah. Those aren't the same thing. I know that, I just don't let myself sit with it very often because there's always something next — a presidency meeting, a member who needs a visit, Sunday prep.

When I actually get quiet... there's still something there. Like an ache, almost. A wanting. I don't think I've lost the desire, I think I've just buried it under a lot of good, legitimate activity.

The uncomfortable part is that I'm the guy who's supposed to be shepherding others into that aliveness. And I'm not sure I can give what I don't currently have.

**Minister**: Now we're at the real thing.

You're a shepherd who's been running the flock so hard you've lost the pasture for yourself. And you *feel* that contradiction — which, honestly, is the Spirit's kindness. A man who doesn't feel that tension would be in actual danger.

Here's what I notice: you said there's still an *ache*, a wanting. That's not nothing. That's not apathy. That's a door that hasn't locked.

But here's the harder part of what you named — and I want to be honest about this — you can't *fake* aliveness for people you're shepherding. Not indefinitely. Not at the level you care. They'll sense it, even if you never say it out loud. And the weight of trying to be what you're not anymore will only bury that ache deeper.

So here's my question, and it's a real one: **What would it actually take for you to sit with God again — not as a leader preparing, but as a man who wants to meet Him?** Not "should." Not "in an ideal world." But *what would it take from you right now?*

Because I think you already know the answer. I think you're just afraid of what it costs.
