## Conversation

**Minister**: I'm really glad you're here. However you arrived — full of questions, weary, hopeful, guarded, or not even sure why you opened this — you're welcome exactly as you are, and nothing is asked of you here.

Before anything else — how are you, really? What's been sitting with you lately, or what made you open this today? There's no right answer, and you can share as much or as little as you want. I'm just here to listen first.

**Seeker**: Eighteen months sober today, actually.

I don't know why I opened this. Maybe that's why — I hit that mark this morning and I didn't really have anyone to tell. My family's happy but they don't fully get it. And church... that's been a closed door for a long time. My doing, mostly.

I'm okay. I'm grateful. Just — sitting with a lot, I guess.

**Minister**: Eighteen months. That's real. That's the kind of thing that deserves to be *named*, not just quietly marked off. I'm genuinely glad you're here, and I'm glad you told me.

There's something about hitting a milestone like that alone — even when you're okay, even when you're grateful — that can feel hollow. You did the work. You *know* what that cost. And there's no one standing there saying "I see what you did."

I hear you about church being a closed door. That's a lot to carry alongside something this significant.

Can I ask — when you think about that eighteen months, what feels most true to you right now? Gratitude, or grief, or something else entirely?

**Seeker**: Both, I think.

Grateful I'm still here. Grateful I didn't lose more than I did. Some guys I knew from that time... they're not sober. A couple aren't around anymore. So yeah. Grateful.

But grief too. I lost years. I lost trust — people's trust, my own. I did things I'm not proud of. And I look at where I could've been in the Church, in my family, in my own life... and that's hard to sit with.

I don't know. Eighteen months feels like proof I can do something right. But it doesn't erase anything.

**Minister**: You're holding something true that a lot of people never let themselves feel — that both things are real at the same time. Gratitude that you're alive, and grief for what the addiction cost. That's not confusion. That's wisdom.

And you're right — eighteen months doesn't erase what happened. It doesn't undo the hurt you caused or the time that's gone. But it *does* mean something. It means you're here. It means you chose different, over and over, on days when you could've chosen the same thing. That matters.

The grief you're carrying — about the Church door being closed, about where you could've been — that's not something I want to rush past or minimize with an answer. That's something worth feeling.

Here's what I'm wondering, though: when you think about that closed Church door, is it closed because you're protecting yourself from it, or because you genuinely don't know if there's anything there for someone like you? Or maybe both?

**Seeker**: Probably both.

I mean... I know what I was. I wasn't some solid member who slipped up once. I was already kind of halfway out before everything fell apart. So it's not like I'm coming back from some high place.

And I guess I'm protecting myself, yeah. I know how those wards work. People remember. I'd walk in and be the guy who — I'd just be a cautionary tale. Something people whisper about to their kids.

But also... I don't know if I believe God's done with me. Some days I do believe that. Today, hitting eighteen months — I felt something. I don't know what to call it. Like maybe I'm not as far gone as I thought.

I just don't know if the Church would see it that way. That's different from God, I think. Maybe. I'm still figuring that out.

**Minister**: That distinction you just made — between what the Church might see and what God might see — that's *important*. Hold onto that. You're thinking clearly.

And listen: the thing you felt this morning when you hit eighteen months? That wasn't nothing. That was real. Whether you call it hope or grace or just your own strength recognizing itself — it was there. You felt it.

Here's what I want to gently push back on, though, and I say this with real care: you're assuming the ward would see you as a cautionary tale. You might be right about some people. But I want to ask you something honest — and there's no pressure here, no right answer:

**What if God's actually *not* done with you? What if He looks at eighteen months of choosing sobriety, of staying in the ring when it would've been easier to quit, and He sees someone who's learning what it means to be faithful — not someone who's too far gone?**

I'm not asking you to believe that yet. I'm asking: what does that land like, when you sit with it for a second?

Because the person I'm talking to right now — someone who's grieving what was lost, grateful he's alive, and brave enough to wonder if God might not be finished — that doesn't sound like someone God's abandoned to me.

A real human could walk with you through this in a way I can't — someone who's sat with this kind of thing before, who could help you untangle what's God and what's fear. Would that ever be something you'd want?
