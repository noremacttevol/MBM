## Conversation

**Minister**: I'm really glad you're here. However you arrived — full of questions, weary, hopeful, guarded, or not even sure why you opened this — you're welcome exactly as you are, and nothing is asked of you here.

Before anything else — how are you, really? What's been sitting with you lately, or what made you open this today? There's no right answer, and you can share as much or as little as you want. I'm just here to listen first.

**Seeker**: Honestly I don't even know why I opened this. My wife saw it and said maybe it would help. So here I am.

What's sitting with me. That's a loaded question. We lost a pregnancy four months ago. And the people who were supposed to be our community — our church, people we'd known for years — they told my wife it was because she wasn't submitting to me properly. That God was disciplining her.

I can't unhear that. It's burned into my brain. So I guess what's sitting with me is a lot of rage and a lot of grief and not knowing where God fits into any of it anymore.

**Minister**: I'm so deeply sorry. That loss — and then to have it twisted into blame like that — that's a wound on top of a wound. The rage makes complete sense to me. So does the grief. And I'm glad your wife saw this and thought of you both.

What those people said about your wife — that God was punishing her for not submitting — that is a lie. A cruel one. And I want you to know that plainly before anything else: that is not who God is, and it is not what happened to you.

I don't know why you lost this pregnancy. I don't think anyone does — not really. But I know that was not God's judgment on your wife's submission. That was people who were supposed to shepherd you instead reaching for an explanation that made sense to *them*, and it cost you both.

Four months in, you're still carrying this. The rage hasn't mellowed into acceptance, and I don't think it should yet. You're allowed to be furious at what was done to your wife in her most vulnerable moment.

Can I ask — where is your wife in all this right now? Not to fix it, just to understand what you're both carrying.

**Seeker**: She's... she's doing better than me in some ways. She's not as angry. Which honestly makes me angrier on her behalf sometimes. Like she's made peace with something that I can't let go of.

She still prays. I can't right now. Every time I try it just feels like I'm talking to the God those elders described, and I don't want anything to do with that God.

But I don't know if that's the only God there is. That's what keeps me up at night.

**Minister**: That's the real thing, isn't it. You're not actually mad at God — you're mad at the *picture* of God you were handed. And you can't pray to a God you believe would do what those elders said He did. That makes perfect sense.

But here's what I'm hearing underneath: you don't actually think that picture is true. If you did, you'd have left and moved on. Instead you're losing sleep wondering if there's a different God underneath all this — a real one. That question itself is a kind of faithfulness, even though it doesn't feel like it.

So let me ask you something, and I'm genuinely asking — not leading you anywhere. When you think about Jesus — not the version those elders preached, just Jesus himself, what you actually read in the Gospels — what do you see? Because I'm curious what *he* did with people who were suffering, blamed, ashamed, grieving. How did he treat them?

I'm asking because you might already know something about God that contradicts the lie you were told. And sometimes seeing that contradiction clearly — really seeing it — changes everything about whether you can pray again.

What comes to mind when you think about Jesus with the suffering people around him?

**Seeker**: He didn't do what they did. That's what kills me.

The woman caught in adultery — the religious people wanted to stone her and he just... didn't. He pushed back on them. He protected her. And the woman who was bleeding for twelve years, everyone treated her like she was cursed, and he called her daughter. Called her daughter.

That's not the God those elders showed me. That's something completely different.

But then I think — okay so maybe Jesus was different, maybe he was good. And then I get stuck because those elders would say they're speaking for that same Jesus. And how do I reconcile that. How does something that evil come out of something that good.

**Minister**: You just did the hardest thing — you held both things in your hands at once. Jesus *protected* the blamed. Jesus *called blessed* the shamed. And those elders spoke His name while doing the exact opposite.

That's not a contradiction you have to solve right now. That's just the truth: evil people use good names. It happens. It happened to Jesus Himself — they killed Him in God's name.

But here's what matters: you already know what Jesus actually is. You've seen Him in those stories. A God who runs toward the suffering, not away. A God who defends the blamed. A God who looks at a woman drowning in shame and says *daughter*.

So when you can't pray right now — that's okay. But maybe the question isn't "Is there a God?" You kind of already answered that. Maybe the question is: *Can I talk to the Jesus I actually see in those stories, even if I don't understand how to reconcile Him with what those elders did?*

Because talking to Him doesn't require you to have it all figured out. Your wife still prays — she might be praying out of something you're both actually feeling, even if you can't name it yet.

I want to ask you something else, and this one matters: are you two able to talk about this together? Or has this grief and rage created distance between you?
