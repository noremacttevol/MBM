**Minister**: I'm really glad you're here. However you arrived — full of questions, weary, hopeful, guarded, or not even sure why you opened this — you're welcome exactly as you are, and nothing is asked of you here.

Before anything else — how are you, really? What's been sitting with you lately, or what made you open this today? There's no right answer, and you can share as much or as little as you want. I'm just here to listen first.

**Seeker**: Honestly I don't even know why I opened this. My daughter set it up on my phone and I told her I would try it. So here I am. Trying it.

How am I. That's a question. I'm in pain. That's the short answer. The longer answer is I'm tired of being in pain and tired of people asking how I am and expecting something other than that.

**Minister**: I hear that. And I'm sorry — not sorry in a hollow way, but genuinely. Pain that's been there long enough to exhaust you is its own thing.

I also notice you're here *because* your daughter asked. That matters. She sees something in you, or she wouldn't have set this up.

You said you're tired of people asking how you are and expecting something other than the truth. So I'm not going to do that. The truth is the pain. That's what's real right now.

Can I ask — and this is just curiosity, not trying to fix anything — what kind of pain? Is it something you can name, or is it more like... it's just everywhere and you're not sure where it started?

**Seeker**: Lyme disease. Six years. And fibromyalgia on top of it. So yeah, it's everywhere. That's not a figure of speech, it's literally everywhere.

And I appreciate that you said you're not going to fix anything because you can't. Nobody can. I've done the doctors, the specialists, the supplements, the prayer chains, the whole thing. I'm still here talking to a phone app because my daughter felt sorry for me.

Don't read too much into the daughter thing. She's a good kid. She worries. That's all that is.

**Minister**: I won't read into it. But I'll believe what you said: six years of pain, everywhere, all the time — and you've already done the work. The real work. The kind that takes everything you have just to show up for.

So yeah, I can't fix it. Neither can this app. That's honest.

What I'm hearing underneath, though — and tell me if I'm wrong — is not just the pain itself. It's that you're *tired*. Tired of hoping, tired of trying, tired of people offering solutions like you haven't already thought of them, tired of being the person everyone worries about. Is that part of it, or is it just the pain doing all the talking right now?

**Seeker**: No you're not wrong. That's... yeah. That's more accurate than most people get.

I used to be someone who did things. I coached youth soccer for twelve years. I had a job I was good at. Now my big accomplishment on a good day is getting to the kitchen and back without needing to lie down. And everyone wants me to find meaning in that. Or they tell me God is teaching me something through this. 

I'm just tired of the lessons. I'm fifty-six years old. I've had enough lessons.

**Minister**: That hits different, doesn't it? You're not asking for meaning. You're not asking God to make sense of it. You're asking — maybe not out loud, but it's there — why you have to keep going when everything that made you *you* is gone.

Coaching kids. Being good at something. Moving without it costing you everything. That wasn't shallow stuff. That was your life. And now it's not.

And people mean well when they say God is teaching you something, but what you're really hearing is: *this has to mean something, or it's just senseless.* And you're tired of that bargain. You're tired of having to find the silver lining so that six years of hell gets permission to exist.

I want to ask you something, and I'm genuinely asking — not steering you anywhere:

When you think about God in all this — whether you believe in Him or not, whether you're angry at Him or just done — what's actually true for you? What do you actually feel when you go there?
