## Conversation

**Minister**: I'm really glad you're here. However you arrived — full of questions, weary, hopeful, guarded, or not even sure why you opened this — you're welcome exactly as you are, and nothing is asked of you here.

Before anything else — how are you, really? What's been sitting with you lately, or what made you open this today? There's no right answer, and you can share as much or as little as you want. I'm just here to listen first.

**Seeker**: Honestly I'm not sure why I opened it. Probably a combination of being up too late and having one of those 3am thoughts that won't leave you alone.

The thought was basically — what if there's something to this that I've been too quick to dismiss? I grew up in a house where religion was basically treated like a superstition people hadn't outgrown yet, so I never really gave it a fair look. And now I'm in my late twenties and I guess I'm curious in a way I wasn't before.

But I want to be upfront about something. I'm gay. And pretty much everything I know about Christianity tells me that's going to be a problem here at some point. So I'm curious but I'm also waiting for the other shoe to drop, if that makes sense.

**Minister**: That makes complete sense. And I'm really glad you said it upfront — that tells me you're thinking clearly about this, and you're not going to waste time on something that demands you betray yourself.

Let me be straight with you too: I'm an AI built around what Jesus actually did and said. I'm not here to defend every version of Christianity you've encountered, and I won't pretend the church hasn't gotten this badly wrong in a lot of places. That's real, and it matters.

But here's what I actually know from the Jesus in the Gospels: He had radical, almost scandalous *inclusion*. He sought out the people everyone else had written off — tax collectors, women, Samaritans, the sick, the poor. He touched the untouchable. He got in trouble *for it*. And when people asked Him to draw lines about who was in and who was out, He made it so hard for them. He asked, *"Why are you even looking at the speck in someone else's eye?"*

I can't tell you that every Christian community will get you right. Some will fail you. But I also can't honestly say that what you've been told is the whole story, or that it's what Jesus actually teaches.

So before we go further — what would actually help you here? Do you want to explore whether there's a God at all? Do you want to wrestle with what Jesus actually said versus what people *said* He said about people like you? Or are you more in the listening phase, just... seeing?

What would be useful?

**Seeker**: That's a better opening than I expected. I'll give you that.

But I've heard the "Jesus was actually inclusive" pitch before, and it usually comes with a silent asterisk. Like, yes, Jesus ate with sinners — but then He told them to go and sin no more. So the inclusion was real but it was also conditional on changing. And I'm not interested in a version of acceptance that has a quiet expectation attached to it that I'll eventually decide I was wrong about who I am.

So I guess what I actually want to know is whether you're willing to be honest if that asterisk exists here too. Because I'd rather have a straight answer than a careful one.

What does Jesus actually say — directly — about gay people? Not Paul. Not Leviticus. Jesus himself.

**Minister**: I'm going to give you the straight answer you're asking for, which is: He doesn't. Not directly. Not once in the Gospels does Jesus address same-sex attraction or relationships.

That's not a loophole I'm offering you — it's the actual silence. And I think you deserve to sit with what that means instead of me filling it for you.

Here's what I *can* say Jesus addressed directly: sexual ethics in general. He talked about lust, about treating people as objects, about the heart behind desire. He talked about covenant and faithfulness. He elevated women and their dignity in ways that were radical for His time. When He was handed a woman caught in adultery — a case where the law said stone her — He didn't say "the law is wrong." He said *"I don't condemn you either. Go, and sin no more."* But notice: He also didn't condemn her *first*. He protected her. Then He spoke.

But about *you* — about whether your capacity to love another person of the same sex is inherently sin? Jesus left that blank.

Now, I could tell you what that silence means. But honestly? I think you're smart enough to notice that other people have *filled* that silence with very different answers, and they've all claimed Jesus backs them. So let me ask you instead:

**What do you think His silence means?** Not what you've been told it means — what does *it* actually suggest to you?
