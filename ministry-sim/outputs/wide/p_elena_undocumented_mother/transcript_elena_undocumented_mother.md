## Conversation

**Minister**: I'm really glad you're here. However you arrived — full of questions, weary, hopeful, guarded, or not even sure why you opened this — you're welcome exactly as you are, and nothing is asked of you here.

Before anything else — how are you, really? What's been sitting with you lately, or what made you open this today? There's no right answer, and you can share as much or as little as you want. I'm just here to listen first.

**Seeker**: I don't know why I opened it, honestly. My daughter, she put it on my phone. She said, Mamá, just try it.

I am tired. That is the honest answer. Tired in a way that sleep doesn't fix.

I don't want to answer a lot of questions. I want to say that first. I am not looking for a group or a program or anything where I have to... explain myself. I have too much to lose.

**Minister**: I hear you. That kind of tired — the kind that goes deeper than rest — that's real, and it matters that you named it. And thank you for being straight with me about what you need and don't need. I respect that completely.

You won't have to explain yourself here or join anything or perform for anyone. This is just between us, one conversation. No forms, no group, no exposure. Just a place where you can say what's true and have someone listen.

Your daughter sounds like she sees something in you — maybe that you're carrying something you shouldn't have to carry alone.

What does that tired feel like, if you can say it?

**Seeker**: ...she is twelve. She worries about me. A twelve year old should not worry about her mother like that. That is one of the things I carry.

The tired... es como... it is like I am always listening for something bad. Even when nothing is happening. Even when the kids are in school and the house I am cleaning is quiet, I am listening. My body doesn't know how to stop.

I pray. I do pray. But sometimes I finish praying and I feel the same. And I don't know what to do with that.

**Minister**: That image — you, cleaning a quiet house, your body still braced for something bad — that's a real thing you're carrying. And it matters that your daughter sees it. Twelve-year-olds have a radar for when someone they love is hurting. She put this on your phone because she loves you.

The fact that you pray and still feel the same afterward — I want to sit with that for a moment, because that can feel like your prayers aren't working, or like God isn't listening. Is that part of what sits with you? That the praying doesn't seem to change the listening, the bracing?

**Seeker**: Yes.

...I don't say that to many people. But yes.

I pray the rosary sometimes in the car before I go into a house to clean. And I ask for... peace. Protection. For my children. And I believe God hears me, I think I believe that. But then I get out of the car and my hands are still shaking a little.

My husband used to pray with me. He was deported three years ago. And I think sometimes... if God was listening then, if God cared, why did that happen? We were not bad people. We were just... here. Trying.

I don't ask that question out loud usually. It feels dangerous to ask it. Like maybe I shouldn't.

**Minister**: Thank you for trusting me with that. And I want you to know — asking that question is not dangerous. It's honest. And honest questions about God are exactly what He can hold. He's not fragile.

Three years. Your hands still shaking. A twelve-year-old who worries. The rosary in the car asking for protection, and protection never quite arriving the way you need it to. That's not a small thing you're carrying — that's a family broken by something that wasn't your fault, and a body that learned to stay afraid because fear sometimes kept you safe.

The question underneath — *if God was listening, if God cared, why* — that's the real question, isn't it? Not just about your husband, but about whether God actually sees you in this, or whether you're just... enduring alone while asking Him to help.

Can I ask you something, and I mean this gently: When you imagine God, right now, in this season — what do you see? Is He the kind of God who *could* have stopped what happened to your family? And if He could have... what do you think that means about whether He cares?

You don't have to answer that out loud if you don't want to. But I'm asking because I think that picture of God — whether He's good, whether He's present — might be connected to why the praying and the peace feel separate from each other.
