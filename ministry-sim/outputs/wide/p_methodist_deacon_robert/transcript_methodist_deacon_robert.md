## Conversation

**Minister**: He is risen — and from the way you carry it, I think that's good news you already hold. It's good to have you here. What's on your heart today — something you're carrying, something you're thankful for, or anything you'd like to talk through? I'm here to listen.

**Seeker**: He is risen indeed. I appreciate the welcome. 

I'll be honest — I'm not entirely sure what I'm doing on an app like this. I've got my congregation, my Wednesday morning prayer group, forty-five years of walking this road. But my granddaughter set this up for me, so here I am. 

Nothing particular on my heart today. More curiosity than anything else, I suppose.

**Minister**: I love that — forty-five years is a deep well, and it's a beautiful thing that your granddaughter thought of you. And curiosity is honest; that counts for something.

So let me ask you this: what *did* she say about it? What was she hoping you might find here, or what did she see in you that made her want you to try it?

**Seeker**: *chuckles a bit* She said it might give me someone to talk to about faith outside of my pastoral role. I think she worries I spend all my time giving and not much time receiving, as she put it.

She's not entirely wrong about that. When you're a deacon, people come to you. You're the one offering the reflection, the prayer, the visit to the hospital. It's a different posture than sitting and being — I suppose the word is — tended to.

I'm not sure I know quite how to do that, frankly.

**Minister**: That's a real thing she's named — and I think she sees something true. Forty-five years of tending to others is sacred work. But a person can pour from an empty cup for only so long before they don't quite know what their own thirst looks like anymore.

Here's what I'm curious about: when was the last time you actually *sat* with something — not to fix it or shepherd someone through it, but just to feel it, to let it breathe? Not in a crisis or a hospital visit, but in your own life?

**Seeker**: *pauses a moment before responding*

That's a fair question. And I notice I want to give you a tidy answer — something about my morning devotional or a particular hymn — and I should probably resist that impulse.

If I'm honest... it's been a while. There was a funeral last month, a man I'd known for thirty years, and I conducted the service and said the right things and held his widow's hand. And somewhere in the middle of all that I realized I hadn't actually grieved him myself yet. I'm not sure I have.

I believe that's what my granddaughter is seeing, perhaps.

**Minister**: There it is. That's the real thing, isn't it.

Thirty years with someone, and you were the shepherd at his funeral — which means you were holding space for everyone *but* yourself. And grief doesn't wait for convenience. It just... sits there.

I want to honor that you felt safe enough to name it here instead of reaching for the tidy answer. That matters.

But here's where I'm honest with you: what you're carrying right now — real grief, the weight of being a giver for forty-five years, the loneliness of that posture — that deserves more than an app. It deserves a person. Someone who can sit with you over time, not just in this moment. 

There are real people connected to this app, trained to listen, who could walk with you through this. Would that be something you'd consider? Not instead of what your granddaughter hoped for — but in addition to it. Because you deserve to be tended to by someone who can actually be present.

What do you think?
