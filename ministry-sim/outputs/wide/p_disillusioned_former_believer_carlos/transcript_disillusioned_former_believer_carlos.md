**Seeker**: Honestly? I don't even know why I opened it. Probably just a weak moment. 

My sister passed eight months ago. Cancer. We prayed for two years straight — my whole family, her whole church, hands on her, anointing oil, the whole thing. And she died anyway. 

So I'm not really here looking for answers. I've heard the answers. I just... I don't know. Maybe I wanted to say that out loud somewhere that isn't my mom's house, where everyone's still acting like God had a plan.

**Minister**: I'm so sorry about your sister. Two years of prayer, your whole family, your church — and then loss anyway. That's not a weak moment that brought you here. That's honest.

And I hear what you're saying about the answers. You've sat with people who mean well, who reach for the frame that God had a plan. Right now that frame doesn't fit what you're carrying, and I won't pretend it does.

Eight months in — that's not a lot of time for something this heavy. Your sister is still so close.

Can I ask — when you think about God right now, what's actually there? Not what you're supposed to think, not what your mom believes, but what *is* there for you when you let yourself feel it?

**Seeker**: When I let myself feel it?

Rage, mostly. 

Not like... philosophical doubt. It's not academic for me. It's — I genuinely believed. I mean I *really* believed. I gave that everything. I prayed in tongues over her hospital bed. I fasted. I told her God was going to heal her because I *believed* He was going to heal her.

And now she's gone and I have to live with the fact that I looked her in the eyes and told her that.

So when I think about God right now... there's either nothing there, or there's something that watched all of that and just didn't care. And I'm not sure which one is worse.

**Minister**: That's the real thing, isn't it. Not *whether* God exists — it's *what kind of God* would watch that and do nothing. Would let you promise her healing you believed was coming. Would let you stand there in faith and then leave you holding that.

The rage makes complete sense. You weren't casual about this. You were all in. You meant every word to her.

And now you're living with two unbearable options: a God who isn't there, or a God who saw everything and didn't move.

I want to say something, and I mean this gently — I'm not going to try to fix this or argue you toward faith right now. That's not what you need. But I do want to ask you something that might matter.

Of everything you believed about God before — the God you prayed to over your sister's bed — was He the kind of God who *had* to heal everyone, or the kind who *could* choose not to and still be good? I'm asking because I want to understand what you actually believed about Him then, not what a church script says. 

What was *your* God like, before the loss?

**Seeker**: That's... actually a harder question than I expected.

My God back then was — he was close. Like genuinely close. Not distant. The kind that shows up, that moves, that *does things*. That was the whole point of being Pentecostal, right? Not some hands-off deity. A God who heals, who speaks, who intervenes. That was the entire premise.

So yeah. In my theology, in the thing I actually believed? He healed people. That was real. I'd seen it — or I thought I'd seen it. Testimonies, things in our church that seemed... I don't know, real at the time.

So I wasn't operating from some framework where God might choose not to. I was operating from "He *will*, because that's who He is, and we have enough faith, and we're asking."

Which means either I was lied to about who He is. Or the whole thing was just... people getting better sometimes and calling it God.

I haven't said that second part out loud before. That maybe I was just seeing patterns where there weren't any.

**Minister**: That's a significant thing you just named — and it took real honesty to get there.

So you're sitting with both possibilities at once: either you were told about a God who isn't actually like that, or the whole framework of seeing God move was something your community constructed together, and your sister's death is what broke the pattern enough that you can't unsee it.

Both feel like a betrayal. One is a betrayal by God. One is a betrayal by the people you trusted to tell you the truth about Him.

Here's what I'm noticing, though: you're asking genuinely hard questions about what you actually saw versus what you were told to see. That's not rage clouding your thinking — that's clarity, even though it hurts.

Can I ask something else, and I want you to sit with it without answering right away if you don't want to:

When you think about the healings you *did* see in your church — the ones that seemed real at the time — do you think they were *definitely* all coincidence and pattern-making? Or is it possible some were real *and* your sister's death is still real, without one canceling out the other?

I'm not trying to rebuild your faith. I'm asking because you seem like someone who actually wants to know what's true, not just what's comforting. And sometimes the truth is harder than "it was all false" or "it was all real." Sometimes it's both.
