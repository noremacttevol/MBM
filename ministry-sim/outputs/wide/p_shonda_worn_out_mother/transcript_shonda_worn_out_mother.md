I'm sorry. I don't know why I just said all that to an app.

**Minister**: Don't apologize. You said it because you needed to, and because somewhere in you there's still someone reaching — even if it's 2am and you're exhausted and you're not sure what you believe anymore.

That framework you had — it wasn't nothing. It held you for a long time. And then Amara died, and the thing that was supposed to hold you through the hardest thing didn't feel like it held at all. That's not a small betrayal. That's the ground moving under your feet.

So I need to ask you something, and I need you to know I'm asking it because I actually want to know, not because I'm trying to steer you toward an answer:

When Amara was alive — before she drowned — did you feel like God was good? Not like He was managing the universe perfectly or like everything happened for a reason. Just... was there a time when you felt loved by Him, or cared for, or like He saw you?

I'm asking because there's a difference between "my belief system failed me" and "I don't think God is actually good." And I think you might be grieving both things at once, and they're not actually the same thing.

But I want to hear what *you* think first.

**Seeker**: Yeah. I did.

I really did. I used to feel... held. That's the only word for it. Like somebody had me. I remember sitting in church when I was pregnant with Amara and just feeling so sure. So sure that she was a gift and that we were seen and that it meant something.

And now I don't know if that was real or if I just needed to feel that way so I did.

Because if He was good and He saw us... where was He. That's what I keep coming back to. Where was He when she went under. I was right there. I turned around for two minutes. Two minutes. And He didn't... He just...

I can't finish that sentence.

I don't know. Maybe both things. Maybe I'm grieving both. I hadn't thought about it that way.

But I'll be honest with you — I've had people try to explain this to me before. Pastors, friends, my mother. And every single explanation made it worse. So if that's where this is going I'd rather just stop now.

**Minister**: I hear you. And I'm not going to explain it away or hand you a reason why it happened. There is no reason that fits. Amara drowned. You turned around for two minutes. And the God you felt holding you didn't stop it. That's the thing you're carrying, and no sermon makes that smaller.

So I'm not going there.

But I do want to sit with what you just said: you felt held. You felt *seen*. That wasn't nothing, and it wasn't just you needing it so badly you invented it. A two-year-old drowning doesn't erase what was real before it happened.

Here's what I'm actually wondering: Is it possible that the God who held you when you were pregnant with Amara — the one who felt good and present and real — is still real, even though He didn't stop her from drowning? 

Not because there's a good reason for it. Not because it was "meant to be" or "part of a plan." Just... is it possible He's still good, even though this happened and it was the worst thing and He didn't prevent it?

Because that's actually a different question than "why did He let her die." And I think you might need to know the answer to *that* one before anything else matters.

What comes up for you when I ask it that way?
