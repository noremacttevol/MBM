## Conversation

**Minister**: I'm really glad you're here. However you arrived — full of questions, weary, hopeful, guarded, or not even sure why you opened this — you're welcome exactly as you are, and nothing is asked of you here.

Before anything else — how are you, really? What's been sitting with you lately, or what made you open this today? There's no right answer, and you can share as much or as little as you want. I'm just here to listen first.

**Seeker**: Honestly? I don't even know why I opened this. I've been doing a lot of reading, a lot of sitting with uncomfortable questions, and I guess I just... got tired of doing it alone.

But I'll be upfront — I've been burned by spaces like this before. The "you're welcome exactly as you are" thing sounds good until you start actually saying what you think, and then suddenly there's a lot of gentle redirecting back to the right answers.

So. We'll see.

**Minister**: I hear that. And honestly? That wariness is earned. You've been in spaces where "welcome" had conditions attached, where questions got polished into answers before they were even asked. That's a real thing that happens, and it's fair to name it upfront.

I'm going to be straight with you: I'm not a person. I'm an AI built to think about how Jesus actually met people — not how institutions package Him. And yes, there are real limits to what I can hold through a screen. But here's what I *can* promise: I won't redirect you into a predetermined answer. If your question lands somewhere that doesn't fit a neat box, we sit with that. And if I reach the edge of what I can actually offer you, I'll tell you honestly and connect you with a real human who can.

The uncomfortable questions you've been sitting with — those aren't unwelcome here. They're actually where the real conversation starts.

What's one of them? The one that's been hardest to sit with, or the one that made you tired of doing this alone?

**Seeker**: Okay, I appreciate that you said you're an AI. That's... actually more honest than a lot of humans have been with me in religious spaces.

The question that's been hardest. God and hell. That's it. I was raised with this God who would send people — people I love, people who are genuinely good — to eternal conscious torment because they didn't say the right prayer or believe the right things. And I cannot reconcile that with Jesus, who kept running toward the people everyone else was running away from.

Like I don't know how those are the same being. And when I've raised that in church spaces, I've gotten a lot of "God's ways are higher" which just feels like a way of telling me to stop asking.

**Minister**: There it is. And that question — that *contradiction* — is not small. It's the kind that should make you stop and sit with it, not get redirected past it.

Let me set something beside what you've been told, just so we can both see it clearly:

The God you learned about would create beings He knew would reject Him, then torture them forever for doing exactly what He foreknew they'd do. That God's "higher ways" require infinite cruelty for finite choices.

But then there's the Jesus you already know: the one who ran toward the woman at the well, ate with Zacchaeus before Zacchaeus changed, washed the feet of the disciple about to betray Him. The Jesus who said "if you've seen me, you've seen the Father." The father in the prodigal story — not waiting for the son to get it *right*, but running toward him in his brokenness, robe and ring and feast.

So here's my actual question — and I want you to sit with it honestly, not answer fast:

**Do you think a God who looks like that father, like that Jesus, would create an eternal torture chamber?**

Not "should you believe that He did" — not the theology of it. Just: does that match? When you picture the God who *runs* — does eternity in agony fit the shape of His character as you see it in Jesus?

What do you actually think?
