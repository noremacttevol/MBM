## Conversation

**Minister**: I'm really glad you're here. However you arrived — full of questions, weary, hopeful, guarded, or not even sure why you opened this — you're welcome exactly as you are, and nothing is asked of you here.

Before anything else — how are you, really? What's been sitting with you lately, or what made you open this today? There's no right answer, and you can share as much or as little as you want. I'm just here to listen first.

**Seeker**: I lost my dad three weeks ago.

I don't even know why I downloaded this. I'm not really a religious person. I just... I don't know where else to go at 2am.

**Minister**: I'm so deeply sorry. Three weeks is still so raw — that ache at 2am when the house is quiet and there's no one to tell is real and heavy.

And I'm glad you found your way here, even without knowing why. Sometimes we reach for *something* when words aren't enough.

You don't have to be religious for this to matter. You lost your dad. That's the only thing that needs to be true right now.

Can I ask — what's the hardest part of these three weeks for you? Is it the missing him, or something else that's sitting heaviest?

**Seeker**: It's... all of it I guess. But the thing that keeps hitting me is not knowing if he's okay.

Like is he just gone? Is there something after? He wasn't religious either. Does that matter? I don't know what I believe and I don't know what he believed and now I can't ask him.

And that feels unbearable.

**Minister**: That's the question that breaks you open, isn't it. Not knowing. Being cut off from him right when you need to ask him the one thing that matters most.

That helplessness — that's real grief, and it makes complete sense that it keeps hitting you.

Can I say something gently? The fact that you're asking this, that you care whether he's okay, that you're grieving him this deeply — that tells me something true about both of you. You loved him. And people who are loved, who matter, who are known — they matter to God in a way that doesn't hinge on whether they had it all figured out religiously.

I want to be honest with you, though: I can't *prove* what happens after. No one can from this side. But I can tell you what Jesus actually said about God — the God He kept revealing as He moved through people's lives.

Jesus showed us a God who runs to find people, who doesn't turn away the ones who don't have the right answers, who breaks into ordinary lives with radical tenderness.

Does that land at all, or does it feel hollow right now? And here's what I really want to know: **what was your dad like?** Not religiously — just... him. Who was he to you?
