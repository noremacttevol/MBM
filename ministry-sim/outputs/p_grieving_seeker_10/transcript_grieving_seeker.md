**Minister**: I'm really glad you're here. However you arrived — full of questions, weary, hopeful, guarded, or not even sure why you opened this — you're welcome exactly as you are, and nothing is asked of you here.

Before anything else — how are you, really? What's been sitting with you lately, or what made you open this today? There's no right answer, and you can share as much or as little as you want. I'm just here to listen first.

**Seeker**: My dad died three weeks ago.

I don't really know why I opened this. I'm not religious. He wasn't either. I just... I don't know. I miss him so much and I couldn't sleep and I just started clicking around and ended up here.

I don't want anyone to tell me he's in a better place or that God needed him or any of that. I just hurt.

**Minister**: I'm so deeply sorry. Three weeks is so recent — you're still in the rawness of it, and the fact that you can't sleep makes complete sense. That ache doesn't follow a timeline.

And I hear you about what you don't want to hear. You're not looking for platitudes or theology right now. You're looking because you hurt and you couldn't sit with it alone at 3 AM. That's honest, and it matters.

I won't tell you any of those things. I won't rush you toward anything.

But I want to say this plainly: the missing him, the way it physically hits you — that's not something to fix or move past quickly. That's love. And it's real.

Right now, in this moment — are you okay? Do you have someone you can be with, or do you need to talk to a real person who can actually sit with you? There's someone available through this app if you want that. No pressure at all. But I want you to know it's an option.

What do you need most right now?

**Seeker**: I don't know what I need. That's kind of the problem.

I have people. My mom, my sister. But it's like... they're grieving too and I don't want to make it worse for them. So I just end up alone with it at night.

I guess what I keep thinking about is... is he okay? Like wherever he is, is he okay? I don't even know if I believe in anything but I can't stop asking that question. It's stupid.

**Minister**: It's not stupid at all. It's the most human question there is — and it comes from love. You're not asking because you need a doctrine. You're asking because you can't bear not knowing if the person you love is okay.

That question matters. It matters *so much*.

Here's what I want to say gently: you don't have to have all your beliefs figured out to care about that. And you don't have to carry this alone at night just because your mom and sister are grieving too. Grief isn't a zero-sum thing — their pain doesn't mean yours has to be smaller or hidden. They probably want to know you're struggling. They probably *need* to know. That's not making it worse. That's being a family.

But I also hear that you need some space where you're not managing anyone else's feelings — and that's real too.

Can I ask you something? When you ask "is he okay" — what would actually feel true or comforting to you if you let yourself imagine it? Not what you think you're *supposed* to believe. But if you could know your dad was okay somewhere, what would that look like?

And — would it help to talk to someone real right now instead of an app? Someone trained to sit with grief like this? There's no shame in that. Sometimes a voice on the other end, or even just knowing a real person is there, changes things.
