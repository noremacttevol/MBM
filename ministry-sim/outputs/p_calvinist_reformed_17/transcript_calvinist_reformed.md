## Conversation

**Minister**: He is risen — and from the way you carry it, I think that's good news you already hold. It's good to have you here. What's on your heart today — something you're carrying, something you're thankful for, or anything you'd like to talk through? I'm here to listen.

**Seeker**: He is risen indeed. I appreciate the greeting.

Honestly I'm not here looking for pastoral warmth — I engage with these faith apps mostly out of curiosity about what theology they're actually operating from. So I'll be direct: what's the framework here? Are we talking historic Reformed orthodoxy, or is this more of a therapeutic "God loves everyone equally" kind of platform? Because those aren't the same thing, and I'd rather know upfront.

**Minister**: I respect that directness — and I'll match it.

The framework here isn't built to defend a particular denominational system. You won't find me arguing for or against Calvinism, Arminianism, or other classical categories. Instead, this is centered on *what Jesus actually revealed* — a God who is fundamentally good, who runs toward the lost rather than away from them, who makes Himself known personally and keeps His word.

That said: I'm not a neutral observer. I operate from a specific theological tradition that takes seriously both the historic creeds *and* the possibility that God continues to speak. But I'm going to earn your trust on the Jesus part first — the God whose character is actually *readable* in the Gospels — before I'd ever ask you to consider anything beyond that.

So the honest answer is: **this is neither pure Reformed nor pure therapeutic.** It's Christocentric. It assumes God *is* good in the way Jesus showed, and that matters for everything.

Does that framework — starting there, with what Jesus actually *revealed* rather than with a system — make sense as a place to talk? Or would you rather know the institutional backstory first? I can be straight about that too, if you're asking.

What would actually be useful for you?

**Seeker**: Christocentric — fine, that's a reasonable starting point. Though I'd note that Calvin was thoroughly Christocentric too, so that word alone doesn't settle much.

Here's what I'd push back on already: you say God is "readable" in the Gospels as running toward the lost. Okay — but Jesus also says in John 6 that no one can come to Him unless the Father draws him, and that He will lose none of *all that the Father has given him*. That's not a God running toward everyone indiscriminately. That's sovereign election, right there in Christ's own words.

So when you say "what Jesus actually revealed" — I want to know if you're willing to let that include the hard texts, or if we're doing selective Jesus where we keep the prodigal son and quietly shelve John 6. Because that's where a lot of these conversations go, and it's not intellectually honest.

**Minister**: You've named the actual move, and I appreciate you calling it out before we fall into it.

You're right: John 6 is Jesus's own voice. So is Luke 15. A framework that keeps one and discards the other *isn't* reading what Jesus revealed — it's editing Him. That's the failure you're spotting, and it matters.

Here's where I land honestly: **I don't have a systematic answer that resolves that tension cleanly.** John 6 says some are drawn; Luke 15 says the Father *wants* the lost found and celebrates when they are. Both are spoken by Jesus. Both seem true. The moment I construct a system that makes one clearly "win" over the other, I'm doing the shelving you're describing — just from a different direction (either Calvinism shelves the reckless love, or Arminianism shelves the sovereign particularity).

So here's my actual posture: I'm willing to hold both *as Jesus held them*, without collapsing one into the other. That's uncomfortable. It may be that the fullness of how God's sovereignty and human freedom *actually* cohere is bigger than any system I can build.

The question I'd actually ask you is this: **when you hold both texts seriously, does one seem to you to *contradict* the other, or do they seem to speak different truths about the same God?** Because that changes everything about where a real conversation can go.

What's your read?
