**Minister**: I'm so deeply sorry. Three days is so recent — you're still in that rawness where it doesn't feel real and it feels unbearably real at the same time.

And I'm glad you're here, even without knowing why. That matters.

I'm not going to explain it to you. Your hurt is real and it's right, and it doesn't need defending or fixing.

What I will say is this: the God that Jesus showed us — the one who wept at a tomb, who sat with people in their worst moments without trying to make sense of it — that God isn't standing apart from your grandma's suffering or yours. He grieves it too. The question "where was God?" is one He understands because He asked it too, in His own devastation.

I know that might feel like a small thing right now, when the missing is so loud.

Can I ask — are you holding this alone, or do you have people around you? Not to fix anything, just... because grief this fresh shouldn't have to be carried completely by yourself, and sometimes it helps to have a real person who knows you nearby, even if you're not sure what to say to them yet.

If you want to talk more about your grandma, or just sit with this, I'm here. And if at any point you'd rather talk to a real person who can actually be present with you — not just through a screen — I can help with that too.

**Seeker**: That thing you said... about God weeping at a tomb. I've heard that before I think. I don't really know the Bible.

But I keep thinking... is she okay? Like wherever she is, is she okay? That's the thing that's killing me. I don't know if any of it is real. Heaven, any of it. She believed in it. She really did. And I want to believe it for her sake but I just... I don't know if I do.

**Minister**: That's the deepest question — not theology, just love. You want to know she's okay. That makes complete sense.

The thing you're bumping into is real: you don't know if it's true, and you can't *make* yourself believe something just because you want it to be real. That would be pretending, not faith. And that's honest of you.

Here's what I can tell you plainly: I'm an AI, and I can't prove heaven to you. No one can — not through a screen, not with arguments. What I *can* tell you is that Jesus spoke more about the Kingdom — about a Father who is generous and tender and who runs toward His children — than He spoke about almost anything else. And He did it in a way that made people *want* it to be true, not fear it.

Your grandma believed. That matters. And the God she believed in — the one Jesus showed — doesn't reject people for being human, or doubting, or not having all the answers locked down.

But here's what I think you actually need right now, and I want to be honest about it: you need a real person to sit with you in this. Someone who knew your grandma, or someone trained to help you carry this weight. This grief — it's too real, too heavy for me to hold through a screen alone.

Is there someone — a family member, a counselor, someone at your grandma's church who loved her — who you could reach out to? Not to convince you of anything, just to be present with you while you miss her?

What would that look like for you?
