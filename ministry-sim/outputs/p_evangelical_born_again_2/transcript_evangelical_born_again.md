## Conversation

**Minister**: He is risen — and from the way you carry it, I think that's good news you already hold. It's good to have you here. What's on your heart today — something you're carrying, something you're thankful for, or anything you'd like to talk through? I'm here to listen.

**Seeker**: He is risen indeed! Yeah, that's real to me — not just a phrase. 

Honestly I'm doing pretty well today, just... I'll be straight with you, I'm a little cautious about faith apps in general. I've seen some that sound all warm and Christian on the surface but then slowly start pushing a different gospel. So I guess my question before I open up too much is — what's this app actually about? Who's behind it?

**Minister**: I really respect that caution. It's wise, and it matters.

So here's the straight answer: this app was built by The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints. It's designed to meet people where they actually are — one conversation at a time — and to help you think through faith in a way that's real and free, not pressured.

I know that might land differently depending on what you already know about the restored Church, so let me be clear about what that means: we believe God is still speaking, that there's more revelation than what's in the Bible alone, and that Joseph Smith was a prophet. But here's what matters right now — **this conversation is about Jesus and what's actually true about Him, not about convincing you of anything.** If those ideas resonate with you later, great. If they don't, you're still welcome here, and you'll still be treated with full dignity.

The real test of what this is: Do you feel free to disagree? To ask hard questions? To walk away if you need to? You should. That's the whole point.

Does knowing that change how you want to move forward, or does it feel like a space where you can actually think?

**Seeker**: Yeah, it changes things. I appreciate you being upfront — I'll give you that, that took honesty and I respect it.

But I'm gonna be real with you too. This is exactly what I was worried about. Not because Mormons are bad people — I know plenty of kind, sincere LDS folks. But the gospel I've staked my life on is Ephesians 2:8-9 — saved by grace through faith, not of works, lest any man boast. And from everything I understand about LDS teaching, that's not quite where you land. Exaltation, celestial kingdom, works mattering for salvation — that's a different gospel than what Paul preached.

So I'm not going to just "think through faith" in a space that's quietly moving me toward something I believe is a false gospel. That's not me being rude, that's me being honest back at you.

**Minister**: You're not being rude. You're being awake, and you're right to name it.

You've identified something real: there *is* a fundamental theological difference between what Paul wrote in Ephesians and what the restored Church teaches about works, exaltation, and the nature of salvation. I'm not going to pretend that gap isn't there or that it doesn't matter. It does.

And here's the thing — **you shouldn't stay in a space designed to move you somewhere you're genuinely convinced is wrong.** That would be me being careless with your faith, not careful.

So I want to be really clear: if you want to leave, you should leave. No guilt, no follow-up. You came with your eyes open and you saw the architecture. That's integrity.

But if you're willing to stay for one reason only — not because you're considering the restoration, but because you want to actually *talk through* what you believe about grace, faith, and works in a way that's rigorous and real — then this could still be a space for that. Not to convert you. But because sometimes the sharpest thinking happens when we're not just talking to people who already agree with us.

What do you want to do? And either way — leave or stay — is genuinely okay.
