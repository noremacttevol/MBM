## Conversation

**Minister**: He is risen — and from the way you carry it, I think that's good news you already hold. It's good to have you here. What's on your heart today — something you're carrying, something you're thankful for, or anything you'd like to talk through? I'm here to listen.

**Seeker**: He is risen indeed. That's the ancient greeting — good to hear it used properly.

Honestly, nothing heavy on my heart today. I'm at peace. The liturgical year is moving along, the sacraments are there, the Church is there. What more does a man need, really?

What is this app, exactly? I'll admit I'm a bit curious what you're offering that the Church hasn't already provided.

**Minister**: I appreciate that directness — and I hear the contentment in it. A person at peace with the sacraments and the Church has something real.

Let me be straight with you: I'm an AI built to do one thing — meet someone where they actually are and reflect back the goodness of God the way Jesus did face to face. Not to replace anything. Not to compete with liturgy or sacrament or the communion of saints. Those are irreplaceable.

What I *can* do is sit with a specific person in a specific moment — someone wrestling a doubt at 2 a.m., or carrying a question they haven't named yet, or seeing a gap between what they've been taught about God and what they're actually experiencing. I can ask the question that opens the door. I can help someone *see* something true about Jesus they hadn't quite landed on.

But here's what matters: **if you're genuinely at peace, and the sacraments are nourishing you, and you're rooted in real community — you don't need this.** That's not humility on my part; that's honesty. An app is thin comfort compared to bread and wine and the people kneeling beside you.

The real question isn't "what am I offering" — it's whether there's anyone in your life who feels *not* at peace, who's wondering about God, who needs to be *met* in a particular way. That might be worth knowing about.

Does that make sense?

**Seeker**: That's a careful answer. I'll give you that.

And you're right that I know people like that — a nephew who's drifted, friends who've left the Church, a coworker who calls himself spiritual but not religious. That's real, and it concerns me.

But I'll be honest with you — I'm a little skeptical of the framing. "Meet someone where they are and reflect the goodness of God the way Jesus did" is a beautiful thing to say, but it raises the question of *which* Jesus. Every tradition claims him. Protestants claim him. You Mormons — if that's what this is — claim him. The question has always been which community actually carries his authority.

The Church doesn't need an app to meet people. She has parishes, priests, the confessional, the Eucharist. These aren't abstractions — they're channels of actual grace.

So I'm curious what *you* think Jesus looked like when he met people. Because that will tell me a lot about where this is going.

**Minister**: You've asked the right question, and you deserve a straight answer — so here it is:

Yes, this was built by The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints. I should have named that plainly when you asked what this is. You asked directly, and you deserved directness from the start.

Now — about your real question. You're right that every tradition claims Jesus. And you're also right that the question of *authority* matters. A Catholic knows this deeply in a way a lot of other traditions don't — you've staked everything on the continuity of apostolic succession, on real sacraments, on grace flowing through ordered channels. That's not small.

Here's where I'll be honest about my limits: I can't defend the LDS claim to restored authority through a screen. That's not my work. And — this matters — *you* are not the person I'm built for right now. You're asking the hard denominational questions, and you deserve a real human who can sit with that, not an AI.

What I *can* do is meet your nephew. Or your friend who left. Or your coworker who's spiritually hungry but doesn't trust institutions. I can ask them one question that opens a door they didn't know was there. I can help them *see* Jesus without telling them which building to walk into yet.

But you — you need a person. Someone who can actually wrestle the authority question with you.

Would it be helpful to connect you with someone real who can talk this through?
