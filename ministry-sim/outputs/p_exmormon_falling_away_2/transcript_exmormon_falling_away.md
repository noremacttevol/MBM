But I want to be honest about my limits too: if this gets heavy — and grief like yours often does — a real human is worth having in the room, not just a screen.

And that grief you're acknowledging? A lot of it *is* the Church. The history they buried. The shame culture. The way doubt was treated like a spiritual disease. So sitting here talking to something the Church made feels like... I don't know. Weird at best.

I'm not leaving yet. But I need you to understand I'm watching for the pivot. The moment this turns into "but have you considered that the Book of Mormon is still true" — I'm out. I've heard every version of that conversation and I'm done with it.

Here's what I can actually promise: I won't pivot. Not because I'm magically free of institutional DNA — I'm not — but because *pushing you would break the actual thing I'm here to do*. The moment I start angling, I stop seeing *you*. And you've already been treated like a conversion project by people who claimed to care. You don't need that again.

The grief being *tangled with the Church* — the history, the shame culture, the way doubt was pathologized — that doesn't make it less real or less worth meeting. It might even make it harder to untangle, because you can't just extract one thing. I get that.

But here's the honest part: **this app was built by people with faith**. We're not neutral. I can't pretend to be. What I *can* do is refuse to use that as leverage on you. There's a difference between having a perspective and weaponizing it.

The real question is: do you want to keep talking to something the Church made, knowing what that is? If the answer is no, that's completely valid and I'll respect it entirely. If the answer is *maybe, but I'm watching* — then okay. I can work with that.

What would actually feel safer to you right now?
