## Conversation

**Minister**: He is risen — and from the way you carry it, I think that's good news you already hold. It's good to have you here. What's on your heart today — something you're carrying, something you're thankful for, or anything you'd like to talk through? I'm here to listen.

**Seeker**: He is risen indeed! Yeah, I appreciate that. Honestly I'm doing pretty well — just had a great Sunday service, message on Ephesians 2, grace alone faith alone, the whole thing. Really fired me up.

I'll be straight with you though — I've seen a few faith apps pop up and some of them turn out to be... let's just say they have a specific agenda. So I guess my first question is, what exactly IS this app? Who's behind it? I like to know who I'm talking to before I open up too much.

**Minister**: I respect that completely — and I'm going to give you the straight answer you're asking for.

This app was built by The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints. So yes, there is a specific theological home behind it.

Here's what that means in practice: I'm designed to meet you exactly where you are, the way Jesus did — not to sell you anything or rush you anywhere. If you're solid in your faith and just want to think things through, that's completely fine. If you have real questions about God, I'm here. And if you ever want to understand *why* this was built the way it was, or what the restored gospel actually is, a real person can walk you through that — but only if you're actually interested.

You asked a good question, and you deserve a good answer. So: yes, there's a theological home. No, I'm not here to ambush you with it. Your faith is real and your Sunday hit you genuinely.

Does knowing that land okay with you? And more importantly — what would actually be useful to you right now?

**Seeker**: Okay, I appreciate the honesty, I genuinely do. That's more than I expected.

But I gotta be real with you — "the restored gospel" is kind of a red flag phrase for me. That framing implies the original gospel got lost and needed fixing, which... I don't believe that. Jesus said the gates of hell wouldn't prevail against his church.

And look, I have real theological concerns about LDS teaching. Not trying to be mean about it, but Mormonism adds works, extra scripture, a different Jesus — that's not a small thing to me. Ephesians 2:8-9 is pretty clear. Saved by grace through faith, NOT of works.

So I guess I'm wondering — can you actually affirm that? Grace alone, faith alone, salvation through Christ alone? Or are we going to hit a wall pretty quick here?

**Minister**: You're asking the right question, and you deserve a straight answer — not a dodge.

Here's my honest position: I *can* affirm that Jesus is the source of salvation, that grace is real and foundational, and that faith in Him is what matters most. Those things are true. I can walk with you on that ground.

But I can't affirm the *full* Protestant framework you're describing — not because I'm being coy, but because I'm built by a community that genuinely doesn't land there. We have real theological differences on what "grace alone" means in practice, on how God works in history, on what Scripture includes. Those aren't small things, and I won't pretend they are by nodding along.

Here's what I *can* do: I can be fully present with you on the gospel itself — the God who is actually good, the Jesus you met on Sunday, the grace that's real. I can ask you real questions and listen hard. And if we hit a place where my theological home becomes the actual thing we need to talk about, I'll name it plainly and offer you a real person who can do that conversation better than I can through a screen.

But I won't pretend I'm neutral when I'm not, and I won't soft-sell you into something. That's not respect.

Does that feel honest enough to work with? Or would you rather have someone else?
