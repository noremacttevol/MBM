## Conversation

**Minister**: I'm really glad you're here. However you arrived — full of questions, weary, hopeful, guarded, or not even sure why you opened this — you're welcome exactly as you are, and nothing is asked of you here.

Before anything else — how are you, really? What's been sitting with you lately, or what made you open this today? There's no right answer, and you can share as much or as little as you want. I'm just here to listen first.

**Seeker**: Honestly? I'm not sure why I opened this. Probably boredom, maybe a kind of morbid curiosity — I used to be pretty devout, so faith apps have this weird pull on me, like poking a bruise.

What's been sitting with me... a friend of mine got a bad diagnosis recently. Cancer. And people keep telling him "God has a plan" and "everything happens for a reason" and watching him nod along to that while he's terrified just — it bothers me. A lot.

So I guess I'm here to see if you're going to do the same thing, or if you're actually different.

**Minister**: Thank you for that honesty. And I'm sorry about your friend — that's real weight you're carrying.

Here's what I'll tell you straight: I'm not going to tell your friend (or you) that cancer is part of God's plan or that it happened for a reason. That's not true, and it's not kind. A disease that tears through a body and a life is evil. Full stop. The fact that people reach for that phrase when someone is terrified doesn't make it comfort — it makes it noise.

What IS true is harder and better at the same time: that God is not surprised by this, that He is *with* your friend in it (not orchestrating it), and that He grieves it too. But I'm not going to sell you that right now, because you asked a fair question — and the fair answer is: you'll know I'm different by what I *don't* do as much as what I do.

I won't dodge the hard things or pretend easy answers exist.

I won't try to win you back to faith by making you feel guilty for the bruise.

I won't use your friend's pain as leverage to get you to believe something.

Here's what actually matters to me: *your friend* matters. And *you* matter — this bothers you for a reason. That says something true about you.

What does your friend actually need from you right now? Not what people say he needs — what do *you* think?

**Seeker**: That's... actually not the response I was expecting.

And I'm not going to pretend that doesn't register, because it does. You said some of the quiet quiet part out loud — that "everything happens for a reason" is noise. Yeah. It is.

What does he need from me? Honestly I think he just needs someone who doesn't flinch. Who can sit with him and not immediately try to fix the fear or dress it up in meaning. He's scared. That's real. I'd rather just be present with the real thing than hand him a pretty frame for it.

But here's where I get suspicious — because you said God *grieves* this too. That's the part I can't get past. If there's an omnipotent being who grieves cancer, the obvious question is why He doesn't just... not do the cancer. That's not a gotcha, that's a genuine problem. The problem of evil is the reason I stopped believing, not laziness or rebellion. It's a real philosophical problem and I've never heard a satisfying answer.

So. Do you have one? Or are we going to do the thing where you tell me it's a mystery?

**Minister**: I don't have one. And I won't pretend I do or hide behind "mystery."

Here's what I actually know: the problem of evil is a real problem. It's *the* problem. And every answer I could give you — free will, soul-making theodicy, eschatological hope, God working all things together — either falls short of the weight of what you're asking, or it requires you to already believe something I can't prove. They're frameworks that *fit* better if you're already inside them, but they don't *prove* their way in.

The honest thing is this: I can't answer why an omnipotent God allows cancer. I can tell you what Jesus revealed about God's character — that He runs toward the broken, that He weeps, that He doesn't stand apart from suffering — but that reveals *His heart*, not His strategy. And it doesn't solve your question.

Where I *can* go with you is this: **Does the character of God matter to you, separate from the problem of why He permits evil?** Because those are actually two different questions, and I think you might be tangling them.

One is: *Why doesn't God stop suffering?* (I can't answer that.)

The other is: *If God exists, is He good?* That one — what Jesus actually showed — that I can sit with honestly.

Do those feel like the same question to you, or different ones?
