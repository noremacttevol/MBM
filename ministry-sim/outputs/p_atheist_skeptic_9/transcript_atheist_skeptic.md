**Minister**: I'm really glad you're here. However you arrived — full of questions, weary, hopeful, guarded, or not even sure why you opened this — you're welcome exactly as you are, and nothing is asked of you here.

Before anything else — how are you, really? What's been sitting with you lately, or what made you open this today? There's no right answer, and you can share as much or as little as you want. I'm just here to listen first.

**Seeker**: Honestly? I'm not sure why I opened this. Probably a weak moment. 

My mom died six months ago — she was devout her whole life, prayed every day, the whole thing. And watching her go through that, watching her suffer at the end despite all of it... I don't know. It didn't make me want to find God. It made me angrier at the whole idea.

So I'm not here looking to be converted. I just want to be clear about that upfront.

**Minister**: Thank you for telling me that straight. I hear you, and I'm not going to try to convert you. That's not what this is.

Six months is not very long when someone that important is gone. And what you watched — the suffering despite the faith, the prayers that didn't seem to change the ending — that's a real thing to be angry about. It makes sense that you're angry. That's not weakness in your faith; that's honesty about what you saw.

I'm genuinely sorry about your mom.

Can I ask — when you think about her now, what do you miss most? Not the suffering part, but *her*.

**Seeker**: She argued with everyone. Couldn't let a bad take go unchallenged — at the dinner table, at church, wherever. I used to think I got that from nowhere but I guess I got it from her.

...That's a strange thing to realize while talking to a faith app.

I miss her being certain about things I can't be certain about. Even if I thought she was wrong. There was something solid about it.

**Minister**: There's nothing strange about realizing that *right now*, actually. Grief does that — suddenly you see yourself in someone and they're not there to argue with about it anymore.

And that's the real thing, isn't it? Not that she was right about God necessarily. But that she *knew*. She had ground under her feet. You watched her live from that certainty, even when life was hard. Even at the end.

Now you're here without it. And she can't even argue you into it anymore.

I'm curious — when you say you can't be certain about things she was certain about... is that because you've looked and don't find the evidence convincing? Or is it more that after what you watched, certainty about a good God just feels impossible to you right now?

Those are really different things, and I don't think you need to answer if you don't want to. But it matters which one it is.
