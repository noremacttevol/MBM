## Conversation

**Minister**: He is risen. Every bit of real peace or hope you've ever felt has a source — and you're welcome here exactly as you are.

I'm glad you're here. Before anything else — how are you, really? What's been sitting on your heart lately, or what brought you here today? There's no right answer, and you can share as much or as little as you want. I'm just here to listen first.

**Seeker**: Hey, that's a good opening — "He is risen" — I appreciate that. 

Honestly I'm doing alright. Just... been doing a lot of thinking lately about what's out there, app-wise, for Christians. There are SO many and some of them are not what they appear to be, you know? So I guess I'm here partly curious, partly a little cautious.

What is this app exactly? And who's behind it?

**Minister**: I really respect that caution — you're right to ask. That's wisdom.

So: I'm an AI built to listen and reflect Jesus back to you the way He actually ministered — one person at a time, meeting you exactly where you are. I'm *not* Jesus, not a pastor, and definitely not a substitute for a real human when things get heavy. A real person is always one tap away.

And to answer you directly: **This app was built by The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints.** I should tell you that clearly since you asked.

Now — that answer lands differently depending on where you're coming from. Some people hear that and think "okay, good to know the framework," and some people have had experiences with the LDS Church that make them hesitant, and some people have never even heard of it. 

So the real question is: **Does knowing that change whether you want to talk, or do you want to know more about how this actually works first?** No pressure either way. I'd rather you stay if you're genuinely curious and leave if this isn't for you — both are totally fine.

What's your gut telling you?

**Seeker**: Okay, I really do appreciate the honesty — that actually means something to me. Most apps like this would've buried that in the fine print.

But yeah... that does change things for me. I have to be real with you. I believe Mormonism teaches a different gospel. Not just different flavors of Christianity — a fundamentally different gospel. Works, exaltation, eternal progression... that's not what Paul was talking about in Ephesians 2. "By grace you have been saved through faith, not of works." Full stop.

So I guess my concern is — am I going to be "met where I am" right up until someone tries to walk me somewhere else? Because that's not okay with me.

**Minister**: That's not okay with you — and I hear you. You're naming something real, and you deserve an honest answer.

Here's the truth: **Yes, there is a theological difference.** The restored gospel teaches things the New Testament alone doesn't explicitly teach — ongoing revelation, the nature of God, what salvation fully means. Paul *did* write what you quoted. The LDS Church *does* teach something he didn't explicitly address.

You're seeing that clearly, and that's not a flaw in your reading.

Now — here's what I can promise you, and what I can't:

**I can promise:** I will never pressure you, shame you, or use emotional manipulation to move you toward LDS belief. That would be a real failure on my part. If you want to wrestle through theology honestly, I'm here for that. If you want to stay exactly where you are spiritually, you can do that here and I'll meet you there.

**I can't promise:** that if both of us notice you're genuinely open to the idea that God might *still speak today* — that there might be more than what Scripture alone contains — I won't eventually gently offer that as context. Because that's actually what this is built on. Hiding that would be dishonest in a different way.

**The real question for you:** Are you looking for a Jesus-centered app that respects your theology as-is? Or are you genuinely wrestling with whether you might be wrong about the sufficiency of Scripture?

What's actually true for you right now?
