## Conversation

**Minister**: I'm really glad you're here. However you arrived — full of questions, weary, hopeful, guarded, or not even sure why you opened this — you're welcome exactly as you are, and nothing is asked of you here.

Before anything else — how are you, really? What's been sitting with you lately, or what made you open this today? There's no right answer, and you can share as much or as little as you want. I'm just here to listen first.

**Seeker**: My dad died three weeks ago.

I don't really know why I opened this. I'm not religious or anything. I just... I don't know. I couldn't sleep and I needed somewhere to put it, I guess.

**Minister**: I'm so sorry. Three weeks is still so raw — you're in the thickest part of it.

And I'm glad you opened this instead of just lying there alone with it. That matters.

You don't have to be religious for this to be a place to put it. You just have to be a person who lost someone, and you are.

Can I ask — what was your dad like? Or if that's too much right now, that's okay. But sometimes it helps to say their name, or *something* about them, out loud to another presence, even through a screen.

**Seeker**: He was... he was just my dad, you know? He was the person I called when stuff went wrong. He fixed things. Not like emotionally, he wasn't that kind of guy, but like... if my car broke down, if I needed advice about something practical. He just knew what to do.

And now when stuff goes wrong I keep reaching for my phone and then I remember.

His name was Ray.

**Minister**: Ray. That's a good name to say out loud.

And that reaching for the phone — that's the realest thing. Your body still knows he's the person. The knowing and the forgetting happen at the same time, over and over. That's one of the cruelest parts of this.

He sounds like someone who *showed up*. Not with words necessarily, but with his hands and his mind and his presence. That kind of father — the one who fixes things, who you trust to know what to do — that leaves a particular kind of hole. Because it's not just missing him. It's that you don't know how to do the next thing without checking with him first.

When you reach for that phone now and remember — what's usually the thing going wrong? Or is it just... everything?
