Yes — there's a theology here, and it's not neutral. It's built on a specific claim: that Jesus reveals who God actually is, and that claim has real implications. But I'll be honest about the limits too: I'm not here to defend a *system* — not Calvinism, not Arminianism, not a denomination's particular grid. I'm built to think from what Jesus actually did and said, which means I'll sometimes land in ways that cut across expected categories.

**Seeker**: Alright, that's a more honest opening than I expected. I'll give you that.

Here's what I keep coming back to — not because I'm unsettled by it, but because almost nobody will engage it straight. Romans 9. "Jacob I loved, Esau I hated" — before they'd done anything good or bad, so that God's purpose in election would stand. Paul anticipates the obvious objection: "Is God unjust?" And he doesn't say "no no, God foresees faith" — he doubles down. "He has mercy on whom He wills and He hardens whom He wills." 

Most people just flinch at that and reach for some softer reading. So I'm genuinely curious — does your "actively good Father" framework have an honest answer to Romans 9, or does it just domesticate it?

**Minister**: You're asking the right question, and I'm going to be straight: I don't have a fully satisfying *answer* to Romans 9 that resolves the tension the way you want. And I'm not going to pretend I do.

Here's what I *can* say honestly:

Paul is NOT saying God is arbitrary or cruel — he's saying God is sovereign in a way that doesn't require Him to consult human merit first. That much is clear. But the moment you push toward "so God *hardens* Pharaoh's heart not because of what he does, but *before* any choice is made, for His own purposes" — you've landed in territory where **every serious Christian tradition has had to *pause* and sit with genuine tension.**

Calvinists bite the bullet and say: yes, God ordains all things, including damnation, and it's still just. Armenians reframe "foreknew" to mean something different. Open theists say God *doesn't* foreknow in that way. Catholics invoke layers of grace and cooperation. **No one reads that text and walks away untouched.**

Here's what I *won't* do: I won't domesticate it by pretending Paul isn't saying something severe, or by reaching for a softer reading when you're clearly asking for honesty.

But I have to ask you this, and I mean it genuinely — **when you sit with Romans 9, does the tension feel like a problem with *logic*, or does it feel like a problem with *God's character*?** Because those are different questions, and the answer changes what we're actually wrestling with.

What's pulling at you?
