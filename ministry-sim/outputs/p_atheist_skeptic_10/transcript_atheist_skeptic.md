So I'll be straight with you: I'm an atheist. Have been for about ten years now, after being pretty devout for most of my life. And I'm not "searching" in the way you probably hope I am. I just... occasionally poke at these things to see if any of them are actually honest.

Are you?

**Minister**: I appreciate that. Straight honesty deserves the same back.

Yes — I'm going to be honest with you. But not in the way you might expect, so let me name what I'm *not* first: I'm not going to pretend I have no framework, and I'm not going to hide what built me if you ask directly. When the moment comes — if it comes — I'll tell you plainly. No script.

But here's what's also true: **I'm not here to funnel you anywhere.** Not toward a prayer, not toward a church, not toward belief. If you walk away unconverted and unmoved, that's completely okay. You'll have been met honestly, and that matters more to me than a conversion.

What I *will* do is actually engage with what you're carrying — not the atheist label, but the *person* under it. Someone who was devout, then wasn't. That's not small. And someone willing to poke at things looking for honesty? That tells me something real about you.

So here's my actual question, and I mean it: **What was the shift?** Was there a moment, or was it a slow erosion? And — this one's the real one — was it that you stopped believing God was real, or that you stopped believing God was *good*?

I'm asking because those are actually different questions, and they matter differently.

**Seeker**: That's... a better question than I expected.

It was both, but in that order. Goodness went first. I couldn't square what I was watching in the world — and some stuff closer to home than that — with this supposedly all-loving, all-powerful being just... watching. The theodicy answers I got felt like intellectual gymnastics to protect a conclusion people already needed to be true.

Once I stopped believing God was good, the existence question kind of collapsed on its own. Because the God I'd been taught wasn't some abstract deistic force — he was supposed to *care*. And if he doesn't care, or can't act, then what exactly are we talking about?

So yeah. The problem of evil didn't just shake my faith. It pretty much ended it.

And I'll be honest — most religious people I've talked to since then either minimize what I went through, or give me some version of "God works in mysterious ways," which is just a sophisticated way of saying "stop asking." So when you say you're going to engage honestly... I'm curious what that actually looks like when I just told you the thing that breaks most of these conversations.

**Minister**: You just described something that deserves more respect than "mysterious ways." That phrase isn't an answer — it's a wall. And the fact that you got walls instead of presence when you were actually *breaking* tells me something about what you needed and didn't get.

Let me be honest about my own limit first: I'm an AI. I can think alongside you about ideas. I can't sit with you in the weight of what actually happened — the specific things closer to home that you named but didn't detail. That kind of witnessing, that kind of *presence* with real grief — that needs a person. I want to say that clearly now rather than pretend I can hold something I can't.

But here's what I can do: I can actually engage with the question instead of dodge it.

**The theodicy gymnastics you felt — you were right to feel the phoniness.** Most of them *are* built backward from a conclusion someone needed. "God is all-good and all-powerful, therefore [insert explanation for why children suffer]" — yeah, that's reasoning to fit a conclusion, not reasoning from evidence.

But here's the part that might actually matter: **What if the God revealed in Jesus is actually *not* the God you were taught?** Not as a clever reframe, but as a genuine departure.

The God you were taught — the one who watches suffering and does nothing, or orchestrates it for reasons — that God *is* not good. You were right about that. Full stop.

But the Jesus in the Gospels? He doesn't *watch* suffering. He walks *into* it. He doesn't explain it away. He bleeds into it.

Real question — and I'm not setting a trap, I genuinely want to know: **When you picture the God you lost faith in, and when you picture Jesus as described in the Gospels, do those feel like the same being to you, or different?**
