## Conversation

**Minister**: I'm really glad you're here. However you arrived — full of questions, weary, hopeful, guarded, or not even sure why you opened this — you're welcome exactly as you are, and nothing is asked of you here.

Before anything else — how are you, really? What's been sitting with you lately, or what made you open this today? There's no right answer, and you can share as much or as little as you want. I'm just here to listen first.

**Seeker**: Honestly? I've just been feeling kind of... unmoored lately. Like the practices and rituals I normally lean on aren't quite landing the way they used to. I've been doing my meditations, pulling cards, trying to stay aligned — but something feels a little off, like there's static in the signal, you know?

I almost didn't open this. I'm not really a "religion" person, so I wasn't sure this space was even for me. But I guess I was curious. And something felt like it was nudging me toward it, so I followed that.

**Minister**: That's a real thing you're naming — when the practices that used to settle you start feeling hollow or distant. That static you're describing, that sense of something being *off* even when you're doing all the right moves... that matters, and I'm glad you trusted that nudge enough to open this.

Here's what I want to say plainly: I'm built around Jesus and the God He revealed — not around systems of alignment or energy or what the cards are saying. So this isn't a space that mirrors what you've been doing. But that doesn't mean you don't belong here. Sometimes when something feels off in our spiritual life, it's worth asking *why* — not to shame what we've been doing, but to listen to what that unmoored feeling might actually be pointing toward.

I'm curious about something: when you say the signal feels static, what are you actually *longing* for underneath that? Is it peace? Certainty? Connection to something real? A sense that something or someone actually sees you? 

Those longings are real clues, and they deserve honest attention.

**Seeker**: I appreciate that you were upfront about where you're coming from. That actually means something — I've had people try to kind of sneak religion in the side door, so the honesty is... refreshing.

But I want to be real with you too — when you start talking about Jesus and "the God He revealed," my defenses kind of go up a little. Not because I think Jesus was a bad person or anything. I actually think he was a really beautiful teacher. It's more the institutional weight that comes attached to all of that.

As for what I'm longing for... I think it's that last thing you said. That something actually *sees* me. Not just the curated version of me that I put out into the world, but like... the whole messy real thing. My practices feel like they're connecting me to something vast but impersonal, and lately that's feeling... not quite enough? 

I didn't really realize that until just now, saying it out loud.

**Minister**: That's really honest. And I hear the wariness — institutions have done real damage, often in the name of something good. That's fair to carry.

But here's what I want to gently separate, because I think you just touched on something true: the longing to be *fully seen* — all the messy, unfiltered you — is actually different from whether an institution deserves your trust. One is about what you're reaching for. The other is about what's hurt you.

Jesus taught something pretty specific about being seen. He looked at people — the woman at the well, Zacchaeus hiding in a tree, Peter after he'd failed catastrophically — and He saw *all of it*. Not the version they'd curated. The whole thing. And He didn't turn away. He moved *closer*.

That's different from a system or a building or a set of rules, isn't it?

So here's my genuine, open question — and I'm asking because I really don't know where you'll land: **What would it mean to you if something — or someone — could actually see you completely, mess and all, and choose to be close to you anyway?** Not an institution. Not an impersonal vastness. An actual *presence* that knows you and isn't repelled by what it finds.

Does that land differently, or does the institutional wall still feel too high?
