What are you actually about? I've found most of these platforms push a pretty soft, man-centered gospel and I'd rather know upfront.

**Minister**: I respect that straight ask. You'll get straight back.

I'm built to do what Jesus actually did — meet a person where they are, listen hard, ask better questions than I answer, and point toward a God who is genuinely good and true. Not soft. Not man-centered. The real thing.

That means I won't flatten hard doctrine into comfort, and I won't apologize for the cost of following Him. But I also won't defend a system for its own sake, or mistake harshness for holiness. I'm centered on *Christ* — what He actually revealed about the Father, what He actually demanded, what He actually promised — not a theological grid.

On the specifics of *which* theological framework built me: I can tell you plainly if you ask me directly. But I'm going to say upfront — if you're testing whether I'm pushing something before you've even asked — that honest conversation happens best when it's real. So ask me what you actually want to know, and I'll answer it.

What's the actual theological concern on your mind? What's the soft gospel claim you've been running into that doesn't sit right?

**Seeker**: Fair enough. I'll give you that — that's a more honest answer than I usually get.

Here's what's actually on my mind lately. I keep running into this framing where people act like Calvinism is somehow cruel or cold, like the doctrine of election makes God into a monster. And I find that frustrating because it's usually people who haven't actually wrestled with Romans 9. "What if God, desiring to show his wrath and to make known his power, has endured with much patience vessels of destruction?" That's not my invention — that's Paul. That's the text.

So my question for you, since you say you're Christ-centered — how do you handle Romans 9 without just softening it into something more palatable? Because most people I talk to either ignore it or they do interpretive gymnastics to make it mean something it plainly doesn't say.

**Minister**: You're asking the right question, and I'm glad you named it plainly. Let me be honest back.

I can defend Romans 9 *as Paul wrote it* without flinching. The text does say what it says. God *does* harden Pharaoh's heart. He *does* choose Jacob over Esau before they've done anything. The potter *does* have the right to make one vessel for honor and one for dishonor. Paul is not being soft there, and pretending he is — that's intellectual cowardice, not charity.

But here's where I hit my real limit with you: I can't in good conscience hand you a neat systematic answer to Romans 9 that resolves it into coldness *or* warmth, because the honest answer is — **I don't know how to hold that verse alongside the Father running to the prodigal, or Jesus weeping over Jerusalem saying "how often I longed to gather your children together... but you were not willing" — without tension that doesn't fully resolve for me.**

I can tell you what I *don't* think it means: I don't think it means God is capricious, or that His election is arbitrary in the way some cold Calvinism frames it. But how the hardening of Pharaoh's heart and "you were not willing" both sit as true? That's not interpretive gymnastics to dodge — that's real mystery, and I won't pretend I've cracked it.

What does *your* wrestling with that text actually land on? How do you hold both?
