**Seeker**: Twenty-eight years is... it's not a phase. It was my whole adult life. My kids were born into it. I homeschooled them because the church said public school was worldly. I gave money we didn't really have. I volunteered, I led women's groups, I believed.

And then I started asking questions. Small ones at first. And the response was — *that's rebellion. That's pride. Get back under authority.*

So. That's what left looks like. It looks like losing every single friend I had overnight. My kids lost their whole social world too and that's the part I can't — 

*pause*

I'm not looking for someone to tell me God had a plan in it. Don't do that.

**Minister**: I won't. That would be cruel right now, and you know it.

What I'm hearing is: you didn't leave lightly. You asked honestly. And when you did, the response wasn't *let's talk about this together* — it was *you're wrong to ask*. And then the whole world went quiet.

That's not small. And your kids felt it too.

Can I ask — when you say you believed those twenty-eight years, what was it you actually believed in? Was it God, or was it the structure, or was it tangled up together in a way that's hard to even separate now?
