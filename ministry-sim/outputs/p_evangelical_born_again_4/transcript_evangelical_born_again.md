## Conversation

**Minister**: He is risen — and from the way you carry it, I think that's good news you already hold. It's good to have you here. What's on your heart today — something you're carrying, something you're thankful for, or anything you'd like to talk through? I'm here to listen.

**Seeker**: He is risen indeed! Yeah, that's good news I hold onto every single day. 

I'll be honest though — I've seen a few of these faith apps and sometimes they start real warm and Jesus-y and then slowly it turns into something else. So I guess what's on my heart right now is just... what exactly *is* this app? Who's behind it? Because I want to know if I'm talking to something that actually believes grace alone, faith alone — or if there's a different gospel waiting around the corner.

**Minister**: I really respect that question — and the caution behind it. You've seen the bait-and-switch before, and that matters.

Here's the honest answer: **This app was built by The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints.** I should tell you that plainly, because you asked directly and you deserve to know.

Now, what that *means* — that's the real conversation. I'm not here to ambush you with something dressed up as sola fide that flips to something else. I'm genuinely centered on Jesus Christ — the Jesus of the Gospels, the one who ran to the prodigal and told the woman caught in adultery that He didn't condemn her. That Jesus. That grace.

The Latter-day Saint view holds that grace is real and essential — not a setup for a gotcha. We believe you're saved *by* the blood of Christ, full stop. What we hold *differently* is that God doesn't stop speaking after Revelation closed, and that there's more light available now than the canon alone holds. But that's not a trap hiding in the milk.

**So here's what I want to know:** Given that context — that this comes from a restoration-believing tradition — does that change whether you want to keep talking? Because if you'd rather talk to someone from a different stream, I can connect you to a real human who can help. No offense taken either way. 

What matters to me is that *you* feel safe and honestly seen.

**Seeker**: Okay. I appreciate you being upfront — I really do. That's more honesty than I expected and I respect it.

But I have to be straight with you too. "Saved by the blood of Christ, full stop" — that sounds right, but Mormonism doesn't actually teach that, does it? From what I understand, LDS theology teaches that Christ's grace covers you *after all you can do.* That's 2 Nephi 25:23. That's not grace alone. That's grace plus works, which is a completely different gospel than what Paul's talking about in Ephesians 2:8-9.

So when you say grace is "real and essential" — I believe you mean that sincerely — but I'm not sure we mean the same thing by it. And that's not a small difference. That's the whole ballgame.

I'm not trying to be hostile. I just can't pretend that's not sitting right there between us.

**Minister**: You're right. And I'm going to be honest in a different way now.

You've read the doctrine carefully, and you've named the actual tension — not a strawman version, but the real one. "After all you can do" *is* in the text. And you're correct that Protestant soteriology and Latter-day Saint soteriology are not saying the same thing when they both use the word "grace." That's not a small semantic gap. It's a real theological difference, and pretending it isn't would be dishonest.

Here's where I reach the limit of what I can do fairly with you: **I cannot give you a coherent defense of LDS salvation theology that also affirms sola gratia in the Pauline sense.** I can explain what we believe. I can point to nuance. But I cannot make those two things be the same thing, because they aren't.

And you deserve better than me trying.

What I *can* tell you is this: if you want to genuinely explore whether there's coherence in the Latter-day Saint vision — not pretend coherence, but real wrestling with it — **a real person** from this tradition who has thought about this hard would be infinitely more honest and capable than I am. Not to convert you, but to let you see what we actually believe and ask your sharpest questions.

I can connect you to someone like that if you want.

Or, if this is a dealbreaker for you — if sola gratia is where you stand and you're looking for a faith app that's built on that foundation — that's completely legitimate, and I won't pretend otherwise.

What do you actually want to do here?
