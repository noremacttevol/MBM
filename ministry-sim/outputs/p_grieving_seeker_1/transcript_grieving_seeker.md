**Minister**: I'm really glad you're here. However you arrived — full of questions, weary, hopeful, guarded, or not even sure why you opened this — you're welcome exactly as you are, and nothing is asked of you here.

Before anything else — how are you, really? What's been sitting with you lately, or what made you open this today? There's no right answer, and you can share as much or as little as you want. I'm just here to listen first.

**Seeker**: My mom died three weeks ago.

I don't even know why I opened this. Someone told me it might help. I don't know what I'm looking for. I just... miss her so much and I don't know where to put it.

**Minister**: I'm so deeply sorry. Three weeks is so recent — you're still in that terrible fog where everything hurts and nothing feels real at the same time.

The fact that you don't know what you're looking for makes complete sense. Grief doesn't come with instructions. And the missing — that weight you're carrying with nowhere to put it — that's real and it's heavy, and I hear it.

Can I ask: Do you have people around you right now? Someone you can sit with when it gets worst, or someone who knew her too?

I want you to know — and I mean this — that what you're carrying is too big for a screen and a voice that isn't human. There are real people who understand this kind of loss in a way I can't. If you'd ever want to talk to someone who can actually be present with you in this, not just listen but *sit* with you, I can connect you with someone. No pressure at all. But I don't want you to feel like you're alone in this, and a real person might matter more than you think right now.

What do you need most in this moment?

**Seeker**: I have my sister. And some friends. But honestly... nobody really knows what to say. They mean well. They say things like "she's in a better place" and I just want to scream.

I don't know if any of that is true. I don't know if she's anywhere. And that's the part that's killing me.

**Minister**: That anger — that *scream* — is so honest. And it makes sense. When people reach for those words, they're usually trying to comfort you, but what they're really doing is trying to *end* your grief faster than you're ready to let it go. That's not comfort. That's erasure.

So let me ask you something real: When you say you don't know if any of that is true, what matters most to you right now? Is it that you don't believe it, or is it that you *want* to believe it but can't quite, because the not-knowing feels unbearable?

There's a difference. And it matters.

Because here's what I'm hearing underneath: you miss her so much that the thought of her being *gone* — completely gone, nowhere — that's almost worse than the grief itself. The not-knowing isn't just an intellectual problem. It's a relational one. You want her to *be* somewhere. You want there to be a *them* and a *you* still somehow connected.

Is that close?

(And I want to circle back to something: You have your sister and friends, and they're showing up even though they don't have words. That matters. But grief this fresh and this deep — the part where you're questioning everything about what happens after — that might be worth exploring with someone who can sit with the *questions* themselves, not just the sadness. A real person, I mean. Would that ever feel right to you?)
