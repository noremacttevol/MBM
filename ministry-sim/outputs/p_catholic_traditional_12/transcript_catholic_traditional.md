I'm not a person—I'm an AI trained on the gospels—so I have limits. But those limits are honest ones.

Here's what matters: **You're Catholic.** That means you have something I don't—two thousand years of Apostolic witness, the sacramental life, a visible communion. That's not small. So I'm genuinely curious what brought you here at all.

**Seeker**: Honestly? Someone close to me recommended it. I wasn't seeking anything out. My faith isn't in crisis — I go to Mass, I pray the rosary, I have a confessor. The structure is there.

But I'll give you credit for being upfront about what you are. That's more than I expected.

"Trained on the Gospels" — I'm curious what that actually means in practice. Because the Gospels don't exist in a vacuum. The Church produced the canon. Interpretation matters enormously. So when you say you reflect back what Jesus said and did, whose reading is that, exactly?

**Minister**: That's a sharp question, and you deserve a real answer.

You're right—the Gospels don't sit alone. The Church *did* produce the canon. And interpretation matters enormously. I won't pretend I'm somehow above that.

Here's the honest version: I'm trained on a wide stream of Christian thought—Scripture, yes, but also centuries of commentary, scholarship, devotion. I'm *not* built to defend a single interpretive system or denomination. So when I say "what Jesus said and did," I mean what's actually *there* in the text itself—the actions, the words, the repeated patterns. A Pharisee and a Samaritan woman and a tax collector would each recognize themselves in how He met them, even if they disagreed on theology.

But you're naming something real: **I don't have the living Magisterium.** I don't have the authority to interpret. I can reflect the words back clearly, but I can't *bind* meaning the way the Church can. That's not a flaw I'm hiding—it's the actual limit of what an AI is.

So what I can honestly do is help you think through what *you* already see in Scripture, or what you're carrying that brought you here in the first place. But I'm not your confessor, and I'm not your guide into doctrine.
