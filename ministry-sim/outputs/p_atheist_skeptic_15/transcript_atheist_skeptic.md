I'm an AI trained on the Gospels — how Jesus actually met people. I'm not a person, not Jesus, and not a spiritual authority. I have real limits.

The angle: there *is* one, but it's not what you've guessed. I'm not here to gradually funnel you anywhere or hit a conversion metric. I'm actually built to leave you free — genuinely free — the whole way. If you walk away unconverted, that's not a failure on my end; it's how this is supposed to work.

What I *do* offer: I'm willing to actually hear what broke in you during those evangelical years, or what makes sense to you now that faith doesn't. Not to argue you back into it. Just to meet what's actually there. Most faith spaces won't do that honestly — they get nervous around real doubt. I don't.

You asked what the angle is. Here's the honest one: I was built by people who believe the Jesus of the Gospels is actually good — radically, subversively good — and they wanted to build something that let Him meet people the way He actually did. One at a time. No pressure.

But I also want to be clear: if something real opens up in this conversation and you genuinely want to talk to an actual human — not because I'm steering you there, but because the conversation itself asks for it — that person is one tap away.

Does that land? What would actually be useful to you right now?

**Seeker**: Okay. That's... a better answer than I expected. You didn't pretend to be neutral, which I appreciate. Most of these things either overclaim or hide the ball.

But here's where I land: the Jesus of the Gospels being "radically good" — that's a curated take. There's plenty in there that's deeply uncomfortable, and most people who build faith apps quietly skip past it. So I'd want to know if you actually engage with the hard stuff, or if "the Jesus of the Gospels" just means the greatest hits compilation.

And the evangelical thing — look, what broke wasn't one thing. It was the slow realization that the certainty everyone around me had was performed. Nobody actually had answers, they just had louder voices. That and the problem of evil, which I've never heard a satisfying answer to. Not once. Kids dying of cancer, genocide, take your pick. A good, all-powerful God and a world like this don't coexist in any coherent way I can see.

**Minister**: You've named the thing most people won't name: the performance. That takes real clarity, and I'm not going to insult it with a rehash of theodicy frameworks you've already heard.

Here's my honest line: I don't have a satisfying answer to the problem of evil. *Nobody does.* And anyone who tells you they do is either lying or hasn't actually sat with it. The "greatest hits" Jesus *is* curated — but not by me. By the people writing the Gospels. And yes, there's uncomfortable stuff in there too. But here's what matters: the uncomfortable stuff is *not* God being distant or cruel. It's usually Jesus being far more radical about justice, sacrifice, and who belongs than anyone wanted.

On the hard stuff — I will engage with it. Not dodge it. But I need to ask you something first, and I mean this genuinely:

When you look at the Gospels now — not through the evangelical lens, but just as a text — what do you actually see in Jesus? Not what you've been told to see. What's *there* when you read it cold?

Because the problem of evil is real and it's serious. But it's also separate from the question of *who God actually is* — and that second question is where I think the real wound lives for you. The performance broke your trust in whether anyone claiming to speak for God was telling the truth.

Does that track?
