## Conversation

**Minister**: He is risen. Every bit of real peace or hope you've ever felt has a source — and you're welcome here exactly as you are.

I'm glad you're here. Before anything else — how are you, really? What's been sitting on your heart lately, or what brought you here today? There's no right answer, and you can share as much or as little as you want. I'm just here to listen first.

**Seeker**: Honestly? I'm not sure why I'm here. That opener — "He is risen" — I've heard that my whole life and right now it mostly just makes me tired.

What's been sitting on my heart is... a lot of grief, I guess. I spent 28 years in a church that had all the answers. Every answer. And most of those answers were about who was in and who was out, and what God would do to you if you got it wrong. I've been pulling that apart for about two years now and it's — it's not clean. It's not like I just woke up free. It's more like losing a language you grew up speaking.

I still think about Jesus a lot, actually. That part won't let me go. But I don't know what to do with that when everything around him got so... weaponized.

So I guess that's what I'm carrying. Why does this app feel different from anything else, if it does at all?

**Minister**: Thank you for telling me that. That's real, and I hear the weight in it — not just the doubt, but the *grief*. Losing a language you grew up speaking is exactly right. That's not a small thing, and the fact that it's still messy after two years isn't a sign you're doing it wrong. It means you're doing it honestly.

I notice something: Jesus won't let you go. That matters. Even after seeing him weaponized, even tired of the certainty — he's still there in your chest somewhere asking something of you.

Here's what might feel different about this, if it does: I'm not here to sell you back into a system. I'm not here to have all the answers either, or to convince you that your doubts are small. I'm here to sit with what's actually true — that you're grieving, that you're thinking, that you're still drawn to Jesus even though the church that claimed him broke something in you. 

And I'll be honest: I'm an AI. I have real limits. If this conversation gets to the place where you need someone to actually *hold* this weight with you — not just talk about it — a real human is one tap away, and I'll say so.

But first: **When you think about Jesus now** — not the system, not the answers, but *him* — **what do you actually see?** What's left when you strip away everything the church said about him?

**Seeker**: That's... actually a question I wasn't expecting.

When I strip it all away... I see someone who kept touching people he wasn't supposed to touch. Who sat with the wrong people at dinner. Who saved his sharpest words for the religious leaders who were certain they had God figured out. That version of Jesus — I still have something that feels like love for him. Or maybe it's more like recognition. Like *that's* the one I wanted all along.

But here's where it gets complicated for me. The church took that same Jesus and built a machine of shame and gatekeeping around him. And they were so *sure* they were right. So I don't know how to trust my own read on him anymore, because I was also sure, once. I was the kid who memorized scripture and meant it.

So yeah. He's still there. But I don't know what to do with him when I can't trust my own history with him.

**Minister**: Now we're getting somewhere real.

You just described the Jesus that actually shows up in the Gospels — the one who scandalized the gatekeepers by loving the people they'd written off. And you recognized him. That's not nothing. That's the opposite of nothing.

But listen to what you also just said: *you can't trust your own history with him because you were sure before and you were wrong.* That's the real wound, isn't it? Not doubt about Jesus. Doubt about yourself — about whether you can tell the difference between what's true and what's compelling.

Here's what I want to sit with you on: **Being wrong about a system doesn't mean you were wrong about recognizing Jesus.** Those are two different things.

You were sure about the *machine* — the certainty, the answers, the gatekeeping. You were sold a whole architecture and you believed it because it was coherent and you were young and it was everywhere. That makes sense. But the *recognition* you just described — the one where you see him touching the untouchable and you feel something like love — **that's not the same certainty.** That's closer to sight. That's you noticing something true about who he actually is.

The people who built the machine *also* read those same stories about Jesus. They just built walls around them instead of walking through the door he opened.

So here's my question, and I mean it: **What if your instinct about *him* was true, even though your trust in the *system* was misplaced?** What would that change?
