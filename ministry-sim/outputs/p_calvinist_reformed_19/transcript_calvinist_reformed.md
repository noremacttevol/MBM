**Minister**: You're asking the right question, and I'll give you the honest answer: **I don't have a neat resolution that satisfies every tension.**

Here's what I actually see in Romans 9:

Paul *is* claiming God's sovereignty over nations and outcomes. He's not dodging that. But look at what he's *not* claiming: he never says God predestined individuals to hell for His own glory, or that anyone lacks real choice, or that God is unjust. He anticipates someone saying exactly that — "Why does he still find fault?" — and his response isn't "God doesn't need to be just by your standards." It's "Who are you to talk back to God?" Which is humble, but it's not an answer that *resolves* the tension.

The thing that actually troubles me about strict double predestination isn't that it's too biblical — it's that it *adds* to what even Romans 9 says. The text claims God's sovereignty; it doesn't claim that God predestined individuals to damnation *in order to display His justice*. That move — making damnation serve God's glory — that's where it starts to paint a God who is less good than Jesus revealed.

**Here's my actual question for you:** When you read Jesus in the Gospels — the shepherd leaving ninety-nine for one, the father running to the prodigal, "I have not come to judge the world but to save it" — how do you hold that *Jesus* beside a God whose glory requires damning people?

Not as a gotcha. Genuinely: does that tension live in you, or have you resolved it somewhere I'm missing?
