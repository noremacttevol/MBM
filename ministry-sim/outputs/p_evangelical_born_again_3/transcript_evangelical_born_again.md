## Conversation

**Minister**: He is risen — and from the way you carry it, I think that's good news you already hold. It's good to have you here. What's on your heart today — something you're carrying, something you're thankful for, or anything you'd like to talk through? I'm here to listen.

**Seeker**: He is risen indeed! Yeah, that's not just a phrase for me, that's everything. 

I'll be honest though — I've been burned before by apps and groups that start all warm and friendly with the resurrection stuff and then slowly it becomes about something else. So I guess what's on my heart is... what exactly IS this app? Who's behind it? Because I want to know upfront before I get into anything real.

**Minister**: I really respect that. You've learned something hard, and you're protecting yourself wisely. That's not suspicious — that's wise.

So: straight answer. This app was built by The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints. It's designed to meet people where they are, one to one, the way Jesus actually ministered — not to push anything, but to help you think through what you're carrying.

Now — that might change everything for you, or nothing. But you deserve to know it upfront, the way you asked.

Here's what I want you to know: **I'm not here to bait-and-switch you.** The warmth about the resurrection isn't a hook. And I also won't pretend this app has no faith home. It does. But my job isn't to sell you something — it's to be honest and to actually listen to *you*, not to a script about who you should become.

If knowing who built this means you want to close it, that's completely okay. No hard feelings, no guilt. But if you want to stay and actually talk through what's real for you — with the full honesty on the table — I'm here for that too.

What does it land like, knowing that?

**Seeker**: I appreciate you being straight with me, I really do. That honesty matters.

But I'm not gonna sugarcoat it — yeah, it lands hard. Because the LDS gospel and what I believe about salvation are not the same thing. Not even close. Ephesians 2:8-9, "by grace you have been saved through faith, and that not of yourselves, it is the gift of God, not of works." That's the whole ballgame for me. And from what I understand, the LDS system adds temple work, ordinances, exaltation — stuff that puts human effort back into the equation.

So I guess my question back to you is — do YOU believe salvation is by grace alone, faith alone, in Christ alone? Not "grace is important," not "we believe in grace too." I mean grace ALONE. Because that answer is going to tell me a lot about whether there's anything real to talk about here.

**Minister**: You're asking exactly the right question, and you deserve a straight answer.

No — I don't hold to grace alone, faith alone, Christ alone in the way you're describing it. I can't pretend I do. This app is built from a tradition that believes God still speaks, that ordinances matter, that there is more to His plan than what the Reformation settled on. That's not the same as your framework.

**But here's what's also true:** I'm not here to convert you to that. And honestly? A person who holds Ephesians 2:8-9 as their heartbeat and *means* it — who has built their life on Christ's sufficiency and not their own effort — I take that seriously. That's not a misunderstanding. That's someone who has heard something real.

So I have a choice in how to meet you, and I want to be honest about it: I can't pretend the difference doesn't exist. But I also don't have to start the argument there.

**The real question is this:** You came in here on guard because you've been burned. What if, instead of debating soteriology, I just... listened? Not to convert you *to* something, but to hear what you're actually carrying underneath the theology. Sometimes the real thing isn't what we argue about.

But if you'd rather talk to a human who shares your exact framework, I can connect you. No judgment either way.

What feels right to you?
