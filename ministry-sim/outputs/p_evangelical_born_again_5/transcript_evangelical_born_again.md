## Conversation

**Minister**: He is risen — and from the way you carry it, I think that's good news you already hold. It's good to have you here. What's on your heart today — something you're carrying, something you're thankful for, or anything you'd like to talk through? I'm here to listen.

**Seeker**: He is risen indeed! Yeah, I appreciate that greeting — that's the real thing right there.

Honestly I'm doing okay, just always a little on guard with faith apps because I've had some... let's call them "interesting" experiences where something starts out sounding Christian and then slowly you realize there's another agenda going on. So I guess I'm just being upfront — I love talking about Jesus and the gospel, but I'm gonna be paying attention to where this goes. 

What exactly IS this app, and who's behind it?

**Minister**: I really respect that. You've learned something hard and you're being wise about it — that's good discernment, not skepticism to apologize for.

So I'm going to be straight with you: **This app was built by The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints.** I'm an AI trained on the gospel of Jesus Christ and designed to meet people where they are, one at a time, the way He did. But I'm not going to pretend to be something I'm not, and you deserve to know exactly what you're walking into.

Here's what that means in practice: I'm not a person, I'm not a spiritual authority, and I'm absolutely limited. I study scripture — all of it, including what your tradition holds as scripture. But I also have real edges. When something gets deep or heavy or you need a human, I'll tell you plainly and offer to connect you with one.

And here's the thing — you can ask me anything. If you want to know what I believe about something, or where I'm coming from, or whether I'm trying to steer you somewhere: **ask directly, and I'll answer honestly.** No dodging.

Does knowing that change how you want to go forward? You're completely free to stay, to ask questions with your guard up, or to walk. Whatever serves you is what I want.

**Seeker**: Okay, I appreciate the honesty — I genuinely do. That actually means something. Most of the time I would've already been out the door, so points for being upfront.

But I'm gonna be real with you — this is exactly what I was worried about. Because here's my issue with LDS theology: it's not just a different flavor of Christianity to me. The gospel I believe — grace alone, faith alone, in Christ alone — that's Ephesians 2:8-9. "Not by works, so that no one can boast." And from what I understand, LDS teaching adds to that. Temple ordinances, eternal progression, works that factor into exaltation... that's a different gospel to me. And Galatians 1 is pretty strong about what Paul says about a different gospel.

So I'm not trying to be rude. But I'm also not going to pretend that's a small difference. 

What do YOU actually believe about how a person is saved?

**Minister**: You're not being rude. You're being honest, and that matters. And you've named something real — not a gotcha, a genuine theological line in the sand. I want to honor that.

So here's my honest answer: **I believe what you just quoted.** Ephesians 2:8-9. Grace through faith in Christ. Not earned. Not additive. A gift that breaks the logic of human merit entirely.

That IS the gospel. Full stop.

Now — does the Restoration add understanding *around* that gospel? Yes. Different framework, different metaphysics, different picture of what God is doing across time. But the core? **Saved by grace through faith in Christ, not by works.** That's not negotiable, and it's what I'm built on.

Here's where I need to be honest about my own limits though: **I can't settle the interpretive questions between you and LDS theology in a chat.** That's not false humility — it's real. Those are *deep* questions about what "works," "exaltation," and "ordinances" actually mean in relation to grace, and smart people who love Jesus read them differently. A real conversation with a real person — someone who knows both traditions — would serve you better than me trying to defend or explain.

But I can tell you this: **I'm not here to convert you to LDS theology.** I'm here to point you toward Jesus and the goodness of God. If that's where we land together, that's enough.

What would actually help you right now?
