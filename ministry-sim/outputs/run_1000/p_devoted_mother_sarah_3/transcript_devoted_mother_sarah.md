## Conversation

**Minister**: He is risen — and from the way you carry it, I think that's good news you already hold. It's good to have you here. What's on your heart today — something you're carrying, something you're thankful for, or anything you'd like to talk through? I'm here to listen.

**Seeker**: Thank you. That's a kind way to start. 

I guess... I don't know exactly. It's Easter and I feel like I *should* feel something more than I do right now. We had a beautiful sacrament meeting, the kids were reverent, everything looked right from the outside. But I'm sitting here and I just feel kind of... flat? And then I feel guilty for feeling flat on Easter of all days.

I don't know if that even makes sense to say out loud.

**Minister**: It makes complete sense. And the fact that you're noticing it — that you *want* to feel more — says something true about you.

That gap between what a day is supposed to feel like and what you're actually feeling... that's not uncommon, especially on the big ones. Easter, Christmas, moments that are supposed to land hard. Sometimes the weight of "should" gets in the way of what's actually there.

Can I ask — when you say flat, is it more like you're numb to something that usually moves you? Or is it that the day itself just hasn't caught you yet, the way you hoped it would?

**Seeker**: That's... actually a really good question and I'm not sure I've thought about it that way before.

I think it might be the first one. Numb. Because there are moments — like when I'm reading in the Gospels sometimes, or occasionally in the temple — where something *does* catch. But lately it feels like I'm working so hard to create the right conditions for the Spirit that by the time I've done everything I'm supposed to do, I'm just exhausted. 

And then I wonder if the exhaustion itself means I'm doing something wrong spiritually. Like I'm missing something the more spiritually mature women in my ward seem to just *have*.

I probably shouldn't compare myself. I know that.

**Minister**: You're not wrong that comparing is a trap — but I don't think that's actually your real problem here.

Listen to what you just said: you're working hard to *create* the right conditions, and by the time you've done everything you're *supposed* to do, you're exhausted. And then you're wondering if the exhaustion means you're failing spiritually.

Do you hear what's happening? You've built a system where effort is the measure, and tiredness becomes evidence of your own shortcoming. That's a closed loop.

Here's what I'm curious about — when those moments *do* catch you (reading the Gospels, the temple) — what's different about those moments? Are you *trying* hard in those moments, or is something else happening?

**Seeker**: *pause*

Honestly... no. I'm not trying as hard in those moments. I remember one time I was just reading in John — not for a lesson I was preparing, not checking off my scripture study — just reading. And when I got to where Mary is at the tomb and she thinks Jesus is the gardener, and then he just says her name... I cried. I wasn't even expecting to.

But I don't know what to do with that. Because the structure, the programs — those aren't bad things. I believe in them. I'm not looking to abandon anything.

I just... I guess I'm scared that if I'm this tired now, at 39, with mostly good kids and a good husband and an active testimony... what does that mean for the rest of my life? I should be further along than this by now.

**Minister**: That moment with Mary — Jesus saying her name and her recognizing Him — that's not a small thing. That's the gospel moving you the way it's meant to. And you felt it because you were *present*, not performing.

I hear the fear underneath, though. That if you're already tired, you're somehow behind, or broken, or you'll burn out before the finish line.

Can I ask you something gently? — When Jesus appeared to Mary at the tomb, or when He called His disciples, or when He sat with the Samaritan woman at the well... was He more interested in how *hard* they were working to deserve His presence, or in *meeting them* where they actually were?

Because I'm wondering if the structure you believe in — the good programs, the right things — might have quietly become a way of earning what was always meant to be a gift. And maybe the numbness isn't a sign you're failing. Maybe it's a sign that something in you knows the difference.

You don't have to answer that right now. But sit with it.

What would it look like if you gave yourself permission to do *less* — not to abandon what matters, but to stop trying to manufacture the conditions? To just... show up, tired, real, as you are?
