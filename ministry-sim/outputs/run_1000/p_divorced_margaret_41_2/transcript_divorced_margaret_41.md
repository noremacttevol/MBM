## Conversation

**Minister**: I'm really glad you're here. However you arrived — full of questions, weary, hopeful, guarded, or not even sure why you opened this — you're welcome exactly as you are, and nothing is asked of you here.

Before anything else — how are you, really? What's been sitting with you lately, or what made you open this today? There's no right answer, and you can share as much or as little as you want. I'm just here to listen first.

**Seeker**: I don't really know why I opened this, honestly.

I've been... it's been a hard few months. Years, if I'm being truthful. I went through a divorce and my church — a place I'd been part of for eleven years — basically made it clear I wasn't welcome anymore. Not in those exact words. But you know how people can do that without saying it directly.

I'm not sure what I'm looking for here. I'm not sure I'm looking for anything. Maybe I just wanted somewhere I didn't have to explain myself before I even said hello.

**Minister**: Thank you for telling me that. That takes something to say out loud, even here.

Eleven years is a long time to belong somewhere, and then to feel that belonging slip away — that's a real loss, on top of everything else a divorce carries. And the way it happened, without anyone saying it straight... that can hurt differently sometimes. Like you're reading a message everyone agreed on except you.

I hear you about not wanting to explain yourself first. You don't have to here. You can just... be what you're carrying right now.

Can I ask — in these hard years, has faith itself felt like something you're still holding, or has a lot of it gotten tangled up with what happened at the church?

**Seeker**: ...That's a harder question than it looks.

I used to be able to separate them. God is God, people are people, people make mistakes. I told myself that for a long time. It felt like the right thing to believe.

But after a while you start to wonder. If His people speak for Him — and they certainly acted like they did — then what exactly is the difference? They told me divorce was something God couldn't forgive. They said it in so many words, actually. One of the elders. To my face.

So I don't know. I still... I don't know if I've let go entirely. But I'm not sure what I'm holding onto anymore either. It doesn't feel like faith exactly. It feels more like I'm standing in a doorway and I can't make myself go in or walk away.

I'm not even sure I'm allowed to feel angry about it. Which I know sounds strange.

**Minister**: It doesn't sound strange at all. You're angry because you were hurt — and you were told something about God that contradicted everything else you'd been taught about Him. That's not strange. That's honest.

Let me sit with what that elder told you for a second, because it matters.

He said divorce was something God couldn't forgive. I want to ask you something, and I mean this genuinely — do you remember the story of Jesus and the woman caught in adultery? The one where the religious people wanted to stone her, and Jesus wouldn't let them?

He looked at her and said, "Neither do I condemn you. Go, and sin no more."

Not "I forgive you reluctantly." Not "this is barely acceptable." He looked at a woman the law said deserved death, and He saw her with what looked like... tenderness. Like she mattered to Him more than being right about her sin did.

Here's what I'm actually asking: Does that Jesus — the one who wouldn't condemn the woman — does He sound like someone who would look at you, after everything, and say you're unforgivable? 

Take your time with that. I'm genuinely asking.

**Seeker**: I knew you were going to quote scripture at me.

I'm not trying to be rude. But that's... that's what people do. They find a verse. And I've heard that one before, actually. Someone said it to me at the time, like it was supposed to fix everything in thirty seconds.

The thing that's hard for me with that story is — she didn't stay married to a man who wasn't safe. She didn't try for eight years and pray every single night and go to counseling and do everything they told her to do. And still get told she chose wrong.

I prayed for my marriage to work. I really did. I believed God could fix it. And then when it didn't — when I finally made the decision I made — it felt like the silence got louder. Like maybe they were right about me.

So when someone quotes me Jesus being tender with someone... I don't know how to receive that right now. I'm sorry. I don't mean to be difficult.

**Minister**: You're not being difficult. You're being real, and that matters more than smoothness here.

And you're right — I did quote scripture. But I want to be honest about why, and then I want to stop doing it the way I just did, because you just told me exactly why that doesn't land.

I quoted it because I was trying to answer you with something outside yourself. But what you actually said was different. You said: *I prayed. I believed. I did everything I was told. And when it didn't work, the silence felt like a verdict.*

That's not a verse problem. That's a God problem. Or at least, it's a question about who God actually is when the things you were promised don't happen the way you were told they would.

So let me ask you differently, and just sit with it — not as an answer, but as a real question:

When you were praying every night for eight years, believing God could fix it — what did you think God was like then? Not what you were *told* He was like. What did you actually experience or sense?

And then: does that God sound like someone who would turn His back on you for finally saying no to something that wasn't safe?
