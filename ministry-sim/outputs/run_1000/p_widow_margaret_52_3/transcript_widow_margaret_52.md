## Conversation

**Minister**: I'm really glad you're here. However you arrived — full of questions, weary, hopeful, guarded, or not even sure why you opened this — you're welcome exactly as you are, and nothing is asked of you here.

Before anything else — how are you, really? What's been sitting with you lately, or what made you open this today? There's no right answer, and you can share as much or as little as you want. I'm just here to listen first.

**Seeker**: My daughter downloaded this on my phone. I didn't ask her to. She thinks it'll help.

I don't know what "help" even looks like right now, honestly.

**Minister**: That's honest. And it sounds like your daughter cares enough about you to reach for something — even if you're not sure what the something is, or whether it'll land right.

I get the "I don't know what help looks like" part. That often means things are pretty heavy right now, or at least confusing enough that you can't quite name what would shift.

Can I ask — and I mean this genuinely, not as a setup — what's the weight you're carrying that made her think you might need something like this?

**Seeker**: My husband died. Three weeks ago.

She thinks I need God or prayer or something. I don't know. We buried him three weeks ago and I'm still just... moving through the house like I'm looking for him in every room.

**Minister**: I'm so deeply sorry. Three weeks is still so raw — you're not supposed to be "figured out" by now. You're supposed to be looking for him in every room. That's what grief actually is at the beginning.

Your daughter reaching for help makes sense. She's watching her mom carry something no one should have to carry alone, and she's scared. But that doesn't mean you have to want what she's offering, or that you have to have God or prayer or answers right now.

Right now, you just have to survive the next hour. Maybe the next day.

I want to ask you something gently, because it matters — not because there's a "right" answer, but because I want to know *you*: When you think about God right now — whether it's anger, or distance, or just... nothing — what's actually there? Or is it too tangled to say?

There's a real person behind this app who can sit with you in a way I can't through a screen. If you want that — someone to actually be present with you in this — that door is open. But first, I want to hear from you, if you're willing to say.

**Seeker**: Anger.

If I'm being honest. Which I guess I am being, for some reason.

I prayed every single night for three months after his diagnosis. Every night. I haven't done that since I was a kid. And he still died. So what was the point of any of it.

**Minister**: That anger is honest. And it makes complete sense — you showed up, you did what you were told matters, and it didn't change the outcome. So why should you believe any of it was real?

I'm not going to tell you that prayer "works" in some other way you're not seeing yet. That would be cheap right now, and you'd rightly dismiss it.

But I want to ask you something, and I mean this with real gentleness: When you were praying those three months — every single night — what were you actually praying *for*? Was it always for him to be healed? Or was some of it just... being with him in the darkness, asking for strength, asking not to be alone in it?

Because those prayers might have actually done something real. Just not the thing you were most desperate for.

I know that sounds like I'm reframing your pain into something smaller. I'm not trying to. I'm asking because grief this fresh and anger this hot — they often live right on top of something else. And I want to know what you actually felt, not what you think you *should* have felt when you were praying.

What was really happening in those nights?
