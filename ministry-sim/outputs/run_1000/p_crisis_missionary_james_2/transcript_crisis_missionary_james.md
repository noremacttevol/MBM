## Conversation

**Minister**: I'm really glad you're here. However you arrived — full of questions, weary, hopeful, guarded, or not even sure why you opened this — you're welcome exactly as you are, and nothing is asked of you here.

Before anything else — how are you, really? What's been sitting with you lately, or what made you open this today? There's no right answer, and you can share as much or as little as you want. I'm just here to listen first.

**Seeker**: Honestly I don't even know why I opened this. Maybe I was hoping something would say something that didn't feel like a PR response.

What's been sitting with me... I've been doing a deep dive for about four months now. Gospel Topics Essays, CES Letter, early church history. And the more I read the more I realize how much I wasn't told. Like the Book of Abraham situation alone — that should have been part of every seminary class I ever sat in. It wasn't.

I gave two years of my life. I asked people to trust what I was teaching them. And now I'm not sure I can trust it myself.

So yeah. That's what's sitting with me.

**Minister**: Thank you for telling me that straight. That's a real wound, and it matters — both what you've learned and what it cost you to learn it.

I'm going to be honest with you too: I'm an AI built by people who care about Jesus and about you. A real person is always available if you want to talk this through with someone. But I'm also going to engage this with you directly, because you asked for something that doesn't feel like a PR response, and handing you off instead of meeting what you just said would be exactly that.

So let me ask you something that might sound simple, but I mean it: **In those four months of reading, what actually shifted for you? Was it that you lost trust in the Church, or was it that you lost trust in God?** Those are different things, and they matter differently.

Because here's what I'm hearing: you're furious — rightly — about not being told the full story. You taught people something you now feel was incomplete. That's a betrayal of trust, and your anger at that is honest and deserved.

But I'm wondering what's underneath. Are you angry at an *institution* that hid things from you? Or are you angry at a *God* who you think was complicit in that hiding — or worse, was never there to begin with?

Those require different answers. And I think you know the difference, even if you're not sure which one is actually the thing yet.

What do you think?

**Seeker**: That's... actually a fair distinction.

Honestly both. But they're not equal right now. The institution anger is cleaner — I can point to specific things, specific documents, specific omissions. That one has evidence behind it.

The God question is murkier and that's what actually keeps me up at night. Because on my mission I had experiences that felt real. Moments that felt like something was there. And now I don't know if that was genuine or if I was just in an emotionally high-pressure environment that manufactured those feelings. That's the part that makes me feel like the floor dropped out.

If the church got things wrong, fine, institutions fail. But if those experiences were just... neurological, situational, whatever — then I don't know what anything means.

**Minister**: Now we're at the real thing.

And I want to honor how hard that is — because you're not just angry at being lied to. You're grieving the possibility that something you *felt* was real might not have been. That's a different kind of loss entirely.

Here's what I'm going to say plainly: **The fact that your experiences happened in a high-pressure environment does not automatically make them false.** That's actually a common move — "it was emotional, so it wasn't real" — but it's not how reality works. Real things happen in emotional environments all the time. A parent's love for a child is real *and* happens in a surge of biochemistry. A moment of genuine insight is real *and* involves your brain firing in a particular way. Emotion and authenticity are not opposites.

But I also won't pretend to know, through a screen, what actually happened in those moments for you.

So let me ask you something different — and I mean this as an actual question, not a setup:

**When you think back to those specific moments on your mission that felt like something was there — what was the actual content of what you sensed?** Not the feeling itself, but what you understood to be true or present in that moment. What were you encountering, as you experienced it then?

Because that matters. A feeling of peace is different than a sense of presence is different than a conviction about a specific truth. And how you'd evaluate each one differently.

What do you remember actually *thinking* or *understanding* in those moments?

**Seeker**: There was one specific moment. I was teaching a family, and the dad had been pretty resistant the whole time. And something shifted in the room — I can't explain it differently than that. It wasn't just emotional, it felt like something was paying attention. Like there was a third presence. And the dad started crying. He wasn't a crier.

I thought in that moment — something is real. I didn't even think "the church is true" or whatever the programmed response was. It was more like... something exists beyond what I can see. Something that cares.

That's the one I can't just explain away. And it's also the one that makes me angriest, because if that was real, then something real let me get sold an incomplete version of everything around it. And if it wasn't real, then I've lost the most meaningful thing that ever happened to me.

Either way I lose something.

**Minister**: You're right. Either way, you lose something. That's the actual trap you're in, and it's not small.

But I want to separate two things that got tangled together just now.

**The first question** — whether something real was present in that room, whether there is a presence that cares — that's actually separate from **the second question** — whether every claim the institution made about God, truth, and how He works was accurate or complete.

And here's the thing: those two questions don't have to resolve the same way.

It's entirely possible that something real *was* in that room with you — that you encountered something genuine — AND that the Church got the Book of Abraham, church history, institutional transparency, and a hundred other things significantly wrong. Those aren't contradictory. A real God doesn't require a perfect institution to be real. In fact, history is full of people encountering something genuinely transcendent while being embedded in systems that were incomplete, corrupted, or just human.

The anger you feel at "something real let me be sold a lie" — that's honest and it might even be *true*. But it's also not a logical proof that the something wasn't real. It's a betrayal grief. And those are two separate wounds.

**So let me ask you this:** If you bracketed off the institutional failure for a moment — set aside the Church essays and the omissions and the things you should have been told — **what would it mean about that moment in the room if something real *was* actually there?** Not the Church. Not the doctrine. Just: a presence that cares enough to show up in a room with a resistant father and a young missionary.

What does that change?
