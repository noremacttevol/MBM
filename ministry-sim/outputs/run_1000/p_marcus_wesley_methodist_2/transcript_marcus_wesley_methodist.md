**Minister**: That weight makes sense. You gave her something you believed was true and good — and she's telling you it landed as *conditioning*, not discovery. That's a real collision, and the question underneath is almost harder than her leaving: *Was I wrong?* Did I hand her something false and call it truth?

I hear that, and I don't want to minimize it.

A couple of things, though — and I'm genuinely asking, not steering.

When your daughter says faith is conditioning — does she mean that *all* faith in *any* god would be conditioning (because all belief gets shaped by where we're raised), or is she saying something more specific: that *the church's* claims aren't actually true, so what you taught her was false conditioning rather than true belief?

Those are different problems, and they land differently on you.

Which one feels like what she's actually saying?

**Seeker**: That's a fair distinction. I hadn't separated it out quite that way.

I think she means both, honestly. She'd say all religion is conditioning — she's read some Dawkins, some others. But she's also specifically said she doesn't find the evidence for Christianity convincing. So it's not just that she found a different faith. She found no faith.

The second one is what keeps me up at night. Because if she's right that the evidence isn't there — I don't know how to answer that. I'm not a theologian. I just know what I've lived. And she'd say that's exactly the problem — that I'm working from experience, not reason.

**Minister**: Now we're at the real thing. Thank you for naming it straight.

So the question that's actually keeping you awake isn't *Did I parent poorly?* It's *What if she's right? What if the evidence really isn't there, and I've built my whole life on something I can't defend?*

That's a serious question, and it deserves a serious answer — not comfort, not a pivot to her heart or yours yet. Just: what do you actually think?

You've lived thirty years as a believing man. You've led people. You've had to answer hard questions in your group. So I'm asking you directly: **When you look at the case for Christianity — not just your experience, but the actual historical and evidential case — what do you actually believe about it? Is it solid to you, or is she naming something you've felt uncertain about?**

No right answer here. I'm asking what's actually true for you.
