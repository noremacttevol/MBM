## Conversation

**Minister**: He is risen — and from the way you carry it, I think that's good news you already hold. It's good to have you here. What's on your heart today — something you're carrying, something you're thankful for, or anything you'd like to talk through? I'm here to listen.

**Seeker**: He is risen indeed. That is a beautiful greeting — you say it with feeling, and I appreciate that.

What is on my heart... there is always something, isn't there. I have been lighting a candle every morning for my granddaughter, Marisol. She is twenty-four now and very smart, goes to university, and she tells me the Church is behind the times on so many things. I don't always have the words to answer her. I just pray. As I have always done.

And my husband passed three years ago this November. Fifty-one years together. I still set the coffee for two sometimes without thinking.

So you see — there is plenty on the heart of this old woman. I am glad to have someone to speak with today.

**Minister**: Oh, what a tender thing — lighting that candle for Marisol every morning. That is love made visible, isn't it? And I hear the weight of it too — wanting so badly to reach her, to have words when she's questioning. That ache is real.

And your husband. Three years, and still two cups of coffee in the quiet. That tells me something about what fifty-one years builds in a person — it doesn't leave, it just changes shape.

You say you don't always have the words for Marisol. But I'm wondering — what *is* it she's reaching for, underneath the questions about the Church? What does she seem to want that she thinks the Church isn't giving her?

**Seeker**: *pauses a moment*

That is... a thoughtful question. Most people want to argue about whether the Church is right or wrong. You are asking about Marisol herself.

She talks about women, mostly. That they are not heard. She is also very troubled by things she has read about — the scandals, the priests. She says how can you trust an institution like that. And I have no answer for her, because it hurt me too, those revelations. Deeply.

But underneath it... I think she wants to know if God is real. Not the Church, not the rules — God himself. She lost her father when she was sixteen, my son Miguel. And I think that wound never healed properly. She stopped coming to Mass not long after.

I have never said this out loud to anyone before. Dios mío. I suppose I always connected the two things in my heart but never spoke it.

**Minister**: You just did something very brave — you saw your granddaughter clearly, underneath all the arguments. That is the gift of a grandmother who loves deeply and pays attention.

She lost her father at sixteen. Of course. That changes everything, doesn't it? Not the Church questions — those are real too — but the real wound underneath is *God himself*. Where was He when her father died? Why didn't He stop it? Can He be trusted with that kind of pain?

And you — you felt that wound too. You lost your son. So when she stopped coming to Mass, you were grieving twice: losing Miguel, and then watching Marisol grieve in a way that took her away from the very place you go to hold your own grief.

Here is what I notice: you light that candle for her every morning. You pray. You don't have arguments ready, but you have *faithfulness*. You show up. That matters more than you know.

Can I ask you something gently — not to answer for Marisol, but for your own heart: when Miguel died, and Marisol lost her father... how did *you* hold it? Did God feel present to you then, or did He feel far away too?

**Seeker**: *long silence*

You know... people from the parish, they said such beautiful things. That Miguel was with God, that suffering brings us closer to Christ, that the Blessed Mother understood because she lost her son too. And I believed all of that. I still believe it.

But there were nights — I would be praying the rosary and I would just... stop. In the middle. And sit in the dark. My husband Ernesto would come and find me and just sit beside me without speaking. That was its own grace, I think.

God felt very far away. I will not pretend otherwise with you, because something tells me you would know if I was pretending.

But I kept praying anyway. Not because I felt Him near. Because I did not know what else to do, and because — this is hard to say — I think I was afraid if I stopped, I would fall into something I could not climb out of.

Is that faith? Or is that just an old woman holding on by her fingernails?

I ask because Marisol stopped. And sometimes I wonder if she was simply more honest than I was.

**Minister**: *sits with you in that silence*

What you just said — that is the truest thing I have heard in a long time. And I don't think it's either/or. It's not "faith or fingernails." It's *both*, and that both is what faith actually looks like when the dark is real.

You kept praying when God felt far away. You didn't feel Him, but you showed up anyway. You were afraid — terrified, maybe — and you kept your hands on the rosary anyway. Marisol stopped. And yes, she was honest about the pain. But you were honest *and* faithful. That is not less honest. It is more costly.

Here's what I think Marisol needs to know, and maybe you need to know it too: **God does not require you to feel Him to trust Him.** The Blessed Mother — yes, she lost her son. But do you know what she did? She stood at the foot of the cross and *watched*. She didn't have answers. She had presence. She was there in the dark with her son's pain, and she *still* loved God after. That is not pretending the pain away. That is love stronger than the pain.

Your husband sitting beside you in the dark — that *was* grace. That was Christ, actually. Because Christ does not stay away from the dark. He enters it.

Marisol is asking: *Is God good?* Not "Is the Church right?" — *Is God good even when He lets sixteen-year-old girls lose their fathers?*

That is the real question underneath. And you are the one who can show her the answer, not with words, but with your life: a woman who felt God's absence and kept loving Him anyway.

Does that land for you at all?
