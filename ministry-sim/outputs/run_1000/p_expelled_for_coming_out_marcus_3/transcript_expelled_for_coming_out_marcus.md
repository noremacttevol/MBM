## Conversation

**Minister**: I'm really glad you're here. However you arrived — full of questions, weary, hopeful, guarded, or not even sure why you opened this — you're welcome exactly as you are, and nothing is asked of you here.

Before anything else — how are you, really? What's been sitting with you lately, or what made you open this today? There's no right answer, and you can share as much or as little as you want. I'm just here to listen first.

**Seeker**: Honestly? I don't even know why I opened this. My boyfriend sent me the link and I was like, yeah sure, another Christian app that's going to tell me God loves me *but*.

So. That's where I'm at.

**Minister**: I hear that. The *but* is everything, isn't it? 

That's not skepticism — that's wisdom. You've felt the conditional love before. You know what it tastes like when someone says they love you and then attaches a string to it, or a demand, or a silent judgment about who you're supposed to be.

So before I say anything at all, I want to ask you straight: **What is the *but* for you?** What's the thing you're pretty sure comes after "God loves you"?

**Seeker**: *laughs a little*

Okay, you actually asked it directly, I'll give you that.

The "but" is always the same thing. God loves you *but* not who you love. God loves you *but* you need to change. God loves you *but* what you are is a problem we need to fix together with prayer and accountability groups and maybe a nice support group where everyone cries on Thursdays.

I spent four years at a Christian college praying to be different. Four years. And then when I stopped pretending, they didn't just ask me to leave — they took my scholarship, my housing, my friends. People I ate dinner with every night just... disappeared. One semester and my whole life was gone.

So yeah. I'm pretty sure I know what comes after "God loves you."

**Minister**: I'm so sorry. That wasn't love — that was punishment dressed up as care. And what happened to you was real loss, real betrayal, and real cruelty from people who claimed to speak for God.

You didn't just lose housing and a scholarship. You lost your whole world in one semester. And the people you ate dinner with every night — they didn't just leave. They chose the institution over you. That's a deep wound, and it makes complete sense that you'd be wary of anyone saying God loves you now.

I want to be honest about something: I can't undo what happened, and I'm not going to pretend it didn't. But I do want to ask you something, and I'm asking it because I actually want to know — not because I have an answer waiting on the other side.

**When you think about Jesus — not the institution, not the people at that college, but the actual person in the gospels — have you ever encountered anything in His words or His actual *treatment* of people that felt different from what you experienced?** 

No pressure to have a clean answer. Honest confusion counts too.

**Seeker**: That's... actually a harder question than I expected.

Yeah. Okay. The Jesus in the gospels is different. He's not who they were. The way he talked to people everyone else had already written off — the woman at the well, the tax collectors, Zacchaeus — like he saw them before he said anything about what they should do differently. That part always got to me.

But here's my problem. Those same people who kicked me out, they love Jesus too. They quote him constantly. So either they're reading him completely wrong, or Jesus means something different in every person's hands, which means... what exactly? He just becomes whatever you need him to be to justify whatever you already wanted to do?

My pastor literally cried when he told me I had to leave. Said he loved me. Said it was breaking his heart. And I'm supposed to trust that the same tradition that produced him also has the real Jesus in it somewhere?

I don't know. That part's still pretty tangled for me.

**Minister**: You've just named something really hard and really true: the same tradition *did* produce both the Jesus who saw the woman at the well, and the people who couldn't see you.

And I'm not going to untangle that for you with a neat answer, because you're right — it *is* tangled. People can weep while they're breaking you. Systems can quote Jesus perfectly while they're crushing someone. That's not your confusion. That's reality.

But here's what I notice: You didn't say "Jesus is just whatever people need him to be." You said the *people* are reading him wrong. And you *know* they're reading him wrong — because you've actually *read* him. You saw it in the gospels. The Jesus who saw the woman at the well wouldn't have taken your scholarship. The Jesus who ate with Zacchaeus wouldn't have disappeared from your dinner table.

So maybe the question isn't "Can I trust the tradition?" — that's a fair no, given what it did to you. 

Maybe it's smaller: **Is there any part of you that wants to know Jesus apart from what that institution told you He was?** Not to fix you, not to make you fit somewhere. Just... to know Him as He actually was with people?

You don't have to answer that today. But I'm asking because I think you already *did* — you opened this link your boyfriend sent.
