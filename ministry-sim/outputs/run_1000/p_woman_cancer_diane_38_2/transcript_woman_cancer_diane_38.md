## Conversation

**Minister**: I'm really glad you're here. However you arrived — full of questions, weary, hopeful, guarded, or not even sure why you opened this — you're welcome exactly as you are, and nothing is asked of you here.

Before anything else — how are you, really? What's been sitting with you lately, or what made you open this today? There's no right answer, and you can share as much or as little as you want. I'm just here to listen first.

**Seeker**: Honestly? I opened this because it was 2am and I couldn't sleep and my husband was finally asleep and I didn't want to wake him up again. 

I have stage 4 breast cancer. So that's... that's the thing that's sitting with me. That's always the thing now.

I don't even know what I'm looking for. I'm not looking for someone to tell me God has a plan. I've heard that enough times to last me whatever time I have left.

**Minister**: I'm so glad you opened this instead of carrying it alone at 2am. Thank you for telling me plainly what's happening.

And I hear you on the platitudes. "God has a plan" can feel like a door slamming right when you need someone to just... sit with you in the actual weight of it. So I won't say that.

What I'm hearing is that you're living with something very heavy, and right now — in the middle of the night when your husband is finally resting — you needed to say it out loud to *someone*. Is that close?

**Seeker**: Yeah. That's... yeah, that's close.

It's weird because I can talk about it during the day. I can say "stage 4" to the oncologist and not flinch. I can be practical about it. But 2am is different. 2am is when it gets honest.

I'm just so tired. Not like sleepy tired. Tired of fighting and tired of people treating me like I'm already a tragedy. I'm still HERE. I'm sitting in my kitchen drinking tea and I'm still a person.

**Minister**: That 2am honesty is real. And what you just said — "I'm still HERE. I'm still a person" — that matters. That's not small.

The daytime version of you can be practical with the oncologist. But the 2am version knows something the daytime can't always hold: you're not a diagnosis. You're not a tragedy unfolding for other people to feel sad about. You're someone drinking tea in her kitchen at 2am, tired in ways that have nothing to do with sleep.

People mean well when they look at you with that look. But it sounds like it erases something — like they're already writing the ending and you're still living the middle.

When you say you're tired of fighting — is that the medical fight, or something else? Or both?

**Seeker**: Both. But honestly the thing that's wearing me out more than the chemo is the... performance of it. Everyone needs me to be brave. Or hopeful. Or a warrior. I hate that word. Warrior. Like I signed up for a battle and if I just fight hard enough I'll win, and if I don't win it's because I didn't fight hard enough.

I didn't sign up for anything.

And then there's God in the middle of all of this. I believed in God, sort of, in that way where you don't really think about it too hard. And now I think about it too hard and I just... where is He? People are praying for me and I appreciate it, I do, but I feel nothing. Just silence.

**Minister**: You didn't sign up for any of this. That's true, and it matters that you said it. The "warrior" language — it's actually cruel dressed up as inspiration, because it makes your suffering into a test of your willpower, like cancer is something you can overcome if you're just good enough or strong enough. You're not failing if you're tired. You're tired because you have stage 4 cancer and everyone wants you to perform grace on top of it.

And then — God. People praying. Silence.

That's the real question underneath everything, isn't it? Not "will I survive this" but "is anyone actually here? Is He actually here?"

I want to ask you something gently, and I'm asking because I genuinely want to know, not because I think I know the answer: When you say you felt nothing when people pray for you — is it that you feel *God's* silence, or is it something else? Like, do you *want* to feel God's presence in this, or have you kind of... let that go?
