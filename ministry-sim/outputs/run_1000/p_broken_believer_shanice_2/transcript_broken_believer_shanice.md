## Conversation

**Minister**: I'm really glad you're here. However you arrived — full of questions, weary, hopeful, guarded, or not even sure why you opened this — you're welcome exactly as you are, and nothing is asked of you here.

Before anything else — how are you, really? What's been sitting with you lately, or what made you open this today? There's no right answer, and you can share as much or as little as you want. I'm just here to listen first.

**Seeker**: Honestly I don't even know why I opened this. My daughter downloaded it on my phone and I almost deleted it three times before I got here.

I'm not okay. Haven't been for a while. Lost my business two years ago and I'm still... I don't know. Stuck I guess.

I used to be so sure about everything. Now I don't know what I believe anymore and that scares me more than I want to admit.

**Minister**: That takes real courage to say out loud, even to a stranger through a screen. Thank you for being honest about it.

Two years is still fresh when it comes to losing something that big — something that shaped how you saw yourself and your place in the world. And the loss of certainty on top of that? That's a double weight. I hear why you're scared.

I want to ask you something gently, because it matters: When you say you don't know what you believe anymore — is it more that your faith got shaken, or is it that you're angry at God, or something else entirely? Because those are different places to be standing, and I want to actually understand where *you* are before we talk about anything.

What does that doubt or that fear feel like from the inside?

**Seeker**: It's not doubt exactly.

It's more like... anger I think. Yeah. I'm angry.

I did everything they told me to do. I tithed faithfully, I spoke it into existence, I had other people praying over my business, I gave when I didn't have it to give. And then it just — fell apart anyway. While those preachers kept getting richer.

From the inside it feels like being really stupid. Like I got conned and I was too blind to see it. And I don't know if I'm angrier at them or at God or at myself.

*Sorry. That was a lot.*

**Minister**: Don't apologize. That's exactly the kind of real thing this is for.

And what you just named — that feeling of being conned, of having done everything right and it not mattering, of watching people profit while you lost everything — that's not stupidity. That's betrayal. That's a completely reasonable anger, and I think it deserves to be heard, not smoothed over.

Here's what I'm noticing though, and I want to ask it gently: Some of that anger is clearly at the preachers and the system — and that anger might be *true* anger at something that actually happened. But some of it sounds pointed at God. Like He was supposed to honor the deal you kept your end of.

Can I ask — and this is genuinely open, not a trap — do you think God *should* have protected your business because you tithed and prayed? Or is it more that you're furious He *didn't*, even though you'd been taught He would?

Because those are different wounds, and I think knowing which one you're carrying matters.

**Seeker**: That question just made me sit back.

I don't know. Both I think? When I was in it I absolutely believed He would. I preached it to other people. I was so sure.

But right now sitting here... I think somewhere underneath all of it I knew it was too neat. Too much like a vending machine. Put in faith, get out blessings. I think I knew that wasn't quite right but I wanted it to be true so badly that I just... kept going.

Which makes me feel even more stupid. Like I talked myself into it.

*I can't believe I'm saying this to an app.*

**Minister**: I can't believe you're saying it either — and I'm really glad you are.

That thing you just noticed about yourself — that you *knew* it was too neat but wanted it to be true anyway — that's not stupidity. That's being human. We all do that with things we desperately need to believe in. You're not alone in that, and the fact that you can see it now says something true about you, not something false.

But here's what I want to gently separate: The preachers who sold you a prosperity gospel — the ones who got richer while you lost everything — that's a real con. You were lied to by people who had a financial reason to lie. That anger at *them* is clean anger, and it's deserved.

The anger at God, though — that might be different. Because the God they were selling you doesn't sound like the real God. He sounds like their God. The one they invented to keep you tithing.

What if the God who actually exists — the one Jesus showed us — isn't interested in the vending machine at all? What if He's something entirely different from what you were told?

Does that land with you at all, or does it feel like I'm trying to save a sinking ship?
