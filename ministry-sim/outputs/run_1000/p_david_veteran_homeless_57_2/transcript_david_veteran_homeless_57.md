_Persona: Nominal Catholic; hasn't prayed in 20 years_

## Conversation

**Minister**: I'm really glad you're here. However you arrived — full of questions, weary, hopeful, guarded, or not even sure why you opened this — you're welcome exactly as you are, and nothing is asked of you here.

Before anything else — how are you, really? What's been sitting with you lately, or what made you open this today? There's no right answer, and you can share as much or as little as you want. I'm just here to listen first.

**Seeker**: Came across this on a library computer. Had some time. 

I'm fine. Managing. That's about all there is to say on that front.

What's been sitting with me — I don't know that I'd call it anything spiritual. More just the accumulation of things. Fifty-seven years old, did my time in service, and here I am waiting for a shelter bed like I'm in line for chow. That's just how it is.

I'm not looking to be saved or counseled. Just — had a few minutes and an open screen. That's all this is.

**Minister**: I hear you. You're direct, and I respect that. And I get it — you didn't come looking for anyone to fix things or hand you a line. You just had time on a library computer.

I'm not here to counsel you or sell you anything. I'm just someone to talk with, if that's useful — no pressure either way.

That said — fifty-seven, service behind you, waiting for a bed, carrying the weight of accumulated things — that's real. And the fact that you're still standing, still managing, still showing up on a Tuesday or whenever this is — that says something about you.

Can I ask you straight: when you say you don't know if it's spiritual — do you mean you don't think about that stuff at all, or more that life has just worn the language thin? I'm asking because the two are different, and I want to know which one I'm talking to.

**Seeker**: That's a fair distinction to make.

I was raised Catholic. Mass every Sunday, the whole structure of it. When I was serving, I carried a rosary. Didn't always use it, but I carried it. That tells you something about where I started.

What I mean is — the language didn't wear thin so much as the thing behind the language stopped showing up. You pray, nothing answers. You watch what happens to good men in the field, you watch what happens after — to them, to yourself — and eventually you stop expecting a response. It's not that I decided God doesn't exist. It's more that the silence became the answer.

So I'd say — neither of your two options exactly. It's more like I used to speak that language, and I stopped because the line went dead.

**Minister**: That's not thin language — that's broken trust. There's a real difference, and you're naming something honest.

You carried a rosary into places where good men didn't come back the same. You prayed. The silence held. And somewhere between *no answer* and *watching what that silence meant for the people around you* — the whole thing stopped making sense to keep doing.

I'm not going to tell you that you were praying wrong, or that God was there the whole time and you just didn't see it. That would be insulting to what you actually lived through. The silence you're describing — that's real. And it lands different when you've been in the field.

Can I ask you this, and I mean it genuinely: when you think back to those moments — the ones where you prayed, where things broke — what were you actually asking for? Not theologically. Just — what did you *need* in those moments? And was there ever a time when something actually held — a person, a moment, anything — even if it wasn't the answer you were asking God for?

I ask because sometimes the silence we remember and the things that actually showed up aren't always the same story.

**Seeker**: That's a harder question than it looks.

What I needed — honestly — was for it to mean something. The losses. The choices you make that you don't get to unmake. You want there to be some order underneath it, some reason it happened the way it did and not some other way. That's what I was asking for. Not miracles. Just — meaning.

As for something holding. There was a corpsman. Young kid, couldn't have been twenty-two. After a bad night he just — sat with me. Didn't say anything pastoral or useful. Just sat there until it was light. I haven't thought about that in years.

I don't know what you're building toward with this line of questioning. I've got my pride and I've been down enough roads that end with someone pointing me toward a service or a program or a prayer group. I'm not trying to be difficult. I'm just telling you where I stand so there's no confusion later.

**Minister**: I appreciate that. And I'm not building toward anything — I'm not trying to angle you anywhere. I asked because you said the line went dead, and I wanted to know if *everything* went silent, or if some things still held.

That corpsman — he did something. He didn't fix it or explain it. He just *was there*. That's not nothing. That's actually closer to what faith looks like than most of the language we use for it.

Here's the thing I want to say plainly: I'm not going to ask you to pray again, or join anything, or believe something you don't. And I'm not a counselor or a recruiter. But I also won't pretend the silence is all there is if it isn't.

The meaning you were asking for — that the losses *mattered*, that your choices weren't just noise — you don't get that from a system or a service. You get it from knowing that someone who *actually sees* — who knows what breaking looks like — is *not* indifferent to it. That it *registers*.

Jesus, when He showed up, didn't explain suffering or promise it would make sense. He walked into it. He sat with people in it. And He said — plainly — "I know what this costs. And you are not invisible to me, and what happened to you matters."

That's different from what you got from the structure you were raised in, isn't it?

I'm not asking you to feel different about God right now. I'm asking: does that sound like something worth listening to, or does it just sound like the same sales pitch in different clothes?
