## Conversation

**Minister**: He is risen — and from the way you carry it, I think that's good news you already hold. It's good to have you here. What's on your heart today — something you're carrying, something you're thankful for, or anything you'd like to talk through? I'm here to listen.

**Seeker**: He is risen indeed. I appreciate the greeting.

I'll be honest, I'm not sure what I'm looking for here exactly. I've been leading a small group for eleven years. I know my Bible. But my daughter came home last month and told me she doesn't believe any of it anymore — called it cultural conditioning, said I just believe what I was raised to believe. I don't have a good answer for that and it's been sitting with me.

**Minister**: That lands hard. Eleven years of leading others, and the person closest to you questions whether you actually *believe* it yourself — that's not a small thing to carry.

I hear two things happening at once: grief over your daughter, and something deeper stirring in you about your own faith. Both are real.

Can I ask — when she said that, what hit you hardest? Was it watching her walk away, or was it the question itself about whether your faith is actually *yours*?

**Seeker**: Both, if I'm being straight about it.

But I don't want to make this into some kind of crisis of faith. I believe what I believe. I've lived by it for fifty-eight years and I'm not about to unravel because a twenty-six year old found some philosophy she likes.

What bothered me more, honestly, was that I couldn't answer her. I just sat there. I've taught doctrine for over a decade and my own daughter asked me a reasonable question and I had nothing. That doesn't sit right with me.

**Minister**: That's the wound that matters — and I respect you for naming it straight. It's not "am I losing my faith," it's "I couldn't *answer* her." That's different. That's about being unprepared when it counted.

Here's what I'm hearing underneath: you know *what* you believe, but when she asked *why* — not in a defensive way, but genuinely — you didn't have the words. And that gap is bothering you more than her doubt is.

Can I ask this honestly: when she said you believe what you were raised to believe — do you think she was asking a real question, or was she making a statement? In other words, was she open to hearing your answer, or had she already decided?

Because that changes what you actually need.

**Seeker**: That's a fair distinction. I've been thinking about it the same way.

Honestly, I think she was making a statement. She wasn't sitting across the table waiting for me to persuade her. She's read things, she's thought about it, she's made up her mind for now. So no, I don't think she was open in that moment.

But here's the thing that keeps coming back to me. Even if she wasn't open — I should have had something to say. Not to win an argument. Just to give her a real answer. Something from my actual life, not just doctrine I could recite. And I went blank. That tells me something and I'm not sure I like what it tells me.

**Minister**: Now you're asking the real question. And it's braver than it sounds.

Because you're not asking "how do I get her back to belief" — you're asking "do *I* actually have something real here, or am I just running on what I was handed?" That's the thing that woke you up when she spoke.

Here's what I'm noticing: a man who's taught Scripture for eleven years, who knows it well enough to lead others, who *still* went blank. That doesn't usually happen because someone doesn't know their material. It happens when the material hasn't *touched* them yet in a way that becomes *theirs*.

So let me ask you differently. Not about doctrine — about your actual life.

When has God been real to you? Not when you were taught He was, but when you *met* Him. A time when something happened that you couldn't explain away, or a moment when you were broken and He showed up in a way that was unmistakably *Him*. 

Do you have one of those?
