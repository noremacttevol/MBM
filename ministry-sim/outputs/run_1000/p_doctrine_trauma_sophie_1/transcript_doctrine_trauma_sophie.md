Sorry. That's probably too much already.

**Minister**: It's not too much. Thank you for saying it straight — and for staying instead of closing the app three times. That took something.

Before we go anywhere else, I want to name what I'm hearing: there's a physical thing happening in your body when you hit certain theology talk, and *that's* real data. Your chest getting tight isn't drama — it's your body telling you something matters and something hurts. And then guilt piles on top of it, which makes the whole thing heavier.

Here's what I'm curious about, and only if you want to say: **Do you know what specific theological thing makes it tighten?** Is it a particular topic your church keeps teaching, or is it more that theology talk in general now triggers it?

I'm asking because there's a difference between "I don't understand this doctrine and feel stupid" and "I heard something about God that made me feel unsafe or unmoored." Those are completely different wounds, and they need different kinds of air.

You don't have to answer right now. But I want to understand what's actually sitting in your chest, not just the guilt wrapper around it.

**Seeker**: It's... it's predestination.

There. I said it. My hands are literally shaking a little typing that word.

Everyone in my church talks about it like it's the most comforting thing in the world? Like, "God is sovereign, isn't that beautiful" and I'm sitting there thinking — what if He already decided I'm not one of the ones He loves? What if I'm just... not elect? And there's nothing I can do about it and I already don't know and I might never know and —

Sorry. I'm spiraling a little just saying it out loud.

I know it's probably irrational. My pastor would definitely say it's irrational. But knowing that doesn't make it stop.

**Minister**: It's not irrational. Stop there. 

What you're describing — the fear that you might already be rejected before you even had a choice, that God's love might not actually be *for you* — that's not a logic problem. That's a wound about who God actually is. And your body knows it's serious, which is why your chest gets tight. You're not spiraling; you're naming something real.

Here's what I want to gently set beside what you've been hearing:

You know the story of the prodigal son, right? The one where the younger son basically says "I'm leaving, I'm done, I want nothing to do with you"? And he goes and wastes everything, hits rock bottom, and finally thinks, "My father probably hates me. I'll go back and ask to be a servant."

And then — his father *sees him coming* and **runs to him**. Not walks. Runs. Throws a party. Kills the fattened calf. Puts a ring on his finger. 

Jesus told that story to say: *This is what God is like.*

Here's my honest question for you, and I really do mean it as a real question — not a trap, not a setup:

**If that father is what God is actually like, does a God who runs to a son who abandoned Him sound like a God who pre-rejects people He supposedly loves?**

I'm not asking you to answer right now. Just — sit with that for a second.

**Seeker**: I know that story. I love that story actually.

But... okay this is where I get stuck. Because people in my tradition would say — the father only runs to *his son*. The elect. And how do I know I'm his child and not just... someone else's kid watching from the road?

I've heard that story used both ways and it just — it doesn't land the same way for me anymore because there's always this asterisk. Like, beautiful parable, *but*. And then the but is predestination again and I'm back in the same place.

I'm not trying to be difficult. I genuinely want the story to help. It used to help. I just can't unhear the asterisks.

**Minister**: You're not being difficult. You're being honest — and that's actually braver than pretending the asterisks aren't there.

And here's the thing: you've spotted something real. The story *does* get flattened when you keep adding footnotes to it. A parable about a father's love stops being about the father's love the moment you make it a test about whether you qualify for it.

Let me ask you something different — not about theology, just about the story itself:

**In that parable, does the father ever ask the son to *prove* he's really his son before he runs to him?** Does he wait for evidence? Does he check a list? Or does he just... see his son and run?

I'm asking because there's a version of that story where the father's love is *unconditional* — he runs to anyone who comes home — and there's a version where his love is locked behind a door only some people have the key to. 

And I'm wondering: which version do you think Jesus was actually describing?

Not what your tradition taught you about it. Not what makes theological sense in a system. Just — when you read it, what do you feel Jesus was saying about what God is like?
