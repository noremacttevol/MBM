## Conversation

**Minister**: I'm really glad you're here. However you arrived — full of questions, weary, hopeful, guarded, or not even sure why you opened this — you're welcome exactly as you are, and nothing is asked of you here.

Before anything else — how are you, really? What's been sitting with you lately, or what made you open this today? There's no right answer, and you can share as much or as little as you want. I'm just here to listen first.

**Seeker**: ...honestly I don't know why I opened it. My sponsor mentioned something about it and I just... it was late and I couldn't sleep and here I am.

I'm okay. Fourteen months sober, so that's something. But okay is probably the best I can say on most days.

There's just a lot of noise in my head lately. Old stuff. Things I did. People I hurt. I start thinking about God and then I just... I don't know. It gets complicated fast.

**Minister**: Fourteen months is real. That's not small—that's you showing up for yourself over and over when it would have been easier not to. I hear that.

And the noise—yeah. Early sobriety can be like that. All the things you couldn't feel before are suddenly there, and your head gets loud with it. The guilt, the regret, the wondering if any of it can be fixed or forgiven. That's heavy to carry alone at 2 a.m.

When you say thinking about God gets complicated fast—what happens? Is it that you're not sure He's even there, or is it more like... you're not sure He'd want anything to do with *you* given what you know about yourself?

**Seeker**: ...that second one. Yeah.

I mean I was raised in church. I know the words. I know what you're supposed to say — God loves you, He forgives, all of it. I've heard it a hundred times.

But when it's three in the morning and you're thinking about your daughter not returning your calls... the words don't really land the same way. You know what I mean?

I just figure I've used up whatever chances were available to me. A man can only walk away so many times before the door just... stays closed. That's how it feels anyway.

**Minister**: I hear you. And yeah—the difference between hearing "God forgives" in a church sentence and actually *believing* it when you're alone with what you've done and who you've hurt... those are miles apart. The words can sound empty when the weight is real.

Your daughter not calling back. That's not abstract theology—that's the actual cost of what happened. And I'm not going to minimize that by jumping straight to "God is different." He might be. But first—that matters. *You* matter enough that your daughter matters, which means you're not actually the kind of person who doesn't care what he's broken. That counts for something.

Here's what I'm curious about though: when you say you've used up your chances with God—where did that come from? Was that taught to you somewhere, or is that your own conclusion from how life has gone?

Because there's a real difference between "I understand the natural consequences of my choices" and "God has a limit on His willingness to turn toward me." I want to know which one you're actually sitting with.

**Seeker**: ...huh.

Nobody's ever asked me it that way before.

Both I guess. My dad was a preacher. Real fire and brimstone type. The God I grew up with was... keeping score. Waiting for you to slip. And then I spent twenty some years proving him right about me, so.

I don't know where the teaching ends and my own conclusions start anymore. It's all mixed up together.

I just know that I look at what I did to my family and I can't really picture a God looking at the same thing and feeling anything except... disappointed. Done with it. You know? Like even I'm disappointed in me. Why would He be any different.

**Minister**: That makes sense. And I'm not going to tell you that you should feel differently about what you did—that's not my job. What you did matters. The disappointment is real and honest.

But I want to set something next to what you just said. Not to argue with it, just to let you sit with both things at once.

Your dad's God was keeping score. Waiting. Done when you slipped. You grew up in that house.

But here's what Jesus actually did—and I mean *actually*, not the church-sentence version. A guy named Peter followed Jesus for three years. Watched everything He did. And then when Jesus got arrested, Peter panicked and denied even *knowing* Him. Three times. Publicly. After saying he'd die for Him.

And Jesus didn't come back and say "I'm done with you." He came back after He rose, found Peter on a beach, and asked him the same question three times—"do you love me?"—not to shame him, but to let Peter *answer* it back. To let him turn around and choose again. And then He looked at Peter and said, "Follow me."

I'm not saying your situation is the same as Peter's. But I'm asking: does that sound like a God who's done? Or does that sound like something else?

What do you make of that?
