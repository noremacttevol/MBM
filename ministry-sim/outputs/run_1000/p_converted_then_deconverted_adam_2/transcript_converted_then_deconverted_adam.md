**Seeker**: It's both, but they're kind of tangled together in a way that's hard to separate.

I'm just figuring out who I am now, which feels embarrassing to admit at my age.

**Minister**: That's not embarrassing—that's honest. And you just named something real: you didn't just lose a community. You lost the *story* that made sense of you. That's bigger than missing Sunday mornings.

Eleven years is long enough to build an identity around something. It just feels heavier when you're asking it the second time.

Here's what I'm genuinely curious about, though: when you say you lost the story—was the story itself what cracked, or was it the institution around it? Like, did you stop believing the *narrative* (God saved you, you have purpose), or did you stop believing in the *structure* that was telling you that story?

Because those aren't the same thing, and I think it matters for what's actually sitting with you on quiet Sundays.

**Seeker**: That's... actually a decent question. I've thought about it a lot.

It was the narrative itself. I didn't just get burned by a church or a pastor or something like that — I know people who left for those reasons and I get it, but that's not my story. Mine was slower. I just read too much, thought too hard, and lost the ability to unsee the contradictions. The historical stuff, the problem of evil, the way the texts actually fit together when you look closely. It wasn't one thing. It was a thousand small things until the whole structure just... wasn't there anymore.

So yeah, it was the story. Which is almost harder in a way? Because I can't even be angry at a bad pastor or a toxic church. I just think it isn't true. And that leaves me with nothing to push against.

**Minister**: That's a different kind of grief, isn't it? When you can't blame the messenger, you're stuck with the message itself. And you can't even hate it—you just think it's not real. That's lonelier somehow.

Okay, so you read the texts closely, you sat with the problem of evil, you let yourself think. And you landed somewhere. I'm not going to argue you back—you know the arguments already, and you've thought harder than most people ever will. That's not what I'm asking about.

But I want to ask you about one specific thing, because it feels like it's underneath the Sunday-morning circling.

You said you had a narrative: Adam who God saved, who had purpose, who was going somewhere. And when I hear that, I'm hearing that the story worked for you—not just as belief, but as *meaning*. It made your life make sense. It told you that you mattered and why.

So here's what I'm wondering: when you stopped believing the Christian story was true, what happened to that *need* for a coherent narrative? Did it go away, or did it just... go somewhere else? Because you're thirty-five, you're smart enough to see the contradictions, and you're still here on a quiet Sunday, still circling. And I don't think you're circling because you miss the building.

What are you actually looking for now?
