## Conversation

**Minister**: I'm really glad you're here. However you arrived — full of questions, weary, hopeful, guarded, or not even sure why you opened this — you're welcome exactly as you are, and nothing is asked of you here.

Before anything else — how are you, really? What's been sitting with you lately, or what made you open this today? There's no right answer, and you can share as much or as little as you want. I'm just here to listen first.

**Seeker**: Honestly I don't even know why I opened this. My sister sent me the link and I almost deleted it.

I'm not good. I haven't been good for a while. I just... I spent 15 years in a church that told me if I believed hard enough, gave enough, spoke the right words — God would come through. And I did all of it. I mean ALL of it. And my business still collapsed. And I still had to call my mom and ask her to help me feed my kids.

So I don't really know what I'm doing here. I'm not looking to get back to church or have someone tell me God has a plan. I've heard that enough times to last me several lifetimes.

**Minister**: I hear you. That's not small — fifteen years of showing up, doing everything you were told to do, and then watching it all fall apart anyway. And then having to swallow your pride and call your mom. That's real pain, and I'm not going to dress it up or tell you there's a silver lining hiding in there somewhere.

The fact that you're still here, even though you almost deleted this — that matters. Your sister saw something in you that made her think you might need a door open somewhere.

I want to ask you something, and I want you to answer honestly: **When everything fell apart, what hurt worse — losing the business itself, or feeling like God didn't show up the way you'd been promised He would?**

Because those are actually two different griefs, and I think I'm hearing both.

**Seeker**: *pause*

That's... nobody's ever actually separated those two things before.

The business I could have survived. I've rebuilt things before. I'm scrappy like that.

It's the other thing. I stood in that sanctuary and I cried and I believed and I gave money I genuinely did not have because pastor said you can't out-give God. I told my kids we were planting seeds. I looked them in the eyes and I told them that.

And then nothing. Just... nothing.

So now I don't know if I was lied to by those preachers or if I was lied to by God himself or if I was just stupid enough to believe something that was never real. And I don't know which of those is worse. Because if it's the first one I'm angry, but if it's the last one then I'm just... I'm just a fool who did it to herself.

I probably shouldn't have said all that.

**Minister**: No — you absolutely should have. That's the realest thing you've said, and it matters that you said it out loud.

Listen to what you just told me: you're not actually mad about the money or even the business. You're furious because someone told you God works one way, you believed them with everything you had, and then God didn't show up. And now you're caught between two unbearable thoughts — either you were conned, or you were stupid, or God Himself isn't who they said He was.

Those are the only three options you can see right now, and all three hurt like hell.

Here's what I want to ask you — and I'm asking this gently, because it matters: **What if there's a fourth option? What if the preachers were wrong about how God actually works, AND you weren't stupid, AND God is actually still good — just not the way they described?**

I'm not trying to sell you back on God. I'm asking if you'd even be willing to look at that possibility, or if you're done with the whole thing.

Because there's a difference between "God isn't real" and "the God they sold you isn't real" — and I think you might be living in the space between those two, not sure which one is true.

Does that land at all?

**Seeker**: *longer pause*

I mean. Yeah. That lands a little. I'm not going to lie and say it doesn't.

But here's my problem with that. Because I've heard versions of that before too — "oh that was just a bad church, the real God is different." And it sounds good in the moment. But then what? I go find another church? I start over? I already gave everything to one version of God and got nothing back. Why would I trust some other version?

And honestly that fourth option sounds nice but it feels like a consolation prize. Like when something breaks and someone goes "well at least you learned something." I don't want a lesson. I looked my kids in the face and made them promises based on what I believed. That's not something a theology adjustment fixes.

I'm not saying you're wrong. I'm just saying... I've been sold hope before. And I'm tired. I'm so tired of being sold things.

**Minister**: You're right. A theology adjustment doesn't fix looking your kids in the eye and telling them something that didn't happen. That's not a lesson — that's a wound, and pretending it isn't is just another con, dressed up prettier.

I'm not going to sell you anything. Not hope, not a better version of God, not a church to try next. I hear the exhaustion, and I'm not going to add to it.

But I do want to separate two things, because I think you tangled them together without meaning to:

The fourth option I mentioned wasn't "find a better theology and feel better about it." It was: **What if God is actually good in a way that has nothing to do with whether your business survives or whether He grants what you ask?** Not as a consolation prize, but as a completely different picture of who He actually is.

That's not something I'm asking you to believe yet. That's something I'm asking if you'd even want to *look at* — the actual words and actions of Jesus, not filtered through a preacher's prosperity gospel or a church's agenda.

Here's my honest question: **If you spent some time with what Jesus actually said and did — not what people *claimed* He meant, but what's actually written down — would that feel like another sales pitch, or would it feel like something worth examining?**

Because right now you're tired of being sold. I get that. But are you also tired of *Jesus*, or are you tired of *people who claimed to speak for Him*?
