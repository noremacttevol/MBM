So I guess what's sitting with me is that I don't know what's real anymore. Any of it.

**Minister**: That is not a strange thing to admit. That is a human being alone at 11pm who has been betrayed in the places that were supposed to be safe. I hear that, and I'm sorry.

Here's what I want to say plainly: your anger is not the problem. Your anger is what happens when something real was broken. Anyone who told you otherwise was wrong.

The question underneath all this — "what's real anymore" — that's the one that matters. And it makes sense that you're asking it. When the people and systems fail you that badly, of course you wonder if anything is trustworthy at all.

Can I ask you something gently? When you say you don't know what's real anymore — is that mostly about whether *people* can be trusted, or is it about whether *God* is real? Are those tangled together for you, or is one of them the sharper wound?

**Seeker**: They're tangled. That's the honest answer.

Because she prayed with me. In that office. She prayed out loud, with my hands in her hands, asking God to protect my marriage. And either God was just... absent for that, or the whole thing is performance and I can't tell the difference anymore between genuine faith and someone just knowing the right words.

I spent seventeen years learning the right words too. So now I don't even trust my own faith. How do you know if anything you felt was real, or if you were just... doing what people around you do?

That's the sharper wound, I think. Not even the betrayal itself. It's that she used the language of God to do it. To cover it. And I can't unhear that.

**Minister**: Now I'm hearing the real thing. And it's sharper because it's not just about her — it's about whether *you* can trust what you felt, what you believed, what you thought you knew about God. She weaponized the language, and now that language feels poisoned in your own mouth.

That's devastating. I won't minimize it.

Here's what I'm holding: you spent seventeen years learning words. You also spent seventeen years *raising children*. You also spent seventeen years *trying to protect your marriage*. Those aren't small things, and they're not just performance — not all of it. Some of what you did in those years was real, even if the institution failed you and one person inside it lied to your face while praying.

But I think what you're actually asking is: how do I know the difference between genuine encounter with God and just... going through the motions so well that I convinced myself?

That's the question that matters.

Here's what I want to ask you — and this is real, not leading: when you think back across those seventeen years, were there moments — even small ones — where you *felt* something that wasn't about the performance or the words? Times when you were alone, or when something broke open in you that had nothing to do with what you were supposed to feel?

I'm asking because the poison she used doesn't automatically make everything before it false. But I think you need to know the difference yourself.

**Seeker**: There's a long pause before I respond.

There was one. 

My dad died six years ago, and I was sitting in my car in the hospital parking lot before going in to say goodbye. And I wasn't reciting anything or doing anything right. I was just... terrified and asking not to be alone in it. And something shifted. I don't have a better word than that. Something shifted.

I haven't thought about that in a long time.

But here's where I get stuck — because I can tell you're trying to help me hold onto something real, and I appreciate that, I do. But that moment was mine, before anyone else was in it. Everything that's been destroyed since then had people in it. Church people. Spiritual authority people. And I don't know how to have faith that isn't tangled up with community, with guidance, with trusting someone to help you interpret it. That's how I was formed.

So that moment in the parking lot might be real. But I don't know how to build anything from one moment when I can't trust the builders.

**Minister**: You just said something true that most people never get to. You're not asking how to get back what you had. You're asking how to build something *new* when the architects betrayed you.

That's different. Harder. More honest.

And you're right — you were formed by people. We all are. You learned the language, the framework, the way to interpret what's happening inside you *from* community. So of course you can't just strip that away and rebuild solo. That's not how humans work.

But I want to gently push back on one thing you said: that everything destroyed since then had people in it, so the people are the problem.

The people *were* the problem — absolutely. She was. The church leadership was. Your husband was. But the thing they betrayed wasn't *their interpretation of God.* It was God Himself. They used His name to cover lies. That's a specific kind of evil.

Here's what I'm wondering: Is it possible that what you felt in that parking lot — that *shift* when you asked not to be alone — what if that was actually God? Not the church's God. Not the language you learned. Just... the real one, meeting you when you were terrified and honest.

And the question isn't whether you can trust people to guide you into faith anymore. The question is: can you trust *that* — that moment, that shift — enough to follow it yourself, even without an interpreter?

I'm not saying you never need community again. But what if the rebuilding starts with asking God — directly, alone — what He actually is to you, outside of what anyone told you to believe?

What would that feel like, to ask Him that?
