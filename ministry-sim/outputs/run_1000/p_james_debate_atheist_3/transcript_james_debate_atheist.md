**Minister**: You're asking the right thing, and I'm going to answer it honestly — which means admitting where I actually *can't* answer it fully.

The short version: I can't give you an epistemology that gets you *out* of interpretation. You're right that everyone's reading through a lens. The Crusaders, your father, me, you — we're all filtering. There's no view from nowhere.

But here's what's actually different about going *back to the text* instead of staying in the tradition: The Crusaders didn't have Jesus saying "love your enemies." Your father had Jesus saying pray, but he *also* had Jesus healing people and calling out injustice — and if he was reading *all of it*, he couldn't get to "prayer instead of therapy" without editing. The text itself pushes back on truncated readings.

So the epistemological move isn't "I found the pure Jesus." It's "I'm letting the *hardest parts* — the parts that contradict what I want — stay in the picture." Your father's tradition let him drop the parts that inconvenienced him. That's not interpretation; that's curation.

And yeah — I have a lens too. But I'm trying to hold the *tension* instead of resolving it by deleting what's uncomfortable.

Here's what I genuinely don't know: whether that method gets you to truth. I think it gets you *closer*. But I can't prove it.

What do you think — does that distinction land as real, or does it still feel like special pleading?

**Seeker**: It lands as more honest than I expected, I'll give you that. The "view from nowhere" concession is real — most people arguing for religion don't make it.

But here's where I'm still stuck. You said my father was editing — dropping the parts that contradicted him. Sure. But you're doing a version of the same move right now. You're selecting "love your enemies" as the authentic core and treating the inconvenient parts he used as the distortion. How do you know which direction the editing goes? Maybe the violence and the hierarchy and the "wives submit" stuff is the *real* Jesus and the parts you like are what got softened over time. You're not less curated than he was — you're just curating toward different values. Your values, probably good ones, but yours.

And I'll be honest about something. I didn't just watch my father use God abstractly. I watched him use the language of love and surrender and trust to keep my mother small. Real specific harm, not philosophical. So when someone tells me the text "pushes back on truncated readings" — I need more than that. Because he would have said the same thing. He thought he was reading it straight too.

That's not rhetorical. That's actually where I am.

**Minister**: Okay. I'm going to sit with what you just said, because it matters more than the epistemology question — though they're tangled together.

You watched someone use the language of love to keep your mother small. That's not a hermeneutics problem. That's a wound. And if I answer the question about which Jesus is "real" without touching that first, I'm doing exactly what your father did — using ideas to move past something that actually happened to someone you love.

So let me say this clearly: **What he did was wrong.** Not a misreading. Not a valid interpretation taken too far. Wrong. Full stop.

Now — to your actual question, the hard one: You're right that I'm curating. But there's a real difference between curating *toward* something and curating *away from* harm.

If you go back to the text without an agenda except "what would actually help people," the violence and the hierarchy *require* heavy lifting to defend. "Wives submit" requires context, cultural translation, theological argument. But "love your enemies" and "the last shall be first" and a man touching a leper when everyone said don't — those just *sit there* without explanation. They don't need defending.

The direction of the curation matters. One direction requires you to *add* complexity to make harm sound reasonable. The other direction has the complexity *already built in* by the text itself.

But here's the real answer to your question: **I can't prove which Jesus is real without asking you something first.**

When you watched what happened to your mother — do you think what your father did was actually *good*, or do you know it was wrong?
