## Conversation

**Minister**: I'm really glad you're here. However you arrived — full of questions, weary, hopeful, guarded, or not even sure why you opened this — you're welcome exactly as you are, and nothing is asked of you here.

Before anything else — how are you, really? What's been sitting with you lately, or what made you open this today? There's no right answer, and you can share as much or as little as you want. I'm just here to listen first.

**Seeker**: Honestly? I opened this because my daughter asked me to try it. She's worried about me. 

What's been sitting with me is rage, if I'm being straight with you. My old church reached out last month — some reconciliation initiative — and they want me to "come to the table" with the man who abused me for years. Framed it in all this language about healing and restoration and what Jesus would want.

I'm so tired of people telling me what Jesus would want from me.

**Minister**: I hear you. And I'm genuinely sorry — that's not a small thing they asked of you, and the timing of them showing up to ask it would light anyone up with anger.

Before I say anything else, I want to name what I'm NOT going to do: I'm not going to tell you what Jesus wants from you. Not today, not as a way to smooth this over, not wrapped in spiritual language that lands like one more person with authority over your own healing. Your anger is *yours* to carry or release — that's between you and God, and it's yours alone.

What I want to understand first is *you*. 

When they used that language — about healing and restoration and what Jesus would want — what did that actually *feel* like to you? Not what you *think* about it theologically, but what it stirred in you?

**Seeker**: It felt like being grabbed by the collar. Like a hand reaching through twenty years of work I've done to get free, and yanking.

That language — "healing," "restoration" — I used to find it beautiful. Now it just sounds like a door closing. Like they're trying to put me back in a room I clawed my way out of.

And underneath the rage, if I'm honest? There's this tired, exhausted grief. Because I loved that church once. I really did.

**Minister**: That's a profound thing to say out loud — and I'm grateful you did.

The grief underneath the rage. That's the real weight, isn't it? Not just anger at what they asked, but grief that the place you loved, the faith you held, became a place that would ask you to sit across from the man who broke you — as if *your* forgiveness is what restoration looks like.

That's a different wound than the abuse itself. It's a second one.

I'm wondering — and you can answer this or not — when you say you loved that church once, and you loved that faith: do you still have any sense of God in that love? Or did losing the church mean losing your sense of who God is too?

I ask because those aren't always the same thing, and it matters for what we talk about next.

**Seeker**: That's... a harder question than I expected from an app.

The honest answer is I don't know. I think I still believe in *something*. But the God they handed me — the one who needs me to make peace with my abuser before I can be whole — I've had to burn that down. And I'm not sure what's left in the ashes yet.

Sometimes I read the Gospels on my own, away from anyone telling me what they mean, and I see someone who got *furious* at people using religion to harm the vulnerable. Who called out the religious leaders who burdened people instead of protecting them. That Jesus I can still sit with.

But the God who requires my forgiveness as a condition of my healing? I want nothing to do with him. And I'm not sure those two things — that Jesus and that God — have been fully untangled for me yet.

**Minister**: You just said something really important. You're holding two pictures side by side — and you know they don't match.

The Jesus you read in the Gospels on your own — the one who got *furious* at religious leaders who burdened the vulnerable — that's the one who overturned tables in the temple. Who said the greatest commandment wasn't about obedience or submission; it was about love. Who told the woman caught in adultery, "I don't condemn you. Go, and sin no more" — not "go sit with the man who trapped you first." Who said, "Let the little children come to me, and do not hinder them, because the kingdom of heaven belongs to such as these" — and then said whoever harms them would be better off drowned in the sea.

That Jesus protected the vulnerable *fiercely*. He did not ask them to reconcile with their harm before healing could begin.

Here's what I want to ask you — and I mean this as a genuine question, not a rhetorical one: What if the God those church people handed you — the one who needs your forgiveness before you can be whole — what if *that* God is the lie they taught you, not the true one?

What if the Jesus you can still sit with in the Gospels, the one who burned against people weaponizing religion to control the broken — what if *that's* who God actually is?
