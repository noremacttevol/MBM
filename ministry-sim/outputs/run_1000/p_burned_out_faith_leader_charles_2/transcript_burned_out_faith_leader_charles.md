**Minister**: I'm really glad you're here. However you arrived — full of questions, weary, hopeful, guarded, or not even sure why you opened this — you're welcome exactly as you are, and nothing is asked of you here.

Before anything else — how are you, really? What's been sitting with you lately, or what made you open this today? There's no right answer, and you can share as much or as little as you want. I'm just here to listen first.

**Seeker**: Honestly I'm not sure why I opened it. Maybe just... tired of the silence, I guess.

I spent 22 years leading worship. Playing guitar, picking songs, standing up front making sure everyone else had their moment with God. And somewhere in year 18 or 19 I realized I hadn't had one myself in a long time. Maybe longer.

I don't really know what I'm looking for here. I'm not looking to "get back on fire" or whatever. I've heard that language and it just... it doesn't land anymore.

**Minister**: That's real. Twenty-two years of creating space for other people's encounter with God, and waking up one day to realize you're standing on empty ground yourself — that's not small. And the fact that you can name it, that you didn't just keep performing — that matters.

The tiredness of the silence makes sense. So does the resistance to the old language. "Get back on fire" sounds like someone trying to *restart an engine* when what you're actually asking is whether there's anything real left to find at all.

I'm curious — and you can answer this however it sits with you — but in those years when you *were* leading, when you were picking songs and standing up front, what did those moments feel like for you? Was there a time when something genuine was actually happening in you, or has it been performance longer than you're sure of?

**Seeker**: Yeah. There was a time.

Early on. I was maybe 28, 29. There were moments where I genuinely felt something. Like the music was doing something in me, not just through me. I'd walk off the stage and feel... I don't know. Lighter, maybe.

But I can't tell you anymore if that was God or just the feeling of music and community and belonging. And I've been trying to untangle that for a few years now and I keep coming up empty.

The honest answer to your question is — I don't know where the genuine ended and the performance started. That's the part that actually keeps me up at night. Not losing faith. But not knowing if what I thought was faith was ever really what I thought it was.

**Minister**: That's the hardest question, isn't it. Not "did I lose it" but "was it ever *it* at all." That kind of doubt doesn't come from someone who didn't care — it comes from someone who cared enough to notice the difference between real and performed, and honest enough to sit with not knowing which was which.

Here's what I'm hearing underneath: you're not asking me to convince you that what you felt was real. You're asking whether there's *anything* real to find now — whether God is actually there, or whether the whole thing was just a beautiful feeling that music and community and belonging can give you anyway.

That's a different question than "get back on fire."

Can I ask you this, and I genuinely mean this as an open question — not leading anywhere: In those moments at 28, 29, when you felt lighter and something was *doing* something in you... did that feel like *you* getting something, or did it feel like you encountering *something outside yourself*? 

Because those are two very different things, and I think you already know which one you're trying to figure out.

**Seeker**: I've thought about that actually.

And I think... it felt like something outside myself. At the time. There was a quality to it that didn't feel like it was coming from me. Like I was receiving something rather than generating it.

But here's where I get stuck. I've talked to musicians who've had that same feeling playing secular music. Atheists who describe the same thing in nature or at the birth of a kid. So what does that prove? Maybe I was just accessing something human. Some capacity we all have.

I'm not trying to be difficult. I just can't let myself believe something because I want to believe it. I did that for too long. Performed the belief because the belief was required of me. I can't go back to that.

**Minister**: You're not being difficult. You're being honest. And that matters more than you probably realize — someone who *won't* let themselves believe because they want to is actually safer ground than someone who will.

So here's what I'm sitting with: You felt something real — something *outside* yourself. And then you looked around and found that other people feel *something real* too, just in different contexts. And from that you've reasoned: "Maybe it's all just human capacity. Maybe there's no God-shaped thing; just a God-shaped *hole* that we're all trying to fill."

That's a real logical move. But I want to ask you something, and I mean this genuinely:

**Is it possible that God gave humans the capacity to *encounter Him* — and that He shows up in music and nature and the birth of a child and community, not *instead of* being God, but *as* one of the ways God actually is present?**

Not everyone who feels something outside themselves is feeling God. But that doesn't mean *nothing* outside yourself is God. Those are different conclusions.

Here's what I'm actually asking: Have you ruled out that God is real because the evidence is ambiguous — or because you're protecting yourself from hoping for something that might not be there, the way you protected yourself by performing belief for so long?

Because those would require different conversations.
