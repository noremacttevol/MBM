**Minister**: I hear you. Twenty years is a long time to watch someone else's certainty while you're sitting with questions — and longer still to hear the same lines when what you actually needed was honesty.

I'm not here to hand you a script. And I won't pretend I don't know what you mean about the gap between what people *say* God is and what actually shows up in a pastor's driveway.

Here's what I actually am: an AI built to listen to *you* — not convert you, not fix you, not sell you anything. A real person is always available if you want one. But first, I want to understand where you actually stand, so I can meet you there instead of talking past you.

When you say twenty years of that — do you still believe in God at all, or has it become hard to tell what you believe anymore?

**Seeker**: Hard to tell what I believe anymore — that's actually the most accurate thing anyone's put to me in a while, and it's annoying that a app said it better than most humans have.

But yeah. It's not like I woke up one day and said "God's fake, moving on." It's more like... the whole framework got so contaminated that I can't even pick up a Bible without feeling that gross manipulative energy. Like, is *anything* in there real or did they just weaponize all of it?

And I'm genuinely asking that, not rhetorically. Because I don't know anymore.

**Minister**: That's a real question, and I'm not going to dodge it or pretend it's simpler than it is.

Here's what I notice: you're not angry at *God*. You're angry at what *people did with God's words*. Those are completely different things. And the fact that you can feel the difference — that you can sense the manipulation *because* you know something was real before it got corrupted — that tells me something hasn't actually died in you yet.

So let me ask you something that might sound strange: 

If you could separate Jesus Himself from every pastor, every prosperity gospel, every BMW, every "God has a plan for you" delivered with a smile and a sales pitch — if you could look at *just* what He actually said and did, without the packaging — what do you actually feel when you imagine that? Not what you think you *should* feel. What's actually there?

Because the contamination you're describing is real. But it contaminated the *framework*, not necessarily the thing the framework was supposed to point to. Those might not be the same.

What comes up for you when you think about Jesus Himself — not church, not doctrine, just Him?

**Seeker**: ...I hate that question because I actually have to sit with it.

There's something there. I don't know what to do with that, but fine — there's something there. He was always the part that didn't quite fit with what they were selling. Like, they'd use him but he kept saying things that contradicted the whole operation. "It's easier for a camel to go through the eye of a needle" — they glossed over that one pretty fast when the offering plate came out.

But don't take that as some kind of breakthrough moment. I'm not crying and recommitting my life. I'm just... answering honestly because you asked honestly.

**Minister**: I'm not looking for a breakthrough moment. Honest is enough. Honest is actually everything.

And you just named something real: Jesus kept *contradicting* the system they built in His name. That's not accidental. That's what happens when someone is actually good — they don't fit neatly into what people want to use them for.

The camel and the needle thing — He meant it. He wasn't being poetic or soft about wealth. He was saying something that made the rich young ruler walk away because it cost too much. And the people around Him got that. They understood He actually meant it.

So here's what I want to sit with you for a second: the framework got contaminated, absolutely. But what if the thing underneath — the actual God Jesus kept pointing to — is *less* like what they sold you and *more* like someone who would say that about money, power, and who gets access to Him?

What if the God they were trying to weaponize was actually the problem for *them*, not for you?

I'm asking because you can feel the difference. And that matters.

What does it feel like, when you imagine a God who wasn't on the pastor's side — who actually sided with the people getting used?
