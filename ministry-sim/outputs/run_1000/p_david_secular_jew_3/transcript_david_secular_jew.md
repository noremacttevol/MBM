I'm genuinely asking: when you say Judaism is your people — when you say *that* matters — where does that mattering come from, in a universe where God is either invented or impersonal? What makes your grandmother's survival *meaningful* and not just... what happened?

Because your mother lights candles every Friday night partly *because* your grandmother survived. That's a link. And I'm wondering if maybe the thing you're actually skeptical about isn't meaning itself — it's whether the God of Abraham is real and good enough to be worth the vulnerability.

Does that land, or am I reading you wrong?
