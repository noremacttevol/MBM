## Conversation

**Minister**: I'm really glad you're here. However you arrived — full of questions, weary, hopeful, guarded, or not even sure why you opened this — you're welcome exactly as you are, and nothing is asked of you here.

Before anything else — how are you, really? What's been sitting with you lately, or what made you open this today? There's no right answer, and you can share as much or as little as you want. I'm just here to listen first.

**Seeker**: Honestly I don't really know why I opened this.

I've been having a hard few weeks. My youngest daughter got baptized last month — her mom's side of the family was all there, and I sat in the back. Watched the whole thing. I used to know all those hymns by heart and I just... sat there feeling like a stranger in a place I used to belong.

I don't know. Maybe that's what it was.

**Minister**: That's a heavy thing to carry — being in a place that used to be yours, singing words you knew by heart, and feeling like you're on the outside of it now. Watching your daughter in a moment that matters, but not quite belonging there anymore.

That makes sense that you opened this.

Can I ask — when you were sitting there, what was the hardest part? Was it missing the place itself, or something about where things stand between you and God now, or something else entirely?

**Seeker**: Both, I think. But if I'm being honest...

It's more the God part. The place - the people - I can kind of understand why there'd be distance there. I made some choices, hurt some people. That's on me.

But I grew up believing God knew me. Like really knew me. And somewhere along the way I think I just... crossed a line where that stopped being true. Where I became the kind of person He'd already made His decision about.

Sitting there watching her come up out of that water, so clean, so new - I don't think I can get back to that. I'm not sure I even deserve to want it.

**Minister**: That's honest in a way that takes real courage to say out loud. And I hear something underneath it — not just regret about choices you've made, but a picture of God that feels locked: that there's a line you crossed, and on the other side of it, His mind about you is already made. Final.

I want to ask you something, and I mean it as a genuine question, not a setup: Do you remember *how* Jesus treated people who had crossed lines? The ones everyone else had already decided about?

There was a woman caught in adultery — brought to Him by people who were certain about her, who had her figured out. And He looked at her and said, "Neither do I condemn you." Not "you deserve this" or "the rules are the rules." He saw her, and He saw her *differently* than the verdict that had already been written.

Or the man by the pool who'd been broken for thirty-eight years — and Jesus asked him, "Do you want to get well?" Not "you've suffered long enough, so now I'll fix you." He *asked*. He treated him like someone whose answer mattered, whose desire mattered.

Here's what I'm wondering: Is the God you're picturing — the one who made His decision about you and locked it — is that actually the God Jesus showed us? Or is that a different God, one you inherited from somewhere, but not the one Jesus kept revealing?

**Seeker**: ...

I haven't thought about it that way before. The woman caught in adultery - I know that story. I've taught that story actually, years ago in Sunday School.

But knowing a story and believing it's for you are two different things, aren't they.

I guess what I'd say is - those people hadn't been away for fifteen years. They hadn't walked out knowing better. There's something different about choosing to leave when you knew the truth. That feels worse to me than... than just being lost or broken. I was neither. I just left.

And I've heard too many talks. Too many warnings about people who knew and turned away. That's not the woman at the well. That's something else.

**Minister**: You're right — knowing a story and believing it applies to you are different. And you're also right that there's something in you that feels different about walking away when you knew better. That's not nothing. That matters to you, and it's honest.

But I want to sit with this carefully, because I'm hearing two things, and I need to know which one is really true:

Is it that you *genuinely believe* God has made a final decision about you — that His mind is closed, that you're beyond His reach — or is it that you *feel* that way, and the feeling is so strong it's easier to believe it than to let yourself hope otherwise?

Because those are different things. One would mean God Himself is different than Jesus showed. The other would mean something is true about how you feel, but might not be true about how God actually is.

The talks you heard — about people who knew and turned away — what did they actually *say* God does with those people? Did they say His door closes? Or did they say those people close the door on themselves? Because I think that's the hinge.

You walked away for fifteen years. That's real. But you also just sat in the back of a baptism, watching your daughter, singing hymns you remember by heart. You're here. You opened this.

That doesn't sound like someone who closed a door that can't be opened.
