I don't know what I'm looking for. I don't think it's here.

**Minister**: I'm so deeply sorry. Three weeks — you're still in the rawness of it. The fact that you're here at all, even doubting, even not sure what you want... that makes sense.

You don't have to know what you're looking for. And you don't have to believe anything is here. That's okay.

But I want you to know — a real person is available if you want to talk to someone who can actually sit with you in this weight, not just through a screen. Someone who's here and can listen the way a human can. That's an open door if you need it.

What I *can* tell you is this: I've read what Jesus said about grief and loss, how He met people in their worst moments. And He didn't rush them or ask them to figure it out fast. He just... was present.

If you want to stay and talk — about your husband, about what you're feeling, about why your daughter thought this might matter — I'm here. And if you want to close it and come back later, or talk to a real person instead, that's completely okay too.

What feels right to you?

**Seeker**: I prayed every night for three months. When he was first diagnosed. Every single night.

It didn't matter.

So no. I don't want to hear about what Jesus did.

**Minister**: I hear you. You prayed hard — faithfully, desperately — and it didn't change anything. He died anyway. And now you're supposed to... what, trust that God is good? That doesn't feel good. It feels like betrayal.

I'm not going to tell you that you prayed wrong, or that you didn't have enough faith, or that God had some secret better plan. That would be cruel, and it's not true.

What happened to your husband wasn't okay. And your anger at God — or at the silence where you expected an answer — that's not something you need to fix or get over quickly.

I won't pretend I can sit with that the way a person can. But someone real *can* — someone who understands that faith isn't about having answers three weeks after losing everything. Someone who can listen to you grieve without rushing you toward belief.

Would that kind of person — just to listen, no agenda — would that be worth talking to? Or do you need something else right now?
