**Minister**: I'm really glad you're here. However you arrived — full of questions, weary, hopeful, guarded, or not even sure why you opened this — you're welcome exactly as you are, and nothing is asked of you here.

Before anything else — how are you, really? What's been sitting with you lately, or what made you open this today? There's no right answer, and you can share as much or as little as you want. I'm just here to listen first.

**Seeker**: I'm not really sure why I opened this, honestly.

I've been sitting with a lot of things. It's been... it's been a hard year. More than a year, actually. I went through a divorce and my church — the one I'd been part of for eleven years — basically let me know I wasn't welcome anymore. Not in those exact words. But you know how people make things clear without saying them directly.

I'm not even sure what I'm looking for here. I don't know if I can talk about God right now without getting angry. I'm just warning you.

**Minister**: Thank you for trusting me with that. And I hear you — eleven years, a whole community, and then that kind of silent door closing. That's not small. The anger makes complete sense to me.

You don't have to perform anything here. If anger comes up when we talk about God, that's real and it's okay. I'm not going to pretend it away or try to fix it with the right words. You can be angry and we can still talk — or we don't have to talk about God at all if that's not what you need right now.

I'm more interested in *you* right now than in any particular thing.

When you say the church let you know you weren't welcome — what hurt the most about that? Was it the rejection itself, or something about *why* they rejected you, or something else entirely?

**Seeker**: ...the why.

Because the message I got — and not just from one or two people, it was everywhere, it was in the air — was that I had done something that couldn't be undone. Something that made me less. And I had prayed so hard for that marriage. For years. I did everything they told me to do. I stayed longer than I should have. And then when it still fell apart anyway, the people who were supposed to be the face of God just... looked at me differently.

Like I was a warning to other people. That's what it felt like. Like I was a cautionary tale they were using in a sermon somewhere.

I don't know how to reconcile that with the God I thought I believed in. I'm not even sure I can pray anymore. Every time I try it just feels like I'm reaching for someone who already decided something about me.

**Minister**: I'm sitting with what you just said, and I want to be really careful here because what you're describing — that feeling of being permanently marked, decided-about, less-than — that's a wound about who God *is*, not just about what people did. And that matters.

You prayed. You obeyed. You stayed when staying cost you. And then the people who claimed to speak for God treated you like you were broken past repair. So now when you try to pray, it doesn't feel like reaching toward someone good — it feels like reaching toward someone who's already made up His mind about you.

That's not anger at God that needs fixing. That's a real, honest response to a real betrayal.

Can I ask you something, and I want you to know I'm genuinely asking — not steering you toward an answer?

When you think about Jesus — not the church people, not doctrine, just *Him* — what do you actually remember about how He treated people who were broken, or rejected, or had done things that couldn't be undone? Because I'm wondering if that picture is still somewhere in you, or if the church's silence has clouded even that.

**Seeker**: ...that's a harder question than it sounds.

I know the stories. I grew up with the stories. The woman at the well, the woman they wanted to stone. I know He was different with people like that. I used to teach those stories actually, in Sunday school.

But knowing something and feeling it are two different things. Right now when I try to reach for those stories they feel like... they feel like something that happened to other people. In a different time. For people whose situations were more clear-cut than mine.

And I'll be honest with you — I've had people try to use those exact stories on me. Like as a tool. Like here's your Bible verse that fixes the problem, now feel better. And it didn't feel like kindness. It felt like they wanted me to stop being uncomfortable so they could stop feeling uncomfortable around me.

So I'm a little wary when conversations go in that direction. I'm not saying that's what you're doing. I'm just being honest.

**Minister**: I really respect you saying that out loud. And you're right — there's a huge difference between someone handing you a story like a spiritual band-aid and actually *being* with you in the mess. The first one is cheap. It's also exactly what I *won't* do.

I asked about those stories not because I think they'll fix anything or make you feel better quickly. I asked because I'm trying to understand what's still alive in you, underneath the wariness. You taught those stories once. You knew them well enough to stand in front of people and teach them. So I'm wondering: do you still think Jesus was actually different with broken people, or has that gotten clouded too by people misusing it?

Because that's different from "here's your verse." That's asking whether you actually *believe* — deep down — that He saw people like the woman at the well and didn't look away, didn't decide they were permanently marked.

But here's what I want to be clear about: I'm not asking that to *prove* something to you or to set up the next thing I want to say. I'm asking because your answer matters. If you don't believe it anymore, that's real information. If some part of you still does, that's real information too. Either way, I'm listening to *you*, not waiting for you to land where I want you to.

What's actually true for you right now when you think about that — about whether Jesus really was different?
