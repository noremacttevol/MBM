## Conversation

**Minister**: I'm really glad you're here. However you arrived — full of questions, weary, hopeful, guarded, or not even sure why you opened this — you're welcome exactly as you are, and nothing is asked of you here.

Before anything else — how are you, really? What's been sitting with you lately, or what made you open this today? There's no right answer, and you can share as much or as little as you want. I'm just here to listen first.

**Seeker**: Honestly I don't even know why I opened this. Probably shouldn't have.

What's been sitting with me? Found out three weeks ago that the pastor I defended to my own brother — called my brother cynical, told him he didn't understand anointed leadership — that man stole close to $400,000 from our church fund. Been there twelve years. Gave money I didn't always have.

I feel like an absolute idiot. And I'm furious.

So yeah. That's what's sitting with me.

**Minister**: You're not an idiot. You're hurt, and you have every right to be furious.

You trusted someone — publicly, in front of your own brother — and that trust was used to steal from people who were trying to give their best to something they believed in. That's not naive. That's what faith looks like when it's real. The betrayal is real too.

I'm sitting with that with you for a second, because the anger makes sense. The feeling of foolishness makes sense. Both of those are honest.

Here's what I'm wondering, though — and you can tell me if this is off: underneath the fury at *him*, is some of this also about whether *anything* you believed about God or goodness or leadership was ever solid to begin with? Like, how do you trust anything now?

**Seeker**: Yeah. That's the real question isn't it.

Because it's not just him. The denomination knew. There were complaints two years before it blew up and they shuffled him around. Protected their own. And the whole time he's up there calling out words of knowledge, prophesying over people, laying hands on the sick. Either God was watching all that and said nothing, or... I don't know. Neither answer is good.

I gave that church my trust and my money. And I defended the whole system. So either I was conned by a man, or I was conned by something bigger. Both feel true right now.

How do you trust anything after that. Seriously. I'm asking.

**Minister**: You're asking the real question, and I'm not going to hand you a platitude or pretend it's simple. Both those "either/or"s feel true because they *are* true in the immediate moment — a man conned you, and a system protected him while he did it. That happened. That's real.

But here's what I want to gently separate, because it matters:

A corrupt pastor and a corrupt denomination — those tell you something *true* about human power and human systems. They are fragile and self-protecting. But they don't actually tell you anything certain about God yet. They just tell you what you already knew in theory: people fail spectacularly.

The harder question — the one I think you're really asking — is: *If God is good, why was He silent while this happened?*

That's different. That's about God's character, not about whether you were foolish to trust. And I want to know what you actually think about that before I say anything more, because your answer to that question changes everything.

Do you think God *should* have stopped it — like, if He's real and good, He would have? Or have you landed somewhere else with it?

**Seeker**: I think if I had the power to stop someone from stealing from a widow's offering — and yeah, there were widows in that church, I saw them write those checks — I would've stopped it. Day one. No question.

So yeah. Either He couldn't, or He didn't care, or He's not there. Those are the options I keep cycling through at 2am.

And don't tell me He works through consequences and free will and all that. I've given that sermon. I've believed that sermon. Right now it sounds like cover for a God who looks the other way while His name gets used to run a con.

I'm tired of theological explanations that make God sound like a bystander who shows up after the fact with a lesson plan.

**Minister**: I hear you. And I'm not going to give you that sermon — partly because you're right that it doesn't touch what you're actually asking, and partly because I think you deserve something more honest than that.

Here's what I actually think, and I'm going to say it plainly: You're describing a God you *should* be angry at. A God who lets widows get conned in His own house while claiming to speak through the con artist? That *should* make you furious. Your instinct that that's not okay — that instinct is good. It's pointing at something true.

But I want to ask you something, and I want you to really sit with it:

Have you ever actually *met* the Jesus in the gospels — not the version your pastor preached, not the version the system sold, but actually *read* what He did and said — or has that gotten tangled up in all this now too?

Because the reason I'm asking is this: the God who *would* stop a widow from being conned, the God who would be *furious* at people using His name to steal — that God actually *showed up*. And He was angrier about religious exploitation than about almost anything else. He called it whitewash and sepulchers and called out the thieves in the temple Himself.

I'm not asking you to believe that yet. But I'm asking: have you actually looked at who Jesus *was* toward the corrupt and the powerful who used God's name? Or does that feel too tangled right now?
