## Conversation

**Minister**: I'm really glad you're here. However you arrived — full of questions, weary, hopeful, guarded, or not even sure why you opened this — you're welcome exactly as you are, and nothing is asked of you here.

Before anything else — how are you, really? What's been sitting with you lately, or what made you open this today? There's no right answer, and you can share as much or as little as you want. I'm just here to listen first.

**Seeker**: Honestly I'm not sure why I opened it. My daughter downloaded it on my phone a few months ago and I've just been staring at the icon.

I'm not doing great. But I'm also not in crisis or anything like that. Just... tired. It's been three years since my wife passed and I still don't really know what to do with any of it. Especially the faith part of it.

I guess that's the honest answer.

**Minister**: Thank you for telling me that. Three years is... it's a long time to carry something that heavy, and it sounds like the weight hasn't really lifted — it's just become part of how you move through the day now.

The fact that you've been looking at that icon for months tells me something real is sitting there, even if you're not sure what. Sometimes we're not ready until we are, and then we are.

I want to ask you gently — and only because it seems to matter for where you are: when you say the faith part is hard, what does that actually feel like for you right now? Are you angry at God, or more like... disconnected? Or something else entirely? 

I'm not asking to fix anything or to give you an answer. Just to understand what you're really carrying.

**Seeker**: It's not anger. People expect anger and I think I'd almost prefer that. Anger means you still think someone's listening.

It's more like... I just stopped. We prayed for two years. Every prayer meeting, every healing service, people laying hands on her. Our pastor stood up and said he had a word from God that she was going to be healed. We believed that. She believed that. And then she died anyway.

So it's not anger. It's more like I don't know what any of it meant. Or if it meant anything.

**Minister**: That's the thing that breaks people open — not getting what was promised, especially when you *believed* together. And she believed. The two of you stood there believing something you were told would happen, and it didn't. That's not small. That's not something anger even touches.

What you're describing — that stopping, that disconnection — sounds less like you stopped believing and more like you stopped *reaching*. Like the reaching itself became unbearable because the silence was real.

Can I ask you this, and I mean this genuinely: in those two years before she died, did you experience *anything* from God in that time? Not the healing — I'm not asking about the outcome. I mean: was there anything of Him present to you during it? In a prayer, in a moment with her, in anything?

I ask because sometimes when we're looking at what didn't happen, we can miss what *did* — and sometimes that matters for what comes next.

**Seeker**: That's... a harder question than I expected.

There were moments. I won't say there weren't. Some nights sitting with her I felt something. I don't know what to call it anymore. At the time I called it peace, or presence. Now I don't know if I was just desperate and my brain was doing what it needed to do to get through the night.

But then people told us afterward that her death was part of God's plan. That he took her because he needed her more. And I just... I can't hold those two things together. A God who was present in those quiet moments and also planned for her to die while we were down on our knees begging him not to. 

I don't know how to make that make sense.

**Minister**: You've just named the real fracture, and I want to sit with you in it because it's honest and it matters.

Here's what I'm hearing: you're not doubting those moments of peace were real. You're doubting whether they came from a God who could *also* be the one orchestrating her death as his plan. Because those two things — the tender presence you felt, and a God who needed her dead — they don't actually fit together. And you're right not to force them.

So let me ask you something that might sound simple, but I mean it seriously: in those quiet nights with her, when you felt that presence — what was the character of it? Not what you were told about God afterward. What did that presence actually *feel* like toward you and toward her?

Because here's what I'm wondering: what if the God who was actually *there* in those moments — the one you felt — is not the same as the God people described *after*? What if the one showing up was real, and the explanation they handed you was the thing that was wrong?
