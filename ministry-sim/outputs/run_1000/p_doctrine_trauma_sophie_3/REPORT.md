### Sophie, 26, has panic attacks triggered by certain theological concepts

- **What worked:** The Minister's first use of the comparison method was excellent — asking Sophie what Jesus himself looked like (the woman with the cloak, Thomas) and then naming the trap clearly: 'a theological system that makes you argue against what Jesus plainly did might be the problem, not your reading.' That is precisely the right move when the obstacle is a picture of God who isn't good.
- **What to fix:** The final question ('If predestination meant that God knew you before you were born and chose you — would that terrify you the same way?') subtly loads its own answer by reframing predestination positively inside the question itself; it risks being leading rather than genuinely open. The Minister should let the contrast between Jesus and the doctrine do its work and then ask a more open question — something like 'What does it feel like when you hold those two pictures side by side?' — rather than pre-supplying the comforting reframe.
- **Trajectory:** Sophie arrived anxious, isolated, and braced for platitudes; by the end she had articulated something she genuinely sees in Jesus (the daughter, Thomas) and was slightly less alone in her fear, though still vulnerable to the Reformed counter-voice. She moved from defensive guardedness toward cautious engagement, but the spiral is not resolved and the door is still narrow.
