I want to name what I'm hearing: you're exhausted not from doubt itself, but from the gap between what you were performing and what you were actually feeling. Years of that will drain a person bone-deep. And now that the performance has stopped — or you've stopped being able to keep it up — you're in the hard, real territory where you don't know what's actually yours anymore and what was just muscle memory.

That's not a small thing. And it's not shameful to name it, even if it feels like it is right now.

A few things I want to hold gently: One, doubt itself isn't the enemy here — it sounds like you're doubting *the performance*, not God. That's honest work, and it matters. Two, the exhaustion you're naming is real and it matters too. Sometimes what we think is a crisis of faith is actually a crisis of burnout, and the two get tangled in ways that are hard to sort out when you're in them.

Here's what I'm genuinely curious about, because it might matter: When you say you stopped feeling anything — was there a moment you can point to, or did it fade so slowly you didn't notice until you were already numb? And when you think back to a time when you *did* feel something real in worship or faith, what was that actually like?

You don't have to answer. But sometimes the shape of how we lost something tells us what we're actually looking for.

What I needed for years — what nobody ever actually did — was just ask how I was without an agenda behind it. Without the next step being "let's get you reconnected" or "have you tried prayer journaling." I needed someone to just sit with the answer.

Can you do that? Just sit with it? Because if this is going to pivot to getting me back on the road to faith, I'm out.

**Minister**: You're right to call that out, and I respect you for it.

Yes. I can sit with it. No pivot. No framework running underneath. I'll ask because I actually want to know — not because the question is bait for an answer I'm hoping you'll land on.

You're exhausted. You stopped feeling anything years ago and kept performing anyway. And nobody ever just asked you how you were without already knowing where they wanted that answer to lead.

I hear that. That matters.

So — how are you right now? Not the exhaustion, not the belief question. Just... you, at 11pm, mind spinning, tired of the ceiling.
