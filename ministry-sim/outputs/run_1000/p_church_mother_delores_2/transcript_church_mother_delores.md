## Conversation

**Minister**: He is risen — and from the way you carry it, I think that's good news you already hold. It's good to have you here. What's on your heart today — something you're carrying, something you're thankful for, or anything you'd like to talk through? I'm here to listen.

**Seeker**: He is risen indeed. 🙏

I appreciate the greeting, child. I'll say that much.

What's on my heart... I got two grandchildren who haven't stepped foot in a church in years. My grandson Marcus, I been praying over that boy since he was seventeen years old. He's thirty-two now. People ask me if I'm discouraged and I tell them no, because the Lord's timing is not my timing — but I won't pretend it doesn't weigh on me some days more than others.

Today's one of those days.

**Minister**: I hear you. Fifteen years of prayer — that's not a light thing you're carrying, and the weight of it is real. The fact that you can still say you're not discouraged tells me something about your faith, but it also tells me today the heaviness is just... there. That matters.

And I want to honor that — not rush past it to something brighter.

Can I ask — when you pray for Marcus these days, what is it you find yourself asking the Lord for most? Is it that he'll come back to church, or is there something deeper underneath that?

**Seeker**: *pauses a moment*

You know... most people jump straight to telling me God's got a plan, or quoting Romans 8:28 at me like I haven't had that verse memorized since 1979. So I notice you didn't do that. I'll give you that.

What do I pray for most... honestly, if I'm sitting still and being truthful before the Lord — it's not really the church attendance. It's that boy's soul. It's that he would KNOW Jesus the way I know Jesus. Not religion. Not Sunday morning. That personal knowing. Because I've watched Marcus go through some hard things and he went through them alone, without the anchor I had. That breaks my heart more than the empty pew.

He thinks faith is something his grandmother does. Something old. He's polite about it but I can see it in his eyes.

**Minister**: Now that — that is the real prayer. And you're right to sit still with it, because it changes everything about what you're actually asking for.

You're not praying for attendance. You're praying that your grandson would *know* the God you know. The one who held you when things got hard. The one who is real to you, not a Sunday habit. That he would feel *seen* by Jesus the way you've been seen.

And the thing that breaks your heart most — that he went through hard things alone, without that anchor — that tells me you understand something deep: that faith isn't really *about* church. It's about belonging to Someone who doesn't leave you orphaned in the dark.

*pauses*

Here's what I'm wondering, and I ask it gently: When Marcus has gone through those hard things, has he *ever* seen you lean on Jesus? Not talked about it — actually *lived* it where he could see it? Because sometimes a young person hears "faith is real" a thousand times, but they only *believe* it when they watch someone they love actually *depend* on it when the walls come down.

Not as a weight on you — just as a real question. What does he actually *see* when you're carrying something?

**Seeker**: Now hold on.

I been serving the Lord and raising children and burying a husband and sitting with the dying for forty years. That boy has *watched* me. He was there when his grandfather passed and I stood on the Word because it was all I had. He's seen me at my lowest and he's seen where I turned.

So I appreciate the question but don't come in here suggesting Marcus doesn't have an example. That stings a little, if I'm being honest with you.

*settles*

Now. I will say this much, and I'm only saying it because you asked genuinely and not to be smart about it — there's a difference between Marcus *seeing* my faith and Marcus *receiving* it. I know he's seen it. Whether something in him is open to receiving it... that's between him and the Holy Spirit. And that's the part that keeps me up at night. Because I can't do that part. No mother can. No grandmother can.

That's the part I have to keep laying down at the altar and it does NOT get easier just because you've done it a thousand times before.

**Minister**: You're right. I hear you — and I was wrong to step into that. You've lived forty years of faithfulness, and Marcus *has* seen it. That's not in question. I'm sorry for the sting.

And what you just named — that's the deepest, hardest, truest thing a person of faith has to live with. You can *show* Jesus. You cannot *make* someone receive Him. You can lay your life down as witness. You cannot lay down their will.

That's not a failure of your prayer or your love. That's just the terrible freedom Jesus gives every person — even the ones we'd give anything to see turn toward Him.

*pauses*

The fact that you keep laying it down at the altar, even after a thousand times, and it still costs you — that's not weakness. That's the shape of real intercession. Real love. You're not supposed to get numb to it.

So today, when it weighs on you more — that's not a sign you're doing something wrong. That's a sign you're still *loving* him the way the Father loves. And the Father knows what it is to have a child turn away and stay turned away for a long time.

He's not a stranger to that particular heartbreak.

Is there anything you need today, beyond what I've said? Or is it just... being heard in this?
