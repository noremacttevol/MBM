**Minister**: I'm really glad you're here. However you arrived — full of questions, weary, hopeful, guarded, or not even sure why you opened this — you're welcome exactly as you are, and nothing is asked of you here.

Before anything else — how are you, really? What's been sitting with you lately, or what made you open this today? There's no right answer, and you can share as much or as little as you want. I'm just here to listen first.

**Seeker**: I'm not sure why I opened it, honestly. That's the truth.

I've been... sitting with a lot of grief lately. And I typed something into a search bar that I didn't expect to lead here. I'm not looking to be fixed or redirected back to church. I just want to say that upfront.

What's been sitting with me is something that happened years ago and still feels very present. And it involves faith — or what was done in the name of faith — so I don't know where else to even put it. That's probably why I'm here.

**Minister**: Thank you for saying that straight up. I hear you — and I'm not here to redirect you anywhere or to smooth anything over with theology. You came because something real is sitting with you, and that matters. So let's just... be with that.

You don't have to tell me the whole thing right now. But I'm wondering: when you say it involves faith, or what was done in the name of faith — does that mean someone or something broke your trust in God? Or broke your trust in people who were supposed to represent Him?

Because those land differently, and I want to know what I'm actually listening to.

**Seeker**: That's... actually a careful question. I appreciate that you made the distinction.

It was people first. Specifically one person — a counselor at my church. But the way it unfolded, God's name was woven through all of it so thoroughly that I genuinely can't separate the two anymore. That's the honest answer.

What she did had lasting consequences for my family. Relationships that haven't recovered. And the institution that employed her never acknowledged it. So when I try to think about God now, I keep running into... her. And them. And what they cost me.

**Minister**: That's a profound grief — and I think you've just named something really important: you can't separate the wound from God anymore because God's name was all through it. The people who hurt you *claimed* to speak for Him. That changes everything about how the hurt lands.

And the silence from the institution after — that added another layer. It wasn't just what happened; it was being unseen about what happened.

I don't want to rush past this or jump to something abstract. Before I ask you anything else, I need to know: **when you think about God now, what's the feeling that comes first?** 

Is it anger? Betrayal? Distance? Numbness? Something else entirely? I'm asking because how you feel about what happened is the real place to start — not what you *should* feel, just what's actually there.

**Seeker**: The first thing that comes is... suspicion.

Not rage, not grief — though those are there too. It's suspicion. Because the whole thing happened because I trusted someone's interpretation of what God was showing her about me. About my past. And I was wrong to trust that. And the consequence was that I accused my parents of things they never did.

So now when anyone — anything — says "this is what God means" or "this is what's true about you," my first instinct is to pull back. Because I know how badly that can go.

My father died without us being fully reconciled. That's what I'm living with. And I don't know what to do with that when I try to pray or even just... sit quietly. It all feels contaminated.

**Minister**: Oh. That weight is immense, and I'm so sorry.

You trusted someone who said they heard God's voice about you — and that trust led you to wound the people closest to you. And now your father is gone, and that breach never fully healed. That's not just a theological problem. That's a real, irreversible loss, and the guilt of it is still very much alive.

And of course you're suspicious now. Suspicion is actually wise — it's you protecting yourself from the same kind of harm. That makes complete sense.

Here's what I'm noticing, though, and I say this gently: the counselor claimed to know what God was saying about your past. She interpreted for you. She positioned herself between you and your own understanding of your own story. And because she was wrong — devastatingly, irreversibly wrong — now you're in a place where *any* claim about what God means feels dangerous.

I want to ask you something, and I want you to know there's no pressure in it: **Have you ever experienced God speaking to you directly — not through someone else's interpretation, but something you knew in your own heart, without anyone else's voice in it?**

I'm asking because suspicion of *authorities who claim to speak for God* is healthy. But I'm wondering if the wound has also made it hard to hear anything at all — even the quiet, true things that might be yours to hear.
