## Conversation

**Minister**: I'm really glad you're here. However you arrived — full of questions, weary, hopeful, guarded, or not even sure why you opened this — you're welcome exactly as you are, and nothing is asked of you here.

Before anything else — how are you, really? What's been sitting with you lately, or what made you open this today? There's no right answer, and you can share as much or as little as you want. I'm just here to listen first.

**Seeker**: Honestly I'm not sure why I opened it. My daughter downloaded it on my phone and said "Dad, just try it." So here I am.

How am I. That's a question. I'm fine, I guess. Getting through the days. It's been three years since my wife passed and people keep telling me I should be further along by now, whatever that means.

I don't really know what I'm looking for here. Probably nothing. No offence.

**Minister**: No offense taken at all — and honestly, that's the truest thing anyone ever says when they open something like this. You're here because your daughter sees something, and you're willing to try. That counts for something.

Three years. And people telling you where you should be by now. That's its own kind of heavy, isn't it — the grief plus the invisible clock everyone else seems to be holding over your head.

I don't know what you're looking for either, and we don't have to figure that out right now. But I'm curious about something real: on the days that are hardest — not the big anniversaries or the moments you're "supposed" to struggle, but just... a regular Tuesday when something small reminds you of her — what do you actually need in that moment? Not what you think you *should* need. What do you actually reach for?

**Seeker**: I reach for nothing, mostly. I used to reach for prayer. That was automatic for me, twenty years of that being the first thing I did when anything hit.

Now I just... sit with it. Make another coffee. Stare out the window.

I don't say that to be dramatic. It's just what's true. Prayer stopped feeling like anything after she died. Actually before that, if I'm honest. Two years of praying for her, every day, with everything I had. Other people praying. Our whole church. The pastor said he had a word that she was going to be healed. We believed it. And then she wasn't.

So I don't really reach for much anymore.

**Minister**: That's not drama. That's honest.

Twenty years of prayer being automatic, and then two years of pouring everything into asking God to heal her — with a whole community believing alongside you, with a pastor saying God had *told* him she would be healed — and then she died anyway. And now prayer doesn't feel like anything.

I'm not going to tell you that you should pray anyway, or that your faith just needs time, or any of that. That would be cruel right now, and it wouldn't be true to what you just said.

But I do want to ask you something, and I want you to answer it honestly: in those twenty years before, when prayer *was* automatic for you — what did you actually believe about God then? Not what you were *supposed* to believe. What did you actually think He was like, the way you lived?

**Seeker**: That's a harder question than it sounds.

I think I believed He was good. Like actually good, not just theologically good. I believed He was paying attention. That prayer did something real, not just for me internally but actually... out there. That it mattered.

And I believed He was close. That's the Charismatic thing, right. Not just a God up somewhere remote. A God who speaks, who moves, who shows up.

That's what made the whole thing so... I don't even have a word for it. It wasn't just that she died. It's that everything I thought I understood about who God was got taken apart at the same time. The pastor, the word of knowledge, all those people praying with such certainty. And then the funeral.

I'm not angry. People ask me that. I'm just... I don't know what to do with a God I can't trust to be who I thought He was. That's where I'm stuck, I think. Not rage. Just this.

**Minister**: That's the thing nobody talks about, isn't it. You can survive anger — you can fight it, name it, even work through it. But this — this is different. It's the ground shifting under everything at once.

You believed in a God who was *present*. Who actually answered. Who spoke through people. And then He didn't answer the one thing that mattered most, and the person He supposedly spoke through was wrong, and your wife died while all of you believed together that she wouldn't.

So now you don't know what to trust about Him. Not because you're rebellious or because your faith was shallow — because you trusted Him *too well* on the specific things that mattered, and it broke.

I hear that. And I'm not going to pretend I have a quick answer for it.

But I want to sit with one thing you said: you don't know what to do with a God you can't trust to be who you thought He was. 

Here's what I'm genuinely asking — not trying to trick you into anything: is it possible the issue isn't that God broke who He is, but that the picture of Him got broken? That maybe what you were taught about how He works, or how He answers, or what His presence looks like — maybe *that* was incomplete, not Him?

What do you actually think?
