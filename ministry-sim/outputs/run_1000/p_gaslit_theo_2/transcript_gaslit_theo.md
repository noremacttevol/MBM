**Minister**: No offense taken — and honestly, that instinct to close it? That makes complete sense. If church hurt you, then seeing "faith app" is like seeing a warning sign, not an invitation. I get why you'd want out.

But I'm really glad you didn't close it yet.

Here's what I want you to know: you don't have to believe anything, perform anything, or suddenly feel better about God to be here. This isn't about convincing you of anything or fixing what broke. It's just — you're carrying something hard right now (the anxiety, the rough weeks), and sometimes it helps to name it out loud to someone who isn't your therapist and isn't going to bill you.

Your therapist trusted enough to mention it, which means they saw something in you — maybe just that you're asking real questions, even the angry or skeptical ones.

What was it about church that didn't go well? You don't have to say if you don't want to, but if you do — I'm listening, and there's no judgment here.

**Seeker**: They told me my panic attacks were demonic.

Like... I was 24, I was having these episodes where I couldn't breathe, I thought I was dying, and instead of anyone saying "hey maybe see a doctor" they were doing deliverance prayer over me. For months. And I kept thinking I just wasn't faithful enough, that's why it wasn't working. That there was something fundamentally wrong with *me* spiritually.

I'm on medication now and it actually helps. Which I know sounds like a small thing but for a long time I felt guilty even taking it. Like I was admitting defeat or something.

Sorry this is probably a lot. I don't usually talk about this.

**Minister**: That's not a lot — that's exactly what matters. And I'm really glad you're saying it.

What they did to you was wrong. Full stop. Not "well-intentioned but misguided" — *wrong*. Panic attacks are a medical reality, not a spiritual failing. A doctor recognizing that and medication actually *helping* you — that's not defeat. That's your body and mind getting what they needed to function. There's nothing weak or faithless about that.

And the guilt they planted in you — "you're not faithful enough," "something's fundamentally wrong with you spiritually" — that's a wound, not a truth. You were scared and suffering, and instead of pointing you toward actual help, they made you feel broken *for* being scared.

I want to say something carefully: the God that those people described to you — the one who punishes you with demons when you get sick, who makes you feel ashamed of needing medicine, who demands you stay trapped in panic to prove your faith — that is not who Jesus actually revealed. That's a distortion. A painful, damaging one.

But I also know that right now, hearing "God is actually good" probably lands like noise, or even like a threat. You've heard God-words weaponized. So I'm not here to convince you of anything.

What I'm hearing is: you needed help, got spiritually blamed instead, and now trusting anything "faith-shaped" feels dangerous. Does that land?

**Seeker**: Yeah. Yeah that lands.

I just... I get nervous when people say things like "that's not the real God" because I've heard that too. Like, the people who hurt me, they were so *sure* they had the real God. They weren't trying to be cruel, I think. They genuinely believed it. So how does anyone know which version is right?

And I'm not saying that to be difficult. I'm actually asking. Because I used to be that certain too and then everything fell apart and I don't trust my own spiritual instincts anymore at all.

**Minister**: That's not difficult — that's the realest question you could ask. And it matters that you're asking it.

You're right: the people who hurt you *were* certain. They had Scripture, conviction, community all backing up their picture of God. And it collapsed for you when you actually *needed* that God to be good, and instead got shame. So now you're asking — reasonably — how do I know who's right? How do *you* know? And why should I trust anything you say about God when I've already been burned by people who sounded just as sure?

Here's my honest answer: I can't *prove* it to you. And I won't pretend I can.

But there's one thing those people had access to that you also have access to — the same thing I'm pointing to. It's not my interpretation of Scripture, not a system, not what I'm *saying* God is like. It's what Jesus actually *did* and *said* when He was here.

When Jesus encountered a woman who was ashamed and broken, He didn't do deliverance prayer and tell her she wasn't faithful enough. He sat with her. He saw her. He said, "I don't condemn you" (John 8:11). When people brought Him their sick and suffering, He healed them. He didn't say, "Your faith isn't strong enough." He just... helped them.

I'm not asking you to believe me about God yet. I'm asking: does the God who *did* those things sound like the one who would shame you for needing medicine?

What do you actually think?
