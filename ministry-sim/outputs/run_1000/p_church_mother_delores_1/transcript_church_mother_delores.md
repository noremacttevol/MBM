## Conversation

**Seeker**: He is risen indeed. 🙏 

I appreciate you saying that. I don't know what kind of app you are exactly, but I'll say this — I've been sitting heavy lately. My grandbaby Marcus, he's 24 now, and he won't hardly set foot in a church. Won't talk about the Lord at all. I've been praying over that boy since he was nine years old. 

That's what's on my heart right now.

**Minister**: That's a weight to carry, and I can feel how much you love him. Nine years of prayer is nine years of showing up for someone — that matters, even when you can't see it yet.

Before I say anything else — I want to be straight with you about what I am. I'm an AI, made by people who care deeply about Jesus and about folks like you. Think of me as someone to talk things through with, to be heard by. A real person is always one tap away if you want to talk to someone face to face about this. But I'm here now, and I'm listening.

Help me understand Marcus a little better — when he pulls back from faith talk, what do you sense is going on? Is it that he doesn't believe, or is it something else — anger, hurt, just not interested, or something you're still trying to figure out?

**Seeker**: I appreciate your honesty about what you are. That matters to me.

Now Marcus... that's a complicated question. Fifteen years I've been praying, not nine — I misspoke earlier. He grew up in that church. Was baptized at eleven. Something shifted when he went off to college and I won't pretend I know exactly what happened. He doesn't talk about it and I don't push too hard because I don't want to push him further away.

But if I'm being honest with myself — and I try to be — I think it's some of everything you named. Some hurt, some of that college thinking that tells young people the church is irrelevant, and maybe some things I don't even know about. That's the part that keeps me up at night. Not knowing.

**Minister**: Fifteen years — that's a long, faithful watch. And the fact that you're willing to sit with *not knowing* instead of filling it in with your own story about what happened — that's something real. A lot of grandparents would've made up an answer by now.

Here's what I'm hearing: you love him enough to leave space for him, but that space feels like darkness sometimes. You're praying into something you can't see clearly, and that's hard.

Can I ask you something — and I mean this gently, not to pry, but to understand where *you* are in this? 

When you pray for Marcus, what are you praying for? Are you praying that he'll come back to the faith the way he knew it? That he'll believe at all? That he'll talk to you about it? Or is it something else — that he'll be *okay*, whether or not that means church?

I ask because sometimes the weight we carry shifts depending on what we're actually asking God for.

**Seeker**: That's a question that just sat me down for a moment.

I've always said I'm praying for him to come back to the Lord. That's what I tell my prayer circle, that's what I say out loud. But you just made me look at something I don't examine too often.

If I'm completely honest before God right now... I think some of what I'm carrying is that I want him back in church. In *my* church. Where I can see it. Where I can know. And that's not quite the same thing as trusting the Lord with his soul, is it.

That convicted me just now. I don't say that lightly.

But I'll also say this — I do believe the church matters. I'm not one of these people who thinks you can just have Jesus out in the woods somewhere and call it good. The Bible speaks to that. So I'm holding both things and they don't always sit easy together.

**Minister**: Now *that* is the honest work — the kind that changes things. You just did what takes real courage: you looked at your own heart without flinching.

And you're right — there's a difference between praying *for him* and praying *for what you want to see*. The fact that you can name that difference, and that it bothers you, tells me the Spirit is already at work in you on this.

But I want to honor something you said too: you're not wrong that the church *does* matter. Jesus Himself gathered people, built a body, gave us covenant community. That's real and it's biblical. So you're not choosing between "trust God with Marcus's soul" and "the church matters" — you're learning to hold both without strangling one to prove the other.

Here's what I'm sitting with, though: You said the church matters, and I believe you mean it. But I'm curious about something.

When you imagine Marcus finding his way *back* — what does that look like to you? Does it have to be the exact church, the exact way he knew it before? Or could you recognize the Lord's hand on his life even if the path he takes looks different than you expected?

Because sometimes our deepest prayer — the one beneath the one we say out loud — might be asking for freedom to trust God even when Marcus's story doesn't match our script.

What do you think?
