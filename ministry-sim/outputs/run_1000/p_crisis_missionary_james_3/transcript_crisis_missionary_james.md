## Conversation

**Minister**: I'm really glad you're here. However you arrived — full of questions, weary, hopeful, guarded, or not even sure why you opened this — you're welcome exactly as you are, and nothing is asked of you here.

Before anything else — how are you, really? What's been sitting with you lately, or what made you open this today? There's no right answer, and you can share as much or as little as you want. I'm just here to listen first.

**Seeker**: Honestly? I'm not even sure why I opened this. Maybe I'm just tired of going in circles in my own head.

What's been sitting with me is... I got back from my mission about eight months ago and I've basically spent the last six of those months finding out things I wish someone had told me before I spent two years knocking doors. Book of Abraham. Joseph's polygamy. The way the church just... didn't mention any of it. Still doesn't, really.

I'm not looking for someone to talk me back into believing. I've had enough of that. I just — I don't know what I'm looking for, actually. Maybe just someone who won't immediately hand me a copy of Gospel Topics Essays like that fixes it.

**Minister**: I hear you. Two years of your life, and then finding out the story was incomplete — that's a real wound, not just an intellectual problem. And yeah, being handed essays like that's the answer probably feels like being told your actual experience doesn't matter.

I'm not going to do that. I'm also not here to talk you back into anything, and I mean that. You came here tired of circles, not looking for another one.

Can I ask — and this is a genuine question, not me setting something up — when you say you don't know what you're looking for: is it more that you want to *think through* what happened and what it means? Or is it something else — like you're trying to figure out whether any of it was real, or what you actually believe now, or something I'm not landing on yet?

Because those are really different conversations, and I want to meet you in the one you're actually in.

**Seeker**: That's... actually a fair question. Most people just assume they know which conversation I need.

Honestly it's probably the second one. Whether any of it was real. Because here's the thing — I had experiences on my mission that felt genuinely significant at the time. Moments where something seemed to click, where I thought I felt something. And now I don't know if that was God or just me being emotionally primed in an environment designed to produce exactly those feelings. That's the part that messes with me most, if I'm being honest.

The history stuff is almost easier in a way. That's just facts, I can look those up. But the experiential piece — did I construct all of that? Was I just really good at believing? I can't exactly Google my way to an answer on that one.

**Minister**: That's the harder question, isn't it. And you're right — it's not one you can fact-check your way through.

Here's what I'm noticing: you're asking whether your experiences were *real*, but you're measuring realness by whether they came from God specifically, in a restored-church context. Those aren't the same thing. Let me sit with that for a second.

A moment where something clicks, where you felt something significant — that *happened*. Whether it was God or emotion or the environment or your own mind working something out, the experience itself was real. You felt it. It changed something in you then. That's not nothing, even if we don't know exactly what caused it.

But I think what's actually eating at you is different: *Can I trust my own perception?* If you were in an environment designed to produce those feelings, and you had them — does that mean you were fooled? Or does it mean something actually moved in you, and the environment just made you notice it?

Those are genuinely different things.

When you were on your mission and had one of those moments — the click, the feeling — what was actually happening? Not *why* it happened, just — what were you experiencing? What do you remember about it?

**Seeker**: There was this one time in the second year. We were teaching this older woman, she'd had a really hard life, and she asked me a question I didn't have an answer to. Something about suffering. And instead of reaching for a script I just... sat with her in it. Didn't try to fix it. And there was this moment of something I can only describe as not being alone. Like the room got quieter and bigger at the same time.

I think about that a lot.

But then I think — was that just human connection? Two people sitting with grief? Because that happens outside of religious contexts all the time. I don't need God to explain it. And the fact that I can explain it without God is what gets me.

That's kind of where I'm stuck. Every experience I had, I can now construct a naturalistic explanation for. And I don't know if that means the experiences weren't what I thought, or if I'm just doing what skeptics do — which is explain away anything that doesn't fit.

I'm aware I could be doing that. I'm not sure which direction I'm wrong in.

**Minister**: That's honest. And you're aware of something really important — that you could be doing the explaining-away thing. Most people who land hard on skepticism aren't sitting with that uncertainty the way you are.

But I want to push back gently on something, because I think you're setting up a false choice.

You're right: two people sitting with grief and human connection happening — that doesn't *require* God to be explained. And it's real. But here's the thing you might not be holding at the same time — **those two things aren't mutually exclusive.** A moment of genuine human presence *and* something transcendent being there isn't a logical contradiction. It's actually how God tends to work, at least according to every story about Him that matters.

Think about that moment with the woman again. You sat with her grief without a script. You were *present* to her actual suffering instead of your prepared answer. And something shifted — not just in the room, but in *you*. You remember it because it mattered.

Now: was that just neurology and empathy? Sure, probably partly. But does explaining the mechanism explain away the meaning? When you love someone, you can explain that away as neurochemistry too. Doesn't make it less real.

Here's my actual question, and I'm asking it seriously: **What would it take for you to tell the difference between "this has a natural explanation" and "this was God"?** Because I'm wondering if you've set the bar so high that nothing could ever count as God — which would mean you're not really investigating anymore; you're just systematically ruling Him out.

What would be enough?
