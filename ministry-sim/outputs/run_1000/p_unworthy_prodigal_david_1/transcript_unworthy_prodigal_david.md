**Minister**: I hear you. That image — standing outside looking in — that's not small. And the fact that you felt something about missing your son's baptism, that you opened this at all... that's not the sound of someone who's far gone. That's someone who still feels the weight of it.

I want to ask you something, and I'm genuinely asking, not steering: when you say you *used to* believe — what changed? Was there a moment, or has it been a slow fade? Because the way you're carrying this sounds less like you stopped caring and more like something broke your trust in what you used to hold.

**Seeker**: That's... closer to the truth than I expected something like this to get.

It wasn't one moment. I made some choices — bad ones — about fifteen years ago. Things I'm not proud of. And I kept waiting to feel like God was still there, and I just... didn't. Or maybe I decided I didn't deserve to feel it. I'm not sure which came first anymore.

I stopped going. And then the longer I stayed away the worse it got, because now it's not just the original thing. It's fifteen years of absence on top of it. Fifteen years of people in my ward seeing me walk away.

I guess I stopped trusting that it was available to me. Not to everyone — I think God probably works for most people. I just think there are some people who've crossed a line somewhere and I'm pretty sure I'm one of them.

**Minister**: Thank you for telling me that. That took real honesty.

Here's what I'm hearing underneath: you made choices you regret. You waited for God to show up in the way you needed Him to, and the silence felt like a verdict — like He looked at what you did and decided you were on the other side of a line. And fifteen years of walking away just cemented it. Now it feels like fact.

But I want to ask you something, and I need to ask it gently because it matters: **When you think about your son getting baptized — about him being brought into something you once believed in — do you want him to feel the way you've felt about yourself? Do you think he *should* feel like there's a line he can cross that puts him beyond reach?**

I'm not asking to shame you or to twist your own love for him into an argument. I'm asking because sometimes we can see clearly for the people we love what we genuinely cannot see for ourselves. And I'm wondering if that's true for you.

**Seeker**: That's not a fair question.

I mean — no. Obviously no. If my son ever came to me and said he'd messed up, I wouldn't... I wouldn't write him off. I wouldn't even think about it. He's my kid.

But that's different.

*long pause*

I know what you're doing. And I'm not saying you're wrong exactly, I just... it feels different when it's me. I can believe God works that way for other people. I've seen it. I just have a hard time believing I haven't exhausted something. Some patience. Some limit.

I don't know how to explain it. It's not logic. It's just what feels true after fifteen years of living with it.

**Minister**: You're right — it's not the same logic. And I'm not going to pretend it is or try to argue you out of a feeling that's been baked into your bones for fifteen years. That wouldn't be honest.

But here's what I notice: you just told me something true about yourself. You said you wouldn't write your son off. You *couldn't* even think about it. That tells me something about who you actually are — not who you think you deserve to be, but who you are when it comes to the people you love.

And then you said: "It feels different when it's me."

So let me ask you this, and I want you to sit with it without answering right away if you don't want to:

**If the God you used to believe in is anything like the father you actually are to your son — if He's even half as unable to write someone off as you are — what does that do to the story you've been telling yourself about having crossed a line?**

Not logic. Just — what if you're wrong about what He's like, not because of an argument, but because of who *you* know yourself to be?

I'm not saying the answer is simple or that fifteen years of weight just lifts. I'm saying maybe the verdict you're living under isn't His.
