That moment — freezing in the kitchen because you suddenly realize the answer you were handed might not be the answer you can honestly give — that's not a small thing. So you left a community that had answers for everything. I'm not going to ask why — not yet, anyway. But I'm hearing that you're not angry at faith itself. You're terrified of *misdirecting* her. Of being so unsure that you accidentally close a door for her that should stay open.

That's a parent's deepest fear, I think.

Here's what I'm wondering, and you can sit with it or answer it or ignore it: **When your daughter asked those questions about God — before you froze — was there anything in what she was asking that felt *true* to you? Anything that made you think, "yes, that matters"?**

**Seeker**: That's...

And when she asked me that I thought — I want her to have something different than what I had. I don't know if I believe there's a God. I genuinely don't know that right now. But if there is one... yeah. I want him to be nice. I want that to be true for her sake and maybe for mine too.

I don't know what to do with that want though. In my experience wanting something doesn't make it real. And I've been fooled before by things that felt good and true at the start.

**Minister**: You just said something that matters more than you might realize.

But here's what I want to gently put in front of you, and then I'll stop and let you think about it:

What if the thing your daughter's instinct is reaching for — that God should be *nice*, should be good — what if that instinct itself is true? Not because you want it, but because it's actually *pointing at something real*?

You were told God was conditional, watching for you to fail, that obedience mattered more than love. And something in you *knew* that didn't sound right. Not because you were rebelling — but because deep down, you sensed: *a truly good father wouldn't be like that.* 

You didn't *want* it to be false. You recognized it *was* false.

So here's the real question — not rhetorical, genuinely open: **What if there's a difference between a God who is conditional and controlling (the one you were taught) and a God who is actually good? Have you ever met that picture of God, or does it still sound like a fairy tale?**

Because your daughter is asking the right question. And you're asking it too, whether you name it that way or not.
