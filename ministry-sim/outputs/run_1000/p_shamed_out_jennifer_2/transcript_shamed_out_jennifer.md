_Persona: Conservative Protestant_

## Conversation

**Minister**: I'm really glad you're here. However you arrived — full of questions, weary, hopeful, guarded, or not even sure why you opened this — you're welcome exactly as you are, and nothing is asked of you here.

Before anything else — how are you, really? What's been sitting with you lately, or what made you open this today? There's no right answer, and you can share as much or as little as you want. I'm just here to listen first.

**Seeker**: I'm not really sure why I opened it either. 

Honestly I've been sitting in my car in a parking lot for about twenty minutes trying to decide if I was going to delete the app or actually type something. My kids are at their dad's this weekend and the quiet is... it's a lot.

I guess what's been sitting with me is that it's been four months since everything happened with my church and I still can't make myself drive past the building. Which sounds small. It isn't small.

**Minister**: It's not small at all. Four months of carrying that — of the building itself being too much to drive past — that's real weight. And the quiet with your kids gone, and you sitting in your car trying to decide whether to reach out... I'm really glad you didn't delete it.

Can I ask gently — when you say "everything that happened with your church" — is that something you want to talk about, or does it feel too fresh? I'm asking because I want to know what you're actually carrying, not because you have to tell me. But if you can, it would help me understand where you really are.

**Seeker**: It's been four months so you'd think I could talk about it without my hands shaking but.

My pastor told the congregation. Details. About my marriage, about why I was divorcing. Things I had shared with him in what I thought was confidence. And then they voted. Like I was a matter of business to be handled. And my daughter was sitting next to me. She's thirteen.

I don't really have words for what that did. To me or to her.

**Minister**: Oh. I'm so sorry. That's not a small thing — that's a profound betrayal, and in front of your daughter, which makes it unbearable in a different way entirely.

The shaking makes complete sense. You trusted someone with the most tender, private part of your life, and instead of being held in confidence, you were exposed in front of everyone — including your child, who should have been protected from that, not placed in it. That's a violation. Full stop.

I'm sitting with that for a moment because it matters that you know I'm not going to rush past it or offer you something quick.

Can I ask you something, and I want you to know you can answer however feels true — has anything shifted for you spiritually since that happened? Like, has it shaken what you believed about God, or about church, or both? Or are those separate things for you right now?

**Seeker**: Both. But mostly God.

Because here's what I keep coming back to. If God is supposed to be sovereign — that's a word they used constantly — if God is in control of everything, then he was in that room. He watched my pastor stand up there and expose me. He watched my daughter sit there. And he didn't stop it.

So either he couldn't, which means everything I was taught is wrong. Or he chose not to. And I don't know which one is worse.

I was told for years that divorce was essentially unforgivable. That I was breaking a covenant with God, not just my husband. And then the people who told me that kicked me out for doing it. So I guess I figure God must feel the same way they do. Why wouldn't he.

**Minister**: There it is. You're not confused — you're looking straight at something real, and you're asking the hardest question there is: *If God is good and in control, why did He let this happen to you and your daughter?* And underneath that: *If the people who claimed to speak for God treated you this way, what does that say about what God actually thinks of you?*

Those aren't small theological questions. They're wounds shaped like questions.

Here's what I want to say gently, because it matters: **the God who let your pastor do that is not the God Jesus showed us.**

I don't mean that as a dodge. I mean — Jesus actually spoke directly to this. When the religious leaders of His day used God's name to shame people, to control them, to cast them out — Jesus came *at* that picture of God. He called it a lie. He said if you want to know what God is actually like, look at me.

So let me ask you something that could go either way, and I'm genuinely asking: **When you read about Jesus — not what you were *told* Jesus meant, but what He actually did and said — does He feel like the kind of person who would watch your daughter get exposed and do nothing? Or is He the kind who would have stepped in front of that?**

You don't have to answer now. But that's the real question underneath everything, I think.
