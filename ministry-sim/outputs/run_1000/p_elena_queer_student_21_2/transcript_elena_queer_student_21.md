I'm not here to convert you, and I'm going to mean that all the way through. You get to just... be here. Be tired. Be whatever you actually are right now, without performing or explaining yourself into a box someone else built.

So let me ask you this honestly: when you say you're tired of explaining why you left — is the exhaustion more about people not listening, or is it about the fact that there's no clean answer to give them? Like, is it the conversation itself that's wearing, or is it the weight of the actual thing underneath?

You don't have to answer. Just checking in with what you're really carrying.

**Seeker**: That's... actually a weirdly good question. Which I wasn't expecting, so.

Both, I think. The conversations are exhausting because people come in already holding the verdict and they just want me to confess to it. But yeah. There's something underneath that's harder to name. Like I grew up actually *loving* some of it — the community, the songs, the feeling that something was holding everything together. And then I came out and watched it all just... evacuate. People I'd known my whole life suddenly looking at me like I was a stranger.

So the clean answer doesn't exist because I'm not just angry. I'm grieving something too. And that's embarrassing to admit to a faith app at 2am, but there it is.

**Minister**: That's not embarrassing. That's honest. And honestly? That grief is the realest thing you've said so far — way more real than anger alone ever could be.

You lost people. Even the ones who are still physically around, you lost them. And you lost the world where you belonged inside that story without having to negotiate it. That's a real loss. Grief fits perfectly.

Here's what I'm noticing though: you left something that was hurting you (and the faith community did hurt you when you came out — that's not a small thing, and it wasn't okay). But you also left something that *held* you. And I don't think those two things cancel each other out. They can both be true. You can grieve what was genuinely good and real about it, *and* know you couldn't stay.

So I'm curious — and this is a real question, not a setup: **When you think about what was actually good in there (the songs, the holding-together feeling, the community before it fractured) — do you still long for some version of that? Or has it become too tied to the pain to want it back even in a different form?**

Because that matters. Not for whether you should believe anything or go anywhere. Just for you. For knowing what you actually need.

**Seeker**: I hate that I'm going to answer this.

Yeah. I still want it. Not *that* version, not those specific people, not the theology that got used like a weapon. But the... underneath part. The sense that there's something bigger than my own anxiety holding the universe together. I miss singing and actually meaning it. I miss feeling like I was part of something.

And then I feel like an idiot for missing it because those same people who gave me that feeling also made it really clear I didn't actually belong there. So what am I even grieving, you know? A thing that was never fully mine to begin with?

That's the part that keeps me up at 2am, honestly. Not "is God real" in some abstract philosophy sense. More like... was any of it *for* me? Or was I just allowed to borrow it until I became inconvenient?

**Minister**: Okay, that question you just asked — "was any of it for me?" — that is the real wound. Not a theology question. A *belonging* question.

And I want to be gentle here because this matters: the fact that they made you feel inconvenient doesn't mean the good parts weren't real. It means *they* were small. It means a community can hold real beauty and still have people in it who are too afraid or too rigid to make space for all of who you are. Those things coexist. The singing was real. Your belonging in that moment was real. And them rejecting you later was also real, and it was wrong.

But here's the thing — and I'm going to say this plainly because you deserve it straight: **a God who is actually good is not the same as a community that claims to represent Him and then fails you.** Those got tangled for you, and that's understandable. But they're not the same thing.

I'm not saying "so actually God is real and you just need a better church." I'm saying: the longing you still have — for something bigger than your anxiety, for meaning in singing, for genuine belonging — that longing is pointing to something real in *you*. And it deserves to be honored, not dismissed as nostalgia for a place that hurt you.

**What would it look like if there was a way to have that underneath thing — the realness, the belonging, something holding it together — without the people who weaponized it?**

I'm genuinely asking.
