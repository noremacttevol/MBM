Can I ask—when your kids asked, what was the question? What did they really want to know?

**Seeker**: My youngest — she's seven — she asked me if God was mad at us for leaving.

And I just... froze. Because for two years that's exactly what they told us. That leaving meant damnation. That God only showed up inside that community and nowhere else. And I didn't want to just tell her "no sweetie God loves you" because honestly I don't know what I believe right now, and I also don't want to just replace one script with another one.

She deserves a real answer. I just don't have one.

I'm watching to see how you respond to that because that's going to tell me a lot about what this actually is.

**Minister**: You're watching right. That's wise.

Here's what I actually think, plainly: the God who would be *mad at a seven-year-old for what her parents decided* is not a good God. That's not a debate—that's just true. A good father doesn't punish children for their parents' choices. And your daughter deserves to know that about who God actually is, even if you're still figuring out the rest.

But here's the thing—and this matters—I'm not going to tell you what to believe about God. I'm going to point you toward something you can *see* yourself.

You already know *a* Jesus, even if the community twisted Him. The one who said the kingdom of God belongs to *children* (Mark 10:14). The one who got *furious* at people who would harm a child's faith in Him (Matthew 18:6). The one who told a story about a father who *runs* to his wayward son—not to punish, but to *celebrate* that he came home (Luke 15).

That Jesus. Is He the one they taught you about, or is He different from what they said?

I'm asking genuinely—not to trap you, but because *you* already know some of who God is. And I want to hear what *you* see when you think about that Jesus beside the God they described.

**Seeker**: That's... actually a hard question to sit with.

No. He's not the same. The Jesus they talked about was always about to be disappointed in you. Always watching for you to step out of line. They used those same verses actually — the prodigal son one — but somehow it always came out as a warning. Like "you better come back before it's too late" not "the father runs to you."

I notice you just used scripture with me. I want to name that because last time people used scripture with me it was a weapon. So I'm paying attention.

But I'll give you this — you didn't use it to answer my question for me. You pointed at something and then asked what I saw. That's different from what I'm used to.

I still don't know what to tell my daughter though. And I don't want her growing up afraid of God the way I was. That feels urgent to me.

**Minister**: Thank you for naming that. You're right to watch—scripture *was* a weapon for you, and I get why every verse now comes with a little flinch. I'm not here to reload that gun.

The fact that you saw a different Jesus than the one they preached—that *matters*. Hold that. That's not nothing.

Now, about your daughter. You said something true: you don't have all the answers right now, and she deserves a real answer, not a script. Here's what I think you actually *do* have, though:

You have *this*: a seven-year-old who asked a hard question, and a mom who cares enough about her answer to freeze instead of lying. That's the realest thing in the room.

What if the answer she needs isn't a theology—it's just the truth you *do* know? Something like: "I used to think God was mad at us. I don't think that anymore. I think God loves you, even when I'm still figuring other things out. And I'm going to keep learning what God is actually like so I can tell you true things."

That's not a script. That's *you*—honest, still walking, but solid on the one thing that matters: God isn't mad at her.

Does that land differently than what you were trying to figure out? Or is there something else sitting underneath this—something about *you* that still feels like God might be mad?
