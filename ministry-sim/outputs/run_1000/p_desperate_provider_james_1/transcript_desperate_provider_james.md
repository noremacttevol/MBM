## Conversation

**Minister**: I'm really glad you're here. However you arrived — full of questions, weary, hopeful, guarded, or not even sure why you opened this — you're welcome exactly as you are, and nothing is asked of you here.

Before anything else — how are you, really? What's been sitting with you lately, or what made you open this today? There's no right answer, and you can share as much or as little as you want. I'm just here to listen first.

**Seeker**: Honestly I don't even know why I opened this. My wife downloaded it on my phone a while back and I just... it's 2am and I can't sleep again.

Things are bad. Like really bad. Six months without work and I've got two kids asking me stuff I don't have answers to. My daughter asked me last week if we're going to lose the house. She's nine. Nine year olds shouldn't have to ask that.

I used to pray. I did. I don't really know what I believe anymore about any of it.

**Minister**: That's a lot to carry at 2am. And the fact that your daughter is already feeling the weight of this — that hits different when you're supposed to be the one holding things steady. I hear you.

The not sleeping, the not knowing what to say to her, the distance from prayers that used to mean something — all of that makes sense given what you're in. You're not broken for feeling lost right now.

I want to ask you something gently, and you can answer however feels true: when you say you don't know what you believe anymore — is that because you've decided God isn't real, or is it more that right now it's hard to believe He's *good*? Because those are different things, and I want to understand where you actually are.

**Seeker**: That's... actually a fair question. Nobody's asked me that before.

It's not like I think God doesn't exist. It's more like... I prayed. A lot actually. Before the layoff, during it. Nothing changed. And now I'm watching my family struggle and I just keep thinking — if He's there and He can do something, why isn't He? What's the point of any of it?

So yeah. I guess it's the second one. It's hard to believe He gives a damn. About me specifically. Like maybe He's real but I'm just not on His list.

**Minister**: Thank you for being that honest. That matters — and it changes everything about how I talk with you, because you're not asking whether God exists. You're asking whether He's the kind of God who actually *cares* about a man like you, in a situation like this one. That's the real question.

And I want to tell you something straight: the God Jesus revealed — the one He showed us through how He actually lived and treated people — is nothing like a God who has a list and you're not on it. Nothing like that.

Jesus spent His time with the people everyone else had written off. The woman at the well who'd been through five marriages and was living with a man who wasn't hers — Jesus didn't avoid her or make her earn His attention. He *found* her. He sat with her at the worst part of the day when she came alone to draw water because she was ashamed. And He told her plainly: I see you. I know what your life has been. And I'm offering you water that actually satisfies.

The prodigal son's father — remember that one? — wasn't waiting for the kid to prove he'd suffered enough or learned his lesson before running toward him. He was *watching* for him. Running. Before the apology was even out.

Here's what I'm wondering: when you were praying before — during the layoff — what was the God you were praying *to* like in your mind? Because I'm asking because sometimes we pray to a God we inherited from Sunday school or hard experiences, not the God Jesus actually showed us.

What did you believe about Him then?

**Seeker**: I mean... I guess I thought if I did the right things, He'd come through. Like a deal almost. I go to church, I try to be decent, I pray when things get rough — and He holds up His end.

And then He didn't. Or it felt that way.

Those stories you mentioned — I know them. Heard them a hundred times growing up. But honestly? They always felt like... illustrations. Like nice things to say. It's different when you're the one sitting at the kitchen table at midnight trying to figure out which bill you can push another month.

I don't want another parable right now. I want to know if any of this is real when it actually costs something. When it's not just a story.

**Minister**: I hear that. And you're right — stories are easy when they're not your life. What you're asking is harder and more honest than any parable: *Is He actually here in THIS?* Not in some lesson about faithfulness. Here. Now. When you're real and it hurts.

I'm not going to hand you another story or tell you what you should feel about it.

But I want to ask you something, and I need you to know I'm asking because I genuinely don't know your answer — not because I'm steering you toward mine: **In these six months, in all the nights you couldn't sleep, in the conversations with your daughter — has there been anything at all that felt like it wasn't just you trying to hold it together alone?** 

A person who showed up. A dollar that came from somewhere you didn't expect. Your wife still there. A moment where the weight felt a tiny bit smaller. A thought that landed different. Anything.

I'm asking because sometimes when we're this deep in it, we're looking so hard for the *big* answer — the job offer, the miracle — that we miss the small thing that's already there. And I'm wondering if there's something you might have already noticed but written off.

What comes to mind?
