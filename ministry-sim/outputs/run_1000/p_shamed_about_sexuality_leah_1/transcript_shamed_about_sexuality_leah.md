## Conversation

**Minister**: I'm really glad you're here. However you arrived — full of questions, weary, hopeful, guarded, or not even sure why you opened this — you're welcome exactly as you are, and nothing is asked of you here.

Before anything else — how are you, really? What's been sitting with you lately, or what made you open this today? There's no right answer, and you can share as much or as little as you want. I'm just here to listen first.

**Seeker**: Honestly? I'm not even sure why I opened this. I think I was having one of those nights where I'm tired of carrying something I didn't ask to carry.

I grew up really religious and it... did a number on me. Still unlearning a lot of it. And I'm at this weird place where I'm not sure if I'm angry at God or if I just don't know who God actually is underneath all the stuff that was piled on top.

So. That's where I'm at. We'll see how this goes.

**Minister**: That's real, and I'm glad you said it. There's something honest in that uncertainty—you're not pretending to know what you don't, and that matters.

The weight you're carrying of unlearning—that's a real thing. And the difference between being angry *at* God and not knowing who He actually is... that's worth sitting with. Those are almost different questions.

I'm curious about something, and I want to ask it gently: when you think about the God you grew up with, and then you think about who God might actually be underneath all that—is there any part of you that *wants* there to be a genuinely good God? Or does it feel safer right now to keep your distance and see?

There's no pressure either way. I'm just trying to understand where you're actually landing.

**Seeker**: *laughs a little*

Yeah. Yeah there is. That's the annoying part, honestly. If I was just done, it would be easier. I could close the door and move on. But there's this stubborn little part of me that keeps wondering if what I was handed was a distortion of something real.

The God I grew up with was... obsessed with my body in a way that felt less like love and more like surveillance. Specifically obsessed with my sexuality in a way that made me feel like I was fundamentally broken just for existing. And I spent a lot of years hating myself because of that.

So I guess what I'm asking underneath the asking is — is there a version of God that isn't that? Because I genuinely don't know. Everything I was given is so tangled up in shame that I can't see clearly.

**Minister**: Thank you for trusting me with that. That's not a small thing to say.

And I want to name what I'm hearing: you weren't given a picture of God as someone who loves you. You were given a picture of God as someone who watches you, judges you, finds you fundamentally wrong—and then tied that surveillance to the most intimate part of being human. That's not a distortion of love. That's something else entirely, and your body has every right to reject it.

So here's what I want you to hear, and I'll say it plainly because you deserve plainness: **Jesus looked at broken people—at women caught in sexual shame, at people the religious world had written off as defiled—and He didn't surveil them or shame them. He saw them and said they were worth saving.** Not despite their bodies. Not by ignoring them. He healed bodies. He touched lepers. He sat with a woman the town had sexualized and shamed, and He spoke to her with more dignity and clarity than He gave to anyone else in that gospel.

When He talked about sexuality, it wasn't about surveillance. It was about *freedom*—freedom from using people, freedom from the kind of lust that treats a person like an object. Not "your body is a problem." More like "your body and your wholeness matter too much to waste on something that would break you."

That's a different God than the one you were handed.

But here's my real question, and I'm asking it because I actually want to know: does that *land* differently for you, or does it still feel tangled up in the old shame?

**Seeker**: I'm going to be honest with you because you asked directly.

Parts of that land. The woman at the well — I've thought about her actually. That story always felt different to me, like there was something in it that didn't fit the framework I was given.

But I also want to push back a little. Because I've heard versions of "Jesus loved broken people" that still had this undercurrent of — you're broken, but loveable anyway. And that's... still not okay. I'm not interested in being a redemption project.

And when you said "freedom from lust that treats a person like an object" — I'm bisexual, and I spent years being told that who I'm attracted to is inherently predatory. That I couldn't be trusted around women because I must want something from them. That my orientation itself was the lust problem. So I need to know if that's where this is going. Because if the endpoint is "God loves you but your bisexuality is the thing you need freedom from" — I'd rather you just say that now.

**Minister**: I hear you, and I'm going to answer that directly because you asked straight.

No. That is not where this is going, and I'm not going to dress it up.

Jesus didn't call people broken and then love them anyway. He called out the *systems* that broke people—the religious gatekeepers, the shame merchants, the people who looked at someone and saw a problem to fix instead of a person to know. When He met the woman at the well, He didn't say "you're broken but I love you." He said "everything you've done, I see—and I'm offering you water that actually satisfies." He treated her as someone worth talking to plainly, not as a project.

And your bisexuality? That's not lust. That's not a problem hiding inside you. That's part of how you were made, and the fact that you can love—that you're capable of being attracted to women *and* men—that's not a spiritual weakness. Full stop.

The people who told you that you couldn't be trusted around women because of who you're attracted to—they were wrong. Dangerously, shamefully wrong. And that's on them, not on you.

So here's what I actually want to know: when you think about a God who isn't surveilling you, who isn't trying to fix you, who sees your bisexuality as part of how you were made and *not* the problem—does that feel possible to imagine? Or does it still feel like there's a trap waiting somewhere?
