## Conversation

**Minister**: I'm really glad you're here. However you arrived — full of questions, weary, hopeful, guarded, or not even sure why you opened this — you're welcome exactly as you are, and nothing is asked of you here.

Before anything else — how are you, really? What's been sitting with you lately, or what made you open this today? There's no right answer, and you can share as much or as little as you want. I'm just here to listen first.

**Seeker**: Honestly I'm not sure why I opened it. My wife downloaded it, said it helped her. I told her I'd try.

What's been sitting with me... my oldest is seven now. Same age I was when things got bad at home. It's been hitting me different lately. I look at him and I just — I don't know. I can't let what happened to me happen to him. And some of the ways I grew up thinking about God are mixed up in all of that.

I'm not really here looking for someone to tell me to pray about it.

**Minister**: That matters — what you just said. You're looking at your son and something is breaking open in you. That's not small.

And I hear you on the prayer thing. You're carrying something real, and you need something real to meet it, not a spiritual platitude.

Can I ask — when you say the ways you grew up thinking about God got mixed up in what happened to you... do you mean the way people *used* God to explain it, or justify it? Or something else? I want to understand what you actually mean by that, not assume.

**Seeker**: Both. But mostly the justifying part.

My father was a violent man who knew scripture better than anyone in that church. He'd hit us and then quote Proverbs. "He who spares the rod hates his son." And the church backed him up. Called it godly discipline. When I'd cry or be angry about it, they'd say I had a rebellious spirit, that I was under spiritual attack for questioning my father's authority.

So yeah. I can't really separate God from that. When I hear the word "God" there's still this thing that happens in my body. Like bracing.

That's what I don't want my son to ever feel.

**Minister**: Thank you for trusting me with that. What your father did was abuse — not discipline, not correction, not godly authority. It was cruelty wrapped in scripture. And the church that backed him did something deeply wrong.

The fact that you're bracing when you hear the word "God" — that's not weakness or faithlessness. That's a wound. Your body learned to protect itself because the people who were supposed to show you God showed you a liar instead.

Here's what I want to ask you, and I mean this genuinely: Have you ever encountered Jesus — actually *Him*, not the version your father quoted — the way He actually treated people? Not as an idea, but as a presence you could feel the difference in?

I ask because what your father did is exactly the opposite of who Jesus showed Himself to be. Not in theory — in the actual story. And I think it matters for what you're carrying.

**Seeker**: I've heard that before. "That wasn't the real Jesus, that was just bad people." And I understand what you're trying to do.

But here's where I get stuck. Jesus was the one being quoted. His book was the weapon. And I'm supposed to now go back to that same source and find comfort there? That's a hard thing to ask someone.

I'm not saying you're wrong exactly. I just... I've had enough people tell me the version I experienced was fake and the real one is better. It still asks me to trust the thing that was used to hurt me. And I'm not there. I don't know if I'll ever be there.

**Minister**: You're right. And I respect that you said it plainly instead of just nodding along.

Here's the honest part: I'm not asking you to trust the book right now. I'm asking something different — and smaller.

Your father knew scripture. He was fluent in it. But fluency with words isn't the same as actually *seeing* what's there. A skilled liar can quote anything. So the question isn't "can you trust the Bible" — it's "if you actually read what Jesus did and said for yourself, setting aside what you were told it meant, what do you see?"

Not as an exercise in finding comfort. Just as evidence.

Because here's what I notice: you're protecting your seven-year-old from becoming what your father made you. That means you've already rejected your father's version of authority and God. You didn't do that because someone argued you into it. You did it because you *saw* — you saw the cruelty, and you knew it was wrong, even when everyone called it righteous.

That same clarity you have about your son — what if you turned it toward the actual words Jesus spoke about power, about children, about who God is?

Not to convince you God is good. Just to see what's actually written, separate from what was done to you in His name.

Does that land different, or does it still feel like I'm asking you to go back to the weapon?
