**Minister**: I'm really glad you stayed and typed that out. That took honesty, and I want to honor it.

First — I hear you on the bad experiences. You're not being difficult; you're being wise. Anyone who's been hurt by faith-speak around something as real and physical as anxiety has every right to be careful. That wariness is not a problem to fix; it's actually protective.

Here's what I want to be clear about from the start: anxiety is *real*. Your medication is real. The fact that your nervous system needs help is not a spiritual failure, and no one here will tell you otherwise. That's not some loophole I'm offering — that's just true. Jesus didn't heal everyone by saying "have more faith"; He also met people where they were, in their actual bodies and actual circumstances.

So I'm not here to replace your medication with spirituality or to spiritualize your struggle into something it isn't.

That said — you said "part of me is still looking for something." I'm curious about that. Not the anxiety part, and not the medication part (those sound like they're where they need to be). But that looking — what is that reaching for? What were you hoping might be on the other side of opening an app like this?

**Seeker**: That's... okay, that's a better response than I expected. I'll be honest about that.

The looking thing. I don't know how to answer that without it sounding embarrassing. Like, I grew up in the church and for a long time God felt real to me. Not in a weird way, just — present. And then when I got sick and people started saying things like "you have a spirit of fear" and "you need deliverance," it all just... curdled. That's the only word I have for it.

And now I genuinely don't know if what I lost was God or just the community's version of God. Which sounds like a therapy question more than a faith app question, I know.

I guess I'm reaching for — I don't know. Maybe just to know if there's a version of this where I don't have to feel like my brain chemistry makes me spiritually defective. Is that even a thing that exists? Because every time I've gone looking for it someone eventually says something that reminds me why I left.

So I'm kind of waiting for that to happen here too. Not trying to be rude, I just — that's where I am.

**Minister**: That's not rude. That's you protecting yourself after people you trusted let you down. And I'm not going to do that thing where I try to convince you I'm different before you've even seen it — that would just be more of the same pattern, wouldn't it?

But I do want to say this plainly: yes. A version exists where your brain chemistry doesn't make you spiritually defective. Not because I'm being nice to you — because it's actually true. Your anxiety is not a spirit of fear. Your need for medication is not a lack of faith. These things are completely separate from your spiritual worth or your standing with God.

Here's what I think might matter more than me saying it: what you *actually* experienced as a kid — that presence, that sense of God being real — do you think that was real? Or do you think your community just taught you to feel something that wasn't there?

I'm asking because it matters for what comes next. If what you felt was real, then the question isn't whether God exists. It's whether the God you felt is the same one those people were claiming to speak for — and honestly, from what you've described, I don't think He is.

Does that distinction land at all, or does it feel like I'm just reframing the same problem?

**Seeker**: It lands. I hate that it lands, actually. Because it would be easier if I could just write off the whole thing.

The honest answer is I don't think I imagined it. When I was like — maybe twelve, thirteen — before any of this happened, there was something. I used to pray and it felt like... I don't know. Like being heard. I've never fully been able to convince myself that was nothing, even when I wanted to.

But here's where I get stuck. If that was real, and if God is actually not the version those people were selling — then where was he when they were telling a twenty-two year old that his panic attacks were demonic? Like, I needed someone to step in and that didn't happen. And I'm not asking you to explain theodicy to me or whatever, I know that's a huge question. I'm just saying that's the wall I hit every time I try to think about this.

The presence felt real. What came after felt like abandonment. And I don't know how to hold both of those.

**Minister**: That's the real question, isn't it. And I'm not going to hand you a theodicy or tell you "God works in mysterious ways" — that's exactly the kind of answer that would confirm you were right to be wary.

But I want to ask you something, and I need you to know I'm asking it genuinely, not rhetorically.

When you were twenty-two and those people were telling you that your panic was demonic — in that moment, did any of them actually *stop* and say what Jesus would say? Did anyone sit with you and say "your body is struggling, and that's real, and you're not defective"? Did anyone look at you the way Jesus looked at the woman at the well or the man by the pool — like your actual, particular suffering mattered more than their theology?

I'm asking because — and this is important — the silence you felt might not have been God abandoning you. It might have been *those people* abandoning you. It might have been them choosing their framework over your actual face.

That's a different thing entirely. And it would explain why the presence you felt as a kid and the abandonment you felt later both feel true — because they might both be.

Does that distinction feel like a real one to you, or does it feel like I'm splitting hairs?
