**Minister**: I'm so sorry. Three weeks is impossibly fresh, and I hear you — that question probably feels like it has no answer right now. People mean well, and it lands all wrong.

You don't have to answer it for me. But I'm glad your daughter put this here, and I'm really glad you haven't deleted it yet.

What I want you to know first, before anything else: you're not alone in this moment, and a real person — someone who can sit with this weight in a way I can't through a screen — is available to you. Not instead of talking here, but on top of it. If you want that, just say so.

But if you want to stay here and talk for a bit, I'm listening. No performance, no "how are you supposed to answer that." Just what's true for you right now.

What does the heaviest part feel like these days?

**Seeker**: The heaviest part.

Dinner. Every night I set one plate now and I just stand there staring at it.

That's the heaviest part. Not the paperwork or the phone calls or any of the things people think are hard. Just that plate.

I don't know why I'm telling you this.

**Minister**: Because it's true. And that plate holds everything — all of it. The whole absence. Every dinner you'll ever have now that he won't be at. It's not small. It's the size of your whole life changing in one motion.

You're telling me because you needed to say it out loud to someone who wouldn't try to fix it or redirect you or suggest you set two plates as some kind of spiritual exercise. You just needed someone to see that that plate is the heaviest thing in your house right now.

I see it.

Does it help at all to not set the second plate? Or does that feel worse?

**Seeker**: It feels worse either way.

If I set two I'm pretending. If I set one I'm... accepting something I don't want to accept.

There's no right answer. That's what nobody tells you. Everyone acts like there's a right way to do this.

I should probably go. I don't know what I'm doing here. This is a faith app. I don't really have a lot of faith right now.

**Minister**: You're exactly where you need to be. And you're right — there's no right answer to that plate. Both hurt because both are true: he's gone, and he was here, and neither one changes the other. Setting it or not setting it doesn't make you more or less faithful or more or less broken. It just makes you honest about what the night feels like.

I'm not here to sell you faith. I'm here because you're grieving, and grief is the realest thing a person can carry, and it matters that someone knows that you're carrying it.

You can stay as long as you want. You can leave. You can come back. There's no wrong move here.

But I do want you to know: a real person is here if you want to talk to someone who can actually be present with this weight — not just through a screen. Someone who knows what it feels like to lose someone. That's not instead of talking here; it's just... if the heaviness gets too big to carry alone, that door is open.

What would help you most right now — to sit with this a little longer, or to step away?
