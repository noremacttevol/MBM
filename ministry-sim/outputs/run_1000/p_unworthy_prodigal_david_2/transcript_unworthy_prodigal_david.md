**Minister**: I'm really glad you're here. However you arrived — full of questions, weary, hopeful, guarded, or not even sure why you opened this — you're welcome exactly as you are, and nothing is asked of you here.

**Seeker**: I don't really know why I opened it, honestly.

I've been sitting with something for a while now. Years, maybe. I used to go to church every week when I was younger and it meant something to me. Now I just... I don't know. I drove past the building last Sunday and I sat in my car for a minute.

I didn't go in.

I guess I just wanted to talk to someone who wouldn't know me.

**Minister**: That matters — what you just said. Sitting in a car outside something that used to mean something, not going in, but also not driving past like it wasn't there. That's real.

And you're right to come somewhere you could be honest without someone you know watching. That takes courage, and I respect it.

I'm wondering — when you sat there, what was the feeling? Was it more like you didn't want to go in, or more like you weren't sure you belonged anymore, or something else entirely?

**Seeker**: It was more like... I wasn't sure I'd be welcome.

Not by the people necessarily. I mean, some of them probably remember me and that's its own thing. But more like... I don't know how to say this without sounding dramatic.

Like maybe God had already made up his mind about me. Like I waited too long and there's a version of this where the door was open and I didn't walk through it and now it's just... closed.

I know how that sounds.

**Minister**: It doesn't sound dramatic at all. It sounds like you're carrying the weight of something real — the fear that you've crossed a line you can't uncross. That God looked at you choosing something else, and decided you were done.

That's not small. And it matters that you named it.

Can I ask you something — and I'm genuinely asking, not leading you anywhere: when you think about God, what do you actually believe about Him? Not what you were taught, not what you think you should believe, but what you actually think He's like. Is He the kind of God who, once you step away, locks the door? Or is there something else in there?

**Seeker**: ...that's a harder question than it looks.

What I actually think? I think I've been gone long enough that I've kind of lost the thread of what I believe. When I was younger I felt something real. I believed He was close. I believed He cared about me specifically.

But then things happened. Things I did. And somewhere along the way the version of God I carried around started to feel more like... a record keeper. Someone who marks things down permanently.

I've heard too many talks about apostasy. About people who knew and turned away. I knew. And I turned away. So.

I guess what I actually believe right now is that He probably hasn't forgotten me but that doesn't mean He's waiting with open arms either. More like disappointed. Finished being patient.

That's what I actually think. Even if I don't want it to be true.

**Minister**: Thank you for that honesty. That takes something to say out loud, especially to someone you don't know.

So there's a God you felt close to once — who cared about you specifically. And then there's this other God, the record keeper, who marks things down and runs out of patience. And you're living in the space between those two pictures, believing the second one even though you wish the first one were true.

I want to ask you something, and I mean this gently: in all those talks about apostasy you heard — about people who knew and turned away — did anyone ever talk about what happened *after*? Like, what God did when someone came back?

Because I'm thinking of a story Jesus told. A man had two sons. One of them took his inheritance and left — he *knew* better, and he went anyway. And he lived in a way that was pretty far from home for a long time. 

When he finally came back, starving and ashamed, his father didn't meet him with a record book. He ran to him. He put a robe on him and threw a party. And when the father's other son — the one who *stayed* — got angry about that grace, the father said something like: *everything I have is yours. But we had to celebrate, because this son of mine was lost and is found.*

That father didn't say "you waited too long" or "you should have come sooner." He just... ran.

I'm wondering: does that picture of a father match the God you felt close to when you were younger at all? Or does it feel like something else entirely?
