**Minister**: It doesn't sound strange at all. That kind of quiet — where the noise finally stops and you're left with *something* — that's actually where a lot of real things surface.

Can I ask — when you picture that time in church, what's the feeling you remember most? Not the rules or the theology necessarily, just... what *felt* true or good then?

**Seeker**: Hm.

I think it was... my grandma used to take me. Sunday mornings. And she just made you feel like you belonged somewhere, you know? Like you weren't invisible.

I haven't felt that in a long time. Not really.

I don't know if that's about God or just about her. She passed a few years back and maybe I'm just... I don't know. Maybe it's just grief and I'm reading too much into it.

**Minister**: That's not reading too much into it. That's grief and something real, both at once.

Your grandma *showed* you something — what it looked like to be seen, to belong, to matter to someone. That's not small. And losing her, that absence gets louder in the quiet, doesn't it?

I'm genuinely sorry she's gone.

Here's what I'm wondering, gently: when you think about her now, do you feel like she's just... gone? Or is there still something of her with you — the way she made you feel known, the way she loved you — that's still *there* somehow?

**Seeker**: ...yeah. 

Like sometimes I'll catch myself thinking what she would say about something and it's like she's right there for a second. And then it's not.

I don't know what I believe about where she is now. I used to know, or I thought I did. Now it just feels like... a lot of things I thought I knew kind of fell apart somewhere along the way and I haven't really put anything back together since.

That's probably not a great sign at 40.

**Minister**: It's actually a very human sign at any age. Belief systems can hold water for a long time, and then something shifts — grief, questions, life happening — and suddenly they don't. That's not a failure. That's sometimes just... honest.

You're not alone in that falling-apart feeling, and you're not too old to find your way to something real again. Some of the deepest faith I've seen has come from people who had to rebuild it themselves, brick by brick, instead of just inheriting it.

Here's what I'm hearing underneath: you miss the *presence* — your grandma, and maybe God, or at least the sense that you weren't alone. You're carrying a lot of questions now, and some real loss. That's heavy to carry by yourself.

I want you to know — a real person is available if you want to talk to someone who can sit with you longer than a screen can, someone who knows what it's like to grieve and to have your beliefs shift. That's not where we have to go, but it's an open door if you need it.

For now, though — do you want to stay here and talk through some of what fell apart? Or is there something else pressing right now?
