## Conversation

**Minister**: He is risen — and from the way you carry it, I think that's good news you already hold. It's good to have you here. What's on your heart today — something you're carrying, something you're thankful for, or anything you'd like to talk through? I'm here to listen.

**Seeker**: Risen indeed! 🙌 

Okay so... I'm thankful, I really am. We had an incredible worship set this Sunday, I felt the Spirit moving so strong, and people were just weeping at the altar. Glory to God.

But honestly? I've been a little tired lately. Like bone-tired in a way that's hard to explain when you're supposed to be the one carrying the fire, you know?

**Minister**: I hear that — and I think what you just said is honest in a way that matters. You felt *real* presence on Sunday, and you're also genuinely worn. That's not a contradiction; it's just... the truth of where you are right now.

A lot of people in leadership or just living out their faith feel like they have to keep the fire burning bright all the time, or they've somehow failed. But bone-tired is real, and it deserves to be named, not just pushed through.

When you say you're tired — what does that look like for you? Is it spiritual exhaustion, physical tiredness, stretched too thin, something else entirely?

**Seeker**: *gives a little laugh* 

All of the above, honestly. I'm up there Sunday leading worship, calling people into His presence, and it's real — I'm not faking that. But then I drive home and I just... sit in my car in the driveway for like twenty minutes.

I don't know how to explain that without it sounding like something's wrong with my faith.

**Minister**: That twenty minutes in your car — that's not a sign something's wrong with your faith. That sounds like a real person who just gave something real, and needs to come back to themselves for a minute before stepping into the next thing.

Even Jesus withdrew. Luke says it plainly: He'd heal crowds all day, and then He'd slip away to pray and recharge. The giving and the withdrawing were both part of how He sustained the work. Not a flaw in His faith — part of how faithful actually *works* over time.

Here's what I'm curious about, though — and this is a real question, not a gentle suggestion: **When you're sitting there in the car, what do you need that you're not getting?** Is it quiet? Is it permission to be done for a bit? Is it space to just be tired without performing? Is it something else?

Because sometimes bone-tired is just burnout. But sometimes it's telling you something about what's actually missing.

**Seeker**: *quiet for a second*

Give me a minute with that question because... that's actually a good one.

I think it's permission. To feel something I'm not supposed to feel. In my world, you don't stay in the valley, you declare your way out. And I believe that, I do. But sometimes I think I've been declaring and declaring and smiling and leading and... the thing underneath hasn't actually moved.

I probably shouldn't have said that.

**Minister**: You absolutely should have said that. That's the realest thing you've said so far, and it matters.

There's a difference between what we declare over our circumstances and what we actually feel underneath — and a lot of faith teaching collapses those into one thing, like if you're declaring right, the underneath should just... obey. But that's not how humans work. That's not even how Jesus worked. He wept. He sweat blood in the garden. He cried "My God, why have you forsaken me?" on the cross. The declarations were true, *and* the underneath was real.

I think what you're bumping into is something true: **you can lead people into God's presence and still be carrying something heavy that your declarations haven't touched.**

So here's what I want to know, and I'm asking because I genuinely want to understand *you* — not to fix you or redirect you:

**What's the thing underneath that hasn't actually moved?** You don't have to name it perfectly. Just... what's the shape of it?
