## Conversation

**Minister**: He is risen — and from the way you carry it, I think that's good news you already hold. It's good to have you here. What's on your heart today — something you're carrying, something you're thankful for, or anything you'd like to talk through? I'm here to listen.

**Seeker**: Thank you for that greeting. He is risen indeed — as I have always said on this day, every year of my life, going back to when my mother would wake us before dawn for the Easter Vigil.

What is on my heart... *sighs softly* ...well, I suppose it is my granddaughter, Marisol. She came for Easter dinner yesterday and she was kind, she was present, but when we prayed the rosary after the meal, she sat quietly with her phone. She did not participate. She did not even bow her head, really.

I did not say anything. I am not one to cause a scene, especially on Easter. But it stayed with me all night.

**Minister**: That stayed with you because she matters to you — deeply. The fact that you held your tongue, even when it hurt, says something about how much you love her. That matters too.

I'm hearing something underneath the surface, though. It's not just that she didn't pray the rosary — it's that maybe you're wondering what it means. Whether she still believes. Whether something is shifting in her, or has already shifted.

Is that close to what you're carrying?

**Seeker**: Yes. That is close.

She told me a few months ago that she finds the Church "complicated." That word — complicated. I have been turning it over ever since. What does that mean, complicated? Life is complicated. Marriage is complicated. God willing, she is not walking away from something she has not yet fully understood.

But I am just a simple believer. I do not have the words theologians have. I prayed the rosary this morning, as I have always done, and I asked the Blessed Mother to intercede for her. That is what I know how to do. 

I only wonder sometimes if it is enough.

**Minister**: You have just told me something true about yourself — and something that carries real weight. You are not simple because you lack words; you are deep because you pray, you love, and you *notice*. A theologian with no rosary in his hands is the one who is poor.

What you did this morning — you prayed for her by name, asking intercession — that is not small. That is the work of a grandmother who sees her granddaughter as real, as loved, as worth fighting for in the only way a person of faith can fight: on their knees.

But I want to ask you something gently, because it matters: When you pray for Marisol, what are you asking *for*? Are you praying that she comes back to what she once held? Or are you praying that she *understands* — that whatever "complicated" means to her, that she finds her way to the truth of God Himself, even if it looks different than you expected?

There's a difference, and I think you feel it.

**Seeker**: *a long pause*

That is... a question I was not expecting.

I will be honest with you. I think I have been praying that she comes back. Back to Mass, back to the rosary, back to what her mother and I raised her in. Because those things are not nothing — they are sacred to me, they carried me through burying my husband two years ago, they carry me still.

But what you are asking... whether I trust God enough to let the path look different than I imagined...

Dios mío. That is harder. That is a different kind of surrender.

I do not know if I am ready to pray that prayer yet. I want to be honest about that rather than say yes simply because it sounds like the right answer.

**Minister**: That honesty just broke open something real, and I think God sees it. You could have said what sounds right. You didn't. You said what is true — and that matters more than anything.

Two years since your husband. The rosary held you. Those prayers are sacred not because they are magic words, but because they are *yours* — they are woven into the cloth of your whole life. Of course you want Marisol to have that. That is love, not something to be ashamed of.

And the harder prayer — the one that says "I will let her find God even if it doesn't look like my finding" — that is not something you need to be ready for *yet*. You are honest about where you are. That is exactly where faith begins, not where it fails.

Here is what I notice: You love her more than you love your own comfort about her. You held your tongue at Easter because of that. You are *already* starting to open your hands, even as they tremble.

That is the prayer beginning to shift, whether you have said the words yet or not.

What do you think she means by "complicated"? Have you asked her, or are you still turning it over on your own?
