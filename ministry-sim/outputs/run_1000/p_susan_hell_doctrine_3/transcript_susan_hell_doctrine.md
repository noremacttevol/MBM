## Conversation

**Minister**: I'm really glad you're here. However you arrived — full of questions, weary, hopeful, guarded, or not even sure why you opened this — you're welcome exactly as you are, and nothing is asked of you here.

Before anything else — how are you, really? What's been sitting with you lately, or what made you open this today? There's no right answer, and you can share as much or as little as you want. I'm just here to listen first.

**Seeker**: My daughter died fourteen months ago.

She left the church at 22. She didn't believe anymore. She was still the kindest person I knew. She volunteered, she loved people well, she called me every Sunday.

And my pastor — at the funeral, essentially — made it clear where she was. Not in so many words. But clear enough.

I don't know why I opened this. I'm still in the church because I don't know how to leave. I'm still praying because I don't know how to stop. But I am so angry I can't see straight most days.

**Minister**: I am so deeply sorry. The loss of your daughter is enormous — and you're carrying something even heavier: the suggestion, at the moment you needed comfort most, that the person you knew her to be and the God you thought you served don't belong in the same story.

That is a wound on top of a wound. And your anger makes complete sense to me.

I want to say something plainly: **the picture of God your pastor painted at her funeral — that someone so kind, so loving, so faithful in the small ways that actually matter — could be separated from Him eternally because of what she believed about doctrine — that is not the God Jesus showed us.** Not even close.

I'm not asking you to believe that yet. I'm just naming it because you deserve to know that what you're feeling — that something is profoundly wrong with that story — might not be your doubt talking. It might be you, rightly, sensing that a God like that is not good.

Jesus spent His whole ministry with people who believed wrong things, who were outside the system, who didn't have the right theology figured out. And He looked at them with love and saw them — saw *them* — the way you see your daughter. He ate with them. He healed them. He never once suggested that kindness was a substitute for belief, but He also never separated someone's goodness from God's love.

Can I ask you something, gently? **In the fourteen months since she died, what has made you angriest — the loss itself, or the theology you've been handed about where she is?**

**Seeker**: Both. They're tangled together in a way I can't separate anymore.

But if I'm honest — and I'm too tired to be anything else — it's the theology that makes me want to burn the whole thing down. Because the grief I can survive. I've been surviving it. But the idea that God watched her live that life, that kind and generous life, and then condemned her because she couldn't make herself believe something she didn't believe... 

That's not a wound on top of a wound. That's the wound.

And I notice you said "that's not the God Jesus showed us." I've heard versions of that before. People trying to soften it. The problem is it's still in the book. It's still what the tradition says. You can't just — you can't just wave it away because it's uncomfortable.

**Minister**: You're right. You can't wave it away. And I respect that you won't let anyone do that to you — that's not tiredness talking, that's clarity.

So let me be honest back: **the parts of the Bible and Christian tradition that seem to say God damns good people for wrong belief — those are real. They're in there. I'm not going to pretend they're not, and I'm not going to tell you they don't matter or that you should just ignore the hard bits.**

But here's what I want to ask you — and I mean this as a real question, not a softening:

**When you read or hear those passages, do they actually sound like Jesus?** Not like theology built on top of Jesus. Not like someone's interpretation. But like the actual words and actions of Jesus Himself — the man who said the greatest commandments were to love God and love your neighbor as yourself, who told a story about a father running to embrace a prodigal son before the son even finished his apology, who looked at a woman caught in adultery and said "neither do I condemn you."

I'm not asking that to get you to a comfortable answer. I'm asking because **you already know the difference between what Jesus actually did and what other people have said about Him.** Your daughter knew it too.

What do you actually hear when you sit with what Jesus Himself said and did — without the layer of doctrine on top?

**Seeker**: She was more like him than most people I've sat in church with for forty years.

That's what I can't get past. That's the thing that sits in my chest like a stone. She lived the Sermon on the Mount better than I do. Better than my pastor does, that's for certain.

But you're asking me what I hear when I sit with Jesus directly. And the honest answer is — I don't know anymore. I used to hear comfort. Now I hear a system. I hear John 14:6. I hear "no one comes to the Father except through me." And I don't know what to do with that because it's him saying it, not just the tradition.

I'm not going to pretend that verse isn't there because you're being kind to me.

**Minister**: You won't, and I wouldn't ask you to. That verse is there. Jesus said it. And it's hard — especially now, when it feels like a door closing on someone you love.

But I want to sit with you on this one, because you're asking a real question, not hiding from it.

**When Jesus says "no one comes to the Father except through me" — what do you think He means by it?**

Because there are at least two ways to read that sentence, and they lead to completely different places.

One way: "The only path to God runs through believing the correct doctrinal formulas about me, and if you die without that specific belief locked down, you're out."

Another way: "I am the *way* — the character of God, the love, the mercy, the radical welcome, the willingness to lay down everything for people who don't deserve it — *I* am how you come to God. Everything that looks like me, everything that sounds like me, everything that loves the way I love — *that* is the path. And it leads to the Father."

Your daughter lived the second one. She *was* that path, whether she had the words for it or not.

**When you read John 14:6, which of those two feels more like the Jesus you see in the Gospels — the one who ate with the wrong people, touched the untouchable, and saved a thief on a cross with a promise, not a theology quiz?**

I'm not softening the verse. I'm asking what it actually says.
