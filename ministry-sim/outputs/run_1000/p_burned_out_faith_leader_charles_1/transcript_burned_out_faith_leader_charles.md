So yeah. The wanting burned out too. Somewhere around year fifteen I think. I just didn't tell anyone for seven more years.

**Minister**: That's honest. And I think what you just described — chasing something real that you felt once, then slowly realizing you're just going through the motions while everyone else thinks you're still *there* — that's its own kind of grief. Seven years of carrying that alone.

Here's what I'm wondering, and I don't know the answer — so I'm genuinely asking: When you say those early moments felt like connecting to something... do you think the problem was that God wasn't there, or that *you* couldn't feel Him anymore? Because those aren't the same thing.

Does that land at all, or am I off?

**Seeker**: That's... a fair distinction. I'll give you that.

But here's where I get stuck. If God was there the whole time and I just couldn't feel Him — okay, maybe. But nobody around me helped me figure out why. Nobody said "Charles, something's wrong, let's actually look at this." They just needed me functional and on the platform. So even if God was there, His people sure weren't.

And after a while those two things kind of... blur together for me. The institution and the deity. I know theologically they're separate. I know that. But when the only access point you have to God is a community that's using you, it gets hard to separate them.

I don't know if God was absent or if I just got buried under too much noise to hear anything real. Honestly. I don't know.

**Minister**: That's not theology you're stuck on, Charles — it's grief and betrayal. And you're right to not separate them neatly. You *shouldn't*. The people who are supposed to reflect God to you were looking at you and seeing a utility, not a person. That's real damage, and it makes sense that you can't cleanly divorce God from the institution that told you it was speaking for Him.

So let me sit with that for a second. You were carrying something real — a wound, a question, a breaking — and the community around you didn't have eyes to see it. They had eyes for what you *produced*. That's a real betrayal of what the people of God are supposed to be.

Here's what I'm asking now, and it's different from before: Not whether God was there or whether you could feel Him. But this — 

When those moments *did* feel real in your late twenties, what was actually happening in you? What made it feel different from the executing that came later?

Because I'm wondering if there's something in *that* — not in the institution, not in performance metrics, not in what anyone else needed from you — something in what actually moved you. That might matter.
