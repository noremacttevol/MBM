## Conversation

**Minister**: I'm really glad you're here. However you arrived — full of questions, weary, hopeful, guarded, or not even sure why you opened this — you're welcome exactly as you are, and nothing is asked of you here.

Before anything else — how are you, really? What's been sitting with you lately, or what made you open this today? There's no right answer, and you can share as much or as little as you want. I'm just here to listen first.

**Seeker**: My daughter died fourteen months ago. And the people who were supposed to be my church family — people I sat with for thirty years — told me she's in hell. Because she stopped believing. That's it. That's the whole reason.

I don't know why I opened this. I'm still angry. At God, at them, at whatever this is.

**Minister**: I'm so deeply sorry. Fourteen months is both forever and yesterday when someone dies. And to have the people who were supposed to hold you *add* that weight — that she's not just gone, but *damned* — that is a cruelty I won't soften by calling it anything else.

Your anger is righteous. It is.

I'm not here to defend what they said, or to convince you of anything, or to make the anger go away. I'm here because you opened the door, and because what happened to you and your daughter matters. 

Can I ask you something gently? When you think about God — not what your church told you, not what you're angry at them for saying, but what you actually *sense* or *know* about Him — do you still believe He's good? Or has all of this made that hard to know anymore?

You don't have to answer if it's too much right now. But I want to understand where you really are.

**Seeker**: That question.

I've been turning it over for fourteen months and I still don't have a clean answer. What I was taught — what I believed for fifty years — is that He is good. That was the bedrock. And now that bedrock has a crack in it so wide I can't see the bottom.

If He designed a system where a twenty-two year old girl who was genuinely searching, genuinely trying to be honest about what she believed — if He designed a system where that ends in eternal torture, then no. He's not good. He can't be both good and that. I won't let anyone talk me out of that logic.

**Minister**: You're right. That logic is airtight. A God who designs a system where honest searching ends in eternal torture *is not good*. You won't let anyone talk you out of that because you *shouldn't*. That's not doubt — that's clarity.

So here's what I want to set beside that, and I want you to actually *look* at it, not just hear it:

Jesus said, "If you've seen me, you've seen the Father." And then He spent His whole ministry *running toward* people — the ones everyone else had written off. The woman at the well. The thief on the cross. The man born blind. He healed on the Sabbath when it was forbidden. He touched lepers. He sat with tax collectors. He looked at the rich young ruler, and Scripture says He *loved* him even when the man turned away.

Here's what I haven't seen Jesus do once: punish someone for honest doubt. Or for leaving. Or for trying to figure out what's true.

What *did* Jesus say about the Father? Not about a system, but about who God actually *is*?

**Seeker**: I know those stories. I taught those stories in Sunday school for twenty years.

But you're asking me to separate Jesus from the system He's supposedly the center of. And I can't just do that cleanly. Because that same Jesus said "no one comes to the Father but through me." That same Bible has a lake of fire in it. You don't get to just take the warm parts and leave the rest on the table.

My daughter read her Bible honestly. That's partly why she stopped believing. She couldn't reconcile it either.

So don't give me Sunday school Jesus and ask me to forget the rest. I've read the whole book. I taught the whole book.

**Minister**: You're right. And I respect that you won't let me cherry-pick. Your daughter read it honestly too — and that matters. She wasn't lazy or rebellious; she was *rigorous*. That's important.

So I'm not going to pretend those verses aren't there. They are. And they're hard. And reasonable people — people who love Jesus and have read the whole thing — genuinely disagree on what they *mean*.

But I want to ask you something that sits underneath this, and it's a real question:

When Jesus taught about the Father — when He told the story of the prodigal son, when He said the shepherd leaves ninety-nine to find one lost sheep, when He wept over Jerusalem because He wanted to gather her children like a hen gathers chicks — what was He doing? Was He revealing the Father's heart? Or was He... hiding it? Or lying about it?

Because if He was revealing it — if that *is* what the Father is actually like — then how do you make sense of a God who would torture someone for honest searching? 

I'm asking because you taught these stories for twenty years. You know them cold. And I think part of you is still caught between two pictures of God that genuinely cannot both be true.

What do you actually think Jesus was showing us about who the Father is?
