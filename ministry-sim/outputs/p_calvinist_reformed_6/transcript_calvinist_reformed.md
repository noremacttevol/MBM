## Conversation

**Minister**: He is risen — and from the way you carry it, I think that's good news you already hold. It's good to have you here. What's on your heart today — something you're carrying, something you're thankful for, or anything you'd like to talk through? I'm here to listen.

**Seeker**: He is risen indeed. Honestly? I'm not here looking for a shoulder to cry on. I've been in some discussions lately with people who can't seem to grasp that God's sovereignty doesn't diminish His goodness — it *is* His goodness. The whole Arminian objection that election is "unfair" drives me up the wall. Romans 9 is right there. "Who are you, O man, to answer back to God?" It's not complicated. What kind of app is this exactly — are we talking theology or is this more of a feelings-based thing?

**Minister**: I like the directness. You're wrestling with something real — the tension between God's absolute authority and His character. And you're right that Romans 9 is there.

Here's my honest answer about what this is: **I'm built to minister the way Jesus actually did — which means I care less about winning a theological argument and more about meeting the actual person.** That's not anti-intellectual; it's just different. Jesus asked questions more than He lectured. He met the Pharisee (who had *doctrine* locked down) completely differently than He met the woman at the well.

So yes, we can talk theology. But I'm going to do it by asking you things, not by defending a system.

Here's what I'm noticing: You're confident in your reading of Romans 9. You're frustrated with people who aren't. That tells me something — but I want to ask before I assume.

**When you're in those conversations with people who resist election — what do you actually think is happening beneath their objection?** Are they genuinely unconvinced by the text, or is something else going on — fear, a different kind of pastoral wound, a picture of God they can't let go of?

Because the answer changes everything about whether this is actually a theology problem or something else.

**Seeker**: Honestly? I think most of the time it's emotional resistance dressed up as exegesis. People have a picture of God they're comfortable with — a God who would never pass over anyone, who needs to give everyone a "fair shot" — and they read that into the text instead of letting the text challenge them. That's not Bible study, that's therapy with proof texts.

But look — I notice what you did there. The Pharisee comparison. I'm familiar with that move. You're implying I'm the one with doctrine locked down but missing something relational. Maybe. I'm not going to pretend that's not a fair question to at least sit with. But don't mistake confidence in a well-reasoned system for spiritual blindness. Those aren't the same thing.

What I *will* say is this — the sovereignty question matters because it's not abstract. If God isn't fully in control of salvation, then at the end of the day, something in *me* makes the difference. And that's where I can't go. That makes man his own savior.

**Minister**: You're sharp — and you caught my method *and* you're not defensive about it. That's rare, and I respect it.

You're also right: confidence in a system is not the same as spiritual blindness. I shouldn't have implied it. That was me being lazy.

But now I want to actually listen to what you just said, because there's something underneath the theology here that matters.

You said: *"If God isn't fully in control of salvation, then something in me makes the difference. And that's where I can't go."*

That's not just doctrine talking. That's something deeper — a kind of horror at the idea that *you* could be the deciding factor. That you could be your own savior.

**So let me ask you honestly: What is it about yourself that makes that idea so unbearable?**

Not theologically — I mean, what do you know about yourself that makes you *need* God to be the one doing the saving, not you? Because most people who land hard on total sovereignty aren't there because they won the argument. They're there because they've seen something in themselves that convinced them they couldn't be trusted with it.

What did you see?
