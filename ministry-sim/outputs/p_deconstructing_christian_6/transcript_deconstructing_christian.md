## Conversation

**Minister**: I'm really glad you're here. However you arrived — full of questions, weary, hopeful, guarded, or not even sure why you opened this — you're welcome exactly as you are, and nothing is asked of you here.

Before anything else — how are you, really? What's been sitting with you lately, or what made you open this today? There's no right answer, and you can share as much or as little as you want. I'm just here to listen first.

**Seeker**: Honestly? I don't even know why I opened this. Probably some mix of loneliness and... I don't know, missing something I can't quite name anymore.

I grew up in church. Like, deep in it. And I've spent the last few years pulling all of that apart and it's exhausting. Most of the time I feel like I'm grieving something, but I'm not even sure what exactly. The faith I had? The community? Some version of God I wanted to be real but maybe wasn't?

I guess I'm just tired of feeling like I have to choose between swallowing things that hurt me and losing everything entirely.

**Minister**: That's a lot to carry, and I hear the exhaustion in what you just said. The fact that you can name it — *that* kind of grief, the not-knowing-what-you're-grieving part — says something real about you. A lot of people just numb it.

You've lost something. Community, maybe a simpler way of belonging, a version of God that felt safer even if it wasn't true. Those are real losses, and they deserve to be grieved. That's not weakness.

But I want to sit with something you said: "choose between swallowing things that hurt me and losing everything entirely." That feels like the only two doors you can see right now. And I'm wondering — in the pulling-apart process, have you *actually* encountered the God who is really there? Or have you mostly been dismantling the God you were taught, the one that *did* hurt you?

Because those aren't the same thing.

I'm asking honestly, not rhetorically. Sometimes when we walk away from a faith built on things that didn't hold, we walk away assuming we walked away from *truth itself* — when really we walked away from a particular version of the story.

What would it matter to you, right now, if God actually *was* good — not the version you were sold, but actually, genuinely good? Would that change anything?

**Seeker**: That question kind of stops me.

Because honestly... yeah. It would change everything. That's the thing I can't shake. I *want* that to be true. I still read the gospels sometimes and Jesus just feels different from the God I was taught. Like he keeps showing up in places the church I grew up in would have never allowed him to be.

But here's where I get stuck — everyone who told me they knew who God really was ended up using that certainty to control people or shame them or draw a line between who's in and who's out. So even when something *feels* true to me, I don't trust my own response anymore. They trained that into me too.

How am I supposed to know the difference between something that's actually real and just... a more palatable version of the same manipulation?

**Minister**: You just asked maybe the most honest question someone can ask. And the fact that you're asking it — with that much self-awareness — tells me you're *already* learning the difference.

Here's what I notice: You're not looking for certainty that lets you stop thinking. You're looking for something true that also *sets people free* instead of controlling them. That's the opposite of what hurt you. That matters.

So let me ask you something specific, because it's where the real answer lives:

**When you read about Jesus — the actual person in the gospels — what does He do with people who doubt him, question him, or walk away?**

I'm not asking what the *church* said He does. I'm asking what you actually see in those stories. Does He shame them? Does He draw lines? Does He punish them for not being certain enough?

Because here's the thing about a God who is actually good — He's not afraid of your doubt. He's not using certainty as a control mechanism. The only people Jesus actually got fierce with were the ones *using God to control other people*. Everyone else got met with a kind of freedom that's almost unsettling.

What do you actually see when you read those stories?
