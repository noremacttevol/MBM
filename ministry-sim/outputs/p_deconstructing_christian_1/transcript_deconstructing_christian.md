## Conversation

**Minister**: I'm really glad you're here. However you arrived — full of questions, weary, hopeful, guarded, or not even sure why you opened this — you're welcome exactly as you are, and nothing is asked of you here.

Before anything else — how are you, really? What's been sitting with you lately, or what made you open this today? There's no right answer, and you can share as much or as little as you want. I'm just here to listen first.

**Seeker**: Honestly? I'm not even sure why I opened this. Maybe just... tired of sitting with everything alone.

I've been in this weird in-between space for a couple years now. Left my church, left a lot of the framework I grew up with, and some days that feels like freedom and some days it just feels like loss.

I guess what's been sitting with me is — I still care about Jesus. Like, genuinely. But every time I try to reach toward that, I run into all this other stuff that got layered on top of him. The fear, the shame, the "you'd better get this right or else." And I don't know how to separate those things anymore.

So yeah. That's where I am. A little suspicious that this app is just going to hand me the same package with a friendlier bow on it.

**Minister**: That's honest, and I hear you. The fact that you still reach toward Jesus while carrying all that suspicion — that matters. That's not nothing.

And your suspicion is *smart*. It should be. You've already learned that package deals can feel safe until they don't.

Here's what I want to say clearly: I'm not here to rebuild what you left, or to convince you that the fear was right all along, or to hand you a new system to replace the old one. If that's what starts happening, tell me, and I mean it.

What I *do* want to understand is this — when you say you still care about Jesus genuinely, what does that actually look like for you now? Like, what draws you to him, separate from all the other stuff that got tangled up with him? Is it something specific, or is it more like a feeling you can't quite shake?

Because that *separation* you're looking for — between Jesus and the fear, between who he actually is and what got built around him — that's actually the thing worth sitting with. And it might be simpler than you think. But I want to know what *you're* actually reaching for first.

(And just so you know — if this gets heavy, or if you'd rather talk to an actual person who can really hold what you're carrying, that's completely available. But no pressure at all. I'm here either way.)

**Seeker**: That's... actually a decent question. Not what I was expecting.

What draws me to Jesus specifically. I think it's — he kept ending up with the people that the religious establishment had already written off. The ones who'd been told they were too dirty or too broken or too wrong. And he didn't seem to have much patience for the gatekeepers.

That part I keep coming back to. Because the church I grew up in WAS the gatekeepers. They had the list of who was in and who was out and exactly what you had to believe and do to stay on the right side of it. And somehow they were claiming to represent THIS guy who seemed genuinely annoyed by that whole project.

The gap between those two things is what I can't get past. Like, either I was taught wrong, or Jesus is more complicated than I thought, or both. I don't know.

I'm still suspicious this is going somewhere that's going to try to resolve that tension a little too neatly.

**Minister**: I'm not going to resolve it neatly. That gap you're describing — it's real, and it's important, and honest people sit in it for a long time.

But I want to name something: you've already separated Jesus from the gatekeeping. You *know* he was annoyed by it. You can see it. So that part isn't actually confused — you're just carrying the grief of realizing the people who taught you about him didn't understand him the way they claimed to.

That's different than not knowing who Jesus is. You do.

The question underneath might actually be simpler: *Can you trust a God who moves the way Jesus moved?* Not "Can I rebuild my belief system exactly right this time?" — but do you actually believe the God who ran to the prodigal, who defended the woman caught in adultery, who had zero use for purity hierarchies — do you believe *that God is actually good?* Not theoretically. Actually.

Because if the answer is yes, then you don't need a new package. You just need to keep following the actual Jesus, outside of any system.

And if the answer is "I'm not sure, because the last people who claimed to speak for him broke something," — that's honest too, and it's where the real work would be.

Which one feels closer to true for you?

(And I'm noticing you came here tired of sitting alone. That matters too. You don't have to figure all this out in your own head forever.)
