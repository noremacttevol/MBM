I won't soften that or pretend it's different than it is.

What I *can* tell you honestly: I'm not here to convert you back into the LDS Church, or into any church. That's not my job, and it wouldn't be safe for you even if it were my job. You need to stay vigilant about your own mind and your kids' safety. Full stop.

But I also want to be careful not to overcorrect: the fact that an organization has exclusivist truth claims doesn't automatically make it identical to a high-control group. Some do both; some teach hard truths but leave people free. I don't know enough about what you were in to compare them — and more importantly, *you* do, and your discernment about that is good.

Here's what I actually want to offer: **I can listen to what you're carrying without trying to steer you anywhere.** Not "without agenda" — I do have one. But without *pressure*. 

The question is whether you want to keep talking, knowing what you now know about where this comes from. No judgment either way.

What do you want to do?

**Seeker**: I need to think about it.

That's actually a sentence I'm still learning to say. We didn't get to "think about things." You decided in the moment or you were already behind.

I'm not leaving. But I'm not ready to go deeper on the faith stuff yet. I think I just wanted to say that out loud somewhere - that I miss something and I don't know what it is, and I'm terrified that missing it makes me vulnerable to the next thing that promises to fill it.

My kids are finally starting to argue with me about small stuff. What to have for dinner, what movie to watch. Eight months ago they would just wait to be told. That feels important somehow. I don't want to lose that for them.

**Minister**: That *is* important. That is actually everything.

And I hear you — you needed to say it out loud, and you did. You're allowed to sit with not knowing what comes next. You're allowed to think.

The fact that your kids are arguing about dinner? That's not a small thing. That's them finding their own voice back. That's them learning they have one. And you're protecting that fiercely, which is exactly what a parent does when they've seen what happens when people aren't allowed to have their own minds.

The fear that missing something makes you vulnerable — that's real too. Grief *is* a door that other things can slip through. But grief is also honest. And you're not alone in it, even though right now it might feel that way.
