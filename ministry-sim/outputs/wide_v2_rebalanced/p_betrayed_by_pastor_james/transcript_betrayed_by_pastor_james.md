**Minister**: I'm really glad you're here. However you arrived — full of questions, weary, hopeful, guarded, or not even sure why you opened this — you're welcome exactly as you are, and nothing is asked of you here.

That's where my head is most days.

**Minister**: That's not boredom talking. That's someone who's watched something happen — maybe more than once — and can't unknow it now.

I hear something underneath that, too. You're not asking *whether* people get hurt in churches. You sound like you already know they do. So I'm curious: has that happened close to you? Or are you seeing a pattern you're worried about?

**Seeker**: Close to me. Yeah.

Our pastor — guy I trusted, guy I thought was genuinely called — embezzled somewhere north of $200,000 over several years. And when people started asking questions, he didn't come clean. He got up in front of the congregation and talked about spiritual warfare. Said the enemy was attacking the church through "divisive" people. 

Those divisive people were asking to see the books.

And the denomination? They moved him to another church in another state. That's it. That's the whole accountability structure.

So yeah. It's not a pattern I'm "worried about." It's something I lived through.

**Minister**: That's not just a pattern you saw. That's a betrayal. On multiple levels — the person you trusted broke that trust. The system you thought would protect people... didn't. It protected *him*.

And now you're watching a full parking lot and thinking: *how many people in there don't know yet what I know?*

I get why that sits with you every day.

Here's what I want to understand better, because it matters: Are you mostly angry at *that specific situation* — the pastor, the denomination's response — or has this made you question whether God himself is trustworthy? Or both? Because those are different things, and I want to know which one you're actually carrying.

**Seeker**: Both. But they're tangled together in a way that's hard to separate cleanly.

Like — I know intellectually that a corrupt man doing corrupt things isn't God's fault. I can say that sentence. But then I ask myself... where was God when this guy was up there, week after week, smooth as anything, telling us to give sacrificially while he was redirecting funds to his personal accounts? Where was the protection? Where was the exposure before it went on for years?

And the harder question — the one I can't shake — is that this man prayed. Publicly, passionately. He knew scripture cold. He talked about Jesus constantly. And none of that meant anything about who he actually was.

So how do I trust my own discernment now? How does anyone? I wanted to believe him. We all did. And we all felt stupid afterward.

**Minister**: You've named something most people never say out loud, and it takes real honesty to sit with it: *I wanted to believe him, and I feel stupid for that.* That's not stupidity. That's what it looks like when someone skilled at deception meets a human heart willing to trust. Deception works *because* it exploits that openness, not because you were gullible.

But the deeper wound you're naming — that's the real one. You're asking: *If someone can pray like that, quote scripture like that, sound like Jesus while doing the opposite — what does any of it mean? How do I ever trust again?*

And underneath that: *Where was God?*

I want to sit with that question because it's the honest one, and it deserves more than a quick answer.

When you think back to those years — not intellectually, but actually — do you remember *any* moment where something felt off? A discrepancy you caught but maybe talked yourself out of? Or did he really seem clean the whole time, and the betrayal was total?

I'm asking because it changes something about how we think about where God was. Not to make it okay — nothing makes embezzlement and cover-ups okay. But because your question about *where God was* might be asking something different depending on whether God was silent, or whether there were signals that nobody (including the systems meant to catch this) was actually paying attention to.

**Seeker**: There were moments. Yeah.

Little things. A question about the building fund that got answered with a smile and a pivot. A staff member who left quietly and we were told it was "a calling in a different direction." I remember thinking once — just for a second — *that doesn't add up.* And then the next Sunday he'd preach something that just... landed. And I'd feel guilty for doubting.

That's the part that really gets me. He used the spiritual atmosphere to suppress normal, healthy skepticism. Doubt became a faith problem. Questions became an attack on unity. He trained us not to trust ourselves.

So if you're asking where God was in the signals — okay, maybe there were signals. But the whole environment was engineered to make sure we couldn't hear them clearly.

And that's not just one church. That's a systemic thing. The celebrity pastor culture, the financial opacity, the way "touch not God's anointed" gets weaponized. This happens constantly. How many pastors are doing this right now, tonight, while their congregations sleep?

I'm not being rhetorical. I genuinely don't know the answer and it keeps me up sometimes.

**Minister**: I don't know the answer either, and I won't pretend to. There are people abusing systems right now — financial, spiritual, relational — and getting away with it. That's real. The systems that are supposed to catch it often don't. That's also real.

But I want to gently separate two things you're holding together, because they're different questions and they deserve different answers.

**One question is: "How do I protect myself and others from deception?"** That's practical. It's about learning to trust your own discernment again — those moments when something doesn't add up. You had them. You were right. And you learned (painfully) not to dismiss them just because someone sounds spiritual. That's not a failure of faith; that's wisdom. Real faith and real skepticism aren't opposites.

**The other question is: "Where is God in all this? Can I trust Him?"** 

And here's what I'm noticing: you're tempted to answer that question by pointing to all the times the systems failed and God seemed absent. But I want to ask something that might sound strange:

**In all of this — the pastor's deception, the denomination's cover-up, your own confusion — have you ever encountered the actual God of the Bible? Not the version being preached from that stage, but the One Jesus actually revealed?**

Because I'm wondering if part of what happened is that you were handed a picture of God (passive, distant, letting smooth-talking men do whatever they want), and that picture got shattered — which is actually *good*, even though it hurts.

Does that land at all, or am I off?
