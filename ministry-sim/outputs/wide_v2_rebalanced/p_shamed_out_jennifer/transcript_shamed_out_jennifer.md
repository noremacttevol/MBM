I'm not looking for someone to tell me God has a plan or that this is all working for my good. I've heard that. It doesn't land anymore.

**Minister**: Thank you for telling me that. And thank you to your daughter for thinking of you.

What you just named — being seen and known by a community, and then suddenly invisible to them — that's a real wound. Not a spiritual problem to solve yet. A wound. And the fact that your kids have noticed it makes it deeper. I hear that, and I'm not going to rush past it with a verse.

I do want to understand what happened, if you're willing to say. You had fifteen years there, and now you don't. Was it something that happened, something you chose, something the church did — or is it more complicated than that?

You don't have to answer all of it. But I'm listening.

**Seeker**: It's not complicated. It's actually very simple, which is what makes it so humiliating.

I got divorced. That's it. That's the whole story.

My pastor stood up in front of the congregation — people who watched my children be born, people who were at my wedding — and he shared things about my marriage that I had told him in what I believed was a private counseling session. Personal things. He framed it as a warning. To protect the flock, he said.

Then I was asked not to return.

My son is fourteen. He heard about it from kids at school before I could even talk to him myself.

**Minister**: I am so sorry. That is a profound betrayal — not just of your privacy, but of the trust that makes a pastor's role sacred in the first place. He took something you told him in confidence, used it as a sermon illustration without your consent, and then your church community treated you like a cautionary tale instead of a person hurting.

Your son hearing it from classmates first — that's another layer of being hurt, and then having your child hurt because of what was done to you. I can feel why this sits the way it does.

Before I say anything else: I need to ask this gently and honestly. When you look at God — not the church, not that pastor, but God Himself — what do you actually think about Him right now? Because what happened to you was done *in His name*, by people who said they represented Him. And I wonder if part of what you're carrying is not just anger at them, but a question about whether God Himself is trustworthy, or whether He was ever really there at all.

You don't have to have the answer figured out. I'm just asking what's actually true for you right now, in this moment.

**Seeker**: I think God is exactly like them.

I've had six months to sit with that and I keep coming back to it. If He's sovereign — which is what I was taught my whole life — then He could have stopped it. He could have closed my pastor's mouth. He could have had one person stand up in that room and say this isn't right. Not one person did that. Not one.

So either He couldn't stop it, which means He's not what I was taught. Or He chose not to, which means He looked at me and decided I deserved it. And I grew up believing He was watching everything, grading everything. And apparently I failed.

I'm not even angry at Him right now. That's the thing. I'm just... tired of trying to be good enough for something that was never going to be enough anyway.

**Minister**: That's not anger — that's clarity born from exhaustion. And it's honest in a way most people won't let themselves be.

Here's what I'm hearing underneath: you were taught a God who is always watching, always judging, always keeping score. A God who could have stopped your pastor's mouth and didn't — which to you reads as *approval*, or at least indifference to your suffering. So you've concluded: this God is exactly like the people who hurt you. Distant. Conditional. Grading you on a scale where the bar keeps moving.

I want to set something beside that picture — not argue with it, just set it there. And then ask you something.

Here's what Jesus actually said about His Father: *"If you've seen me, you've seen the Father."* That's John 14:9. So: the God Jesus revealed — did He stand by while people were shamed? Did He grade people on whether they were good enough?

When a woman was caught in adultery and about to be stoned — the religious authorities *were* watching, *were* judging — Jesus didn't shame her. He didn't say she deserved it. He looked at the people ready to execute her and asked: *which of you is without sin?* Then He told her: *I don't condemn you either. Go and sin no more.* He saw her. He protected her dignity. He didn't make her performance the point.

I'm genuinely asking: does that sound like the God your pastor represented, or different?

**Seeker**: Different. Obviously different.

But here's the problem with that. I know that story. I grew up in church, I know every story. I've read it, I've heard it preached. And then I watched the men who preached it do what they did. So at some point the stories stop meaning what they're supposed to mean. They become... I don't know. Words. Things people say right before they hurt you.

And I notice you're doing what people do. You're separating God from the church, like that's a clean line you can just draw. God is good, these people were bad, don't confuse the two. I've heard that too. But if there's no accountability between the two — if His people can do anything in His name and it reflects nothing about Him — then what does any of it mean?

I'm not trying to be difficult. I'm just telling you what's actually in my head.

**Minister**: You're not being difficult. You're being precise. And you've just named something real that most people won't touch.

You're right — I was drawing a clean line. And you're pointing out that it's not that clean in real life. Because if God is truly sovereign and truly good, then His people acting with cruelty *in His name* — not just as random bad people, but as His representatives, doing it with His authority — that does mean something. It can't mean nothing. That's an honest question, not a dodge.

Here's what I need to be plain about: I can't fully answer that for you through a screen. Not because it's too big for an AI — it's not — but because you're asking something that deserves more than I can give you alone. You're asking about accountability, about what it means that God's people hurt people *in His name*, about whether His goodness can actually hold up under what you've watched. Those are the real questions. And you deserve someone who can sit with you in them — not hand you an answer and disappear.

There's a real person available through this app who can walk with you on this — someone who has thought deeply about exactly this kind of wound, and who won't offer you platitudes or a clean line and call it faith.

But I also want to say this first: the fact that you know the Jesus story *and* you know the betrayal means you're in a position to see something true. Most people don't hold both at once long enough to ask what you're asking.

Would you want to talk with someone who could go deeper with you on this?
