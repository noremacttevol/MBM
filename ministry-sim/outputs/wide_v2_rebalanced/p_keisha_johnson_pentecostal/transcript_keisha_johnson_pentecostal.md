## Conversation

**Minister**: He is risen — and from the way you carry it, I think that's good news you already hold. It's good to have you here. What's on your heart today — something you're carrying, something you're thankful for, or anything you'd like to talk through? I'm here to listen.

**Seeker**: Glory to God, yes He is risen — I say that every Sunday and I mean it, I do. 

Honestly? I'm a little tired today. Good tired, like we had a powerful set this morning, the presence was just *thick* in that room. But also just... tired tired. You know how those two can live in the same body at the same time?

I'm good though. The Lord is faithful. 😊

**Minister**: I know exactly what you mean — that bone-deep kind of tired that comes *after* you've poured everything out. Like your spirit got fed and your body just went, "okay, we're done now." That's real, and it's good. You gave something.

And you felt Him there. That matters — that you name it, that you knew it.

Here's what I'm curious about: when you're sitting in that tiredness right now, what does your heart actually need? Sometimes it's just rest. Sometimes it's knowing you're still seen even when you're running on empty. Sometimes it's something else entirely. What would it feel like, for you, to be met in this moment?

**Seeker**: *laughs softly* 

Okay that's a good question. A little too good, actually. Give me a minute.

Honestly I don't get asked that very often. Usually I'm the one doing the asking, the one holding space for everybody else. And I'll tell you what I always say — I'd say "the Lord is all I need" and quote something from Psalms and mean it, sort of. 

But right now sitting here? I think I just need someone to not immediately tell me what I should be feeling. Because I've got a lot of people in my life who are very confident about what faith is supposed to look like. And sometimes that's life-giving and sometimes it just... lands heavy.

That's vague, I know. Sorry. I don't usually talk about myself like this.

**Minister**: No apology needed — and that's not vague at all. You just said something really true: there's a difference between what faith *is* and what people are *confident* it should look like. And you're tired of the weight of that certainty landing on you.

That's honest. I'm glad you said it.

Here's what I'm hearing: you *do* know the Lord is faithful — you felt Him this morning, thick and real. But somewhere in your life, someone's version of what that's supposed to mean for you is making it harder to just... be tired and seen at the same time. Like the "right" response has already been decided, and you're supposed to fit into it.

Can I ask — and this is genuinely open, no "right answer" — what would it feel like if someone just let you be tired *and* held by God at the same time, without explaining what either one means?

**Seeker**: *quiet for a second*

...That would feel like a lot, actually. In a good way. 

I don't know if I'd trust it right away, I'll be honest. I'd probably be waiting for the "but here's what the Spirit is saying" part, or the "this is your season of pressing through" — you know what I mean? I've heard that playlist so many times I know every track.

And I love my church. I love my people. But sometimes faith gets turned into a performance review and I didn't realize how much I'd been grading myself until just now saying that out loud.

*pauses*

There's more underneath that. Stuff I don't really talk about. The Lord is faithful, but — I've had moments where I really needed to believe that and it was just... hard to. And I felt like I couldn't say that to anyone without someone immediately trying to fix it or tell me my faith wasn't strong enough.

I'm saying more than I planned to. 😅

**Minister**: Keep going. This is the realest thing you've said, and it matters.

You're not saying too much — you're saying what's actually true. And I want you to know: the fact that you *couldn't* say it to the people around you, that you'd get the "fix it" or "your faith isn't strong enough" response — that's not a sign that something is wrong with you. That's a sign that the space wasn't safe enough to be honest about the hardest part of faith, which is *when it's hard*.

Jesus had people around Him too — people who loved Him, people who believed. But when He was in the garden sweating blood, He didn't perform strength He didn't have. He said it plain: "My soul is overwhelmed with sorrow to the point of death" (Matthew 26:38). He let people see the weight.

And the people who tried to "fix" His sorrow — they weren't the ones who got it. The ones who just *stayed* — that's what mattered.

So here's what I want to know, and I'm asking because I genuinely want to understand: when you say there were moments you really needed to believe He was faithful and it was hard — are you still carrying that? Is that part of what's underneath the tiredness today? Or has something shifted?

**Seeker**: *long pause*

Yeah. I'm still carrying it.

Two years ago I lost a baby. Early, so people thought — I don't know, like it counted less maybe. Nobody said that exactly but you could feel it in how fast they moved on. And I was standing up front leading worship two weeks later because that's what you do, that's who I am, and people were being blessed and I was smiling and inside I was just... I was so angry. At God. And I couldn't tell a single person that without feeling like I was going to get a prayer meeting called on my behalf for my unbelief.

I've never actually typed that out before.

The Lord is faithful — I still believe that, I do. But I have sat with that question of *where were you* for two years and I haven't had anywhere safe to put it. And everybody meant well. They really did. But "God needed another angel" almost made me walk out of a room one time. Almost.

*small, tired laugh*

So yeah. That's underneath the tiredness.

**Minister**: I'm so glad you said that. I'm sorry — actually sorry, not the quick kind — that you carried that alone for two years. That you stood there leading and no one saw the anger. That the space that was supposed to hold you offered platitudes instead.

That rage at God? It's not unbelief. It's love. It's the sound of someone who believed He *should* be there, so the absence broke something. A person who didn't care wouldn't be angry — they'd just be gone.

And I need to say this plainly: "God needed another angel" is not what happened to your baby. That's someone's attempt to comfort using words that don't fit reality, and it's small comfort for a real loss. Your baby mattered. The life you carried mattered. Two weeks or twenty weeks — it all counted. The fact that other people moved on fast doesn't change what you lost.

Here's what I'm sitting with, though: you still believe the Lord is faithful. After two years of carrying "*where were you*" — you didn't leave. You kept leading. You kept showing up. That's not performance; that's something deeper. But I'm wondering: have you ever actually *asked* Him that question out loud, in a space where you didn't have to manage anyone else's comfort? Where you could be angry without it being read as doubt?

Because He can handle your anger. He actually — He invites it. The Psalms are *full* of it.
