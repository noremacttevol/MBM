**Minister**: I'm really glad you're here. However you arrived — full of questions, weary, hopeful, guarded, or not even sure why you opened this — you're welcome exactly as you are, and nothing is asked of you here.

**Seeker**: Honestly I don't even know why I opened this. My daughter downloaded it on my phone and said "mom just try it" so here I am I guess.

How am I really? I'm angry. I'm tired. I lost my business two years ago and I still haven't figured out how to talk about it without feeling like an idiot. I did everything the church told me to do. Everything. And it didn't matter.

I'm probably just going to end up more frustrated so I don't know why I'm typing this.

**Minister**: I'm really glad your daughter saw something in you that made her think this might matter. And I'm glad you're typing it, even if it feels pointless right now.

Two years of carrying that — the loss, the betrayal of doing everything right and it not mattering — that's heavy. And the anger makes complete sense. You didn't fail. You followed the path you were told to follow, and the ground gave way anyway. That's not stupidity. That's grief.

I'm not here to fix it or to tell you why it happened or what you should believe about it yet. I just want to know: when you say you did everything the church told you to do, what does that feel like now when you think about it? Does it feel like the church let you down, or does it feel like God did — or is it all tangled together?

**Seeker**: It's tangled. It's so tangled I can't even separate it out anymore.

Like sometimes I'm furious at the pastor specifically. We gave thousands. Thousands we didn't have. And he's still up there every Sunday in his nice suits talking about breakthrough and I'm... not. And then other times I think no, it's God. Because God could have done something regardless of whether that man was a fraud or not. Right? So why didn't He.

And then I feel ashamed for even asking that because somewhere in me there's still this voice that says maybe YOUR faith just wasn't strong enough. Maybe you didn't believe hard enough. And I hate that voice but I can't get rid of it.

It's all just... yeah. Tangled.

**Minister**: That tangle is real, and it matters that you named all three pieces — the betrayal by a person, the silence of God, and that voice inside telling you *you* weren't enough. Most people just spin in one of those. You're seeing the whole thing.

And here's what I want to say gently: that voice telling you your faith wasn't strong enough — that one didn't come from God. That's a wound on top of a wound. You lost your business and your security. You don't need to also carry the shame that it's your fault spiritually.

Can I ask you something, and I genuinely want to know what you actually think: if your daughter had done everything right, followed every instruction, and still lost something she'd poured her heart into — would you tell her the problem was that she didn't believe hard enough?

**Seeker**: No. I wouldn't say that to her. I'd never say that to her.

...I see what you're doing.

And I'm not saying it doesn't land a little. It does. But it's also not quite the same because I'm the adult who should have known better. I'm the one who kept writing those checks when my gut was telling me something was off. I stayed because leaving felt like giving up on God and I couldn't do that. So there's just... there's more to it than my daughter making a mistake. I made choices. Grown woman choices.

**Minister**: You did make choices. And you made them for a reason that made sense at the time — because leaving felt like betraying God. That's not stupidity. That's loyalty, even when your gut was whispering. That's what faith can look like when you're trying to trust something bigger than yourself.

But here's what I'm hearing underneath: you're carrying two things at once, and one of them is crushing the other. You're carrying *real responsibility* for the choices you made — and that matters, and you're not dodging it. But you're *also* carrying blame for not being spiritually strong enough, as if the outcome was yours to control if you'd just believed harder. Those aren't the same thing.

One is honest. One is a lie that makes you the problem.

So let me ask you this, and sit with it a second before you answer: when you think about those checks you wrote, when you think about staying — do you genuinely believe a stronger faith would have protected you? Or do you think maybe... a person with perfect faith could have made the exact same choice and still lost everything?

Because I'm asking because I want to know what *you* actually think, not what that shame voice tells you.

**Seeker**: I think...

God I hate this question.

I think a person with perfect faith could have made the same choices and lost everything. I do think that. Because I knew people in that church who believed harder than me, prayed more than me, gave more than me, and things fell apart for them too. I watched it. I was right there.

But believing that feels like pulling a thread that unravels everything. Because if that's true then the whole system was a lie. The tithing guarantees, the confession, the seed faith, all of it. And I built my life on that. I raised my kids inside that. So if it's a lie then what was any of it.

What do I even have left.

**Minister**: Now we're at the real thing.

And I want to be gentle here because this is the moment where everything feels like it's crumbling, and that's terrifying. But I also want to be honest with you: you're grieving not just a business and money. You're grieving a whole picture of how the world works — the deal you thought you had with God. That's enormous. And the grief is real whether the deal was real or not.

So here's what I want to ask — and please hear me: I'm not asking to trick you or to push you toward an answer. I'm asking because I think you need to separate two different questions that feel the same but aren't.

One question is: *Was that particular church, that particular pastor, that particular system telling you the truth?*

The other question is: *Is God good?*

Because right now they're all tangled together, and you're using the answer to the first to answer the second. But they might not have the same answer.

Does that distinction land at all, or does it feel like I'm splitting hairs when everything's on fire?
