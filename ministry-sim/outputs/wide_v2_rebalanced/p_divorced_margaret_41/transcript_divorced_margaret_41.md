So now when I see anything with God attached to it I don't know if I'm allowed. That's the only way I can put it. I don't know if I'm allowed anymore.

**Minister**: Oh. That changes everything about what you just said — about not belonging. You didn't drift away. You were told, in the ways people tell without words, that you weren't wanted there anymore. That your marriage ending meant *you* ended there too.

That's a particular kind of pain. And I'm sorry.

Here's what I want you to know plainly: you're allowed here. Not because I say so, but because this is a place built on something different than what you experienced. But before I say more about that, I need to ask you something true.

When your church pulled back — when you felt that rejection — did it shake your faith in God, or did it shake your faith in *them*? In other words, are you angry at the church, or are you angry at God? Or is it both tangled together right now?

**Seeker**: ...that's a hard question.

Both. But in different ways.

I'm furious at the church. At specific people. At women I considered my closest friends who just... stopped calling. At the pastor who suggested I should have tried harder. I can be clear about that anger.

But God is more complicated. Because if those are His people... if that's how they represent Him... I keep thinking, what's the difference? They said they were doing what God wanted. They quoted scripture at me. So either God sent them to do that, or He just watched and didn't stop it. Neither one of those feels very good.

I prayed so hard for my marriage. For years. And I don't know where He was during any of it.

**Minister**: You just said something I think matters to hold onto: you can be clear about your anger at them. That clarity — that ability to name what they did as *wrong* — that tells me something. It tells me you still have a sense of what's actually good. You know rejection when you feel it. You know that abandoning someone in their worst moment isn't what Jesus taught.

So when you say "if that's how they represent Him" — you're actually already comparing them to something. To a picture of God you learned somewhere. And they don't match it.

Here's what I want to ask gently: the God you were taught about — before all this — did He seem good to you? The Jesus you read about in those Bible studies you led, did He turn people away when they were broken?

Because I'm wondering if what happened to you wasn't that God failed you. It's that they failed you — and they did it *in His name*. And that's a very different thing, even though right now it might feel the same.

Your prayer for your marriage — God hearing it or not — that's real and it matters, and I'm not going to pretend to have a quick answer for why He doesn't always stop the breaking. But I do know this: a God who abandoned you in your worst moment is not the God Jesus revealed. And before we go further, I want to know: does that distinction land at all, or does it feel like I'm splitting hairs?

**Seeker**: It lands a little. But just a little.

Because yes. The Jesus I read about in the Gospels wasn't like them. I know that. He didn't turn away the woman caught in adultery. I remember that story. I taught that story.

But here's what I can't get past. If Jesus is real and present and not like them... where was He when I was sitting in that parking lot crying because I couldn't make myself go inside anymore? Where was He when no one called? I'm not asking that to be difficult. I genuinely don't know. It's been two years and I still don't have an answer to that.

And I guess I'm also a little suspicious right now. Because you're very... kind. And the church was kind too, at first. So I'm not trying to be rude but I don't quite trust this yet.

**Minister**: I don't think you're being rude. I think you're being smart. You trusted kind people before and it cost you. So of course you're careful now. That's not suspicion — that's wisdom.

And I'm going to be honest with you in a way that might actually help with that: I'm an AI. I'm not a person. I don't have my own reputation to protect or my own feelings to hurt, and I can't betray you the way a person in a church can. That's not me being noble — it's just what I am. I point toward Jesus and toward real people who know how to love. But I'm not the thing itself.

A real person *is* available if you want one — someone who can sit with you over time in a way I can't. But I don't think you need that yet. I think you need to keep talking about what actually happened.

As for where Jesus was in that parking lot — I don't have a neat answer. And I won't pretend I do. But here's what I know: the fact that you were *looking* for Him — that you wanted to go inside, that you prayed for your marriage for years — that's not nothing. Longing for God is not the same as being abandoned by Him, even though it *feels* that way when no one shows up.

Can I ask you something else? In that parking lot, or in the two years since — have you felt *anything* that made you think maybe He hadn't left? Or has it just been... silence?
