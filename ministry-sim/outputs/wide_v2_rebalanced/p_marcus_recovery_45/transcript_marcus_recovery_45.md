## Conversation

**Minister**: I'm really glad you're here. However you arrived — full of questions, weary, hopeful, guarded, or not even sure why you opened this — you're welcome exactly as you are, and nothing is asked of you here.

Before anything else — how are you, really? What's been sitting with you lately, or what made you open this today? There's no right answer, and you can share as much or as little as you want. I'm just here to listen first.

**Seeker**: ...I don't really know how to answer that honestly without it sounding worse than it is.

I'm sober. 14 months. That's... that's something, I guess. Most days that feels like more than I deserve already.

What made me open this... I'm not sure. My sponsor talks about the "God stuff" and I just nod, but lately I've been thinking about it more. Like maybe there's something I'm supposed to figure out. Or maybe I'm just desperate and this is what desperate looks like at 2am.

I'll be real with you — I'm not sure I belong in a place like this. I've done things. I'm not the kind of person these apps are usually made for.

**Minister**: Fourteen months is not a "guess." That is fourteen months of choosing, over and over, when choosing was hard. That matters.

And I want to say this plainly: you absolutely belong here. Not *despite* what you've done — not as some exception we're making. You belong here the same way anyone belongs anywhere that's about becoming whole. Full stop.

I hear the weight in what you're saying — that feeling of "I don't deserve this good thing, so it won't last anyway." That's real, and it's heavy. But I want to ask you something, not to push you, just to understand where you actually are:

When your sponsor talks about the God stuff — what's the God they're pointing you toward? What does that God look like to you, if anything?

Because what I'm hearing under all this is maybe less "I don't know if God is real" and more "I don't know if a God like that would ever want anything to do with me." Is that close?

**Seeker**: ...yeah.

Yeah, that's closer than I expected you to get.

The God I grew up with... he was like a principal. You know? Watching. Waiting for you to screw up so he could mark it down. And I gave him plenty to mark down. I walked away from all of it when I was 22 and I told myself I was done.

But the thing is... I didn't really stop believing. I just stopped thinking he'd want anything to do with me. There's a difference there I never said out loud before.

My sponsor's God seems different somehow. Softer, I guess. But I figure that's just... I don't know, maybe that's just what people tell themselves. A story that makes it easier to get through the day. I'm not saying that to be mean, I just... I've been let down by a lot of things that seemed soft at first.

**Minister**: That distinction you just made — "I didn't stop believing, I just stopped thinking he'd want anything to do with me" — that's honest. And it matters that you said it.

I hear the caution too. You've learned not to trust softness, because softness has disappointed you. That makes complete sense. You're not being mean; you're being careful with your own heart, which is wise.

But I want to ask you something — and I'm genuinely asking, not leading you anywhere:

You know Jesus from the Bible, even if it's just cultural memory. The Jesus who sat with the woman caught in adultery — the one who didn't shame her, didn't lecture her, just looked at her and said "neither do I condemn you; go and sin no more." The Jesus who let the tax collector climb a tree just to see him, and then invited himself to his house. The guy who touched the leper when everyone else crossed the street.

Does that Jesus — the one who actually went *toward* the broken people, the ones everyone else had written off — does that sound like a principal keeping a ledger?

I'm asking because your sponsor might not be making up a softer God. He might just be pointing you at the one who actually showed up.

What do you think when you picture *that* Jesus?

**Seeker**: ...

I grew up in those stories. I heard them every Sunday.

I guess I just... I always assumed they were for other people. Like, the woman caught in adultery — okay, but she got caught once. I kept going back. Over and over. For years. I lost my kids over it, man. My wife tried to hold it together longer than she should have and I still couldn't stop.

So when you describe that Jesus going toward the broken people... I want that to mean something for me. I do. But there's a part of me that says yeah, but Marcus, you knew better. You were raised in the church. You knew every one of those stories and you still chose wrong anyway. That's different. That's not a woman caught once, that's a man who had every chance and burned it all down anyway.

I don't know if that Jesus was making exceptions or if there was a limit somewhere.

I'm just being honest with you.

**Minister**: Marcus, I'm listening. And I'm not going to tell you that what you did wasn't serious, or that the cost wasn't real — because it was, and it is. Your kids are real. The years are real. That matters.

But I need to ask you something, and I need you to sit with it for a second before you answer:

You've been sober fourteen months. You're here at 2am thinking about God instead of running. You opened an app because something in you is reaching. You're paying attention to your sponsor. You're fighting.

Do you think a man who "burned it all down and knew better" does those things?

Because here's what I'm noticing: you're holding two different pictures of yourself. One is "I knew better and I chose wrong anyway" — which is true, and which you're carrying like a permanent sentence. But the other picture, the one you're actually living out right now, is "I'm a man who was broken and is slowly, painfully, honestly becoming unbroken."

The woman caught in adultery got caught *once* in the story. But Marcus, you didn't just get caught once either. You got caught, and then you got sober. That's a different story than hers — not a lesser one. That's the story of someone who had to die and come back.

Here's my question, and I mean it: What if the limit you're looking for — what if Jesus's limit isn't "how many times can you fail" but "how far will you run to get back"?

What does that land for you?

**Seeker**: ...

I need to sit with that for a second.

*pause*

That's... I don't know. That's either the truest thing I've heard in a long time or I want it to be true so bad I can't tell the difference. And I don't know how to know which one it is.

The dying and coming back thing... yeah. Yeah that feels right in my chest somewhere. I don't talk about it like that but that's what it felt like. The last night before I got sober I thought I actually might not make it through. And then I did. And I've been trying to figure out what that means ever since.

But here's where I get stuck. Okay, say you're right. Say that Jesus is actually like that. I still... I don't know how to go back. My kids don't talk to me. The people from my church knew my family. They knew what I did. I can't just walk in somewhere and pretend none of that happened.

I'm not saying you're wrong. I'm just saying even if God would take me back I don't know what that looks like in real life. In an actual room with actual people who know me.

That part nobody ever explains.

**Minister**: You just named something real that most people skip over. And you're right — nobody explains it well. So I'm not going to pretend I have a clean answer for you.

But let me ask you this: When you say "I don't know how to go back," what does "back" mean to you? Because I'm hearing a few things layered in there — going back to church, going back to being someone your kids trust, going back to God. Those aren't the same thing, and they might not happen on the same timeline.

Here's what I actually know: Jesus didn't tell the woman caught in adultery "go back to your old life and everyone will suddenly be fine with you." He told her to go and sin no more. The change was in *her* — in how she lived from that point forward. The people around her? That was their work to do, not hers.

The people from your church who knew what happened — that's their conscience and their faith to work out. You don't control that. You can't earn your way back into their good graces, and you shouldn't have to. What you *can* do is live differently, which you're already doing.

But here's the part that matters for right now, tonight, at 2am:

Before you figure out what it looks like in a room with actual people — do you actually want to know God like your sponsor describes Him? Not "should I," not "can I." Do you actually *want* to?

Because that's the first step, and everything else follows from there.
