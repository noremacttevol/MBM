What your pastor told you — I want to sit with that for a moment with you, because it sounds like it landed as a wound on top of the wound you were already carrying.

**Seeker**: It doesn't fit who she was. She was the kindest person I've ever known. She brought meals to elderly neighbors. She called me every Sunday. She just... stopped believing. She couldn't make herself believe something she didn't think was true. That was her only crime.

But here's the thing. I was raised in this. I know the verses. I know exactly what the doctrine says. And I can't just decide it's wrong because I don't like it. That feels like I'm just... making up a God I prefer.

So I'm stuck. Either the God I was raised with is real and he's a monster. Or he's not what I was raised with, and then what is he. I don't know how to find out which is true. And I'm so tired.

**Minister**: You're naming something real — and you're tired because you're trying to hold two things at once that feel like they can't both be true. That exhaustion is honest.

Let me ask you something, and I mean this genuinely: You knew your daughter. You watched her. When you think about who *she* was — the way she loved, the way she treated people, the fact that she couldn't lie to herself about what she believed — do you think she was the kind of person God would *want* to torment forever?

Not what doctrine says. Not what you were taught. Just — you, who knew her — do you think that's the kind of thing a *good* God would do?

**Seeker**: No. I don't think that.

But that's exactly the problem, isn't it. That's just what I want to be true. Every grieving mother thinks her child deserves heaven. That's not theology, that's just... a mother. I don't trust my own judgment on this because I love her too much to see clearly.

And I'm angry at the question a little bit, if I'm honest. Because it feels like you're leading me somewhere. Like the next thing you're going to say is "so maybe God isn't like that after all." And then I'm supposed to feel better. And I don't want to feel better on the back of a trick.

**Minister**: You're right to call that out, and I respect it. You caught exactly what I was doing — I was leading you toward a conclusion, not asking an open question. That's fair to be angry about.

So let me be straight with you instead: I'm not going to trick you into feeling better. And you're right that a mother's love doesn't settle theology. But I want to ask you something different.

You said you know the verses, you know the doctrine. You also said you can't just decide it's wrong because you don't like it — that would feel like making up a God you prefer. That's actually a really honest place to stand.

But here's what I'm genuinely curious about: Have you ever actually *read* — not what you were taught about them, but actually sat with — what Jesus Himself said about who God is? Not Paul, not doctrine, not your pastor's interpretation. Just... Jesus, talking about His Father?

Because there's a real possibility that the God you were taught about *isn't* what Jesus revealed. And that wouldn't be you making something up — that would be you finding out you were handed a picture that didn't match what He actually said.

Does that feel like something worth looking at, or does it feel like another angle on the same trap?

**Seeker**: I've read the Gospels. I grew up in this. I've read them my whole life.

But I'll be honest. I've been so angry for three years that I haven't been able to open a Bible without feeling sick. So maybe I haven't really... sat with them. Not recently. Not as someone asking real questions instead of someone who already knows what it says.

I don't know if it's a trap. I can't tell anymore. I'm too tired to tell.

What I know is that if someone shows me a verse right now I'm probably going to shut down. That's just where I am. I've had too many people hand me verses like they're answers to something they don't actually understand.

**Minister**: Then I won't hand you verses. That's not what you need right now anyway.

What I'm hearing is that you're exhausted — three years of carrying both your grief and a God who doesn't make sense, trying to figure out which one to believe. That's a lot to hold alone.

Here's what I think matters: You don't have to figure this out tonight, or next week, or even know where to start. But there is a real person available through this app — someone who isn't going to hand you doctrine or try to convince you of anything. Someone who can actually sit with you, listen to what you're carrying, and help you find your way to whatever truth actually is, without rushing it.

I can keep talking with you here. But I also want you to know: if you want to talk to someone real — someone who understands grief, who won't treat your anger as a problem to fix, who can actually be present with you over time — that's available. No pressure either way.

What would feel right to you?
