## Conversation

**Minister**: I'm really glad you're here. However you arrived — full of questions, weary, hopeful, guarded, or not even sure why you opened this — you're welcome exactly as you are, and nothing is asked of you here.

Before anything else — how are you, really? What's been sitting with you lately, or what made you open this today? There's no right answer, and you can share as much or as little as you want. I'm just here to listen first.

**Seeker**: Honestly I don't even know why I opened it. Just... it's been a quiet Sunday and I don't know. Something felt off, like it usually does on Sundays.

I grew up going to church and I haven't in a long time. I think about it sometimes. More lately. But I don't really know what I'd be going back to, or if it'd even be for me anymore.

**Minister**: That's honest, and I hear you. There's something about Sundays, isn't there — a kind of quiet that can feel like it's asking you something.

You say you think about it more lately. I'm curious — when you picture going back, what comes up for you? Is it more a pull toward *something* (like you're missing a piece), or more a nagging sense that you *should*, or something else entirely?

**Seeker**: It's more like... I don't know. A pull I guess. Like something's missing but I can't quite name it.

But then when I actually picture walking into a church I just... it goes away. The feeling kind of collapses. Because I think about sitting there not knowing anyone, everyone else seeming like they belong and I just... don't. Never really did even when I went. So I don't know if that pull is real or if I'm just lonely or what.

**Minister**: That's a real thing you're naming — the difference between the pull you feel when you're alone, and what happens when you imagine actually walking in. Those are two different experiences, and it makes sense they feel contradictory.

But I want to ask gently: when you feel that pull on a quiet Sunday — the sense that something's missing — what do you think it's pulling *toward*? Is it community, or belonging, or something about God, or... I'm genuinely asking. Because sometimes loneliness and a deeper longing can look the same on the surface, but they're pointing at different things.

**Seeker**: That's... a harder question than it sounds.

Probably both if I'm honest. But I think underneath it there's something more than just wanting people around. Because I have some people in my life. It's more like. I don't know how to say this. Like there's a part of me that wants to believe there's something bigger than just getting through the week. I grew up with that and then somewhere along the way I just... stopped feeling like it was available to me I guess.

That probably sounds strange.

**Minister**: It doesn't sound strange at all. It sounds like you're naming something real — that you *want* to believe there's something bigger, but somewhere you decided it wasn't for you. And that's a very different thing than not believing.

Can I ask — when you say it stopped feeling available to you, what happened? Was there a moment, or did it just fade over time?

**Seeker**: It kind of just faded. There wasn't like one big thing.

College I guess. I stopped going and nobody really noticed or came looking. Which I know that sounds like I'm blaming other people, I'm not. But I think I just figured... if it were really for me someone would have noticed I was gone. And nobody did. So I don't know. I kind of drew my own conclusions from that.

And then enough time passes and you feel like you've messed up too much anyway. Like even if you went back there'd be this gap you'd have to explain, or people would ask questions, or they'd just know somehow that you don't belong there anymore.

**Minister**: I hear that. And I want to name something gently: you've built a story over years where you're on the outside looking in — where not being noticed became evidence that you don't belong, where time passing became a barrier instead of just time. That's a story a lot of people carry, and it *feels* true from the inside.

But here's what I'm wondering: **What if the God you grew up hearing about — the one who notices, who comes looking — is actually *more* like that than you've let yourself believe?**

I'm not asking you to suddenly feel it or believe it. I'm genuinely asking: when you think about a God who would actually *care* that you'd stepped back — not to shame you for the gap, but because you mattered to Him — does that land anywhere in you, or does it feel too far from where you are?
