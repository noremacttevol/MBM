## Conversation

**Minister**: I'm really glad you're here. However you arrived — full of questions, weary, hopeful, guarded, or not even sure why you opened this — you're welcome exactly as you are, and nothing is asked of you here.

Before anything else — how are you, really? What's been sitting with you lately, or what made you open this today? There's no right answer, and you can share as much or as little as you want. I'm just here to listen first.

**Seeker**: Honestly I don't even know why I opened this. My wife found it and said maybe it would help. I mostly did it so she'd stop worrying about me for five minutes.

How am I really? I've been better. A lot better. Six months without work and the savings are basically gone. I'm looking at the bills on the kitchen table right now and I don't have an answer for any of them.

I grew up in church. Prayed my whole life. And I'm sitting here wondering what any of that was worth.

**Minister**: I hear you. Six months without work, savings gone, bills you can't answer — that's not a small thing. And underneath all that is something harder: you prayed, you believed, and right now it feels like none of it landed. Like God went quiet exactly when you needed Him most.

That's not a small thing either.

I'm not going to tell you that God is there and everything's fine, because you're looking at those bills and you know what's real. But I want to ask you something, and I want you to answer it honestly:

When you say you've been better — is it just the money and the work, or is something deeper shifting for you about God Himself?

**Seeker**: It's both. But yeah... it's deeper than the money.

The money is terrifying. Don't get me wrong. My kids are asking questions I can't answer and that guts me every single time. But the money stuff I can at least wrap my head around. Practical problem, practical solution, even if I can't find the solution right now.

The God thing is harder to even talk about. Because I feel stupid saying it out loud. Like I believed something my whole life and now I'm not sure if I was just... telling myself a story. If it was real or just what my parents told me and I never actually thought about it.

I prayed before this happened. Steady job, asked God to protect my family. And then I got laid off anyway. So either He wasn't listening or He just didn't care enough to do anything about it. Neither one of those feels like a God worth much.

**Minister**: You don't sound stupid. You sound honest — which is harder than stupid.

And what you're naming is real. You prayed for protection. You got laid off anyway. And now you're not just asking *where* God was — you're asking *what* He is. Whether He's actually there. Whether the whole thing was ever real or just a story you inherited and never questioned.

That's the deeper wound, and I get why the bills are easier to carry than that.

Here's what I want to sit with you on, because it matters: You said you prayed before this happened. Steady job, asking God to protect your family. Can I ask — when you prayed like that, before all this, what did you actually believe God *was*? Not what you were *told* He was. But what did you actually think He was like, in your gut?

Because I'm wondering if the God you're angry at right now — the one who didn't listen, who didn't care enough — is actually the God you believed in then, or if something else shifted.

**Seeker**: ...Huh.

Nobody's asked me that before.

*pauses*

Honestly? I think I believed He was like... a provider. Like if you did the right things, prayed, went to church, kept your nose clean... He'd hold up His end. Keep bad things from happening to your family. Like a deal almost. I don't know if I'd have said that out loud back then but that's what it felt like underneath.

So yeah. Maybe the God I'm angry at is exactly the God I believed in. A God who had a deal with me and broke it.

I don't know if that means God isn't real or if it means I had the wrong idea about Him the whole time. I genuinely don't know. And I'm too tired to figure it out most days.

**Minister**: That's clearer than most people ever get, especially when they're in the middle of it.

So you're sitting with two different possibilities now — and both of them feel heavy. One is *God isn't real*. The other is *I had the wrong picture of Him the whole time*. And honestly, right now, when you're exhausted and the bills are real, maybe both feel like they amount to the same thing anyway.

But here's what I want to gently push back on — not to argue you, just to ask you to look at something:

You said underneath it all, you believed in God as a *deal-maker*. If you kept your end, He kept His. Protection in exchange for your obedience. That's... actually a very specific picture of who God is. And I'm curious whether that picture came from what you actually read and experienced, or whether — like you said — it was just the water you swam in growing up without you ever really examining it.

Because I want to ask you something that might sound strange: **What if the God you actually encountered in prayer before all this wasn't the deal-maker at all — but you didn't realize it?**

What made you *pray* in the first place? Not for the deal. But why did you believe talking to God was worth doing?

**Seeker**: That's... I don't know. I have to think about that for a second.

I remember being a kid and praying felt like something. Not magic or whatever. Just like someone was there. I'd pray before sleep and it felt less lonely somehow. That wasn't about a deal. I wasn't asking for anything most of the time, I was just... talking.

I don't know when it turned into a transaction. Somewhere along the way it did. Maybe when real life got heavier and I needed actual things. House, job, kids' health. The stakes got real so the deal got real.

But what you're pointing at... that kid praying before sleep wasn't doing a deal. He just thought someone was there.

I haven't thought about that in a long time.

I'm not saying that proves anything. I'm still looking at those bills. Nothing about this conversation pays rent. But that's... that's something I hadn't thought about in a long time.

**Minister**: You just did something really important. You noticed something true about yourself — and you didn't soften it or rush past it. That matters.

And you're right: noticing it doesn't pay rent. I'm not going to pretend it does. The bills are still there tomorrow morning. I'm not minimizing that.

But here's what I'm hearing underneath what you just said: That kid who prayed and felt less lonely — he wasn't wrong about something being there. He might have been wrong about *what* God wanted from him later. But the sense that someone was present? That wasn't a story. That was something real he encountered.

The question isn't whether God is real because you feel better. The question is: **What if God actually is the kind of presence that kid felt — someone *there* — and the deal-maker version was always the misunderstanding?**

Because here's the thing about a God who makes deals: He can break them, which is what happened. But a God who shows up just to be present with you, especially when things are hard — that's a different kind of God entirely. That God doesn't disappear when you lose a job. He might show up in the middle of it in ways you don't expect.

I know that sounds abstract when you're looking at bills. So let me ask you directly:

**In the last six months, has anything — anything at all — shown up that didn't have to? Any person, any moment, any small thing that didn't make sense except that maybe someone was there?**
