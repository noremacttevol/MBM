[narrator] Jesus told a story about ten young women waiting for a wedding.

[narrator] In those days, a whole village would wait for the bridegroom to come, late in the evening, and lead everyone in to the feast.

[narrator] So ten young women took their oil lamps and went out into the dusk to meet him.

[narrator] Five of them were wise. Along with their lamps, they each carried a small jar of extra oil.

[narrator] The other five were foolish. They brought their lamps — but no extra oil at all.

[narrator] The bridegroom was delayed. Hour after hour slipped by, and one by one, all ten women grew drowsy and fell asleep.

[jesus] Behold, the bridegroom cometh; go ye out to meet him.

[narrator] Then, at midnight, a cry rang out: The bridegroom is coming! Come out to meet him!

[narrator] They all woke and reached for their lamps. The wise trimmed theirs, and they burned warm and bright.

[narrator] But the foolish looked down in dismay. Their lamps were sputtering out — they had no oil left.

[jesus] Give us of your oil; for our lamps are gone out.

[narrator] Please, they cried out to the others — give us some of your oil!

[jesus] Not so; lest there be not enough for us and you: but go ye rather to them that sell, and buy for yourselves.

[narrator] But the wise couldn't. There isn't enough for all of us, they said. Hurry — go and buy your own.

[narrator] And while the foolish rushed off into the dark to find oil, the bridegroom arrived.

[narrator] The ones who were ready went in with him to the wedding feast. And the door was shut.

[jesus] Lord, Lord, open to us.

[jesus] Verily I say unto you, I know you not.

[narrator] Later the others came back, knocking. Lord, they called, open the door for us! But the answer came from inside: I do not know you.

[narrator] Then Jesus told them why he had shared this story.

[jesus] Watch therefore, for ye know neither the day nor the hour wherein the Son of man cometh.

[narrator] The oil is the one thing you cannot borrow at the last minute — a heart that is truly ready, a faith that is really your own, a lamp you have kept burning.

[narrator] And here is the good news: the door is still open now. Tonight, your lamp can be filled. He is worth being ready for.

[narrator] You cannot borrow someone else's oil. Is your own lamp burning — are you ready to meet him?
