[narrator] Three thousand years ago a shepherd-king wrote a song about being shepherded himself. It starts like this:

[scripture] The LORD is my shepherd; I shall not want.

[narrator] The Lord takes care of me. There is nothing I need that I won't have. That is the whole psalm in one line — everything after it is David showing his work.

[scripture] He maketh me to lie down in green pastures: he leadeth me beside the still waters.

[narrator] He leads me to quiet water and green places to rest.

[scripture] He restoreth my soul:

[narrator] He puts my life back together.

[scripture] he leadeth me in the paths of righteousness for his name's sake.

[narrator] He guides me down the right paths, for His name's sake — not because I earned the guiding, but because of who He is.

[scripture] Yea, though I walk through the valley of the shadow of death, I will fear no evil: for thou art with me; thy rod and thy staff they comfort me.

[narrator] Even in the darkest valley, I'm not afraid. And notice what changes right there: up to now David has been saying He. In the valley he starts saying You. He gets closer to the Shepherd in the dark, not further away.

[scripture] Thou preparest a table before me in the presence of mine enemies:

[narrator] You set a table for me right in front of my enemies.

[scripture] thou anointest my head with oil; my cup runneth over.

[narrator] You honor me;

[scripture] Surely goodness and mercy shall follow me all the days of my life:

[narrator] Your goodness and Your mercy will chase me down every day I have.

[scripture] and I will dwell in the house of the LORD for ever.

[narrator] And I'll be home with You forever.

[narrator] The Shepherd who leads, restores, and walks the dark valley with you is the same One who invites you home. Let Him lead.
