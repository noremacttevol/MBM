[narrator] Israel had walked out of Egypt on a miracle — a sea split open, bread fallen from the sky, water struck out of a rock. But the wilderness was long, and their patience wore through.

[narrator] On the hard road around Edom, tired and discouraged, they turned on the very God who had rescued them, and on Moses.

[scripture] Wherefore have ye brought us up out of Egypt to die in the wilderness? for there is no bread, neither is there any water; and our soul loatheth this light bread.

[narrator] They had bread from heaven every morning — and called it worthless. And the wilderness they blamed for their misery was about to show them what a real danger looked like.

[scripture] And the LORD sent fiery serpents among the people, and they bit the people; and much people of Israel died.

[narrator] The bites spread through the camp. And the same people who had cursed God came running back to Moses, broken.

[scripture] We have sinned, for we have spoken against the LORD, and against thee; pray unto the LORD, that he take away the serpents from us.

[narrator] So Moses prayed for the people who had just turned on him. And the LORD answered — but not the way anyone expected.

[god] Make thee a fiery serpent, and set it upon a pole: and it shall come to pass, that every one that is bitten, when he looketh upon it, shall live.

[narrator] No cure to brew. No ritual to perform. No strength left to muster. Just a shape of bronze lifted on a pole, and one thing left to do — look.

[scripture] And Moses made a serpent of brass, and put it upon a pole, and it came to pass, that if a serpent had bitten any man, when he beheld the serpent of brass, he lived.

[narrator] A dying man didn't have to earn it, or explain it, or even understand it. He only had to trust it enough to lift his eyes toward it.

[narrator] Fourteen hundred years later, Jesus reached back and picked this exact moment to explain his own cross.

[jesus] And as Moses lifted up the serpent in the wilderness, even so must the Son of man be lifted up: That whosoever believeth in him should not perish, but have eternal life.

[narrator] The whole camp was dying, and their healing hung on a pole. All they had to do was look. Look, and live.
