**CHARACTER LOCKS (kept consistent across every still they appear in):**
- **The landowner (s1, s2, s3, s7, s8)** — a dignified, prosperous but warm
  Judean vineyard owner: an older man, neat gray-streaked beard, a good-quality
  deep-dyed russet-and-cream robe with a fine mantle (clearly the owner, not a
  laborer), a leather coin purse at his belt. His face IS shown — generous, calm.
- **The steward / foreman (s4)** — a middle-aged man in a plain sturdy tunic with a
  wide money pouch and a small tally board, standing at the pay table.
- **The first-hired laborers (s1, s5, s6, s7)** — strong working men, sun-darkened,
  dusty, plain undyed rough-wool work tunics belted with rope, sleeves pushed up.
  One lead worker (short black beard, sweat-streaked) is the one who murmurs.
- **The eleventh-hour laborers (s3, s5)** — a smaller, more ragged and idle-looking
  group, hesitant, then astonished and grateful; plainer, poorer clothes.
- **A penny (the KJV denarius)** — a single small silver coin, shown being handed
  over; one coin per worker (never a handful).
- **The vineyard** — a terraced hillside of trellised grapevines, heavy with dark
  fruit, ripening in warm light.

## s1-dawn-hire — Shot 1: hired at first light
[STILL STYLE BLOCK] A dusty village marketplace at dawn, the sky rose and gold. A
dignified older vineyard owner in a deep russet-and-cream robe with a fine mantle
and a coin purse at his belt is agreeing with a group of strong, dusty day-laborers
in rough undyed work tunics, clasping hands on the deal; behind them a terraced
hillside vineyard of trellised grapevines glows in the early light as the first
workers shoulder their tools to head into the rows. One single continuous scene
painted edge to edge, 9:16 vertical.

## s2-marketplace-idle — Shot 2: still standing idle
[STILL STYLE BLOCK] The same marketplace a few hours later, the sun higher and hotter.
The same russet-robed vineyard owner has returned and is gesturing warmly toward his
vineyard, sending a fresh group of laborers who had been standing idle by the wall
with nothing to do. Some gather their bundles to go; the terraced vines rise behind.
Warm mid-morning light. One single continuous scene painted edge to edge, 9:16 vertical.

## s3-eleventh-hour — Shot 3: why stand ye here all the day idle
[STILL STYLE BLOCK] Late afternoon, the light going long and amber, only an hour of
daylight left. The russet-robed vineyard owner stands before a last small cluster of
poorer, more ragged idle men still lingering in the empty marketplace, speaking to
them kindly with an open hand, inviting them in; their faces lift with surprise and
hope. Long shadows, the vineyard glowing gold behind. One single continuous scene
painted edge to edge, 9:16 vertical.

## s4-evening-pay — Shot 4: call the labourers and give them their hire
[STILL STYLE BLOCK] Evening at the edge of the vineyard, warm lamplight and last blue
dusk. The owner's steward — a sturdy middle-aged foreman with a wide money pouch and
a small tally board — stands at a low wooden pay table calling the workers into a line
to be paid, the ragged last-hired men at the FRONT of the line and the tired dawn crew
waiting behind them. Baskets of harvested grapes at the edge. One single continuous
scene painted edge to edge, 9:16 vertical.

## s5-last-paid — Shot 5: the last receive a full day's wage
[STILL STYLE BLOCK] At the lamplit pay table, the steward presses a single bright
silver coin into the open palm of one of the ragged eleventh-hour laborers, whose
weathered face breaks into astonished, humbled gratitude; his fellow latecomers behind
him look at their own single coins in disbelief. Just one coin per man. Warm lamp glow
against the blue evening. One single continuous scene painted edge to edge, 9:16 vertical.

## s6-first-murmur — Shot 6: they murmured against the goodman of the house
[STILL STYLE BLOCK] The same pay table, moments later. The strong dawn laborers, dusty
and sweat-streaked from the long hot day, stare down at the single silver coin in their
own hands with faces darkening into indignation and complaint; the lead worker, short
black beard, throws out an angry hand toward the departing latecomers. The same one
coin sits in each calloused palm. Firelit night. One single continuous scene painted
edge to edge, 9:16 vertical.

## s7-friend-reply — Shot 7: friend, I do thee no wrong
[STILL STYLE BLOCK] The vineyard owner — the same older russet-robed figure — turns
to the angry lead laborer and answers him gently, one hand raised in calm, unhurried
generosity, no anger in his own face, only steadiness and kindness. The worker's fury
meets the owner's quiet goodness. Warm lamplight, the vines dark behind them. One
single continuous scene painted edge to edge, 9:16 vertical.

## s8-vineyard-dusk — Shot 8: so the last shall be first
[STILL STYLE BLOCK] The terraced vineyard under a deep golden-and-violet dusk. Laborers
of every kind — the ragged latecomers and the dusty dawn crew alike — are heading home
down the hillside path, each holding a single silver coin, the russet-robed owner
watching them go with an open, generous look. The heavy grape vines and the last light
glow over the whole hill. One single continuous scene painted edge to edge, 9:16 vertical.
