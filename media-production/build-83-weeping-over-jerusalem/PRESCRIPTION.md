**Row:** 83   **Shelf:** Everyone   **Scripture:** Luke 19:41–44
**Tier:** T2 Encounter   **Target length:** 80–120s   **Pictures:** 10–15 (planned: 8 — see Deviations)

## The impact read
- **The one thing:** God's grief over people who cannot see him is not anger — it's
  love that can see exactly what could have been.
- **The turn:** [s41 / n1b] the King everyone expects to celebrate stops and *weeps*
  instead. The reversal — triumph to tears — is the hinge.
- **Character shown:** tenderness. He cries over the city that's too busy to notice
  the peace standing at its gates.
- **Whose face carries it:** this is the rare case where **Jesus's own weeping face
  carries the beat** — the grief is his and there is no witness to hand it to. Keep
  it via master ref, reverent; go over-the-shoulder onto the city on his spoken line.
- **The card question falls out of:** "He sees you clearly. Don't look away." No change.

## Deviations from the band
Stepped to **8 pictures** (below the T2 band of 10–15) because the passage is a
single sustained moment — his tears over the city — and the graphic siege verses
(Luke 19:43–44: trenches, the city levelled, not one stone left) are deliberately
kept as the narrator's summary, not shown, to hold the build's milk-before-meat
tone. That leaves few distinct *new* visual moments; the weeping face (beat 4) is
meant to hold and breathe. Padding to 10 would mean inventing scenes the words
don't paint. Trusting the beats over the range.
