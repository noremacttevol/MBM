# STILL PROMPTS — Story Video #72: Calling Matthew (matthew-9_calling-matthew)

Matthew 9:9-13 (with Mark 2:14-17 in view). STILLS-ONLY (Law E) — twelve painted
stills, no motion clips.

CARE FLAGS: none — GREEN, plain milk (CONTENT-CARE.md). The "sinners" at the table are
ordinary human beings the town wrote off, never grotesque or villainous; the Pharisees
are sincere religious men, disapproving but not cartoon villains. Milk framing: this is
the gospel in one scene. Jesus walks up to the most hated man in town — a tax collector,
a Jew who got rich working for Rome and cheating his own neighbors — and says "Follow me,"
with no demand that he clean himself up first. Matthew gets up and leaves the money. Then
Jesus goes to his house and eats with a whole room of tax collectors and outcasts, and
when the religious men are scandalized he says the heart of everything: the doctor goes
where the sickness is — "I am not come to call the righteous, but sinners to repentance."
The WHY the whole video carries: he came FOR the disqualified, on purpose. Closing card is
an invitation, never a fear-question.

Jesus appears in FOUR shots — s3, s4, s6 and s9. In every one the camera is BEHIND him or
over his shoulder, the back of his head to us, his face never shown, never visible, at any
angle. The other stills have no divine figure.

JESUS LOCK (identical wording wherever he appears; project-wide look standard): a
first-century Middle-Eastern man, warm olive-brown skin, LONG dark brown-black hair past
the shoulders, one plain undyed off-white cream wool robe, bare-headed, no hood; no glow,
no halo, no rim-light; an ordinary solid man. The camera is always directly behind him or
over his shoulder and his face is never shown, never visible, at any angle.

MATTHEW LOCK (s1, s2, s3, s4, s5, s6, s10, s11) — the tax collector: a man of about forty,
clean and well-kept unlike a laborer, a neatly trimmed dark beard, in a finer muted
TEAL-GREEN tunic with a subtle darker woven border, a leather coin-purse at his belt,
clearly NOT cream or off-white. His face IS shown — guarded and isolated at the booth,
then astonished, then quietly glad. Consistent throughout.

PHARISEES LOCK (s8) — sincere religious men, NOT villains: two or three in fuller formal
wool robes of deep indigo-blue and dark maroon with a visible woven prayer-fringe at the

and disapproving, never sneering caricatures.

GUESTS LOCK (s6, s7, s10) — the "publicans and sinners": a mix of ordinary townsfolk the
town wrote off — a couple of other tax collectors, weathered laborers, one or two tired
older women — in worn dun, brown, ochre and faded red, clearly NONE in cream. Their faces
ARE shown, human and relieved and glad to be welcomed, never grotesque, never menacing.

DISCIPLES LOCK (s6) — a few plain fisherman-type disciples in earth tones of brown and
olive, clearly NOT cream, faces shown, at ease among the guests.
