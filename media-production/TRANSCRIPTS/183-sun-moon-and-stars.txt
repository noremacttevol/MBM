[narrator] When Paul wrote about the resurrection, people asked him what kind of body the dead could possibly rise with. He answered by pointing at the sky.

[narrator] Look up, he said. Not everything that shines shines the same way. There are different kinds of bodies, and different kinds of glory.

[scripture] There is one glory of the sun, and another glory of the moon, and another glory of the stars: for one star differeth from another star in glory.

[narrator] The sun has its own brightness. And no two stars burn quite alike. Then Paul said the astonishing part.

[scripture] So also is the resurrection of the dead. It is sown in corruption; it is raised in incorruption:

[narrator] That is what rising is like, he said. What goes into the ground breaks down; what comes back out never will again. The resurrection isn't one flat outcome. Like the lights above, there are glories — plural — and every one of them is a gift of light.

[narrator] The same God who hung the sun, the moon, and every different star is preparing a brightness for you.

[narrator] The heavens hold more than one kind of glory. Reach for the brightest — you were made for light.
