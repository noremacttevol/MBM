[narrator] The religious leaders were circling Jesus, demanding he say plainly who he claimed to be.

[narrator] Instead of backing down, he reached into their own scriptures — to a psalm where God calls mere men gods.

[god] I have said, Ye are gods; and all of you are children of the most High.

[narrator] His point was sharp: if scripture called men gods because God's word came to them, how could they condemn the one the Father set apart?

[jesus] Say ye of him, whom the Father hath sanctified, and sent into the world, Thou blasphemest; because I said, I am the Son of God?

[narrator] That was his answer. The Father himself set him apart and sent him into the world. Saying I am the Son of God was not blasphemy — it was the plain truth they would not test.

[narrator] He was not making himself a second God.

[narrator] He was showing them their own book exposed their logic.

[narrator] He stood on the scriptures they claimed to love. Come know him as he truly is.
