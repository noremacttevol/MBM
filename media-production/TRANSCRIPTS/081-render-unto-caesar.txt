[narrator] Some officials came to Jesus with a question built to trap him — flattering him first, so he'd let his guard down.

[scripture] Master, we know that thou art true, and carest for no man: for thou regardest not the person of men, but teachest the way of God in truth: Is it lawful to give tribute to Caesar, or not? Shall we give, or shall we not give?

[narrator] Is it lawful to pay taxes to Caesar, or not? Say yes, and the crowd turns on you. Say no, and Rome arrests you. There was no safe answer.

[narrator] Jesus saw straight through it.

[scripture] But he, knowing their hypocrisy, said unto them,

[jesus] Why tempt ye me? bring me a penny, that I may see it.

[narrator] They brought one — a small silver Roman coin. He held it up where everyone could see.

[jesus] Whose is this image and superscription?

[scripture] And they said unto him, Caesar's.

[narrator] Whose face is this, he asked them, and whose name. They said it was Caesar's. And with that, the trap they had built swung shut on them instead.

[scripture] And Jesus answering said unto them,

[jesus] Render to Caesar the things that are Caesar's, and to God the things that are God's.

[narrator] The coin bore Caesar's image, so give it back to Caesar. But you bear God's image — so the real question is what you owe to the One whose face you carry.

[narrator] The coin belonged to Caesar because it bore his face. You bear God's. What does that make you worth to him?
