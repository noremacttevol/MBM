[narrator] And then, through the spray and the dark, they saw something that made grown fishermen scream. A figure. Walking toward them. On top of the water. They cried out that it was a ghost — because nobody walks on the sea. But the voice that came back across the water was one they knew.

[narrator] Why did you doubt? Hear how he asked it. Not from the shore. Not after pulling him into the boat. From the hand already holding him. It isn't a scolding. It's a real question, from someone who had already caught him — as if to say: you were doing it. What made you stop trusting me?

[narrator] The two of them came back to the boat across the water together. And the moment they climbed in, the wind stopped.

[narrator] Not slowly. Not eventually. The sea that had fought them all night simply lay down flat under the stars.

[narrator] And the men in that boat — the same men who minutes earlier had screamed that he was a ghost — knelt down where they sat, soaked and shaking, and worshipped him.

[scripture] Of a truth thou art the Son of God.

[narrator] You really are the Son of God, they said. The storm had taught them who he was. And notice what the story remembers about Peter. Not that he sank. That he walked. And that when he fell, he was caught.

[narrator] When your storm gets loud and your faith slips, look back up — the same hand is already reaching for you. Keep your eyes on him.
