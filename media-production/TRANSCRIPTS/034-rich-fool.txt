[narrator] Jesus told a story about a rich man who had a very good year.

[narrator] His fields produced an enormous harvest — more grain and fruit than he had ever gathered in his life.

[narrator] In fact, he had so much that he ran out of room to store it all.

[jesus] This will I do: I will pull down my barns, and build greater; and there will I bestow all my fruits and my goods.

[narrator] So he thought to himself: What should I do? I know — I will tear down my barns and build bigger ones.

[narrator] There I will store up all my grain and all my goods.

[jesus] And I will say to my soul, Soul, thou hast much goods laid up for many years; take thine ease, eat, drink, and be merry.

[narrator] And then I will say to myself: You have plenty saved up for years to come. Relax. Eat, drink, and enjoy your life.

[narrator] He had it all figured out. Every single plan was about himself — his barns, his goods, his own comfort.

[narrator] But there was one thing he had never planned for. And that very night, God spoke to him.

[jesus] Thou fool, this night thy soul shall be required of thee: then whose shall those things be, which thou hast provided?

[narrator] That night, his life was over. And everything he had piled up — the barns, the grain, all of it — would simply pass to someone else.

[narrator] He had planned for everything except the one thing that was certain: that one day he would stand before God.

[narrator] And Jesus ended the story with these words.

[jesus] So is he that layeth up treasure for himself, and is not rich toward God.

[narrator] His barns were full, but his soul was empty. He was rich in things — and poor in the only wealth that lasts.

[narrator] There is nothing wrong with a good harvest. The real question is quieter than that: are you only storing up for yourself — or are you storing up a life that is rich with God?

[narrator] His barns were full. His soul was empty. What are you storing up for yourself — and what are you storing up with God?
