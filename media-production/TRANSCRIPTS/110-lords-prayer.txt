[narrator] One day his followers asked him a simple question.

[scripture] Lord, teach us to pray, as John also taught his disciples.

[narrator] Teach us how to pray. They expected, maybe, a technique. A ritual. Instead, Jesus gave them a family word.

[jesus] After this manner therefore pray ye:

[jesus] Our Father which art in heaven, Hallowed be thy name. Thy kingdom come. Thy will be done in earth, as it is in heaven.

[narrator] Not a distant judge. Not a force. A Father you belong to. And the very first thing you long for, once you know him, is not for yourself at all — that his name be honoured, and his good kingdom come, everywhere.

[jesus] Give us this day our daily bread. And forgive us our debts, as we forgive our debtors.

[narrator] Then the plain, honest things. Bread — it is fine to ask for the ordinary needs of the day. And forgiveness — asked for, and passed on. We receive mercy with the same hands we use to give it away.

[jesus] And lead us not into temptation, but deliver us from evil: For thine is the kingdom, and the power, and the glory, for ever. Amen.

[narrator] Keep me safe. Lead me away from what would harm me. And it ends where it began — with him: Short. Honest. Nothing showy.

[jesus] But when ye pray, use not vain repetitions, as the heathen do: for they think that they shall be heard for their much speaking.

[narrator] That was his warning, just before this: prayer is not a performance. Not many clever words, not standing on a corner to be admired. The prayer God loves most may be the simplest one a child ever whispered.

[narrator] That is really all it is. Not a speech to impress heaven. A child, climbing into the lap of a good Father, and simply talking to him.

[narrator] So you do not need the right words. You only need to begin. And the beginning is just two words, the ones he gave them first of all: Our Father.

[narrator] Jesus said you can talk to God the way a child talks to a good Father — simply, honestly, no performance. It starts with two words: Our Father. What would you say to him, if you began today?
