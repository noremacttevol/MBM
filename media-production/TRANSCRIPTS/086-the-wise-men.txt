[narrator] Some time after Jesus was born, travelers came from the east — scholars who had read the skies. They had seen his star, and followed it all the way to Jerusalem, where they walked into the king's hall and asked a dangerous question.

[scripture] Where is he that is born King of the Jews? for we have seen his star in the east, and are come to worship him.

[narrator] Understand what they had just done. They asked the sitting king of Jerusalem to point them to the real one.

[narrator] Herod was frightened, and he was clever about it. He sent them on to Bethlehem with an errand of his own:

[scripture] Go and search diligently for the young child; and when ye have found him, bring me word again, that I may come and worship him also.

[narrator] Go and look carefully for the child, he said, and when you find him, come back and tell me, so that I can come and worship him too. Every word of that was a lie. He had no intention of worshipping anybody.

[narrator] They were led at last to a house in Bethlehem.

[narrator] And there he was — not in a palace, but with his mother.

[narrator] The wise men knelt before the little child — everything their long journey had been for. Matthew writes it down like this:

[scripture] And when they were come into the house, they saw the young child with Mary his mother, and fell down, and worshipped him: and when they had opened their treasures, they presented unto him gifts; gold, and frankincense, and myrrh.

[narrator] Then they opened up what they had carried across the world and gave it to him — gold, and frankincense, and myrrh. They had crossed a desert to kneel on a dirt floor in a village nobody had heard of.

[narrator] God warned them in a dream not to go back to Herod. So they went home another way, and the king never got his answer.

[narrator] The nations had come to bow to the King.

[narrator] Strangers from far away recognized him first. The door is open — you're invited to worship too.
