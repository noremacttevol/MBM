[narrator] Early morning at the temple, Jesus was teaching a crowd, when a knot of religious leaders shoved their way through — dragging a woman with them.

[narrator] They stood her in the middle of everyone, where no one could look away. They had caught her, they said, in the act itself. And then they turned to Jesus, put the law of Moses on the table between them, and made him answer for it.

[scripture] Master, this woman was taken in adultery, in the very act. Now Moses in the law commanded us, that such should be stoned: but what sayest thou?

[narrator] It was a trap. Say let her go, and he breaks the law. Say stone her, and he's just another man with a rock.

[narrator] Jesus said nothing at first. He bent down and wrote in the dust with his finger. Then he straightened up.

[jesus] He that is without sin among you, let him first cast a stone at her.

[narrator] Whichever one of you has never sinned, he said — you go first. He did not argue the law with them. He just handed the first stone to anybody who had earned the right to throw it.

[scripture] And they which heard it, being convicted by their own conscience, went out one by one, beginning at the eldest, even unto the last: and Jesus was left alone, and the woman standing in the midst.

[narrator] And he bent down and wrote again. They dropped their stones and walked away, one by one — the oldest first — until it was only the two of them.

[jesus] Woman, where are those thine accusers? hath no man condemned thee?

[woman] No man, Lord.

[narrator] Where are the ones accusing you, he asked her. Has no one condemned you? Three words — the only three the Bible gives her, and she got to say them standing up, in an empty courtyard, to the one person who had not walked away.

[jesus] Neither do I condemn thee: go, and sin no more.

[narrator] The only one with the right to condemn her, wouldn't. What would it change to hear him say that to you?
