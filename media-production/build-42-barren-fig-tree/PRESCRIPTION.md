# PRESCRIPTION — build-42-barren-fig-tree

**Row:** 42   **Shelf:** Everyone   **Scripture:** Luke 13:6–9
**Tier:** T2 Encounter   **Target length:** 80–120s   **Pictures:** 10–15 (planned: 12)

## The impact read
- **The one thing:** the tree that had earned the axe got another year — not because
  it changed, but because someone who cared for it knelt in the dirt and asked for it.
- **The turn:** [jv8/n6] the gardener speaking up for the tree — "Lord, let it alone
  this year also." A story that sounded like a warning turns into a rescue.
- **Character shown:** an intercessor whose first move is to buy you time you could
  never buy yourself, then to go to work on you.
- **Whose face carries it:** the GARDENER (pleading, then kneeling to work the soil)
  and the OWNER weighing the call. The tree is the "witness" the camera returns to.
- **The card question falls out of:** "what would you say to a Gardener like that?"

## Beat list (= the picture plan) — 12 stills
1. [n1] a fig tree standing in a vineyard, the story opening — "a tree that was not doing its job"
2. [jv6/n2] the tree in its prime spot: worked soil, water, a wall, full sun — "the best spot on the whole property... a wall around it, full sun"
3. [n3] the owner reaching in to pick figs — bare leaves, nothing at all — "Bare leaves, and no fruit at all"
4. [n4] the owner walking away empty-handed, three seasons running — "walked back with empty hands every time"
5. [jv7/n5] the owner facing the gardener, ordering it cut down — "cut it down; why cumbereth it the ground?"
6. [jv8/n6] the gardener speaking up for the tree, asking for one more year — "Lord, let it alone this year also"
7. [n7] the gardener kneeling to break up the hard, packed earth around the roots — "break up the hard, packed earth around the roots"
8. [n8] the gardener feeding the tree by hand — the lowest, messiest job on the farm — "done by hand at the foot of a tree that has given him nothing back"
9. [jv9/n9] the axe set back against the wall, the tree given its year — "The axe goes back against the wall, and the tree gets its year"
10. [n10/n10b] the tree still bare and unchanged — saved only because someone stood between it and the axe — "Someone who cared for it stood between it and the axe and asked"
11. [n11/n12] the gardener in the vineyard, the tree still standing — "the tree is still standing"
12. [card] hold on the gardener kneeling at the tree for the closing question
