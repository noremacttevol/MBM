# STILL PROMPTS — Story Video #42: The Barren Fig Tree Spared (luke-13_barren-fig-tree)

Luke 13:6-9. STILLS-ONLY (Law E) — twelve painted stills, no motion clips.

CARE FLAGS: **J — mercy-in-judgment (CONTENT-CARE.md).** This is a story about a tree
that deserves the axe and is GIVEN ANOTHER YEAR instead. The mercy that is IN the text —
the gardener who takes the tree's side, asks for one more year, and offers to do the hard
work himself — is the whole video. It never becomes a threat: the axe is set aside, no
tree is ever chopped on screen, and the closing card is an invitation, not a fear-question.
Milk framing: the reprieve is not earned by the tree — it is asked for by someone who
cares for it. Nobody is harmed anywhere in these frames.

Jesus appears in only TWO shots — s1 and s12, the FRAME where he tells the parable to the
crowd. In both the camera is directly behind him, the back of his head to us, his face
never shown, never visible, at any angle. The parable pictures (s2-s11) have NO divine
figure: the vineyard owner, the gardener and the fig tree are ordinary things and ordinary
people, faces shown. (The vineyard owner is NEVER called "lord" or "master" in these
prompts — he is "the vineyard owner" — so nothing but the from-behind frame figure reads
as the Lord.)

JESUS LOCK (identical wording in s1 and s12; project-wide look standard): a first-century
Middle-Eastern man, warm olive-brown skin, LONG dark brown-black hair past the shoulders,
one plain undyed off-white cream wool robe, bare-headed, no hood; no glow, no bright ring, no
outlining light; an ordinary solid man. The camera is always directly behind him and his face
is never shown, never visible, at any angle.

CROWD LOCK (s1, s12) — the ordinary listeners: Galilean working people of every age, men,
women and a few children, in plain earth-tone wool of dun, faded brown, olive and muted
blue, all clearly darker than the one cream robe of the figure in the foreground; not one
of them wears off-white or cream, and no second man wears a cream robe. Their faces ARE
shown, attentive, consistent throughout.

VINEYARD OWNER LOCK (s3, s4, s5, s11) — a well-to-do older landowner of about fifty-five,
dignified but NOT a villain, sun-weathered with a neatly kept grey-streaked dark beard, in
a fuller wool robe of deep russet-maroon with a woven dark-olive sash, clearly NOT cream or
off-white. His face IS shown, honest and reasonable — a fair man, not a cruel one.

GARDENER LOCK (s6, s7, s8, s9, s11) — the keeper of the vineyard (the vinedresser): an
ordinary working man of about forty, weathered and strong, a short dark beard, in a plain
dun-and-faded-brown wool work tunic to the knee with the sleeves rolled to the elbow and a
cord belt, bare forearms, clearly NOT cream. His face IS shown, kind, patient and steady.
His work tools are a wooden-handled iron mattock and a woven basket. Hands shown working.

FIG TREE LOCK (s2-s11) — one mature fig tree planted inside a walled vineyard among rows of
low grapevines: a stout grey trunk and broad green five-lobed fig leaves, but through s2-s8
it carries NO fruit at all — bare of figs, only leaves. In s9 and later, the first small
green fig-buds are just beginning to form on the branches. The vineyard around it: terraced
brown worked soil, low green grapevines on stakes, a dry-stone wall, warm Judean hills
beyond.

STILL STYLE BLOCK (prepended to every prompt, exactly):
Beautiful hand-painted 2D animation style, reverent and warm, like a classic
illustrated storybook of scripture brought to life. Soft painterly brushstroke
textures, glowing golden light, muted earth tones with warm gold highlights.
First-century Judea. Sacred, hushed tone. Not photorealistic. No text or captions
in the image. Historically modest clothing: rough-woven wool and linen in undyed
earth colors. No modern objects.

Every figure has two arms, two hands, two legs and one head (anatomy guard). Every
prompt ends with "One single continuous scene painted edge to edge." Skip any
comic-strip or multi-panel variant the model returns.

## s2-the-fig-tree — Shot 2: a fig tree planted in his vineyard
[STILL STYLE BLOCK] A wide, peaceful view of a walled vineyard on a terraced hillside in warm golden morning light. In the middle of the frame stands one mature fig tree with a stout grey trunk and broad green five-lobed leaves, planted in the best spot of the whole vineyard — deep worked brown soil around its base, rows of low green grapevines on wooden stakes spreading away on either side, a dry-stone wall enclosing it and gentle Judean hills beyond. The tree is full and leafy but carries NO figs at all, not one — only leaves. No people are in the frame. The painting lingers on how much the tree has been given: rich soil, sunlight, water channels for the grapes running past its roots, a sheltering wall — everything a tree could want, and still no fruit. Warm gold morning light, deep green leaves, brown earth. One single continuous scene painted edge to edge.

## s3-seeking-fruit — Shot 3: he came and sought fruit thereon, and found none

The vineyard owner, a well-to-do older landowner of about fifty-five with a neatly kept grey-streaked dark beard, in a fuller wool robe of deep russet-maroon with a woven dark-olive sash, NOT cream, stands reaching up into the broad green leaves with both hands, parting the branches to look for figs. His face is painted fully and clearly, hopeful and searching, leaning in to find even one fig. But the branches hold only leaves — there is no fruit anywhere on the tree. His right hand lifts a leafy branch and his left hand searches beneath it; every hand is joined to the correct arm and no spare or floating hand appears. Deep green leaves, warm gold light, the low grapevines and stone wall behind him. Every figure has two arms, two hands, two legs and one head. One single continuous scene painted edge to edge.
