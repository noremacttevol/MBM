# STORY-BLUEPRINT SYSTEM — understand the story first, then build it

**Locked by Cameron, 2026-07-24.** This is the UPSTREAM layer that was missing.
Companion to PRODUCTION-BIBLE.md, STORY-COVERAGE-LAW.md, SPEAKER-LAW.md, CAPTION-LAW.md.

The problem this fixes, in Cameron's words:

> "The videos I have now have no rhythm or reason as to where they need more or
> less pictures... we have no idea how much of the story needs to be told and how
> many pictures we need for it and who needs to talk and say what and what the
> narrator needs to say in between."

STORY-COVERAGE-LAW is **reactive** — it fixes a video after Cameron denies it.
This system is **proactive**: before a single picture is made, a session decides
what the story *is*, how much of it to tell, how long the video runs, how many
pictures it gets and where, and who speaks. That decision is written to a
`PRESCRIPTION.md` in the build folder and becomes the contract the builders obey.
When the prescription is right, Cameron stops approving/denying picture counts —
the vibe is written down, not guessed.

---

## PART 1 — THE MISSION (why every one of these exists)

One sentence: **show, in short painted stories with a narrator and the real
voices of the Bible, how good Jesus is — the way a Latter-day Saint sees him —
so a stranger meets a truly good God, and is invited toward the restored gospel
in milk-before-meat order.**

Every feed card is the same four things for every user:
**story video + one verse + one question + one invitation.** The three shelves
never change the card; they only change the pool the algorithm draws from.

| Shelf | Rows | Who it serves | The job of a video here |
|---|---|---|---|
| **EVERYONE** | 1–100 | Anyone, no signals | The whole Jesus, birth to ascension, pure Bible. A stranger meets a good God. |
| **BRIDGE** | 101–150 | "God is good AND I want more" | Still 100% Bible, but each scene answers a *wound* in an inherited picture of God (silent heaven, angry God, dead religion, one-size heaven). Tone: "the God who hurt you isn't the God of this book — look." Also "come remember" for the drifted. |
| **MEMBER** | 151–200 | Self-attested members | The Restoration proven from the Bible itself. Each ends pointing to the matching Gospel Library page. **These are the "extra verses" a member unlocks** — their scripture expands into their approved wants. |

Shelves are **cumulative** — a member sees 1–200, a bridge viewer 1–150, everyone
1–100. Nobody loses content moving up. Because it's cumulative, **one verse gets
one video across the whole pool** (the 2026-07-20 repeat purge law) — never the
same scene told twice.

North-star rules that outrank timeline and precedent:
- **Jesus wouldn't rank his own stories.** Do not privilege chronology or "which
  came first." The order is buried on purpose; the algorithm prescribes what a
  given person needs, not a syllabus.
- **The picture serves the story, the story serves Christ.** If a change makes the
  video show more plainly how good Jesus is, it's a good change. If it just adds
  spectacle, cut it.
- **Never violate SPEAKER-LAW, CAPTION-LAW, the face rule (face IS shown via
  master ref), or the character locks.** This system sits on top of those, never
  around them.

### The test for changing a story
You may propose changing which story a row tells, or how it's framed, **only** when
you can finish this sentence for Cameron in one line: *"Jesus would approve this
change because ______."* (e.g. "because the current cut ends on judgment and leaves
the viewer with fear, not with how reachable he is.") Anything you can't justify
that way, leave alone. Fix everything else first; put the one story-change question
at the very end of your report.

## PART 2 — UNDERSTAND EACH STORY (the impact read)

Before length or pictures, answer five questions about the story. This is the
"understand the impact" step. Write the answers into the prescription — they drive
every later decision.

1. **The one thing.** In a single sentence, what does this video make a viewer
   *feel or know about God/Jesus that they didn't before*? (Not the plot — the
   point. Prodigal's one thing isn't "a son came home," it's "the father ran.")
2. **The turn.** Every strong story has a hinge — the moment it changes. Name it.
   That hinge is always a picture, usually the most important one.
3. **The wound or the doctrine** (shelf-dependent).
   - Everyone: what does this reveal about his character? (compassion, authority
     over death, welcome of the outsider…)
   - Bridge: which specific wound does it answer? (silent heaven / angry God /
     dead religion / one-size heaven / come-remember)
   - Member: which Restoration truth, and which Gospel Library page does it hand off to?
4. **Who carries it.** Whose face is the camera on in the key beat? (STORY-COVERAGE
   rule: "the picture is the people" — almost never Jesus's face in the emotional
   beat; the *witness's* face carries it.)
5. **Where it must land.** The closing question on the card should fall out of the
   one thing. If the story's point and the card's question don't line up, the story
   read is wrong — redo it.

## PART 3 — SCOPE TIERS → length → picture count (the part with the missing rhythm)

**There is no fixed picture count and never was** (STORY-COVERAGE-LAW). The number
is decided by reading the story, beat by beat (below). The four scope tiers are a
way to *understand what kind of story you're holding* — not a formula that spits out
a count. They come with rough ranges only so you can sanity-check yourself: if your
honest beat read lands you far outside the range for that kind of story, stop and
re-read — you've either mis-tiered it or missed/padded beats. **The range is a
mirror, never a target.** Trust the beats over the range every time.

| Tier | The story is… | Tends to run | Tends to want | Real examples |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **T1 — Saying / Verse** | one truth, one image, little or no action | **45–75s** | **6–9** | most member verses, the "I am" sayings, mustard seed |
| **T2 — Encounter** | one scene, one exchange or one healing, a small clear arc | **80–120s** | **10–15** | Zacchaeus, the leper, Nicodemus, father-forgive-them |
| **T3 — Full Arc** | multiple turns / a reversal / an emotional build | **120–165s** | **16–24** | prodigal, Jairus, feeding 5000, still small voice, Emmaus |
| **T4 — Multi-scene Epic** | several distinct scenes in one video (reserved — the 200 usually split these into their own rows) | **150–210s** | **22–32** | (rare — use only when a row genuinely holds a sequence) |

The tier for all 200 is assigned in
[STORY-BLUEPRINT-TABLE.md](./STORY-BLUEPRINT-TABLE.md) as a starting read. The "tends
to want" numbers are observed ranges, not caps or targets — a story that reads to 27
beats gets 27 pictures even if its tier "tends to want" fewer. Write what the beat map
gave you, and if it's outside the range, one line in the PRESCRIPTION saying why.

### THE PICTURE COUNT IS THE BEAT COUNT — no clock, no formula
**Do not divide the runtime by anything. Do not aim for a number.** Read the
narration and find every distinct visual moment the words paint — every place the
picture in a listener's head *changes*. That count is the picture count. It is
different for every story because every story moves differently, and that is the
whole point.

A picture stays on screen exactly as long as the words stay on that moment.
Sometimes that's three seconds — a fast exchange, three beats in a single breath.
Sometimes it's twenty — a held, holy pause where the narration lingers on the same
image *on purpose* (Gethsemane's silence, a parent's face after the girl breathes).
**A long hold is not dead air.** Silence or a slow line over the *same* moment is
the story breathing, and it is often the most powerful frame in the video.

There is exactly one failure, and it is not measured in seconds (STORY-COVERAGE-LAW's
test):

> A miss is when **the words have moved to a new visual moment and the picture has
> not.** The narrator says she rose, and we're still looking at her lying down.
> That — and only that — is a missing picture.

So the question is never "how long has this still been up." It is always: *"do the
words on screen right now describe something the current picture isn't showing yet?"*
If yes, that's the next picture. If no, hold — however long the story wants to hold.

This is why the audit flags **build-57 Jairus's daughter**: the narration walks
through the death news, "only believe," the mourners' scorn, "talitha cumi," the
girl rising, the girl walking, the parents' wonder, "give her something to eat" —
each a new thing the words make you see — over ~10 stills. Beats got skipped, not
seconds mismeasured. Read the story, it's ~21 distinct moments; give each its frame.

### How to build the beat list (the actual coverage plan)
1. Read `make_narration.py` SEGMENTS in order.
2. For each segment, list every *distinct visual moment the words describe* — an
   action, a reaction, a realization, an arrival, an exchange. A beat = something a
   camera would cut for. A long segment that moves through several moments gets
   several stills, switched **mid-segment** at the timestamps where the words turn.
3. One still per beat. Two segments share a still ONLY if nothing visual changes.
4. Count the beats. That number — not a quota — is the picture count. Check it lands
   inside the tier band; if it's wildly outside, your tier read is probably wrong.
5. Every still must **agree** with the exact line under it (direction, position,
   scale, emotion, likeness) — a picture that contradicts its words is worse than a
   missing one (STORY-COVERAGE-LAW, the Emmaus failure).

## PART 4 — WHO SPEAKS AND WHAT THE NARRATOR SAYS BETWEEN

Voice and caption colour are the same decision, declared once in `make_narration.py`
SEGMENTS as `(id, SPEAKER, text)` — governed by **SPEAKER-LAW**, do not re-invent it:

- **NARRATOR** (white) — carries the story, sets each scene, says the connective
  tissue between quoted lines. Never speaks scripture in a Bible character's place.
- **JESUS** (red) — every word the red-letter KJV puts in his mouth, *including
  words he quotes other people saying inside his own parables* (see build-02: the
  father's and the elder brother's lines stay Jesus-red because Jesus is the one
  telling it).
- **GOD/Father** (green), **scripture read aloud** (light blue), **women** (pink),
  other named Bible voices per SPEAKER-LAW.

### The narrator's real job (the "what to say between" question)
The narrator is not filler. Between quoted scripture the narrator does exactly three
things, and nothing else:
1. **Sets the scene** the next picture will show ("He was still a long way off…").
2. **Names the turn** so the picture lands ("Old men in that world did not run. He
   ran anyway.").
3. **Never explains the moral** — the closing question on the card does that. Let the
   pictures and the quoted voices carry the meaning; the narrator only gets us from
   one to the next.

Prescription rule: for each narrator segment, the beat list says *what picture sits
under it*. If a narrator segment has no picture that moves the story, either it needs
a beat or it needs cutting. No narrator line rides over a still that already said its
piece 11 seconds ago.

## PART 5 — THE PER-VIDEO PRESCRIPTION (the artifact this session produces)

For each build, write `build-NN-name/PRESCRIPTION.md` **before** building. This is
the contract. Template:

```markdown
# PRESCRIPTION — build-NN-name

**Row:** N   **Shelf:** Everyone/Bridge/Member   **Scripture:** <ref>
**Tier:** T2 Encounter   **Target length:** 80–120s   **Pictures:** 10–15 (planned: 13)

## The impact read
- The one thing: <one sentence — the point, not the plot>
- The turn: <the hinge moment / the key picture>
- Wound answered / character shown / doctrine + GL page: <...>
- Whose face carries it: <the witness, not Jesus>
- The card question falls out of: <the one thing>

## Beat list (= the picture plan)
1. [seg n0] <beat — what the picture shows> — agrees-with: "<narration line>"
2. [seg n1] <beat> ...
   ... one row per still, switched at the timestamp the words turn ...

## Speaker map
- NARRATOR: n0,n1,n3,... (scene-setting + turns only)
- JESUS (red): j1,j2,...  GOD (green): ...  WOMEN (pink): ...
- (Member only) Extra verses unlocked / closing GL handoff: <topic/chapter>

## Story-change note (only if proposing one)
Jesus would approve this change because ______.

## Deviations from the band (only if any)
Stepped to N pictures / Ms because ______.
```

Anything the builders (picture session, audio session) need to obey lives here. They
do not re-decide scope — they execute the prescription and verify against it.

## PART 6 — THE LOOP (why Cameron stops re-approving)

The point is a process he can trust, not a pile of one-off fixes.

1. **This session owns understanding.** It reads the story, writes the PRESCRIPTION,
   and that prescription is now the standard the video is judged against.
2. **Self-check before Cameron ever sees it.** A video is only "ready for the board"
   when it passes its own prescription: tier length hit, picture count inside band,
   density law satisfied (no still > 11s), every still agrees with its line, speaker
   map matches SPEAKER-LAW, `admin/verify-mp4.sh` green. A prescription + a passing
   self-check is what replaces Cameron eyeballing picture counts.
3. **Cameron only weighs in on genuine forks** — a story-change proposal (with its
   "Jesus would approve because…" line) or a doctrine call. Everything mechanical is
   already decided by this system.
4. **When Cameron denies something anyway,** the fix goes into the *prescription
   rubric or the tier bands*, not just the one video — so the vibe gets learned once
   and every future build inherits it. That is the loop closing.

## PART 7 — ORDER OF WORK (what a session using this system actually does)

1. Read this file, PRODUCTION-BIBLE.md, STORY-COVERAGE-LAW.md, SPEAKER-LAW.md,
   CAPTION-LAW.md. Run `bash admin/sync-shared-libs.sh` before any rebuild.

   count is far below band are the biggest offenders** (Jairus is #1 — 164s/10).
3. For each: write `PRESCRIPTION.md`, build to it (Flow is unlimited — generate every
   beat, never trim to "save" Flow), self-check against the prescription, gate with
   `admin/verify-mp4.sh`, ship to the board so Cameron *sees* it (worktree-push path
   in KICKOFF-STORY-COVERAGE.md when the 39GB repo push 500s).
4. Never touch `approvals.json`; never ship over an approved cut (dump approvals
   first). A fix is done only when it's on the board and verified byte-for-byte.
