## 1. THE SCRIPTURE CARD

### The passage, in full (Luke 18:9–14, KJV)

> **9** And he spake this parable unto certain which trusted in themselves that they
> were righteous, and despised others:
> **10** Two men went up into the temple to pray; the one a Pharisee, and the other a
> publican.
> **11** The Pharisee stood and prayed thus with himself, God, I thank thee, that I am
> not as other men are, extortioners, unjust, adulterers, or even as this publican.
> **12** I fast twice in the week, I give tithes of all that I possess.
> **13** And the publican, standing afar off, would not lift up so much as his eyes unto
> heaven, but smote upon his breast, saying, God be merciful to me a sinner.
> **14** I tell you, this man went down to his house justified rather than the other: for
> every one that exalteth himself shall be abased; and he that humbleth himself shall be
> exalted.

### Context (studied before storyboarding — required)

Luke 18 opens with the persistent widow (18:1–8, our video #38) — *keep praying, God is
not the reluctant judge*. Immediately after ours comes the little children (18:15–17) and
the rich young ruler (18:18–30, our video #9). Luke has stacked three pictures of the same
truth: the kingdom belongs to people who come with **empty hands** — the nagging widow,
the broken publican, the child — and it slips through the fingers of the man who arrives
holding something (the Pharisee's record, the ruler's wealth). Our video sits in the middle
of that argument and must land the same point.

**Verse 9 is not optional.** It names the audience and the reason: *certain which trusted
in themselves that they were righteous, and despised others.* Cutting v9 would leave a nice
story about humility. Keeping it makes the story an act of rescue aimed at the men who were
sure they didn't need any (Full-Story Law).

### MUST SHOW (facts the text states)

- Jesus tells this parable **to** people who trust in their own righteousness and despise
  others (v9). They are the on-screen audience of the frame beats.
- **Two men**, both **going UP into the temple**, both there **to pray** (v10).
- One is a **Pharisee**; one is a **publican** (tax collector).
- The Pharisee **stood** and prayed **with himself** — grandly, publicly, and about himself.
- The Pharisee's prayer names his own record: **fasts twice in the week**, **gives tithes of
  all he possesses** — and explicitly despises "**this publican**" *inside his prayer* (v11–12).
- The publican **stands afar off** (v13) — at a distance, not up front.
- The publican **would not lift up so much as his eyes unto heaven** — eyes DOWN, breaking
  the normal standing/eyes-lifted posture of Jewish prayer.
- The publican **smites upon his breast** — a mourning gesture (same word used of the crowds
  at the crucifixion, Luke 23:48).
- The publican's prayer is **seven words**: *God be merciful to me a sinner.*
- **The publican goes DOWN to his house JUSTIFIED — and the Pharisee does not** (v14). The
  verdict is the money moment. The good man goes home exactly as he came.
- The closing principle (v14b): self-exalting → abased; self-humbling → exalted.

### MUST NEVER SHOW

- **Jesus's face — never, at any angle, in any frame.** (The #1 Law.) He appears in exactly
  two shots, both from **directly behind**: s1 (he tells the parable) and s9 (he gives the
  verdict). Long dark hair past the shoulders, one plain undyed cream wool robe, warm Middle
  Eastern olive-brown skin, **no glow, no halo, no rim-light**, never a hooded void.
- **Jesus inside the parable.** Verses 10–13 are a story he is telling. The Pharisee, the
  publican, and the temple crowd are ordinary people, drawn fully with faces. **No divine
  figure, no God-figure, and no Jesus appears in any parable shot (s2–s8, s10–s12).**
- **A cartoonish villain Pharisee.** The text does not say he lied. He really did fast and
  really did tithe. If the picture makes him a sneering hypocrite, the story dies — the
  shock is that the *genuinely good man* went home wrong. Paint him dignified, devout,
  impressive, and blind.
- **A pitiable, filthy publican.** He is a well-off man (tax farming paid). His problem is
  guilt, not poverty. Paint him well-dressed and wrecked.
- **The publican looking up.** v13 is explicit: he would not lift his eyes to heaven. Any
  still with his eyes raised is an automatic regenerate.
- **Painted teardrop beads** on any cheek (No-Fake-Tears law). Wet, shining eyes at most.
- Night or golden-hour drama. The text puts them at the temple **for prayer** — the
  morning hour of prayer and sacrifice. Clear, bright, ordinary day.
- Any baked-in text, letters, or scroll writing in an image.
- Any AI-animated clip anywhere in the delivery (Law E).

## 2. THE POINT (Clarity / WHY-Law — a viewer with zero Bible background must be able
to say this in one sentence)

**The man who had everything right went home wrong, and the man who had everything wrong
went home right — because one came to God with a résumé and the other came with the truth.**

Mercy is not a prize handed to the qualified. It is a gift handed to the honest. And Jesus
told this to the good men *not to shame them but to let them in too* — the only thing
keeping the Pharisee out was his certainty that he was already in. **End on invitation,
never on pressure** (Gospel Principles).

### Study gems woven in (STUDY-GEM TIDBITS check — small, never lectures)

- **The Pharisee was genuinely devout.** The law of Moses commanded **one** fast a year
  (the Day of Atonement). He fasted **twice a week** — far past what God asked. He tithed
  "all that I possess," beyond the required crops, down to garden herbs. Every neighbor
  would have named him the best man in the city. That is what makes v14 detonate.
- **"Prayed thus with himself."** He addresses God, but the prayer never really leaves the
  room. In one short prayer he says the word **I** five times — *I thank, I am not, I fast,
  I give, I possess* — and he cannot get through it without stepping on the man behind him.
- **A publican was not merely disliked — he was a traitor with a money box.** He bought the
  right to collect Rome's taxes from his own countrymen and lived on whatever extra he could
  squeeze out. He was barred as a witness in court and lumped in with sinners. In the eyes
  of the synagogue, his road back was effectively closed: restitution to everyone he had
  defrauded, plus a fifth — a sum a tax farmer could not even calculate, let alone pay.
- **"Standing afar off."** He does not approach the inner court. He stops as far back as a
  man can stop and still be in the temple at all.
- **"Would not lift up so much as his eyes."** The normal posture of Jewish prayer was
  standing with eyes and hands raised to heaven. He physically cannot do it.
- **"Smote upon his breast."** Beating the breast was a gesture of raw grief, rare for men
  — the same thing the crowds do walking home from the cross (Luke 23:48). He strikes the
  chest, the place the sin lives.
- **"God be merciful to me."** The Greek verb behind "be merciful" (*hilastheti*) is the
  atonement word — the mercy seat, the lid of the ark where the blood was sprinkled on the
  Day of Atonement. Standing in the temple, at the hour the lamb is killed for the sins of
  the whole nation, he is asking God to let **that** mercy fall on **him**, personally.
- **"A sinner"** is, in Luke's Greek, *the* sinner — with the definite article. He does not
  count himself as one sinner among many. He speaks as if he were the only one in the
  building.
- **"Went DOWN to his house."** The temple sat up on the mount; you went *up* to it and
  *down* home. Luke's little geographic word is also the verdict: the man who lowered
  himself is the one who walked home lifted.

**Why n8 is split (n8a/n8b):** as one segment it ran 75 words — a 13-line caption wall (and
captions are verbatim), and it would have parked ~30s of narration on a single picture. Split,
each caption is a readable block and the altar gets its own image. Same for `s5b`: without it,
`s5` carried 47s of narration alone, breaking "never lean on one image to cover a long stretch."

**Two sacred silences:** the music bed dies to **FULL SILENCE before j2** ("God be merciful to
me a sinner") and stays out through the altar beat; it swells back under n9 to carry us toward
the verdict, then dies again so **j3 also lands in true silence**. A warm bed returns after the
verdict and is gone before the closing card. The narrator never stops — only the music does, so
No-Dead-Air is unaffected.

### Closing question card (the Seed)

> **One man brought God his record. The other brought God the truth. If you stopped
> performing for a moment — what would you actually say to him?**
