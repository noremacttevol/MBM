# Story Video #13 — Through the Roof (Mark 2:1–12)

Runtime 60–75s. Prepend the STYLE BLOCK from 00-MASTER-PLAN.md. Jesus: below
frame / a seated silhouette in the dusty light-shaft / a voice — never his
face. The visual signature of this film is the ROOF OPENING: daylight
breaking into a dark packed room from above.

## Narration script (app's exact words)

> Four friends carried a paralyzed man on a mat to the house where Jesus was
> teaching. The crowd was packed so tight there was no way through the door.
>
> So they climbed onto the roof, dug through it, and lowered their friend
> down on ropes — right in front of Jesus, mid-sermon, dust and daylight
> pouring in.
>
> The text says something remarkable: "When Jesus saw THEIR faith" — the
> faith of the friends who did the carrying — he turned to the man on the
> mat. *(held beat)*
>
> The first thing he said was not about the man's legs. "Son, your sins are
> forgiven." He went to the deepest wound first. *(peak — music cuts to
> silence on "the deepest wound first")*
>
> Then he healed the legs too, and the man walked home carrying the mat that
> had carried him.

Closing card (hold 6s): *"Four people tore a roof open because their friend
could not get there alone. Where do you find yourself in that room?"*

## Shot list

### Shot 1 — Carried
```
Four men carry a fifth on a woven mat through village streets, one at each
corner, sweating, coordinated, determined. The man on the mat is thin, his
useless legs covered by a blanket, his face a mix of embarrassment and
fragile hope. Handheld tracking, urgency without panic.
```

### Shot 2 — No way through
```
The doorway of a small stone house overflows with people — backs, shoulders,
craning necks — spilling into the street. The four bearers stand with their
friend's mat, sizing up the impossible crowd. Then one of them looks UP, at
the roofline. The others follow his gaze.
```

### Shot 3 — Digging through
```
On the flat earthen roof: four pairs of hands tear through packed clay and
reed thatch with fingers and a borrowed tool, coughing in the dust,
grinning at each other like men doing something magnificent and slightly
insane. Below their hole, lamplight and upturned faces appear.
```

### Shot 4 — Lowered into the light (the money shot — extra takes)
```
Interior, from inside the packed dark room: the ceiling cracks open and a
shaft of blazing daylight pours down through falling dust and straw — and
into that column of light, a man on a mat descends slowly on four ropes,
swaying gently, lowered with enormous care into the middle of the stunned
crowd. Shot from low, looking up into the light and the four silhouetted
faces above.
```

### Shot 5 — THEIR faith
```
Looking up through the torn roof from below: four dusty, sweat-streaked
faces ring the opening, framed against the sky, breathing hard, eyes fixed
downward with fierce hope — a portrait held long enough to read each face.
```

### Shot 6 — The deepest wound first
```
Close on the face of the man on the mat, lying in the column of dusty light:
he braces for words about his legs — and what lands instead visibly
undoes him. His face crumples with relief in a place deeper than his body,
tears sliding into his hair. His legs have not moved yet. It is already the
miracle.
```

### Shot 7 — Carrying the mat that carried him
```
The crowd parts, awestruck: the man stands — new legs trembling like a
colt's — rolls up his mat, tucks it under his arm, and walks out the door
into daylight. On the roofline above, four filthy friends pound each other's
shoulders and laugh toward heaven.
```

### Closing card
Text on cream, serif. No narration.

## The Seed (what this video must leave behind)

"When Jesus saw THEIR faith" — the friends' faith counted for the man on the mat. One person can be carried by the faith of others, and heaven honors it. And he healed the deepest wound first. Quiet question: *if their faith could count for him, how far does God let love work on someone else's behalf?* Carried by shots 4–6.
