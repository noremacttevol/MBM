> Paul steadying a frightened church, then foretelling the falling away. The beats lean

## The impact read
- **The one thing:** the falling away was foretold by Paul himself — so the apostasy is
  not proof God failed, it is the promise that a restoration was always coming.
- **The turn:** n6/n7 — from dread to hope: "you do not warn people about a night unless
  a morning is meant to follow."
- **Doctrine + GL page:** the Great Apostasy Paul foretold — the falling away that had to
  come first. **GL handoff: Apostasy.**
- **Whose face carries it:** Paul at his writing, and the Thessalonian believer's face —
  first rattled, then steadied, then hopeful. No Jesus's face.
- **The card question falls out of:** "When the light returns, will you take hold of it?"
  — lands on the one thing.

## Beat list (= the picture plan) — 7 stills
1. [n1] the young Thessalonian believers rattled and frightened by rumors; Paul at his writing table — agrees: "frightened and confused. So Paul sat down to write them a steadying letter"
2. [n2,n3,kv2] Paul's calm counsel steadying them — do not be shaken by rumor or hearsay or the loudest voice — "be not soon shaken in mind, or be troubled... by every excited rumor"
3. [n4] Paul warning what to watch for — that day will not come until a falling away comes first — "there would come a falling away"
4. [n5,kv3] the dimming — a light going out, hearts turning away, apostolic truth lost from view — "A dimming of the light... turning away... except there come a falling away first"
5. [n6] the believer's face turning from dread to hope — Paul foretold it, so God did not fail — "A falling away would not mean God had failed"
6. [n7] the night-before-morning image — a lamp that can be relit, a truth that can be restored — "you do not warn people about a night unless a morning is meant to follow"
7. [n8,card] the light offered again; a hand reaching to take hold — warm hold for the closing question — "When the light returns, will you take hold of it?"
