# STILL PROMPTS — Story Video #48: New Wine, Old Bottles (mark-2_new-wine-old-bottles)

Mark 2:18-22. STILLS-ONLY (Law E) — ten painted stills, no motion clips.

CARE FLAGS: none — GREEN, plain milk (CONTENT-CARE.md). Nothing violent: the bursting
skin is an OLD LEATHER BOTTLE failing, never a person harmed — no one is hurt, no gore.
Milk framing: this is GOOD news — the joy he brings is a NEW thing, too full of life
to be crammed back into the old rigid forms. The WHY the whole video carries: they asked
why his disciples do not fast; his answer is that you do not mourn at a wedding — the
Bridegroom is HERE, and God is pouring out something so new it needs a new container.

Jesus appears in only TWO shots — s1 and s10, the FRAME where he answers the men who
question him. In both the camera is directly behind him, the back of his head to us, his
face never shown, never visible, at any angle. The parable pictures (s3-s9) have no
divine figure: the bridegroom, the seamstress and the wineskins are ordinary things and
ordinary people, faces shown.

JESUS LOCK (identical wording in s1 and s10; project-wide look standard): a first-century
Middle-Eastern man, warm olive-brown skin, LONG dark brown-black hair past the shoulders,
one plain undyed off-white cream wool robe, bare-headed, no hood; no glow, no halo, no
rim-light; an ordinary solid man. The camera is always directly behind him and his face
is never shown, never visible, at any angle.

QUESTIONERS LOCK (s1, s2, s10) — the disciples of John and the Pharisees who come asking:
earnest, sincere religious men, NOT villains. Two kinds together: PHARISEES in fuller
formal wool robes of deep indigo-blue and dark maroon, well-kept dark beards, a visible
woven prayer-fringe at the hem; and JOHN'S DISCIPLES, plainer and leaner, in coarse dun
and faded-brown wool, gaunt and hollow-cheeked from fasting. Every one of them is clearly
darker than cream — not one wears off-white or cream, and no second man wears a cream
robe. Their faces ARE shown, honestly puzzled and questioning, consistent throughout.

BRIDEGROOM LOCK (s3, s4) — the young bridegroom of the wedding feast: a glad young man in
a festive deep-BLUE embroidered wool robe with a bright woven red-and-gold sash and a
simple flower garland, clearly NOT cream or off-white. His face IS shown, joyful. The
wedding GUESTS around him are ordinary villagers of every age in warm festal reds, ochres
and greens, reclining at a long lamp-lit table with bread and cups — none in off-white or
cream.

GARMENT LOCK (s5, s6) — a worn OLD outer cloak of soft faded grey-brown wool, threadbare
and thin, with a tear in it; and a piece of stiff BRIGHT NEW undyed cream cloth being
sewn onto it as a patch. A seamstress, an ordinary older village woman in a dun headscarf
and a faded-olive dress (NOT cream), works it with needle and thread, her face shown.

WINESKIN LOCK (s7, s8, s9) — traditional whole-hide leather wine bottles. The OLD skin is
dark, stiff, sun-hardened and cracked. The NEW skins are pale, supple, freshly oiled and
soft. The NEW WINE is fresh, frothy, deep red-purple must. No person is harmed anywhere in
these frames — only the old leather bottle fails.

STILL STYLE BLOCK (prepended to every prompt, exactly):
Beautiful hand-painted 2D animation style, reverent and warm, like a classic
illustrated storybook of scripture brought to life. Soft painterly brushstroke
textures, glowing golden light, muted earth tones with warm gold highlights.
First-century Judea. Sacred, hushed tone. Not photorealistic. No text or captions
in the image. Historically modest clothing: rough-woven wool and linen in undyed
earth colors. No modern objects.

Every figure has two arms, two hands, two legs and one head (anatomy guard). Every
prompt ends with "One single continuous scene painted edge to edge." Skip any
comic-strip or multi-panel variant the model returns.

## s7-the-old-wineskin — Shot 7: old bottles, stiff and cracked
[STILL STYLE BLOCK] A still, quiet corner of a stone cellar in soft cool daylight. On a low bench sits an OLD leather wine bottle — a whole-hide skin gone dark, stiff and sun-hardened, its surface dry and beginning to crack, brittle with age, clearly worn out and no longer supple. Beside it a shallow clay bowl and a wooden stopper. No people are in the frame. The painting lingers on the tired old leather so the viewer feels how hard and unyielding it has become — a bottle that has lost all its give. Soft cool cellar light, warm earth tones. One single continuous scene painted edge to edge.

## s8-new-wine-bursts — Shot 8: new wine bursts the old bottles — but must go into new
REF: s7-the-old-wineskin, s9-new-wine-new-skins
[STILL STYLE BLOCK] The cool stone cellar, two LEATHER wine bottles side by side on a low wooden bench. BOTH bottles are made of animal-hide leather — there is no glass and no clay jug anywhere in the picture. On the LEFT, the OLD bottle: dark, stiff, sun-hardened cracked leather that has SPLIT along a seam, and fresh frothy deep red-purple new wine is bursting out of the crack and spilling down onto the bench and the stone floor — the brittle old skin could not hold the living wine. No person is anywhere near it and no one is harmed: it is only the old leather bottle that fails. On the RIGHT, in clear contrast, a NEW wine bottle made of pale tan SUPPLE freshly-oiled soft LEATHER — the same kind of soft new leather skin as in the character-lock image — stands whole and full and sound, its soft rounded sides holding the same deep red-purple new wine safely without a single crack. The two leather bottles side by side tell the whole lesson at a glance: the old split and spilled, the new holds and keeps. Soft cool cellar light, the spilled wine deep and rich, the new leather skin sound and pale. One single continuous scene painted edge to edge.
