Luke 13:10-17. Jesus red-letter lines (the Jesus voice speaks ONLY these):

  ought not this woman, being a daughter of Abraham, whom Satan hath bound,
  lo, these eighteen years, be loosed from this bond on the sabbath day?"

### FULL-STORY check (the law that killed half-parables)
The production pack STOPS at "daughter of Abraham" and omits the ruler of the
synagogue entirely — but j2 is Jesus ANSWERING that ruler. Without the
indignant objection (13:14), the rebuke has no target, and the ending
(13:17 — adversaries ashamed, ALL the people rejoiced) is the story's actual
last verse. Both are restored in this build. Also restored: 13:13 "and
glorified God" — the first thing she did with a straight back.

### MUST SHOW (facts the text states)
- Bent eighteen years, "could in no wise lift up herself"
- Sabbath, in the synagogue, Jesus teaching
- HE saw her and HE called her — she never asked (the Seed hinges on this)
- "Loosed" spoken before any touch; hands laid on her; immediately straight
- She glorified God
- The ruler's indignation + his "six days" speech to the people
- The ox/ass watering custom (j2's logic)
- "Daughter of Abraham" as a declaration of belonging
- Adversaries ashamed; all the people rejoiced

### MUST NEVER SHOW
- Jesus's face — NEVER, in any frame, any distance
- Jesus's hands — NEVER (PRODUCTION-BIBLE §5 hands law overrides the pack's
  older "hands allowed in healing" note; the pack predates the locked look).
  The touch is carried by NARRATION + light gathering on her back; visually
  Jesus appears only as a dark back/silhouette at frame edge.
- The woman standing straight BEFORE the healing beat (she is bent in s1-s5)
- Any daylight-outdoor look inside the synagogue scenes (interior shafts only)
- Modern objects; text baked into images

cream fringed prayer shawl over a dark blue robe, stern deeply lined face.
