# Story Video #15 — The Centurion (Matthew 8:5–13)

Runtime 60–75s. Prepend the STYLE BLOCK from 00-MASTER-PLAN.md. Jesus: robed
figure from behind / a voice the centurion answers — never his face. The
film's engine is the collision of two kinds of authority: Roman armor
kneeling to a word.

## Narration script (app's exact words)

> A Roman centurion — an officer of the occupying army, the last person
> anyone expected — came to Jesus about his servant, who was suffering
> terribly at home.
>
> Jesus said: "I will come and heal him."
>
> The centurion said something no one saw coming: "Lord, I am not worthy to
> have you come under my roof. Just say the word, and my servant will be
> healed. I am a man under authority myself — I say go, and men go." *(held
> beat)*
>
> Jesus was amazed. The text says he marveled — one of the only times it
> says that. "I have not found faith like this in all Israel." *(peak —
> music cuts to silence on "he marveled")*
>
> The servant was healed that hour, from a distance, on nothing but a word.

Closing card (hold 6s): *"\"I am not worthy to have you under my roof — but
say the word.\" Which half of that sentence is easier for you to say?"*

## Shot list

### Shot 1 — The suffering servant
```
A dim Roman villa room: a young servant lies rigid on a low bed, jaw
clenched in pain, skin gray with sickness. Beside him, incongruous, sits a
hard-faced Roman officer in his undertunic, armor set aside, wringing a
cloth in water with hands more used to a sword — helpless and staying
anyway.
```

### Shot 2 — The last person anyone expected
```
A Roman centurion in full armor and red cloak strides through a Jewish
village street, and the street goes silent — mothers pull children close,
men stare with hard eyes. He feels all of it and keeps walking, helmet
under his arm, jaw set, toward a small crowd gathered ahead.
```

### Shot 3 — Not worthy / say the word
```
The armored centurion stops before a robed figure seen from behind — and
this commander of a hundred men slowly bows his head, and speaks with the
flat, precise honesty of a soldier's report. Close on his weathered face:
zero performance, complete conviction. His fist rests on his chest over his
heart.
```

### Shot 4 — He marveled (the money shot — extra takes)
```
The robed figure, seen from behind against the light, goes visibly STILL —
the stillness of astonishment — then turns partway toward the watching
crowd, gesturing back toward the Roman officer. On the surrounding Jewish
faces: disbelief, offense, wonder, in that order. The centurion stands at
attention under words he can't quite believe are about him.
```

### Shot 5 — That hour, at a distance
```
Cross-cut feel in one shot: in the dim villa room far away, the gray-faced
young servant suddenly draws a huge clean breath — color flooding back into
his face like dawn into a room — and sits up, whole, looking at his own
steady hands. No one else is there. It happened on a word spoken miles
away.
```

### Shot 6 — The centurion returns
```
The centurion arrives home at a run, armor jangling, and stops dead in the
doorway: his servant stands at the table, well, pouring water like any
ordinary morning. The two men look at each other. The hard-faced officer
covers his mouth with his hand and his soldier's composure breaks —
quietly, completely.
```

### Closing card
Text on cream, serif. No narration.
