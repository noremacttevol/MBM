# Story Video #19 — Breakfast on the Shore: Peter Restored (John 21)

Runtime 75–90s. Prepend the STYLE BLOCK from 00-MASTER-PLAN.md. Jesus: a
figure on the shore at dawn distance, firelight silhouette, hands tending
the fire — never his face. The charcoal fire is the emotional key: it is
the SAME kind of fire Peter denied him beside. Let the fire do the
remembering.

## Narration script (app's exact words)

> Peter had sworn he would die before denying Jesus. Then, in one terrible
> night, he denied him three times — and the rooster crowed, and Jesus
> turned and looked at him, and Peter went out and wept bitterly.
>
> After the resurrection, Peter went back to fishing. Back to the old life.
> It is what people do with unbearable failure — they go back to what they
> knew before.
>
> At dawn, a figure on the shore called out to the boat. It was Jesus —
> with a charcoal fire burning, the same kind of fire Peter had denied him
> beside. Breakfast was already cooking. *(held beat)*
>
> Three times — once for each denial — Jesus asked: "Peter, do you love
> me?" Not "how could you," not "prove it." Just: do you love me. *(peak —
> music cuts to silence on the line "do you love me")*
>
> And three times, to the man who had failed him worst: "Feed my sheep." He
> gave his greatest failure his greatest job.

Closing card (hold 6s): *"Peter went back to fishing because he thought his
failure was final. Is there a failure you have quietly decided is final?"*

## Shot list

### Shot 1 — The night of the denial (prologue, cold tones)
```
Night, a courtyard fire: a big fisherman's face lit by charcoal glow as he
shakes his head hard at questioners, backing away — and freezes at a
rooster's cry. Across the courtyard, seen from behind, a bound prisoner
turns his head. The fisherman flees into the dark, fist against his mouth.
Cold blue night, orange fire.
```

### Shot 2 — Back to the old life
```
Gray pre-dawn on the lake: the same big fisherman hauls an EMPTY net over
the gunwale — again — his movements mechanical, face shut down. Around him
other tired men. The boat sits in colorless water. This is what unbearable
failure looks like: competence with the light gone out of it.
```

### Shot 3 — The figure on the shore
```
From the boat, far across dawn water: a small figure stands on the beach
beside a thread of rising smoke, and calls out. The fishermen squint. Then
one of them goes very still — and the big fisherman doesn't wait for the
boat: he plunges over the side into the cold water, swimming hard for
shore, leaving everything.
```

### Shot 4 — The charcoal fire (the money shot — extra takes)
```
The soaked fisherman wades out of the dawn surf and stops dead: on the sand
burns a small CHARCOAL fire with fish laid over it, tended by a robed
figure seen only from behind. Close on the fisherman's face — the smell of
charcoal physically hits him with the memory of the courtyard. Shame and
hope fight on his dripping face. The fire crackles. Dawn gold spreads on
the water behind him.
```

### Shot 5 — Do you love me — three times
```
Two figures walk slowly along the water's edge at sunrise, waves erasing
their footprints behind them — the robed figure from behind, the fisherman's
face in profile carrying the scene: pain at the first question, worse at
the second, breaking open at the third — and each time, answering with his
whole ruined honest heart. The camera keeps pace, unhurried.
```

### Shot 6 — Feed my sheep
```
Close: the fisherman's big scarred hands, hanging useless at his sides —
then a hand enters frame and grips his shoulder, and the big hands slowly
close into steadiness. His face lifts: a man being handed back his life,
plus a purpose heavier and better than the one he lost.
```

### Shot 7 — The failure who feeds
```
Full sunrise: the group sits around the fire eating together, and the big
fisherman — restored, laughing through the tail end of tears — BREAKS BREAD
and hands it around the fire to the others. First act of the new job. The
lake blazes gold behind them.
```

### Closing card
Text on cream, serif. No narration.

## The Seed (what this video must leave behind)

To the man who failed him worst, over the same kind of charcoal fire the failure happened beside: not "how could you" but "do you love me" — and then his greatest job. God calls the failed, the unqualified, the ashamed, and hands them the work. Quiet question: *if failure disqualified people, why does God keep choosing the failed?* Carried by shots 4–6.
