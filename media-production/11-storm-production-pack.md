# Story Video #11 — Calming the Storm (Mark 4:35–41)

Runtime 60–75s. Prepend the STYLE BLOCK from 00-MASTER-PLAN.md. Jesus: the
sleeping form under a cloak, then a standing silhouette against the storm —
never his face. The edit itself should perform the miracle: violent motion
and sound, then ONE cut to absolute stillness.

## Narration script (app's exact words)

> A storm came up on the lake at night — sudden and violent, the kind that
> frightened even the fishermen who had worked that water their whole lives.
>
> Waves broke over the boat. It was filling with water. And Jesus was asleep
> in the stern, on a cushion.
>
> They woke him with a question that people have been asking in storms ever
> since: "Teacher, don't you care that we are about to drown?" *(held beat)*
>
> He got up, spoke to the wind and the waves — "Peace. Be still." — and the
> sea went flat calm. *(peak — ALL sound cuts to silence exactly here)*
>
> Then he turned to them, soaked and shaking, and asked gently: "Why were
> you so afraid?" He never denied the storm was real. He was just bigger
> than it.

Closing card (hold 6s): *"\"Don't you care that we are drowning?\" — have you
ever asked that question, or wanted to?"*

## Shot list

### Shot 1 — The storm hits
```
Night on open water: a wooden fishing boat is thrown sideways by a wall of
black wave, lightning tearing the sky, rain horizontal. Veteran fishermen —
men with decades on this lake — bail frantically, and their faces show the
thing that frightens everyone else: THEY are afraid.
```

### Shot 2 — Asleep in the stern
```
In the chaos, camera finds the stern of the pitching boat: a figure lies
ASLEEP on a cushion under a soaked cloak, body rising and falling with easy
breath while water crashes over the gunwale beside him. Face turned away
into shadow. The contrast is the whole shot.
```

### Shot 3 — Don't you care?
```
A drenched fisherman grabs the sleeping figure's shoulder, shouting over the
wind, face desperate and betrayed, lit stroboscopically by lightning. Behind
him other men cling to the mast and lines as the boat drops off a wave. The
question is visible on his face even without sound.
```

### Shot 4 — Peace. Be still. (the money shot — extra takes)
```
A robed figure stands up in the stern of the storm-tossed boat, silhouetted
against churning black sky, one hand rising toward the storm — and the wall
of wind and towering waves halt like a struck animal. In one continuous
moment the water falls glassy flat, the clouds tear open to stars, and the
only motion left is spray drifting down like dust. His face never visible.
```

### Shot 5 — Flat calm
```
Held wide shot, no camera movement: the boat sits motionless on a black
mirror of water under sudden stars, moon-path across the flat sea, ropes
dripping. The soaked men stand frozen mid-bail, chests heaving, staring at
water too still to be believed. Absolute silence.
```

### Shot 6 — Why were you afraid?
```
Close on the fishermen's faces one by one, water running off their beards,
as their terror of the storm visibly gives way to a deeper awe directed at
the silhouetted figure in the stern — the dawning question that ends the
scene: what manner of man is this. Gentle warm lantern light returns.
```

### Closing card
Text on cream, serif. No narration.

## The Seed (what this video must leave behind)

He never denied the storm was real — he was just bigger than it. He did not send the storm to teach a lesson or glorify himself; he STILLED it. Quiet question: *is God the author of your storm — or the one standing up in your boat?* Carried by shots 4–5.
