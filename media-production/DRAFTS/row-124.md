## NARRATION — numbered segments

Two-Voice Law: narrator = plain modern English, never echoes KJV wording. Jesus = exact KJV only.

**n1** — NARRATOR:
"Partway up a hillside in Galilee, Jesus sat teaching the crowd we now call the Sermon on the Mount — farmers, fishermen, mothers, day laborers. And underneath everything, one hard fact: this was an occupied country. Foreign soldiers in the streets. Collaborators at the tax booths. These people did not have theoretical enemies. They had names and faces in mind before he finished his sentence."

**n2** — NARRATOR:
"All through this sermon, Jesus used one pattern: here is what you've been taught — and here is what I say. Then he reached the most natural rule in the world."

**j1** — JESUS (exact KJV, Matthew 5:43 ⚠ verify before audio):
"Ye have heard that it hath been said, Thou shalt love thy neighbour, and hate thine enemy."

**n3** — NARRATOR:
"Half of that really was in the law of Moses: love your neighbor. The other half — hating your enemy — appears nowhere in the law at all. It was the part people had bolted on, because it made the command comfortable: love the people close to you, keep your hate for everyone outside the circle. Everyone on earth lives that way by default. Then Jesus tore it up."

**j2** — JESUS (exact KJV, Matthew 5:44):
"But I say unto you, Love your enemies, bless them that curse you, do good to them that hate you, and pray for them which despitefully use you, and persecute you;"

**n4** — NARRATOR:
"Look how practical that is. It isn't a feeling — it's four moves. When someone runs you down, you answer with kindness. When someone works against you, you look for a way to help him. When someone makes your life genuinely hard, you take his name into your prayers — and actually pray for his good. Love, here, is not something you feel about your enemy. It's something you do about him."

**n5** — NARRATOR:
"And then Jesus gave the reason — and the reason is the whole point."

**j3** — JESUS (exact KJV, Matthew 5:45):
"That ye may be the children of your Father which is in heaven: for he maketh his sun to rise on the evil and on the good, and sendeth rain on the just and on the unjust."

**n6** — NARRATOR:
"Watch a sunrise sometime and think about who it lands on. The same morning light warms the field of the kindest man in town and the cruelest one. The same rain fills both their wells. That is your Father's daily habit — he does good, every single day, to people who hate him. So when you love an enemy, you're not doing something unnatural. You're doing the family thing. You start to look like your Father."

**n7** — NARRATOR:
"Then he closed the escape hatch — the idea that loving the easy people counts for something."

**j4** — JESUS (exact KJV, Matthew 5:46–47 ⚠ verify before audio):
"For if ye love them which love you, what reward have ye? do not even the publicans the same? And if ye salute your brethren only, what do ye more than others? do not even the publicans so?"

**n8** — NARRATOR:
"Tax collectors were the most despised men in the country — and even they loved the people who loved them back. Even they were friendly to their own crowd. Loving your circle isn't wrong; it's just not remarkable. Everybody already does it. The love Jesus is describing only becomes visible — only proves anything — at the exact moment it crosses over to someone who has earned the opposite."

**n9** — NARRATOR:
"And then the summit — one line people have misread for centuries."

**j5** — JESUS (exact KJV, Matthew 5:48):
"Be ye therefore perfect, even as your Father which is in heaven is perfect."

**n10** — NARRATOR:
"That word perfect has crushed a lot of honest people, so hear what it actually means. In the language Matthew wrote in, it means complete — whole, finished, fully grown. Jesus isn't demanding a flawless scorecard on the spot. He's describing love with no missing pieces — love that goes all the way around, the way your Father's does, covering friend and enemy alike. That's the family resemblance he's after. And he's the one who grows it in you."

**n11** — NARRATOR:
"So let it get specific. Somebody came to mind a few sentences ago — the name you'd rather not pray for. Start there. Tonight, one honest sentence to God on that person's behalf. It will feel impossible, then merely hard, and then — slowly — it will start to feel like the family trait. Sons and daughters grow into the likeness of their Father."

**card** — NARRATOR (read aloud over closing question card):
"Anyone can love the people who love them back — that's the easy half of the heart. Who is the one person you could pray one honest sentence for tonight?"
