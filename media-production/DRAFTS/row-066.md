# DRAFT row-066 — Malchus's ear — Luke 22:47–51

> Row 66 · Ref: Luke 22:47–51 · Jesus FACE-SHOWN (adult). His LAST free act before the cross — healing an enemy. CONTENT-CARE: gentle, no violence shown; the cut is implied only, never depicted.

## HOMOGRAPH FLAGS: Clean. No bow/wound/wind/tears/lead/sow/live. Ear-check anyway.

## NARRATION (narrator = plain modern English, NEVER KJV; Jesus = EXACT KJV only)
1. NARRATOR: In the dark garden, the men who came to arrest Jesus closed in. One of his own friends struck out — and cut the ear off the servant of the high priest.
2. NARRATOR: It would have been the moment to fight back. Instead, Jesus did the opposite of what anyone expected. *(sacred-silence beat)*
3. NARRATOR: He spoke, and then he reached out and healed the man who had come to take him.
4. JESUS (KJV, Luke 22:51): "Suffer ye thus far. And he touched his ear, and healed him."
5. NARRATOR: The last thing Jesus did as a free man was mend the ear of his enemy. He would not let even this moment be about violence.
6. CLOSING CARD (invitation, never fear): "He healed the one sent to seize him. There is no enemy too far for his mercy — including the one in you."

## STORYBOARD (8–10 beats — Jesus FACE-SHOWN, adult master face; violence implied, never shown)
1. Exterior night, olive trees, torchlight: armed men approaching through the dark; tension, not gore.
2. A sudden movement in the firelight — a swordsman's arm raised, a figure staggering back; the moment of the cut implied, no blood, no wound detail.
3. Jesus stepping forward, calm, between the two — Face SHOWN, cream robe, Middle Eastern, warm brown eyes, no halo. Master-locked.
4. Close on Jesus's face, unhurried, choosing mercy; the scene holds its breath. *(sacred-silence beat — the still rests on his calm face in the torchlight)*
5. Jesus's hand reaching toward the servant's ear — gentle, healing; the servant's face turning from shock toward wonder.
6. The servant, whole, touching his ear, staring at the man being arrested.
7. Jesus surrounded now by the arresting crowd, still composed, having already given the last free gift.
8. Closing still: the garden at dawn, quiet — the place where mercy interrupted the worst night. Reverent, not grim.
