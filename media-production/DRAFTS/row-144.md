# DRAFT row-144 — "I am the resurrection and the life" — John 11:25–26

> Row 144 · Ref: John 11:25–26 · Jesus FACE-SHOWN. CONTENT-CARE (gentle, death handled with hope, no gore).

## HOMOGRAPH FLAGS: live(/liv/) — appears in KJV John 11:25–26 "yet shall he live" and "whosoever liveth" (say /liv/, not /laiv/). No bow/wound/wind/tears/lead/sow. Ear-check anyway.

## NARRATION (narrator = plain modern English, NEVER KJV; Jesus = EXACT KJV only)
1. NARRATOR: Lazarus had been in the tomb four days. His sister Martha came to Jesus, heavy with grief but still full of faith.
2. NARRATOR: She said she believed her brother would rise again at the last day. Jesus answered with a promise bigger than the last day. *(sacred-silence beat)*
3. JESUS (KJV, John 11:25): "I am the resurrection, and the life: he that believeth in me, though he were dead, yet shall he live:"
4. JESUS (KJV, John 11:26): "And whosoever liveth and believeth in me shall never die. Believest thou this?"
5. NARRATOR: Not a theory about life after death. He said He Himself is the life — and that trusting Him beats the grave.
6. NARRATOR: Martha said yes. And moments later, her brother walked out of the tomb.
7. CLOSING CARD (invitation, never fear): "Death is not the end of the story. He is the life."

## STORYBOARD (8–12 beats — CONTENT-CARE: hope-filled, faces carry it, no tomb gore)
1. Exterior, daytime, Bethany: a small gathering of mourners outside a stone tomb, gentle daylight.
2. Close on Martha's face as she speaks to Jesus, grief and faith mixed.
3. Close on Jesus's face as He meets her — steady, tender. Jesus FACE-SHOWN, master-locked, warm brown eyes, only-he-cream, no halo. *(sacred-silence beat — the still holds on His face)*
4. Two-shot: Jesus and Martha, He speaking the promise of resurrection.
5. Illustrative: a closed tomb, then light spilling from the seam — hope, not dread.
6. Close on Jesus's face as He asks "Believest thou this?" — searching, kind.
7. Wide: the village of Bethany under a warming sky, the weight lifting.
8. Closing still: an open tomb with light, a figure walking free — resurrection, not horror.
