## NARRATION (narrator = plain modern English, NEVER KJV; JESUS lines = EXACT KJV only)
1. NARRATOR: A question was put to Israel long ago — who gets to climb the hill of the LORD and stand in his holy place?
2. NARRATOR: The answer was not about bloodline or rank. It was about clean hands and a pure heart — someone who has not lifted their soul to what is false.
3. NARRATOR: Such a one receives blessing from the LORD, and righteousness from the God of their salvation.
4. NARRATOR: Then the call goes out to the gates themselves — lift up your heads, you ancient doors, and let the King of glory come in. *(sacred-silence beat)*
5. NARRATOR: Who is this King of glory? The LORD strong and mighty, the LORD mighty in battle. He is the one who comes in.
6. CLOSING CARD (invitation, never fear): "The door is open to the one with a clean heart. Come and stand in his presence."

## JESUS LINES: none — this is OT Psalm poetry; Jesus does not appear in frame. (Per spec: for passages where Jesus doesn't appear, say so.)

## STORYBOARD (8–12 beats — CONTENT-CARE: reverent, symbolic, no Christ figure)
1. Exterior dawn: the hill of the LORD, mist over the valley, no figure — the land set apart.
2. A traveler's sandaled feet pausing at the base, looking up — approach motif.
3. Close on weathered hands lifted, open palms — "clean hands" symbol, soft gold light.
4. A person bowed in stillness, face calm, "pure heart" — morning, interior.
5. The ancient city gates, heavy wood, shut, with a thin line of light at the seam.
6. The gates trembling, light pressing through the cracks — anticipation. *(sacred-silence beat — the still holds on the doors)*
7. Doors flung wide, dawn pouring through, glory implied by radiance, not a face.
8. Wide reverent shot: hill, sky, open gate — the King of glory has come in.
9. Closing still: an empty path up the hill, invitation, hopeful dawn.
