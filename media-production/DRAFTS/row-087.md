## NARRATION (narrator = plain modern English, NEVER KJV; Jesus = EXACT KJV only)
1. NARRATOR: Every year Jesus's family went up to Jerusalem for Passover. The year he was twelve, they went as always — but the trip home didn't go as planned.
2. NARRATOR: A full day into the journey, Mary and Joseph realized he wasn't with the group. They hurried back, searching the whole city with rising panic. *(sacred-silence beat)*
3. NARRATOR: Three days later they found him — in the temple, sitting among the teachers, listening and asking questions. Everyone around him was amazed at his understanding.
4. NARRATOR: Mary said what any mother would: son, why have you done this to us? Your father and I have been searching everywhere, sick with worry.
5. JESUS (KJV, Luke 2:49): "How is it that ye sought me? wist ye not that I must be about my Father's business?"
6. NARRATOR: Even at twelve, he already knew whose he was. Then he went home with them to Nazareth and was obedient — and grew in wisdom, and in favor with God and people.
7. CLOSING CARD (invitation, never fear): "Even as a boy, he knew exactly who his Father was. He can help you know it too."

## STORYBOARD (8–16 beats)
1. Exterior day, warm: a crowd of pilgrims streaming out of Jerusalem's gates on the road home, families walking together in the dust and light.
2. On the road: Mary turning, scanning the group, a dawning worry on her face; Joseph beside her. 
3. Back inside Jerusalem: Mary and Joseph hurrying through crowded streets, searching, faces anxious. *(sacred-silence beat — the still holds on their searching)*
4. Interior temple court, soft daylight: the boy Jesus (about twelve) seated among bearded teachers, leaning in, engaged; the teachers listening to HIM with surprise.
5. Close on the boy's face — bright, calm, unafraid, wise beyond his years. (Boy Jesus — young, NOT the adult master-ref; but same warm brown eyes, dark hair.)
6. Mary and Joseph arriving in the doorway, relief and exasperation both on Mary's face as she reaches for him.
7. The boy answering gently, gesturing slightly upward — "my Father's business" — teachers and parents caught by the words.
8. Closing still: the family walking home together toward Nazareth in warm evening light, the boy between them.
