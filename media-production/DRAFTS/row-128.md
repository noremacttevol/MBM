# DRAFT row-128 — The famine of hearing — Amos 8:11–12

> Row 128 · Ref: Amos 8:11–12 · Jesus does NOT appear (Old Testament prophecy). CONTENT-CARE: none.

## HOMOGRAPH FLAGS: "hearing" clean. No bow/wound/wind/tears/lead/sow/live. Clean. Ear-check: none.

## NARRATION (narrator = plain modern English; Lord's word = EXACT KJV via Amos)
1. NARRATOR: The prophet Amos carried a warning from the Lord — not about food, but about something people would miss even more.
2. NARRATOR: A day would come when God's voice would seem far away — not because He stopped speaking, but because people stopped being able to hear.
3. THE LORD (through Amos, KJV, Amos 8:11): "Behold, the days come, saith the Lord GOD, that I will send a famine in the land, not a famine of bread, nor a thirst for water, but of hearing the words of the LORD:"
4. THE LORD (through Amos, KJV, Amos 8:12): "And they shall wander from sea to sea, and from the north even to the east, they shall run to and fro to seek the word of the LORD, and shall not find it."
5. NARRATOR: The deepest hunger isn't in the stomach. It's the ache of a soul that can't find a word from God. *(sacred-silence beat)*
6. NARRATOR: The cure was never far. It was to listen while His voice could still be heard.
7. CLOSING CARD (invitation, never fear): "When His word is near, don't let it pass. Listen now — the famine is the silence we choose."

## STORYBOARD (6 beats — no Jesus, Old Testament scene)
1. Exterior, dusk, a lone figure (Amos) on a hill, scroll in hand, addressing a distant town. NO Jesus.
2. A table with an empty bread basket and empty cup — and a person with a faraway, hungry look in the eyes (the real famine is within).
3. Still: people wandering, searching, hands cupped to ear — seeking a voice and finding quiet.
4. Contrast: a single open scroll glowing softly in a quiet room — the word that IS available.
5. Close on a face turned upward, listening — the posture of one who hears. *(sacred-silence beat)*
6. Closing still: an open window, light, a whisper made visible as soft light. Hopeful, not dreadful.
