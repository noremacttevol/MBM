## NARRATION (narrator = plain modern English, NEVER KJV; Jesus = EXACT KJV only)
1. NARRATOR: In a house in Bethany, days before the end, a woman came to Jesus at the table carrying an alabaster jar of pure, costly perfume.
2. NARRATOR: She broke the jar open and poured all of it over his head — a year's wages, gone in a moment. *(sacred-silence beat — narration cuts to silence on the pouring)*
3. NARRATOR: Some at the table were angry. That could have been sold and given to the poor. They scolded her.
4. NARRATOR: Jesus stopped them cold, and defended her.
5. JESUS (KJV, Mark 14:6): "Let her alone; why trouble ye her? she hath wrought a good work on me."
6. JESUS (KJV, Mark 14:8): "She hath done what she could."
7. NARRATOR: She gave the most extravagant thing she owned, and he received it as beautiful. Not wasteful — worship.
8. CLOSING CARD (invitation, never fear): "She gave him her best and he called it beautiful. What would it mean to stop holding back from him?"

## STORYBOARD (8–16 beats)
1. Interior evening, warm lamplight: a low dinner table, guests reclining, Jesus among them. Face SHOWN, cream robe.
2. A woman entering with an alabaster jar cradled in both hands, quiet resolve on her face; the room beginning to notice.
3. Close: her hands breaking the slender neck of the alabaster jar. 
4. The perfume pouring over Jesus's head and hair, a thin fragrant ribbon catching lamplight; his eyes closing, receiving it. *(sacred-silence beat — the still holds, room hushed)*
5. Several guests half-risen, faces indignant, gesturing — the scolding, tension not violence.
6. Jesus, calm and firm, one hand raised to stop them, warm brown eyes defending her. Master-locked.
7. The woman's face — relief, tears held back (painted-teardrop law if shown), gratitude — as he honors her.
8. Closing still: the broken alabaster jar and a soft sheen of spilled perfume in warm lamplight — everything poured out, nothing held back.
