## NARRATION (narrator = plain modern English, NEVER KJV; Jesus = EXACT KJV only)
1. NARRATOR: Some officials came to Jesus with a question built to trap him — flattering him first, so he'd let his guard down.
2. NARRATOR: Is it lawful to pay taxes to Caesar, or not? Say yes, and the crowd turns on you. Say no, and Rome arrests you. There was no safe answer. *(sacred-silence beat)*
3. NARRATOR: Jesus saw straight through it. He asked them to hand him a coin.
4. JESUS (KJV, Mark 12:16): "Whose is this image and superscription?" — They said, "Caesar's."
5. JESUS (KJV, Mark 12:17): "Render to Caesar the things that are Caesar's, and to God the things that are God's."
6. NARRATOR: The coin bore Caesar's image, so give it back to Caesar. But you bear God's image — so the real question is what you owe to the One whose face you carry.
7. CLOSING CARD (invitation, never fear): "The coin belonged to Caesar because it bore his face. You bear God's. What does that make you worth to him?"

## STORYBOARD (8–16 beats)
1. Interior temple court, warm daylight: a cluster of well-dressed officials approaching Jesus with practiced smiles; Jesus calm among listeners. Face SHOWN, cream robe.
2. Close on the officials' faces — false warmth, sharp eyes — the flattery masking the trap.
3. Jesus's face, warm brown eyes, unbothered and knowing, seeing the snare. Master-locked.
4. A hand extending a Roman silver coin (denarius) into the light between them. *(sacred-silence beat — the still holds on the coin)*
5. Close on the coin: a stamped profile portrait and lettering, catching the light in Jesus's open palm.
6. Jesus holding the coin up, asking whose image it bears; the officials answering, already losing the game.
7. Wide: Jesus handing the coin back, the officials stilled and speechless, the listening crowd quietly amazed.
8. Closing still: the coin resting in warm light beside an open hand — Caesar's face on metal, God's image on the person.
