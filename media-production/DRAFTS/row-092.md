# DRAFT row-092 — Peter's denial and the look — Luke 22:54–62

> Row 92 · Ref: Luke 22:54–62 · Jesus FACE-SHOWN (one pivotal look).

## HOMOGRAPH FLAGS: "wept" clean. "crew" (the cock crew) clean. No bow/wound/wind/tears/lead/sow/live. Ear-check "crew" and "wept".

## NARRATION (narrator = plain modern English, NEVER KJV; Jesus = EXACT KJV only)
1. NARRATOR: After they arrested Jesus, Peter followed at a distance and warmed himself by a fire in the courtyard, trying not to be noticed.
2. NARRATOR: A servant girl looked at him and said, this man was with him too. Peter said, woman, I don't know him.
3. NARRATOR: A little later, someone else. Then a third. Each time Peter denied it harder — I don't know what you're talking about. *(sacred-silence beat)*
4. NARRATOR: And right then, while the words were still in his mouth, a rooster crowed.
5. NARRATOR: Across the courtyard, Jesus turned and looked straight at Peter. Just looked at him.
6. JESUS: [no spoken line — the LOOK is the moment; silence]
7. NARRATOR: And Peter remembered — Jesus had told him this would happen. He went outside and wept bitterly. But the look wasn't scorn. It was the face of someone who already knew, and already loved him anyway.
8. CLOSING CARD (invitation, never fear): "He looked at Peter in his worst moment — and still chose him. He looks at you the same way."

## STORYBOARD (8–14 beats)
1. Exterior night, cold blue with warm firelight: a courtyard, a charcoal fire, Peter huddled among servants and guards, hood up.
2. A servant girl in firelight pointing at Peter, others turning; Peter's face tight with fear.
3. Close on Peter shaking his head, denying — fear and shame gathering. 
4. A rooster on a low wall against the pre-dawn sky, mid-crow. *(sacred-silence beat — the still holds on the rooster and the paling sky)*
5. Across the courtyard: Jesus, bound, being led past — turning his head to meet Peter's eyes. Face SHOWN, master-locked — sorrow and love, NOT anger.
6. Peter's face breaking as their eyes meet — the instant of remembering.
7. Peter stumbling out through a gate into the dark, alone, one painted teardrop (painted-teardrop law).
8. Closing still: the dying courtyard fire and the first grey light of dawn — grief that isn't the end of the story.

## NOTES: Jesus FACE-SHOWN only in shot 5 (the look) — master-locked, expression of love/sorrow. CONTENT-CARE: no violence to Jesus shown (he's led past, bound, no wounds); Peter's shame handled with dignity, ONE teardrop. ~60-70s. Narrator Andrew.
