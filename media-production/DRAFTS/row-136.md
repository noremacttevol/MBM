# DRAFT row-136 — The blind man healed in two touches — Mark 8:22–26

> Row 136 · Ref: Mark 8:22–26 · Jesus FACE-SHOWN. CONTENT-CARE (healing, gentle, no bodily detail in focus).

## HOMOGRAPH FLAGS: No bow/wound/wind/tears/lead/sow/live/lives/lived. Clean. Ear-check anyway.

## NARRATION (narrator = plain modern English, NEVER KJV; Jesus = EXACT KJV only)
1. NARRATOR: Friends brought a blind man to Jesus in Bethsaida and begged Him just to touch him.
2. NARRATOR: Jesus took the man by the hand and walked him outside the village. He spit on the man's eyes and laid His hands on him, then asked if he could see anything yet.
3. NARRATOR: The man looked up and said the strangest thing — he could see, but only halfway. People looked like trees walking around.
4. NARRATOR: So Jesus put His hands on the man's eyes a second time and told him to look up again.
5. JESUS (KJV, Mark 8:26): "Neither go into the town, nor tell it to any in the town."
6. NARRATOR: And this time he saw everything clearly — every face, every leaf. The second touch finished what the first had started.
7. CLOSING CARD (invitation, never fear): "Maybe your faith came in stages too. He is patient enough to touch you twice."

## STORYBOARD (8–12 beats — CONTENT-CARE: healing shown gently, never the body in focus)
1. Exterior, daytime, edge of Bethsaida: friends leading a blind man toward Jesus; Jesus in frame, calm.
2. Jesus takes the man's hand, walking him out past the last houses; warm daylight, gentle.
3. Close: Jesus lays hands on the man's eyes, leaning in tender — faces near, not graphic. Jesus FACE-SHOWN, master-locked, warm brown eyes, only-he-cream, no halo.
4. The man squints, unsure — partial sight; he gestures at blurry figures ("I see men as trees, walking").
5. Jesus lays hands on his eyes a second time, close again; the two faces near. *(sacred-silence beat — the still holds the two faces)*
6. The man's eyes open wide, clarity dawning; he looks at Jesus and smiles.
7. Closing still: the man walking back toward the village, seeing the world for the first time — light, hope. Reverent, warm.
