## NARRATION (narrator = plain modern English, NEVER KJV; Jesus = EXACT KJV only)
1. NARRATOR: As Jesus came near Jerusalem, he sent two disciples ahead for a young donkey no one had ever ridden — exactly as the prophets had said the true king would come.
2. NARRATOR: They threw their cloaks over the colt, and he rode down the Mount of Olives toward the city.
3. NARRATOR: The whole crowd of his followers began to praise God with loud joy, spreading their coats on the road in front of him. *(sacred-silence beat — the still holds on the road of cloaks)*
4. NARRATOR: They shouted blessings on the king who comes in the name of the Lord — peace in heaven, glory in the highest.
5. NARRATOR: Some religious leaders told him to make his followers stop. His answer was unforgettable.
6. JESUS (KJV, Luke 19:40): "I tell you that, if these should hold their peace, the stones would immediately cry out."
7. NARRATOR: The praise was too true to be silenced. If people wouldn't say it, the very rocks would.
8. CLOSING CARD (invitation, never fear): "Creation itself was ready to shout who he is. What's holding your voice back?"

## STORYBOARD (8–16 beats)
1. Exterior day, warm gold: two disciples leading a young donkey through a village lane, owners looking on; the Mount of Olives beyond.
2. Jesus mounting the colt, disciples laying their folded cloaks across its back. Face SHOWN, cream robe.
3. Jesus riding down the slope toward Jerusalem, the golden city spread below in the valley — humble king on a humble colt.
4. The road ahead: followers pulling off their cloaks and spreading them on the ground, waving palm branches, faces alight. *(sacred-silence beat — the still holds on the carpet of cloaks)*
5. Wide: a jubilant crowd lining the road, arms raised, children running alongside — joy and celebration.
6. A cluster of stern religious leaders at the edge, frowning, gesturing for quiet.
7. Close on Jesus's face, calm and certain, warm brown eyes, speaking about the stones crying out. Master-locked.
8. Closing still: scattered cloaks and palm branches on the empty road in warm light, a stray stone catching the sun — even it ready to cry out.
