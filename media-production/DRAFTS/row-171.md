# DRAFT row-171 — "Else what shall they do which are baptized for the dead?" — 1 Corinthians 15:29

> Row 171 · Ref: 1 Corinthians 15:29 · Jesus does NOT appear as a character (Pauline teaching). CONTENT-CARE: no violence, hope-forward, mercy spoken.

## HOMOGRAPH FLAGS: live(/liv/) — "for all who belong to Him"; no bow/wound/wind/tears/lead/sow. Clean. Ear-check anyway.

## NARRATION (narrator = plain modern English, NEVER KJV; Jesus = EXACT KJV only — none in this passage)
1. NARRATOR: Paul asked a striking question: if the dead don't rise at all, why would anyone be baptized on their behalf?
2. NARRATOR: The only reason to do such a thing is the quiet hope that the dead are not gone forever.
3. NARRATOR: Baptism stands for new life — a beginning, not an end. *(sacred-silence beat)*
4. NARRATOR: So the work done for those who've passed is built on one promise: that death is not the last word.
5. NARRATOR: Christ rose. And because He rose, the grave loses its grip — for Him first, and then for all who belong to Him.
6. NARRATOR: The ordinance done in love reaches across the veil, offering every soul the chance to choose.
7. CLOSING CARD (invitation, never fear): "Death separates for a while, not forever. Because He lives, there is hope for every name on the other side of the veil."

## STORYBOARD (8–11 beats — CONTENT-CARE: reverent, no violence; Jesus does NOT appear)
1. Exterior, open scroll/letter motif: Paul's words rendered as text on parchment, calm study light.
2. Symbolic: a living plant breaking through soil — baptism as new life, not death.
3. Two figures at a font, one standing in for another not present — the vicarious act, shown with dignity.
4. A doorway of light framing the scene — the hope beyond. *(sacred-silence beat — the still holds on the light)*
5. Empty tomb at dawn, stone rolled, soft radiance — the resurrection that anchors the hope.
6. The veil suggested as a thin luminous curtain, a hand reaching through in love.
7. Many names implied as gentle light-points beyond the veil — every soul invited.
8. Closing still: a single candle passed hand to hand across generations — continuity, not loss.
