## HOMOGRAPH FLAGS: Clean. No bow/wound/wind/tears/lead/sow/live. Ear-check anyway.

## NARRATION (narrator = plain modern English, NEVER KJV; Jesus = EXACT KJV only)
1. NARRATOR: They had nailed him to the cross. The soldiers divided his clothes and cast lots. The crowd watched. And from the cross, Jesus spoke first — not in anger, but in prayer. *(sacred-silence beat)*
2. NARRATOR: He lifted his voice to his Father on behalf of the very ones putting him there. He asked for their forgiveness, because they did not understand what they were doing.
3. JESUS (KJV, Luke 23:34): "Then said Jesus, Father, forgive them; for they know not what they do."
4. NARRATOR: No curse. No call for vengeance. Just a plea for mercy over the people who were killing him.
5. NARRATOR: The first words from the cross were not about his own pain. They were about the souls of his killers. That is the heart of the One who came to save.
6. NARRATOR: If he could ask forgiveness for them in that moment, then there is no one his mercy cannot reach.
7. CLOSING CARD (invitation, never fear): "His first cry from the cross was for mercy on others. Come — that mercy is for you too."

## STORYBOARD (7 beats — CONTENT-CARE: crucifixion reverent distance, faces not suffering)
1. Exterior, midday dimming: three crosses on the hill in dignified silhouette/mid-distance; focus is sky and faces, never suffering detail.
2. The soldiers at the foot of the cross, casting lots for garments; composed, no gore.
3. Close on Jesus's face, lifted toward heaven in prayer. Jesus FACE-SHOWN — master-locked, Middle Eastern, warm brown eyes, only-he-cream, no halo; face tender, not in pain.
4. The crowd below, faces turned up, unaware; dignity kept. *(sacred-silence beat — still holds on Jesus's face in prayer)*
5. A wide reverent beat: the hill, the sky, the center cross — grace offered in the darkest hour.
6. Close again on Jesus's face speaking the prayer of forgiveness — love in his eyes, not bitterness.
7. Closing still: soft light breaking over the hill — forgiveness spoken into the world. Reverent, hopeful.
