## NARRATION — numbered segments

Two-Voice Law: narrator = plain modern English, never echoes KJV wording. Jesus = exact KJV only. The woman's reply (v28) is paraphrased by the narrator in modern English.

**n1** — NARRATOR:
"Jesus had been going nonstop — crowds pressing in, arguments with religious leaders, thousands fed — and he finally slipped away north, across the border into the region of Tyre and Sidon. Foreign territory. He went into a house quietly, hoping to stay unnoticed. It didn't work. Word got out. With him, it always did."

**n2** — NARRATOR:
"Because one woman heard he was there. She was a Greek — Syrophoenician, born on the wrong side of every line that mattered in Israel: wrong nation, wrong religion, wrong history. And she had a little girl at home in the grip of a dark spirit that no one could drive out. A daughter she could not rescue, no matter what she tried."

**n3** — NARRATOR:
"So she came uninvited, walked straight in, and dropped to the floor at his feet — a Gentile mother kneeling in front of a Jewish rabbi — and she begged him, over and over, to free her daughter."

**n4** — NARRATOR:
"And Jesus answered her with one of the hardest sentences he ever said to a person asking for help."

**j1** — JESUS (exact KJV, Mark 7:27):
"Let the children first be filled: for it is not meet to take the children's bread, and to cast it unto the dogs."

**n5** — NARRATOR:
"Sit with how that sounded. The children meant Israel — God's covenant family, first in line for the Messiah's ministry. The dogs meant outsiders. Like her. It sounds like a door closing in her face."

**n6** — NARRATOR:
"But she heard what most of us miss. He said first — and first means the outsiders' turn is coming. And the word he chose was not the word for the strays in the street; it was the word for the little dogs of the household — the ones that live under the family's own table. She wasn't outside the house at all. She was already in the room. Inside that hard sentence she found a door, and she went through it."

**n7** — NARRATOR:
"Her answer has been famous for two thousand years. Yes, Lord, she said — and even the little dogs under the table get to eat what the children drop. She didn't argue with the order of things. She didn't demand a better seat. She took the lowest place in his sentence and asked for the leftovers — because she was certain that even his leftovers were enough to save her daughter."

**n8** — NARRATOR:
"And you can almost hear the smile in his answer."

**j2** — JESUS (exact KJV, Mark 7:29):
"For this saying go thy way; the devil is gone out of thy daughter."

**n9** — NARRATOR:
"Notice what he gave her: no gesture, no visit to the house, no proof. Just his word that it was already done. And she took it. She got up off that floor, walked home, and found her little girl resting on the bed — quiet, and free. It had happened the moment he said it."

**n10** — NARRATOR:
"Some people read that scene as Jesus being talked into something. Read it again. He had just crossed into Gentile country — he brought the bread to her side of the border. The hard sentence wasn't a wall; it was an invitation with a handle on it, and she was the one with faith enough to grab it. He didn't change his mind that day. He showed the whole room — and every reader since — what faith looks like when it refuses to let go."

**n11** — NARRATOR:
"Some of us have been told, or have told ourselves, that we're the wrong kind of person to ask God for anything — wrong past, wrong family, wrong record. This story exists for exactly that lie. Persistence isn't rudeness in prayer; it's faith. Come anyway. Ask anyway. Keep asking. He answers honest boldness — and his crumbs are still bread."

**card** — NARRATOR (read aloud over closing question card):
"She was the wrong nationality, the wrong religion, and she interrupted him — and she went home with everything she asked for. What have you decided you're not allowed to ask God for?"
