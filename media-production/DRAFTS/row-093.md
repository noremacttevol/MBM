# DRAFT row-093 — Barabbas goes free — Mark 15:6–15

> Row 93 · Ref: Mark 15:6–15 · Jesus present, face may be shown briefly (silent, dignified).

## HOMOGRAPH FLAGS: No bow/wound/wind/tears/lead/sow/live. Clean. Ear-check anyway.

## NARRATION (narrator = plain modern English, NEVER KJV; Jesus = EXACT KJV only — no Jesus speech)
1. NARRATOR: It was the custom at Passover for the governor to release one prisoner the crowd chose. Pilate had two men: Jesus, and a killer named Barabbas.
2. NARRATOR: Pilate could see Jesus had done nothing worth death. He thought the crowd would surely pick the innocent one. *(sacred-silence beat)*
3. NARRATOR: But the leaders stirred the crowd, and they shouted for Barabbas. Pilate asked, then what about Jesus? And they cried, crucify him.
4. NARRATOR: So the guilty man walked free, and the innocent one was handed over in his place.
5. NARRATOR: Think about what Barabbas got: he was condemned, and then someone else took his exact sentence. He walked out free because Jesus took his cross.
6. NARRATOR: That's not just Barabbas's story. That's the whole gospel in one swap — the innocent for the guilty, so the guilty could go free.
7. CLOSING CARD (invitation, never fear): "Barabbas walked free because Jesus took his place. So can you. That's the whole point."

## STORYBOARD (8–12 beats)
1. Exterior day, hard light: a stone platform before a crowd; Pilate standing, two prisoners flanking — Jesus calm in cream, a rough hard-faced Barabbas in chains.
2. Close on Pilate's face, troubled, weighing it; the crowd a restless sea below.
3. Jesus standing quiet and dignified, hands bound, no wounds shown, eyes steady. Face SHOWN briefly, master-locked — peace, not fear.
4. The crowd erupting, arms raised, mouths open, shouting a name. *(sacred-silence beat — the still holds on the roaring crowd, silent)*
5. Barabbas's chains being struck off, disbelief on his hard face as he realizes he's free.
6. Barabbas walking away into the light, looking back once at the man left standing on the platform.
7. Closing still: two empty sets of shackles on the stone — one broken open and abandoned, freedom bought by an exchange.

## NOTES: Jesus face SHOWN briefly (shot 3) — dignified, NO wounds, no scourging depicted (content-care). The theology of substitution is the whole point — narration carries it. ~60-70s. Narrator Andrew only.
