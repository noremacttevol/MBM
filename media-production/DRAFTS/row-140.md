# DRAFT row-140 — The road to the far country runs both ways — Luke 15:11–32

> Row 140 · Ref: Luke 15:11–32 · Jesus NOT shown (parable teller, off-frame). CONTENT-CARE (gentle, no violence, no shame).

## HOMOGRAPH FLAGS: No bow/wound/wind/tears/lead/sow/live/lives/lived. Clean. Ear-check anyway.

## NARRATION (narrator = plain modern English, NEVER KJV; Jesus = EXACT KJV only)
1. NARRATOR: Jesus told a story about a father and two sons. The younger one wanted his share of the inheritance now — and he got it.
2. NARRATOR: He left home and went to a country far away. There he spent everything on a life that looked free and felt empty. *(sacred-silence beat)*
3. NARRATOR: A hard season came. He was hungry, alone, and tending pigs — and he came to himself.
4. JESUS (KJV, Luke 15:11): "And he said, A certain man had two sons:"
5. NARRATOR: He decided to go back. Not as a son claiming rights, but as a servant asking for a place.
6. NARRATOR: But while he was still far off, his father saw him and ran. No lecture. No waiting for the apology. He threw his arms around him.
7. NARRATOR: The father gave him the robe, the ring, the sandals — belonging, not probation. And there was a feast, because what was lost had come home.
8. NARRATOR: But the older brother stood outside, angry that grace looked so easy. The door ran both ways — it was open for him too.
9. CLOSING CARD (invitation, never fear): "The road home is always open. However far you've gone, turn around — He's already running to meet you."

## STORYBOARD (8–12 beats — CONTENT-CARE: gentle, faces and the open door carry it)
1. Exterior, daytime, a family homestead: father and two sons, the younger restless. Jesus is NOT shown — this is a parable He tells; only the parable characters appear.
2. The younger son receiving his share, turning to leave — open road, rising dust.
3. Far country, dusk: the son among strangers, spending freely, laughter that fades.
4. Far country, harsh daylight: the son among pigs, hunched, hollow. *(sacred-silence beat — the still holds his empty face)*
5. Interior, night: the son alone, remembering home, a decision forming.
6. The long road back, dawn: a small figure walking toward home.
7. Homestead, morning: the father running, robes flying, arms open — the embrace.
8. Interior, warm lamplight: the robe, the ring, the feast beginning.
9. Exterior, the homestead edge: the older brother standing apart, arms crossed, the open door behind him.
10. Closing still: two roads meeting at one door — the father's house, light spilling out. Invitation.
