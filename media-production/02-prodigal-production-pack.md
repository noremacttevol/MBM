# Story Video #2 — The Prodigal Son (Luke 15:11–32)

Runtime 75–90s. Prepend the STYLE BLOCK from 00-MASTER-PLAN.md to every shot
prompt. This is a parable — Jesus is the storyteller, not in frame; the
father's face MAY be shown (he represents the Father in the story, he is not
a depiction of Deity's actual person — if this ever feels wrong on review,
fall back to filming the father from behind/hands only).

## Narration script (app's exact words)

> A father had two sons. The younger one asked for his inheritance early —
> essentially wishing his father were already dead — and left to spend
> everything on a life that emptied him.
>
> When he had nothing left and was starving, he came to his senses. He
> rehearsed a speech the whole walk home: "Make me one of your servants. I am
> not worthy to be called your son."
>
> He was still a long way off when his father saw him.
>
> **The father ran.** He didn't wait for the speech. He threw his arms around
> him before a single word was said. *(peak — music cuts to silence on "The
> father ran.")*
>
> "This son of mine was dead and is alive again. He was lost and is found."

Closing card (hold 6s): *"Which part of that story feels closest to something
you have carried or are carrying right now?"*

## Shot list

### Shot 1 — The leaving
```
A young man in his early twenties walks away from a prosperous farm estate at
dawn, a heavy money pouch at his belt, not looking back. Behind him in the
soft-focus distance, an older man stands motionless in the gateway watching
him go. Long shadows, cold morning light.
```

### Shot 2 — The emptying
```
Quick tonal montage feel in one shot: the same young man, now gaunt and
ragged, kneels in a muddy pig pen at dusk, rain beginning to fall, holding an
empty wooden feed trough. His fine clothes are torn and filthy. He stares at
his empty hands.
```

### Shot 3 — Coming to his senses / rehearsing the speech
```
The young man walks a long empty dirt road through barren hills, lips moving
silently as he rehearses words to himself, eyes down, rehearsed shame on his
face. Handheld camera walking backward in front of him. Overcast light slowly
warming as the road goes on.
```

### Shot 4 — Seen from far off
```
Wide shot from a rooftop or hilltop: a tiny ragged figure appears at the far
end of a long road at golden hour. In the foreground, an older man on a flat
rooftop snaps to his feet, hand shielding his eyes, body suddenly rigid with
recognition. Wind moves his gray robe.
```

### Shot 5 — THE FATHER RUNS (the money shot — generate extra takes)
```
Slow motion: an older man in long robes RUNS down a dirt road at golden hour,
robes hiked up in one fist, sandals kicking dust, undignified and absolutely
determined, tears streaking into his beard, arms already opening. The camera
tracks alongside him low and fast. Backlit dust glows gold around him.
```

### Shot 6 — The embrace before the speech
```
The father collides into his ragged son and wraps him completely in his arms,
gripping the back of his head, kissing his filthy hair. The son's rehearsed
words die; his face crumples against his father's shoulder. Held long. Dust
settling gold around them.
```

### Shot 7 — Dead and alive again
```
Night, warm firelight: a feast in a courtyard. The son, washed, wearing a fine
robe and a ring, sits stunned with tears in his eyes while the household
celebrates around him. The father's hand rests on his shoulder. Lanterns,
music implied, joy.
```

### Closing card
Text on cream, serif. No narration.

## The Seed (what this video must leave behind)

The father RUNS — before the speech, before the repentance is even spoken. This is not a father who predestined his son's leaving for his own glory, and not one who waits with crossed arms for groveling. His glory IS the running. Quiet question: *is the Father you've been told about waiting — or running?* Carried by shots 4–6.
