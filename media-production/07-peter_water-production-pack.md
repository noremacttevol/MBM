# Story Video #7 — Peter Walks on Water (Matthew 14:22–33)

Runtime 60–75s. Prepend the STYLE BLOCK from 00-MASTER-PLAN.md. Jesus: a
luminous figure at distance / silhouette against the storm / a gripping hand
— never his face. Night exterior water is hard for generators: generate
extra takes on shots 2, 4, 5.

## Narration script (app's exact words)

> The disciples were in a boat at night when they saw Jesus walking toward
> them on the water.
>
> Peter said: "Lord, if it is you, tell me to come to you on the water."
>
> Jesus said: "Come." *(held beat — the word alone)*
>
> Peter got out of the boat. He was doing it — actually walking on water.
> Then he noticed the wind. He looked at the waves. He started to sink.
>
> Jesus caught him immediately. He said: "Why did you doubt?" Not as
> condemnation. As a real question worth sitting with. *(peak — music cuts
> to silence on "immediately")*

Closing card (hold 6s): *"Have you ever had something real — a moment of
faith, a sense of something true — and then watched it slip when the storm
got loud?"*

## Shot list

### Shot 1 — The boat at night
```
A wooden fishing boat pitches on rough open water in windy darkness,
lantern swinging, a handful of weathered men gripping the gunwales, spray
flying. Moonlight tears through fast clouds. Fear on wet faces.
```

### Shot 2 — The figure on the water
```
From inside the boat, the men's point of view: far out across the heaving
black water, a faint luminous figure walks calmly ON the surface of the sea
toward the boat, robes moving in the wind, too distant for any face — only
light holding a human shape above the waves.
```

### Shot 3 — One leg over the side
```
Close on a burly fisherman's face — terror and longing fighting each other —
then his hands on the gunwale, then one bare leg swinging over the side of
the pitching boat toward black water, while the other men grab at his cloak
in disbelief. Wind screams.
```

### Shot 4 — He was doing it (the money shot — extra takes)
```
Wide, impossible, beautiful: a fisherman stands upright ON the surface of
the night sea, arms slightly out, taking one astonished step and then
another across the moving water, moonlight silvering the wave-tops, eyes
fixed forward on the distant luminous figure. Sacred and terrifying at once.
```

### Shot 5 — The wind, the waves, the sinking
```
The fisherman's eyes break away — down to the churning waves, out to the
white spray — and the instant they do, the water stops bearing him: he drops
to his waist in the sea mid-stride, arms flailing up, mouth open in a cry
swallowed by wind.
```

### Shot 6 — Caught immediately
```
Extreme close up, slow motion: a strong hand shoots down into frame and
clamps around the sinking man's forearm the moment it rises — instantly, no
gap, no hesitation. Water sheets off both arms. The grip is absolute. Faces
out of frame: only the two arms, the sea, and the hold that doesn't slip.
```

### Shot 7 — Back in the boat, the calm
```
The soaked fisherman sits shaking in the bottom of the boat on suddenly
gentle water — the storm gone, stars out, the sea flat and silver. He stares
at his own forearm where he was held, then out at the calm, working out what
just happened to him.
```

### Closing card
Text on cream, serif. No narration.

## The Seed (what this video must leave behind)

"Why did you doubt?" — asked GENTLY, by the hand that had already caught him. Doubt is a question worth sitting with, not a damnation; and the catch came IMMEDIATELY, mid-sink. Quiet question: *is the God you learned about one who catches doubters — or drops them?* Carried by shot 6.

## Red-letter lines (KJV) — the Jesus voice speaks ONLY these

1. **Where:** over shot 2 (the figure on the water), replacing any narrator
   paraphrase of his reassurance.
   **KJV (Matthew 14:27):** "Be of good cheer; it is I; be not afraid."
2. **Where:** the held beat — the single word, alone in silence.
   **KJV (Matthew 14:29):** "Come."
3. **Where:** over shot 6 (caught immediately), replacing the narrator's
   "Why did you doubt?"
   **KJV (Matthew 14:31):** "O thou of little faith, wherefore didst thou
   doubt?"
   **Narrator bridge (after, modern):** "Hear how he said it — not from the
   shore, but from the hand already gripping Peter's arm. 'Wherefore didst
   thou doubt' isn't condemnation. It's a real question, asked by the one
   who caught him before he finished sinking."
