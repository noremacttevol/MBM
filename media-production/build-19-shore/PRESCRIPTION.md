## The impact read
- **The one thing:** he takes the man who failed him worst, and instead of throwing the failure back at him, asks about the love — and hands him back his life with a bigger purpose than the one he thought he'd thrown away.
- **The turn:** "Simon, son of Jonas, lovest thou me?" beside a charcoal fire (j1) — three denials by one fire answered by three questions at another. The scene pivots from Peter's shame to his restoration.
- **Character shown:** mercy that restores instead of scolds — Jesus never mentions the denial, has breakfast waiting, and trusts Peter again with "Feed my sheep."
- **Whose face carries it:** PETER throughout — weeping at the denial, the recognition on the water, his face at the charcoal-fire memory, the cost of each of the three answers. Jesus is mostly a figure on the shore / over-the-shoulder; face via master ref at the questions.
- **The card question falls out of:** "Is there a failure you have quietly decided is final?" — falls straight out of the one thing. No change needed.

## The fix
The dawn on the water was all paraphrase — the figure told them where to cast without ever speaking. His two lines (j0a, j0b) are now red on the shore still, and Peter's answer (s16) is a deliberate question-and-answer pair with j1. The charcoal fire deserves its own frame (beat 10) because the whole mercy of the scene hangs on that detail — three denials by one fire, three questions by another. Keep Jesus at a distance / over-the-shoulder through the dawn beats; face only via master ref at the questions.
