# STILL PROMPTS — Story Video #44: The Two Debtors (luke-7_two-debtors)

Luke 7:36-50 (the parable is verses 41-43). STILLS-ONLY (Law E) — eight painted
stills, no motion clips. Paste each prompt into Google Flow (the old faithful
path); download and rename into assets/ as the slug shown in each heading.

TIME OF DAY. The dinner at Simon's house (s1, s2, s3, s4, s7) is EVENING, indoors,
lit only by warm clay oil lamps — a Pharisee's banquet after dark. The parable
Jesus tells (s5, s6) is a PLAIN DAYTIME room, cooler and ordinary, deliberately
different from the lamp-lit dinner so the viewer feels the story-within-a-story.
The closing shot (s8) is soft first-light MORNING — the forgiven woman walks out
into new day. Time of day is stated inside every prompt, and in every dinner shot
Jesus is seen only from behind, his face never shown.

FACE LAW. Jesus is physically present at this dinner, reclining at the table, so
he is the hard case. In every shot that stages him (s1, s3, s4, s7, s8) the camera
stands DIRECTLY BEHIND him or OVER HIS SHOULDER — only the back of his head, his
long dark hair and his shoulders are ever toward us, and his face is never shown,
never visible, at any angle, from any direction. His feet and his hands may show
(the whole story turns on his feet); only his face is withheld. Run
`python jesus_face_gate.py --dir build-44-two-debtors` — it must exit 0 before any
Flow credit is spent.

THE PARABLE HAS NO DIVINE FIGURE. Verses 41-43 are a story Jesus is telling: the
creditor is an ordinary moneylender and the two debtors are ordinary men, all
drawn fully with their faces shown. The creditor is never called lord or master —
he is "the moneylender" — so that nothing in the parable frames reads as God.
Jesus never appears inside the parable at all; where he appears in the dinner he
is staged from behind and his face is never shown.

JESUS LOCK (identical wording in s1, s3, s4, s7, s8; the project-wide look
standard): a first-century Middle-Eastern man, warm olive-brown skin, LONG dark
brown-black hair falling well past the shoulders, one plain undyed off-white cream
wool robe (a simple Judean tunic with a plain mantle, the same garment in every
shot), bare-headed with no hood; no glow, no bright ring, no outlining light of any kind; an
ordinary solid man. The camera is always behind him or over his shoulder and his
face is never shown, never visible, at any angle.

WOMAN LOCK (s2, s3, s7, s8) — the forgiven woman. A Middle-Eastern woman of about
thirty, warm olive skin, LONG dark hair worn loose and unbound and falling down
her back (she will wipe his feet with it), wearing a simple deep wine-red mantle
over a plain undyed tunic, and carrying a small white alabaster jar of ointment.
Her face IS shown and stays the same woman in every shot — grieving and undone in
the dinner, lifted and at peace in the morning. Her cheeks may be wet only where a
prompt says so; keep it to eyes brimming, not heavy painted teardrops.

SIMON LOCK (s1, s4, s7) — Simon the Pharisee, the host. A well-off Middle-Eastern
man of about fifty, iron-grey streaks in a neat dark beard, wearing a fine deep
indigo-blue mantle with a bordered hem over a clean pale tunic — plainly richer
cloth than anyone else at the table. He carries himself correct and a little cold.
His face IS shown, consistent throughout.

GUESTS LOCK (s1, s3, s4) — three or four other dining men reclining at the low
table, ordinary well-to-do townsmen in muted browns and olives, bearded, watching.
Their faces are shown but they are secondary, never the focus.

MONEYLENDER LOCK (s5, s6) — the creditor of the parable. A calm, fair-looking
Middle-Eastern man of about fifty, trimmed grey beard, a merchant's warm ochre
robe, seated behind a plain wooden table with two rolled account scrolls in front
of him. His face IS shown. He is decent and even-handed, never a villain.

DEBTOR-ONE LOCK (s5, s6) — the man who owed the larger amount. A poor, weathered
Middle-Eastern laborer of about forty, sun-darkened, a rough dun work tunic, heavy
tired shoulders. His face IS shown, consistent in both shots.

DEBTOR-TWO LOCK (s5, s6) — the man who owed the smaller amount. A younger
tradesman of about thirty, a slightly better olive-green tunic, short black beard.
His face IS shown, consistent in both shots.

STILL STYLE BLOCK (prepended to every prompt, exactly):
Beautiful hand-painted 2D animation style, reverent and warm, like a classic
illustrated storybook of scripture brought to life. Soft painterly brushstroke
textures, glowing golden light, muted earth tones with warm gold highlights.
First-century Judea. Sacred, hushed tone. Not photorealistic. No text or captions
in the image. Historically modest clothing: rough-woven wool and linen in undyed
earth colors. No modern objects.

Every figure has two arms, two hands, two legs and one head (anatomy guard). Every
prompt ends with "One single continuous scene painted edge to edge." Skip any
comic-strip or multi-panel variant the model returns.

## s6-frankly-forgave — Shot 6: he frankly forgave them both
REF: s5-the-two-debtors
[STILL STYLE BLOCK] The same plain moneylender's counting room in cool DAYTIME light. The moneylender — the calm Middle-Eastern man of about fifty with the trimmed grey beard and the ochre robe — has risen from behind his table and, with both hands, is tearing one of the rolled account scrolls clean across; the other torn scroll already lies in pieces on the tabletop. He has cancelled both debts and is asking for nothing back. The poor weathered laborer of about forty in the dun tunic and the younger tradesman of about thirty in the olive-green tunic stand in front of him, and their faces have broken open — stunned, disbelieving, flooding with relief, one of them with a hand pressed to his own chest. All three faces are painted fully and clearly. Grey daylight from the small window, torn paper, open hands. This is the hinge of the whole video. Every figure has two arms, two hands, two legs and one head. One single continuous scene painted edge to edge.
