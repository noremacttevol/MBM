# Story Video #20 — The Good Samaritan (Luke 10:25–37)

Runtime 75s. Prepend the STYLE BLOCK from 00-MASTER-PLAN.md. A parable:
everyone in it may be shown fully; Jesus is the narrating voice, and appears
only in the bookend shots as a seated storyteller from behind. Depict the
beating's aftermath with restraint — dust, stillness, a bloodied brow — not
violence on screen.

## Narration script (app's exact words)

> A scholar of the law asked Jesus, "Who is my neighbor?" — the kind of
> question you ask when you are hoping the answer has limits.
>
> Jesus answered with a story. A man traveling to Jericho was beaten by
> robbers and left half dead beside the road.
>
> A priest came along — and crossed to the other side. Then a temple
> worker — also crossed over. The religious professionals kept their
> distance.
>
> Then a Samaritan came — the foreigner, the heretic, the man Jesus's
> audience was raised to despise. He saw the body, and the text says he was
> moved with compassion. He bandaged the wounds, put the man on his own
> animal, paid for his care, and promised to come back. *(peak — music cuts
> to silence on "moved with compassion")*
>
> Jesus turned the scholar's question inside out: not "who counts as my
> neighbor?" but "which of these three WAS a neighbor?" Go, he said, and do
> likewise.

Closing card (hold 6s): *"Everyone finds themselves somewhere on that road.
Where are you on it right now?"*

## Shot list

### Shot 1 — A question with limits in it
```
A learned man in fine scholar's robes stands in a listening crowd, chin
high, asking a question with the posture of a man setting a trap — while
around him ordinary faces turn toward a seated storyteller seen only from
behind. Late golden light through an olive grove.
```

### Shot 2 — Left half dead
```
The Jericho road: a savage rocky descent between cliffs, harsh light. In
the roadside dust lies a stripped, bloodied traveler, unmoving except for
shallow breath, one hand open toward the empty road. Flies. Heat shimmer.
Torn belongings scattered. The aftermath only — no violence shown.
```

### Shot 3 — Crossed to the other side
```
Two sequential passings in matching frames: first a priest in fine white
temple garments, then a Levite with a scroll satchel — each sees the body,
each hesitates a half-step, each angles his path to the FAR side of the
road, eyes forward, pace quickening. Shot from the fallen man's low blurred
point of view: feet approaching, then veering away.
```

### Shot 4 — Moved with compassion (the money shot — extra takes)
```
A dusty foreign merchant leading a loaded donkey rounds the bend — and
stops. Close on his face: the double-take, the look around at the dangerous
empty road, and then compassion visibly overruling fear. He drops to his
knees IN the dust beside the stranger, already unslinging his own wineskin
and tearing cloth from his own garment for bandages.
```

### Shot 5 — On his own animal
```
The Samaritan walks on foot, leading his donkey, steadying the bandaged
half-conscious traveler slumped across the saddle where the merchant's own
goods used to ride — the goods now strapped awkwardly on the Samaritan's
back. Long shot down the brutal road: the slowest, most expensive walk of
his journey. Dusk falling.
```

### Shot 6 — Whatever more it costs
```
Lamplit inn courtyard at night: the Samaritan presses silver coins into an
innkeeper's palm and holds the man's eyes while pointing back toward the
room where the stranger sleeps — a visible promise: whatever more it costs,
I will repay. The innkeeper looks from the coins to this foreigner,
recalibrating everything he assumed.
```

### Shot 7 — Go and do likewise
```
Return to the olive grove at last light: the fine-robed scholar's face has
lost its trap-setting confidence — the question has been turned around
inside him and it shows. Beyond him, the seated storyteller's silhouette
rises against the sunset, and the crowd begins to stand. The road home is
in front of them all.
```

### Closing card
Text on cream, serif. No narration.

## The Seed (what this video must leave behind)

Jesus made the HERETIC the hero — the man his audience was raised to despise carried the compassion of God, while the credentialed religion crossed the road. Quiet question: *what if the love of God shows up in the tradition you were raised to look down on?* Carried by shots 3–4 and 7.
