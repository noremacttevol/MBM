# FLOW PROMPTS — Story Video #21: The Lost Sheep (luke-15_lost-sheep)

Luke 15:1-7. STILLS-ONLY (Law E). This is a PARABLE the Lord tells: he is kept
FULLY OFF-SCREEN and never shown in any still — only his narrating voice + KJV
lines. Because no Lord figure is ever shown, the gate is trivially safe
(`jesus_face_gate.py` exits 0). The SHEPHERD is a character in the parable — his
own face IS shown and kept consistent, exactly like the Samaritan in #20. Nano
Banana 2, 9:16, x2, 2K. Hand-painted 2D storybook style.

PROVENANCE: the 7 delivered stills (assets/s1-gathering … s7-rejoicing) were
generated by Machine C (Cameron Lovett MS) on the shared prep. This sheet is the
record of that shipped 7-still cut. (An earlier 9-still bookend draft that would

Lord never shown on screen at all.)

**CHARACTER LOCKS (kept consistent across every still they appear in):**
- **The shepherd (s2–s7)** — a weathered Judean shepherd, warm brown skin, dark
  hair and a short dark beard, a coarse undyed wool tunic under a worn brown
  mantle, a rope belt, worn leather sandals, a plain wooden crook. Carries a small
  clay oil lamp in the night shots. His face IS shown — the emotional center.
- **The lost sheep (s5–s7)** — ONE young cream-white ewe with a soft dark patch,
  smaller than the grown flock. Always exactly one.
- **The flock / the ninety-nine (s2)** — a large flock of cream-and-tan grown sheep.
- **The crowd (s1)** — tax collectors, outcasts, common folk pressing in to listen.
- **The two religious men (s1)** — well-dressed, fine robes with broad prayer
  shawls, set apart from the poor crowd, stiff and disapproving.
- **The friends and neighbours (s7)** — village men, women, children celebrating.

STILL STYLE BLOCK (prepended to every prompt, exactly):
Beautiful hand-painted 2D animation style, reverent and warm, like a classic
illustrated storybook of scripture brought to life. Soft painterly brushstroke
textures, glowing golden light, muted earth tones with warm gold highlights.
First-century Judea. Sacred, hushed tone. Not photorealistic. No text or
captions in the image. Historically modest clothing: rough-woven wool and linen
in undyed earth colors. No modern objects.

## s1-gathering — the crowd he welcomes; the religious men murmur (Luke 15:1-2)
[STILL STYLE BLOCK] A dusty Galilean courtyard in late golden light. A close-packed
crowd of ordinary, roughly dressed common people — tax collectors, laborers, women,
outcasts — press in and lean forward, open and hungry to listen. Off to one side,
apart from the poor crowd, two well-dressed religious men in fine robes with broad
blue-fringed prayer shawls stand stiff and disapproving, murmuring to each other.
No storyteller figure appears — the frame is only the people and their hunger to
hear. One single continuous scene painted edge to edge, 9:16 vertical.

## s2-flock-dusk — a man with a hundred sheep, one straying (Luke 15:4)
[STILL STYLE BLOCK] A wide green-gold hillside in late afternoon light. A weathered
Judean shepherd (warm brown skin, dark hair, short dark beard, coarse cream wool
tunic under a worn brown mantle, wooden crook) stands among a large contented flock
of cream-and-tan sheep spread across the slope; far up on the ridge, ONE single
sheep has wandered off toward the darkening rocks. Soft grazing, low warm sun. One
single continuous scene painted edge to edge, 9:16 vertical.

## s3-leaves — he leaves the ninety-nine and goes
[STILL STYLE BLOCK] Dusk deepening over broken hill country. The same shepherd turns
from the bedded-down flock behind him and strides out along a rocky path into the
wilderness after the one, crook in hand, resolve in his stride. The safe flock is
small and distant behind; the wild country ahead is vast. One single continuous
scene painted edge to edge, 9:16 vertical.

## s4-searches-night — through the night, until he find it
[STILL STYLE BLOCK] A harsh rocky ravine in full night under a thin crescent moon and
stars. The same shepherd climbs across boulders and thorn scrub, a small clay oil
lamp glowing, his crook in hand, mouth open mid-call as he searches every shadow and
cleft for his lost sheep. Weary but refusing to quit. One single continuous scene
painted edge to edge, 9:16 vertical.

## s5-finds — and when he hath found it (Luke 15:5)
[STILL STYLE BLOCK] A narrow rocky hollow, the shepherd's oil lamp glowing. He has
found the one lost sheep — a single young cream-white ewe with a dark patch, tangled
in thorns, trembling and worn out — and kneels to gather it, both hands reaching
under the frightened animal, relief and tenderness in his face. Just the one sheep.
One single continuous scene painted edge to edge, 9:16 vertical.

## s6-shoulders — he layeth it on his shoulders, rejoicing
[STILL STYLE BLOCK] First pale dawn over the ridgeline. The shepherd climbs the long
way home with the rescued ewe laid across the back of his shoulders, both its legs
held gently in his hands, open tired joy on his face. The wilderness falls away
behind; the green home hills glow ahead. One single continuous scene painted edge
to edge, 9:16 vertical.

## s7-rejoicing — rejoice with me; joy in heaven (Luke 15:6-7)
[STILL STYLE BLOCK] Morning in a small village lane. The shepherd, the recovered ewe
still across his shoulders, throws an arm wide as friends and neighbours — men,
women, children — come from their doorways, faces breaking into delight to celebrate
with him. Warm morning light. Every face turned toward the shepherd and his found
sheep. One single continuous scene painted edge to edge, 9:16 vertical.

## Closing card
Text on cream (#F7F2E9), serif, no narration baked in (spoken + captioned in
assembly): "To him, you were never one of a crowd. Have you ever felt like the one
who wandered too far to be worth coming after?"

## s7b-calling-the-village — NEW (split off S7 / Luke 15:6, was 39.7s): calling everyone in
[STILL STYLE BLOCK] Dusk in the small village lane, the moment the shepherd calls everyone IN. The weathered shepherd — dark hair and a dark beard, in an undyed cream-tan wool tunic with a leather shepherd's bag — stands at his own doorway with the DOOR THROWN OPEN behind him, warm lamplight spilling out into the blue dusk street. The recovered ewe (white with a brown patch) is still held safe in his arms against his chest, and he is turned back OVER HIS SHOULDER toward the street, his MOUTH OPEN, SHOUTING with joy for his friends and neighbours to come and rejoice with him — pure JOY, not relief, the delight of a man who cannot keep the good news to himself. A couple of neighbours in the far street just beginning to turn toward his voice. Warm lamplight glow against the cool dusk. Every figure has one head, two arms and two hands. One single continuous scene painted edge to edge. 9:16 vertical.
