# FLOW PROMPTS — Story Video #21: The Lost Sheep (luke-15_lost-sheep)

Luke 15:1-7. STILLS-ONLY (Law E) + Jesus face-never (the Face Law): the Lord is
shown only from behind, his back to the camera and his face turned away, never
shown. Nano Banana 2, 9:16, x2, 2K download. Hand-painted 2D storybook style
(NOT the stale master-plan photorealistic block).

This is a PARABLE Jesus tells. The SHEPHERD in the story is a character Jesus
describes — his face IS shown and kept consistent, exactly like the Samaritan in
#20. Jesus himself is only the storyteller telling it: he appears ONLY in the
bookend shots (1 and 9) as a seated storyteller seen from behind, his face
turned away and never shown. Passes `jesus_face_gate.py`.

**CHARACTER LOCKS (keep consistent across every still they appear in):**
- **The shepherd (shots 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8)** — a weathered Judean shepherd,
  warm brown skin, dark hair and a short dark beard, a coarse undyed wool tunic
  under a worn brown mantle, a rope belt, worn leather sandals, and a plain
  wooden crook. He carries a small clay oil lamp from shot 4 on. His face IS
  shown — he is the emotional center.
- **The lost sheep (shots 3-gap, 6, 7, 8)** — ONE young cream-white ewe with a
  soft dark patch over one ear, smaller than the grown flock. Always exactly one.
- **The flock / the ninety-nine (shots 2, 3, 4)** — a large flock of cream-and-tan
  grown sheep, calm and grazing.
- **The tax collectors and sinners (shot 1)** — a close crowd of ordinary, roughly
  dressed common people (laborers, women, outcasts), faces open and listening.
- **The two religious men / Pharisees (shots 1, 9)** — two well-dressed men in fine
  robes with broad blue-fringed prayer shawls, set apart from the poor crowd,
  stiff and disapproving. Their faces ARE shown.
- **The friends and neighbours (shot 8)** — village men, women, and children coming
  from their doorways to celebrate. Faces shown.
- **The Lord / the storyteller (shots 1 and 9 ONLY)** — THE JESUS LOOK STANDARD:
  one plain undyed off-white / cream wool robe, LONG dark hair past the
  shoulders, warm Middle-Eastern; shown only from behind, back-of-head or
  back-only, his back to the camera and his face turned away, never shown. No
  halo, no glow.

STILL STYLE BLOCK (prepended to every prompt, exactly):
Beautiful hand-painted 2D animation style, reverent and warm, like a classic
illustrated storybook of scripture brought to life. Soft painterly brushstroke
textures, glowing golden light, muted earth tones with warm gold highlights.
First-century Judea. Sacred, hushed tone. Not photorealistic. No text or
captions in the image. Historically modest clothing: rough-woven wool and linen
in undyed earth colors. No modern objects.

## s1-murmur — Shot 1: this man receiveth sinners
[STILL STYLE BLOCK] Late golden afternoon light in a dusty Galilean courtyard. A
close-packed crowd of ordinary, roughly dressed people — tax collectors in plain
travel clothes, weary laborers, women and outcasts — press in close and lean
forward, open and hungry to listen, all turned toward a seated storyteller. That
storyteller is the Lord, seated on a low stone step in one plain undyed cream
wool robe, long dark hair falling past the shoulders, warm Middle-Eastern, seen
only from behind with his back fully to us and his face turned away, never shown;
no halo, no glow. Off to one side, apart from the poor crowd, two well-dressed
religious men in fine robes with broad blue-fringed prayer shawls stand stiff and
disapproving, murmuring to each other. Every ordinary person looks past the
camera straight at the storyteller. One single continuous scene painted edge to
edge.

## s2-hundred — Shot 2: a man with a hundred sheep
[STILL STYLE BLOCK] A wide green-gold hillside in the late day. A weathered
Judean shepherd — warm brown skin, dark hair and a short dark beard, a coarse
undyed wool tunic under a worn brown mantle, a plain wooden crook in his hand —
stands among a large contented flock of cream-and-tan sheep spread across the
whole slope, quietly counting them with a steady, caring look, his lips moving
over the tally. Soft grazing, low warm sun, the whole flock at peace. One single
continuous scene painted edge to edge.

## s3-one-lost — Shot 3: one of them is gone
[STILL STYLE BLOCK] The same hillside at deep dusk, the light draining to blue.
The shepherd stands at the edge of the flock, now gathered close, and his tally
has come up short — one hand shading his eyes as he stares out toward the
darkening wilderness of ravines and scrub beyond the pasture, worry breaking
across his features. The grown flock huddles safe behind him, with a single
empty gap where the lost one should be. First stars, long cold shadows. One
single continuous scene painted edge to edge.

## s4-leaves — Shot 4: he leaves the ninety and nine
[STILL STYLE BLOCK] Night falling over rough, broken country. The shepherd has
left the flock bedded down safe together in a low stone fold on the hilltop
behind him, and he strides out alone down into the dark wilderness — crook in one
hand, a small clay oil lamp glowing in the other, his mantle pulled tight against
the cold, resolve in his stride, turned toward the wild ravines ahead. The safe
flock is small and distant behind; the darkness ahead is vast. One single
continuous scene painted edge to edge.

## s5-searches — Shot 5: until he find it
[STILL STYLE BLOCK] Deep in a harsh rocky ravine in full night, lit only by the
shepherd's small oil lamp and a thin moon. The weathered shepherd climbs across
jagged boulders and thorn scrub, lamp lifted high, mouth open mid-call, searching
every shadow and cleft in the rock for his lost sheep. Thorns catch at his
mantle; his sandaled feet find hard footing on the stones. Exhaustion and a
refusal to quit both in his look. One single continuous scene painted edge to
edge.

## s6-found — Shot 6: and when he hath found it
[STILL STYLE BLOCK] A narrow rocky hollow, the shepherd's oil lamp set on a
ledge. He has found the one lost sheep — a single young cream-white ewe with a
dark patch over one ear, tangled in a thicket of thorns on a steep ledge,
trembling and worn out — and he is kneeling to gather it, both hands reaching
under the frightened little animal, relief and tenderness flooding his features.
No other sheep here, just the one. Warm lamp glow against the cold night rock.
One single continuous scene painted edge to edge.

## s7-shoulders — Shot 7: he layeth it on his shoulders, rejoicing
[STILL STYLE BLOCK] First pale light of dawn over the ridgeline. The shepherd
climbs the long way home with the rescued ewe laid across the back of his
shoulders, both its legs held gently in his hands, its head resting against his
neck. His whole body carries its weight, and open, tired joy lights his face. The
wilderness falls away behind; the green home hills glow ahead in the sunrise. One
single continuous scene painted edge to edge.

## s8-rejoice — Shot 8: rejoice with me
[STILL STYLE BLOCK] Morning in a small village lane outside the shepherd's low
stone house. The shepherd, the recovered ewe still across his shoulders, throws
his free arm wide as friends and neighbours — men, women, and children — come
hurrying from their doorways, breaking into delight and relief to celebrate with
him. Warm morning light, a scatter of clay cups and bread as a small glad welcome
begins. Every face is turned toward the shepherd and his found sheep. One single
continuous scene painted edge to edge.

## s9-heaven — Shot 9: joy in heaven over one sinner
[STILL STYLE BLOCK] Back in the dusty courtyard at last light. The seated
storyteller — the Lord — has risen to his feet, a tall figure in the same plain
undyed cream wool robe with long dark hair past the shoulders, warm
Middle-Eastern, seen only from behind, his back to the camera and his face turned
away, never shown, dark against a glowing evening sky; no halo, no glow. The
ordinary crowd around him leans in, moved; and the two fine-robed religious men
who had been murmuring now stand still and silent, their disapproval gone. Every
gaze in the frame lifts toward the storyteller. One single continuous scene
painted edge to edge.
