## Why we had duplicates (the real cause)

Cameron's instinct was right, with one sharpening. The duplicates did NOT mainly come
from "the same story in different gospels." They came from the picker listing a scene
**twice**: once as the **event**, and again as a **famous verse from inside that same
event** — not realizing they are the same scene. Example that made Cameron reject a
build: the Prodigal Son was told as its own video AND told again from another framing
(that dup is now #140 → Naaman). The same trap sits under several "one-verse" videos
whose verse is simply a line from a scene already told elsewhere.

## The four rules (law)

1. **A story is a SCENE — who, where, what happened — and every scene is told ONCE.**
   A narrative is never re-told, not even across shelves. If two entries point at the
   same moment of scripture (same people, same place, same event), they are one video.

2. **Famous verses are TAUGHT, not re-narrated.** The "I AM" sayings, the Sermon on the
   Mount lines, and other single-verse videos are told as Jesus *teaching who he is / how
   to live*, with their own fresh imagery — NEVER by replaying a scene we have already
   shown. Done right, "I am the resurrection and the life" is a teaching on Christ's
   power over death, not a second telling of Lazarus. This keeps the verse-videos without
   ever giving the "wait, this is Lazarus again" feeling.

3. **When a story is in several gospels, tell it ONCE and weave in what each writer
   uniquely saw.** John adds the boy with the loaves; Mark counts the crowd; Luke adds the
   tears. One richest telling that does justice to every witness — never four videos of
   one event.

4. **Trim the in-video echo (Cameron, 2026-07-23).** The old habit of saying a line in
   old-English scripture and then repeating it in modern English is dropped. Restate a
   scripture line in plain words ONLY when the old English is genuinely hard to follow;
   if it already lands, let it stand and move on.
