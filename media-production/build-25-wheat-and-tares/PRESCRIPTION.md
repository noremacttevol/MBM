# PRESCRIPTION — build-25-wheat-and-tares

**Row:** 25   **Shelf:** Everyone   **Scripture:** Matthew 13:24–30, 36–43
**Tier:** T2 Encounter   **Target length:** 80–120s   **Pictures:** 10–15 (planned: 13)

## The impact read
- **The one thing:** he would rather wait a whole season than tear up one stalk of wheat — his patience is not slowness, it is mercy, and it is aimed at protecting you.
- **The turn:** the farmer's "Nay... let both grow together until the harvest" (j1) — the moment his restraint is revealed as love, not weakness.
- **Character shown:** patience that protects — he gives time on purpose, because ripping the weeds early would tear the good plants out with them.
- **Whose face carries it:** the FIELD itself and the farmer's patience; the anxious WORKERS who want to pull the weeds now. (Parable — the farmer is God's stand-in.)
- **The card question falls out of:** "He is patient enough to wait for you. Will you grow toward the light?" — falls straight out of the one thing. No change needed.

## Beat list (= the picture plan) — 13 stills
1. [s24/j24] Jesus beginning the parable — a man sowing good seed in his field — "Another parable put he forth unto them, saying," / "The kingdom of heaven is likened unto a man which sowed good seed in his field"
2. [j25/n3] at night, while everyone slept, an enemy creeping in and scattering weed seeds through the wheat, then slipping away unseen — "his enemy came and sowed tares among the wheat, and went his way"
3. [n4] the weed that looks almost exactly like young wheat — indistinguishable until the heads appear — "You cannot tell them apart until they grow up"
4. [n5] the wheat coming up green and strong, and the weeds right in the middle of it — the field full of both — "so did the weeds. Now anyone could see the field was full of both"
5. [j27/n6] the upset workers coming to the farmer — "Sir, didst thou not sow good seed in thy field? from whence then hath it tares?"
6. [n7] the farmer: "an enemy has done this"; the workers asking to pull the weeds now — "do you want us to go and pull all the weeds out right now?"
7. [n8/j1] the farmer saying no — the restraint that shows what kind of man he is — "Nay; lest while ye gather up the tares, ye root up also the wheat with them. Let both grow together until the harvest."
8. [n9/n10] the tangled roots — he'd rather wait than lose a stalk; the wheat and weeds growing side by side all season — "All season long the wheat and the weeds grew up side by side"
9. [j30] the harvest orders — "Gather ye together first the tares, and bind them in bundles to burn them: but gather the wheat into my barn"
10. [n11] harvest — the reapers bundling the weeds away, gathering the good wheat safely into the barn — "gathered all the good wheat safely into the barn"
11. [n12/n13] nothing good lost; Jesus applying it — God is not quick to rip things up, because his patience is mercy — "He gives time, because his patience is mercy"
12. [j2] "Then shall the righteous shine forth as the sun in the kingdom of their Father. Who hath ears to hear, let him hear."
13. [card] hold for the closing question — the card line
