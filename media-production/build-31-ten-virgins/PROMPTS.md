Matthew 25:1-13. STILLS-ONLY (Law E) + the Face Law: this is a parable Jesus
tells. The ten young women (bridesmaids) are the story's characters — their faces
ARE shown and kept consistent. The bridegroom represents the Lord, so he is shown
reverently ONLY at a distance and from behind, his face never shown at all.

storybook style (NOT photorealistic). Passes `jesus_face_gate.py`.

**CHARACTER / PROP LOCKS (keep consistent across every still they appear in):**
- **The five WISE bridesmaids** — young Judean women in warm, deeper festive robes
  (rose, russet, deep ochre, warm gold) with head-scarves. Each holds a small clay
  hand oil-lamp with a warm flame AND carries a small clay oil flask/jar. Their
  lamps stay burning bright. Faces shown, calm and ready.
- **The five FOOLISH bridesmaids** — young Judean women in paler, cooler festive
  robes (pale blue, soft grey, cream) with head-scarves. Each holds only a small
  clay hand oil-lamp — NO flask. Later their lamps go dark. Faces shown, later
  anxious.
- **The oil lamps** — small clay first-century hand-lamps, a warm little flame at
  the spout (dark and smoking when a foolish one goes out).
- **The oil flasks** — small round clay jars/flasks the wise carry.
- **The bridegroom** — a first-century Judean bridegroom in fine festive robes,
  amid a torch-lit wedding procession. Shown ONLY at a distance (the far-off
  arrival) and from directly behind (leading the wise through the door); his face
  is never shown at all.
- **The setting** — a village street / walled courtyard, evening into deep night,
  stars, warm torch and lamp light; the wedding house is a warm glowing doorway.

Beautiful hand-painted 2D animation style, reverent and warm, like a classic

gentle tone. Not photorealistic. No text or captions in the image. Historically

## s1 — Shot 1: the ten bridesmaids set out
[STILL STYLE BLOCK] Warm dusk over a first-century Judean village street of pale
stone and mud-brick, the sky deep gold fading to blue, first stars. Ten young women
in modest festive robes and head-scarves walk out together down the lane, each
carrying a small clay oil-lamp with a warm little flame, faces bright with happy
anticipation, going out to wait for a wedding. A soft, glad, expectant mood. [ANTI-
PANEL CLAUSE]

## s2 — Shot 2: wise and foolish
[STILL STYLE BLOCK] A warm lamplit courtyard at early evening. On one side stand
five WISE young women in deeper festive robes of rose, russet and deep ochre, each
holding her small clay lamp and also cradling a small round clay flask of extra
oil, calm and prepared. On the other side stand five FOOLISH young women in paler
robes of soft blue, grey and cream, each holding only her small clay lamp with
empty hands — no flask at all. The difference between the two groups is clear and
gentle. [ANTI-PANEL CLAUSE]

## s3 — Shot 3: the bridegroom tarries, all sleep
[STILL STYLE BLOCK] Deep night under a sky full of stars. All ten young women have
grown drowsy waiting and are curled asleep along a low stone wall of the courtyard.
Their small clay hand oil-lamps are ALL set down on the ground, resting on the
stone-paved floor on the pavement beside the sleeping women, glowing low and warm.
The stone wall is completely bare — there are NO lamps on the wall, NO lamps
mounted or hung on the wall; every single lamp sits flat on the ground. Peaceful,
hushed, the hour very late. The bridegroom has not yet come. [ANTI-PANEL CLAUSE]
(FIX 2026-07-13: the earlier render stuck three lamps onto the vertical wall,
which read wrong — all lamps must rest on the ground.)

## s4 — Shot 4: midnight — the cry, the distant arrival
[STILL STYLE BLOCK] Deep midnight. Far down the dark village road, a distant torch-
lit wedding procession is approaching — small warm points of torchlight and, among
them, the bridegroom as a far-off robed figure, seen only at a great distance, his
face not visible. In the foreground the ten young women are just waking with a
start, sitting up and reaching for their lamps, roused by the cry that the
bridegroom is coming. [ANTI-PANEL CLAUSE]

## s5 — Shot 5: the oil that cannot be shared
[STILL STYLE BLOCK] The courtyard in deep night. The five FOOLISH young women in
pale robes hold up their small clay lamps in dismay — the flames sputtering out,
dark and smoking — and reach pleading toward the others. The five WISE young women
in warm rose and ochre robes hold their own still-brightly-burning lamps and their
small clay oil flasks close, faces gentle but sorrowful, unable to share enough for
everyone. Warm light on the wise, cold shadow on the foolish. [ANTI-PANEL CLAUSE]

## s6 — Shot 6: the ready go in, the door is shut
[STILL STYLE BLOCK] A warm, glowing wedding doorway spilling golden festive light
into the night. The five WISE young women, lamps burning, follow the bridegroom in
through the threshold — the bridegroom seen only from directly behind as a fine-
robed figure leading the way, his face never shown — into the bright wedding feast
beyond. Beside the doorway a heavy wooden door stands ready to close. Joy and warm
light within. [ANTI-PANEL CLAUSE]

## s7 — Shot 7: shut out in the dark
[STILL STYLE BLOCK] Night outside the wedding house. The heavy wooden door is now
shut fast. The five FOOLISH young women in pale robes stand before it in the cold
dark, their small clay lamps dark and smoking, knocking and pressing their hands to
the closed door, faces stricken and pleading. Only a thin line of warm light escapes
under the shut door. Quiet, sorrowful, a closed door. [ANTI-PANEL CLAUSE]
