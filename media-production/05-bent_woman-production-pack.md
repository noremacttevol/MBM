# Story Video #5 — The Bent-Over Woman (Luke 13:10–17)

Runtime 60–75s. Prepend the STYLE BLOCK from 00-MASTER-PLAN.md. Jesus: hands
and voice only — hands ARE allowed in healing moments, face never.

## Narration script (app's exact words)

> There was a woman who had been bent over and unable to straighten up for
> eighteen years.
>
> Jesus was in the middle of teaching in the synagogue. He stopped, looked
> across the room, and called her over.
>
> She had not asked him anything. She had not pushed through a crowd. He
> called her first. *(held beat)*
>
> He put his hands on her. She stood up straight for the first time in
> eighteen years. *(peak — music cuts to silence on "She stood up
> straight.")*
>
> He called her a daughter of Abraham — which was a title, a declaration:
> you belong. You have always belonged. Before you were healed.

Closing card (hold 6s): *"Is there something you have been carrying so long
that you have stopped expecting it to change?"*

## Shot list

### Shot 1 — Eighteen years
```
An older woman, bent nearly double at the waist, moves slowly through a
morning market, her view only of feet and dust and hems. People step around
her without seeing her. Shot largely from her low, downward-facing point of
view: the world as sandals and shadows.
```

### Shot 2 — The edge of the room
```
Interior of a stone synagogue full of seated listeners. At the very back, by
the wall, the bent woman stands folded over her walking stick — present but
invisible, a fixture no one notices. Shafts of morning light from high
windows fall on everyone but her.
```

### Shot 3 — He stops teaching
```
The seated crowd's heads all turn at once, following something: the teaching
voice has stopped mid-sentence. A hush rolls through the room. Camera drifts
across surprised faces toward the back wall, where the bent woman slowly
realizes everyone is looking at HER.
```

### Shot 4 — Called first
```
The bent woman shuffles up the center of the synagogue between the seated
rows, trembling, her stick tapping the stone floor in total silence, into a
column of light at the front. She has not asked for anything. Her eyes,
angled at the floor, fill with tears.
```

### Shot 5 — The hands (the money shot — generate extra takes)
```
Close up: two strong, gentle carpenter's hands rest softly on the curved,
knotted back of an old woman's shoulders. Warm light gathers where the hands
touch. Under them, slowly, vertebra by vertebra, the spine begins to unbend.
Her head starts to rise. Faces out of frame; only hands, back, and light.
```

### Shot 6 — Standing straight
```
Extreme slow motion: an old woman rises to her full height for the first
time in eighteen years, her face traveling up out of shadow into a shaft of
sunlight, eyes closed then opening WIDE — seeing the room, the windows, the
sky through them — as her walking stick clatters to the stone floor.
```

### Shot 7 — Daughter of Abraham
```
The woman stands tall in the light, laughing and weeping at once, hands
raised, as the congregation rises to its feet around her. She looks — for
the first time in the film — directly into the camera. She belongs. Hold.
Slow fade toward warm white.
```

### Closing card
Text on cream, serif. No narration.

## The Seed (what this video must leave behind)

"Daughter of Abraham" — you belong, you have ALWAYS belonged — declared BEFORE she was healed. Her worth preceded her healing; it wasn't earned by it. This quietly contradicts every theology built on human worthlessness. Quiet question: *what if you already belonged?* Carried by shots 4 and 7.

## Red-letter lines (KJV) — the Jesus voice speaks ONLY these

1. **Where:** just before shot 5 (the hands) — replaces the narrator's
   transition into the healing.
   **KJV (Luke 13:12):** "Woman, thou art loosed from thine infirmity."
   **Narrator bridge (after, modern):** "'Loosed' — untied. Like something
   that had been knotted for eighteen years finally coming undone."
2. **Where:** over shot 7 (standing in the light) — the "daughter of
   Abraham" declaration.
   **KJV (Luke 13:16):** "And ought not this woman, being a daughter of
   Abraham, whom Satan hath bound, lo, these eighteen years, be loosed from
   this bond on the sabbath day?"
   **Narrator bridge (after, modern):** "A daughter of Abraham. That was
   him telling the whole room: she belongs. She always has. He said it
   about her — and he says it about you."
