# Story Video #17 — Jesus Wept: Lazarus (John 11:1–44)

Runtime 75–90s. Prepend the STYLE BLOCK from 00-MASTER-PLAN.md. Jesus: from
behind at the tomb, a bowed silhouette, and — the one exception to standing
back — a single close-up of TEARS falling to the dust (chin and below only,
face never shown). Grief handled with total honesty; the tears come BEFORE
the miracle and must not be rushed.

## Narration script (app's exact words)

> Jesus's close friend Lazarus was dying, and the sisters sent word. Jesus
> stayed where he was two more days — and Lazarus died.
>
> When he finally came, Martha met him on the road with the sentence
> grieving people have said ever since: "Lord, if you had been here, my
> brother would not have died."
>
> He knew how the story would end. He knew resurrection was minutes away.
> And still — standing at the tomb of his friend, among the weeping — the
> shortest verse in scripture happened: Jesus wept. *(peak — music cuts to
> full silence on "Jesus wept," and stays silent through the next line)*
>
> The people watching said: "See how he loved him."
>
> Then he called into the tomb, "Lazarus, come out" — and the dead man
> walked into the light. But the tears came first. He did not skip the
> grief on the way to the miracle.

Closing card (hold 6s): *"\"Lord, if you had been here…\" — grief almost
always has an \"if\" in it. Does that sentence sound familiar to you?"*

## Shot list

### Shot 1 — Word is sent
```
Dusk: a young messenger runs hard along a ridge road with a scrap of
urgency in his fist, silhouetted against a bruised sky. Intercut feel: in a
lamplit room behind him, two sisters keep vigil over a sick man's bed, one
holding his hand, one at the window.
```

### Shot 2 — Too late
```
Bright cruel morning: mourning cloths on a doorway; a courtyard of weeping
neighbors. The two sisters sit apart from each other in the same grief —
one perfectly still, one unable to be still. Village mourning: real, loud
in places, exhausted in others.
```

### Shot 3 — "If you had been here"
```
On the road outside the village, a grieving woman confronts an arriving
robed figure seen from behind — not falling at his feet first, but standing
face-on with the terrible honest sentence, fists tight, grief and faith and
accusation all at once. Her face carries the shot. Wind. Dust.
```

### Shot 4 — Jesus wept (the money shot — generate extra takes, get this right)
```
At a stone tomb cut into a hillside, among weeping mourners: a robed figure
stands seen from behind, head slowly bowing, shoulders beginning to shake.
Then close: from chin height DOWN only — tears fall one after another into
the dust at his feet, each drop raising a tiny crater. No face. The most
powerful being in the story is crying at a grave he is about to empty.
```

### Shot 5 — See how he loved him
```
The mourners around the tomb notice, faces changing mid-grief — a woman
touches another's arm and nods toward the weeping figure. Their own crying
quiets into something like awe: love recognizing love. Held, quiet, human.
```

### Shot 6 — The stone rolled, the call
```
Men strain the great stone away from the tomb mouth — a black opening
exhaling cold air. The robed silhouette steps before it, plants his feet,
and calls INTO the darkness with the full authority the storm heard,
one arm extended toward the black.
```

### Shot 7 — Come out
```
From inside the tomb, looking out through the bright doorway: a figure
bound in white grave linen shuffles into the blazing light, unwinding
strips falling from his hands and face as stunned mourners rush in to free
him — joy detonating through people who were weeping sixty seconds ago.
Sunlight floods everything.
```

### Shot 8 — The tears came first
```
Quiet coda: the freed man embraces his two sisters in a three-way knot of
laughing and sobbing — while in the foreground, soft focus, the robed
figure watches from behind, and in the dust at his feet the dark spots of
his own tears are still wet.
```

### Closing card
Text on cream, serif. No narration.
