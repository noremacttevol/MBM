### MUST SHOW (the text states it)
- A **great multitude** walking with him — this is his biggest crowd, and it is on a road.
- **He turned** (v25). The whole video hangs on that verb: he stopped and faced the crowd
  on purpose. He was not cornered into this; he initiated it.
- The **two parables he chose**: the tower builder, and the king going to war.
- The tower man **sits down first** (v28). The king **sits down first** (v31). The same
  act, twice — that repetition is Jesus's own emphasis and must survive into the video.
- The unfinished tower and the **mockery** it draws (vv29–30).
- The wise king does **not fight** — he sends an ambassage and asks for peace terms while
  the other army is "yet a great way off" (v32).
- **v33 in full** — the verse card, and the verdict the parables were building toward.
- **Salt that has lost its savour** and is cast out (vv34–35), through "He that hath ears
  to hear, let him hear" — the final verse of the discourse (Full-Story Law).

### MUST NEVER SHOW
- 🛑 **Jesus's face — never, at any angle, in any frame** (the #1 Law). He appears in five
  stills only (s1, s2, s10, s13, s15), every one of them with the camera behind him.
- **No crucifixion, no cross, no condemned man, no beam.** v27 says "bear his cross," and
  the temptation is to paint it. It is not painted. The crowd heard those words years
  before Calvary; the narration carries what a cross MEANT to them, and the picture stays
  on the road and the people. (Restraint law; also, a rendered cross here would collide
  with the Passion arc, #120–141, which owns that imagery.)
- **No battle, no blood, no dead men, no weapons in focus.** The king parable's whole point
  is the war that does NOT happen. The opposing army is a distant dust cloud on a horizon.
- No Roman execution imagery of any kind, no bodies by the road.
- No mocking crowd painted as sneering caricatures — the villagers who mock the half-built
  tower are ordinary people, and the builder keeps his dignity even in his shame.
- No writing, letters, numbers, or symbols on any prop (the builder's tablet, the king's
  map, any stone marker) — the model paints gibberish text and the style block forbids
  text in frame.
- No painted teardrops on any cheek (No-Fake-Tears law) — and this video has five
  emotional beats, so the dry-face sentence goes in each of their prompts.
- No second figure in an off-white or cream robe in any frame where Jesus appears
  (ONLY JESUS WEARS CREAM — the #40 lesson).
- No halo, glow, or rim-light on Jesus. No Jesus at a frame edge with the crowd's gazes
  wandering — every face in frame converges on him.

## 2. Care taken anyway (the story is GREEN; the passage is still sharp)

Three lines in this passage do real damage if they are delivered flat, and a MILK video
that leaves a stranger thinking *Jesus demands you hate your mother, carry an execution
beam, and hand over everything you own* has done the opposite of its job. The
CLARITY / WHY-LAW governs all three:

1. **"Hate not his father and mother" (v26).** This is a **Semitic comparison**, not an
   emotion. The language had no comparative "love less" — Genesis 29:31 says Leah was
   "hated," and the same chapter says plainly that Jacob loved Rachel *more*. Matthew
   records the same teaching in the plain form: *"He that loveth father or mother more
   than me…"* (Matt 10:37). The narration says this **out loud** (n3). Without it, this
   video should not exist.
   **But it is not defused into nothing.** He is asking for first place, and first place
   is a seat you cannot give to two people. The cost stays real (n4).
2. **"Bear his cross" (v27).** Explained, never depicted. Rome crucified along the public
   roads; the condemned man carried his own beam through his own town. To pick it up was
   to have already said goodbye to your life. That is a fact of history the narrator
   states — and no frame in this video shows it.
3. **"Forsaketh not all that he hath" (v33).** The verb is about **letting go of your
   claim**, not about a liquidation sale in the road that afternoon. n10 lands it as:
   *not that everything is taken from you — that nothing is off limits to him.*

**The closing card is an INVITATION, never a fear-question.** Banned framings ("are you
ready?", "could you pay it?", "what if you can't finish?") do not appear. The card asks
what the viewer would want to *ask* a man who refuses to lie to them about the price.

## 3. Why this story shows that Jesus is good (the turn — the reason the video exists)

Read fast, Luke 14:25–33 sounds like a man raising his prices. Read honestly, it is the
single most striking piece of integrity in the gospels:

**He had the biggest crowd of his life, and he used it to talk people out of following
him.** Every movement in history inflates its numbers. He deflated his — out loud, on
purpose, in front of everybody, and then let people walk home.

Nothing else in a person's life does that. The loan showed you the payment afterward. The
job showed you the hours afterward. The habit showed you the price years afterward. He
turned around on a road and told a crowd the entire cost **first**, and then let them
choose. He is the only one who ever refused to get you to sign before you had read it.

And the counting itself is a **kindness**: he is not protecting himself from you, he is
protecting you from a half-built life. The whole point of a man sitting down with the
numbers is that **the tower gets finished** (s14).

The last beat (n15) is the one that makes the whole thing bearable: the man asking that
crowd to sit down and count what it would cost *them* had already sat down and counted
what it would cost *him* — he was walking toward Jerusalem while he said it. He knew the
number. He did not turn back. (One line. Light touch. This is milk, not the Passion arc.)
