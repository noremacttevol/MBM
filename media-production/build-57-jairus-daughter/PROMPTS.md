instant — no comic strip, no panels, no stacked or side-by-side frames, no dividing lines.

TIME/SETTING: bright open DAY moving to warm indoor light. A first-century Galilean
lakeside town — pale stone streets and houses. s1-s2 in the crowded street, s3 on the road,
s5-s9 at and inside the house of Jairus, a modest well-kept home with plastered walls, a
small window, a low bed-mat where the child lies. Warm natural daylight; no night.

THE GIRL (twelve years old): a young first-century Galilean girl of about twelve, dark hair,
in a simple, MUTED pale grey and soft dun dress — clearly NOT cream, distinctly duller than
Jesus's warm cream robe. She lies peacefully as if asleep, small and still (s5-s7), then
sits up and stands, alive, warm-cheeked and well (s8). Never gruesome, never morbid.

JAIRUS: a dignified first-century synagogue ruler of middle years, a well-kept beard, in a
fuller formal robe of deep indigo-blue and brown with a woven prayer-fringe, clearly NOT
cream. His face is shown: desperate and pleading (s1), then crushed with grief (s3), then
flooded with disbelieving joy (s8).

THE MOTHER: the girl's mother, a first-century woman, head covered, in muted dun and
grey-blue, clearly NOT cream, grief-worn then overjoyed. THE THREE (Peter, James, John,
s6-s8): plain Galilean fishermen in dun, brown and grey-blue, short hair and beards, NONE
in cream. THE MOURNERS (s5): townsfolk weeping, in dun, faded brown and mourning greys, none
in cream.

ONLY JESUS WEARS CREAM (build law). In every frame he is the ONLY figure in a warm off-white
or cream robe, and the only man with long hair past the shoulders. The girl's dress and all
other clothing are duller, darker colours. Check this on every generated frame.
