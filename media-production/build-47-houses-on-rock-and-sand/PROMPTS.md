# STILL PROMPTS — Story Video #47: Houses on Rock and Sand (matthew-7_houses-on-rock-and-sand)

Matthew 7:24-27. STILLS-ONLY (Law E) — twelve painted stills, no motion clips.

CARE FLAGS: none — GREEN, plain milk (CONTENT-CARE.md). Still handled gently: the
foolish house's fall is about the HOUSE, never a person harmed — the foolish builder
stands safe on the bank watching his work go, dismayed but unhurt. No gore, no one
crushed or drowning. Milk framing: this is an INVITATION to build your life on his
words, not a fear threat. The WHY the whole video carries: BOTH men heard the same
words — the only difference was that one went and DID them.

Jesus appears in only TWO shots — s1 and s11, the FRAME where he is teaching the
crowd at the end of the Sermon on the Mount. In both the camera is directly behind
him, the back of his head to us, his face never shown, never visible, at any angle.
The parable (s3-s10) has no divine figure: the two builders are ordinary men, faces
shown.

JESUS LOCK (identical wording in s1 and s11; project-wide look standard): a
first-century Middle-Eastern man, warm olive-brown skin, LONG dark brown-black hair
past the shoulders, one plain undyed off-white cream wool robe, bare-headed, no hood;
no glow, no bright ring, no outlining light; an ordinary solid man. The camera is always directly
behind him and his face is never shown, never visible, at any angle.

CROWD LOCK (s1, s2, s11) — the multitude on the grassy hillside: ordinary Galilean
people of every age, men, women and a few children, in plain earth-tone wool of dun,
faded brown, olive and muted blue. Their clothes are all clearly darker than the one
cream robe of the figure seen from behind; not one of them wears off-white or cream,
and no second man is in a cream robe. Their faces ARE shown — listening hard, then
astonished.

WISE-BUILDER LOCK (s2, s3, s4, s5, s6) — a sturdy working man of about forty-five,
warm olive-brown skin, sun-weathered, a full dark beard streaked with grey, wearing a
belted RUSSET-BROWN wool work tunic with the sleeves rolled to the elbow, strong
calloused hands, a plain leather tool-belt with a wooden mallet. Diligent, calm,
humble, never grim. His face IS shown, consistent throughout.

FOOLISH-BUILDER LOCK (s2, s7, s8, s9, s10) — a leaner man of about thirty, warm
olive-brown skin, a short scruffy dark beard, wearing a SANDY-TAN wool tunic with a
simple cord belt, quick and easy-going. He is NOT a villain and not comic — a likeable
man who took the fast way. His face IS shown, consistent throughout. (He is plainly a
DIFFERENT, younger, lighter-dressed man than the older grey-bearded russet-clad wise
builder, so viewers can always tell the two apart.)

ROCK-HOUSE LOCK (s4, s5, s6, s10) — a solid single-storey house of fitted stone blocks
standing on higher STONY/bedrock ground, its deep stone foundation cut into the living
rock and clearly visible, one small warm window, a plain wooden door.

SAND-HOUSE LOCK (s8, s9, s10) — a similar-size single-storey mud-brick house built
right on the FLAT smooth pale SAND of a dry riverbed, with NO dug foundation at all,
sitting straight on the surface, one window, a plain door — quick and easy work.

STILL STYLE BLOCK (prepended to every prompt, exactly):
Beautiful hand-painted 2D animation style, reverent and warm, like a classic
illustrated storybook of scripture brought to life. Soft painterly brushstroke
textures, glowing golden light, muted earth tones with warm gold highlights.
First-century Judea. Sacred, hushed tone. Not photorealistic. No text or captions
in the image. Historically modest clothing: rough-woven wool and linen in undyed
earth colors. No modern objects.

Every figure has two arms, two hands, two legs and one head (anatomy guard). Every
prompt ends with "One single continuous scene painted edge to edge." Skip any
comic-strip or multi-panel variant the model returns.

One single continuous scene painted edge to edge.

## s9-great-was-the-fall — Shot 9: and it fell: and great was the fall of it
REF: s8-the-house-on-the-sand
[STILL STYLE BLOCK] The same dry riverbed now a raging brown flash-flood at the height of the storm, rain driving down, wind howling. The floodwater has torn the loose sand out from under the mud-brick house, and the house is COLLAPSING — its walls buckling and breaking apart, mud bricks tumbling into the churning brown water and washing away. There are NO people in the collapsing house and no one is hurt or crushed or drowning: the young foolish builder stands well back on the safe high bank in his sandy-tan tunic, out of the water, watching his house go, his face painted fully and clearly, stricken and dismayed and helpless. The picture is dramatic — a house breaking apart in a flood — but it is only the house that falls; the man is safe on the bank, only heartbroken. Two arms, two hands, two legs, one head. One single continuous scene painted edge to edge.
