# STILL PROMPTS — Video #118: Jonah and the God Who Relents (jonah-1_jonah-god-who-relents)

Book of Jonah. STILLS-ONLY (Law E) — ten painted stills. MILK / mercy-in-judgment. CARE
FLAG: J — the MERCY is the story. God relents; Nineveh is SPARED on screen and in the closing
card. The judgment ("yet forty days") is spoken but never depicted as destruction — the city
is never shown burning or falling. The arc lands on a whole city forgiven and a reluctant
prophet learning that God's compassion is wider than his own. The fish is God's RESCUE (Jonah
would have drowned), shown reverently — never gory horror.

NO DIVINE FIGURE ANYWHERE. Where God speaks, the picture is warm radiant light — no figure,
no face, no shape, no hand-of-God-as-a-person. JONAH is a HUMAN and DOES show his face — keep
him identical every frame: warm Middle-Eastern skin, a weathered man with a greying beard and
greying dark hair, a dull olive-brown robe with a faded ochre cloak. Sailors and the people of
Nineveh show their faces too (kept human and varied). Nobody wears cream or off-white (there
is no Jesus figure in this video — Jesus is never shown, so cream never appears).

STILL STYLE BLOCK (prepended to every prompt, exactly):
Beautiful hand-painted 2D animation style, reverent and warm, like a classic
illustrated storybook of scripture brought to life. Soft painterly brushstroke
textures, glowing golden light, muted earth tones with warm gold highlights.
First-century Judea. Sacred, hushed tone. Not photorealistic. No text or captions
in the image. Historically modest clothing: rough-woven wool and linen in undyed
earth colors. No modern objects.

Every figure has two arms, two hands, two legs and one head (anatomy guard). Every
prompt ends with "One single tall upright vertical scene painted edge to edge, no panels."
Skip any comic-strip or multi-panel variant the model returns.

MUST NEVER SHOW: God as a figure, face or hand-of-God-as-a-person; anyone in cream or
off-white; the city being destroyed, burning or falling; gore or a horror-mouth fish; any
baked-in text or written letters; any modern object; a multi-panel or comic-strip layout.
