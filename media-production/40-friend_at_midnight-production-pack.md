## 1. THE SCRIPTURE CARD

### The passage in full context (KJV, Luke 11:1-13)

The parable does not stand alone. It sits inside one single answer to one single
question — *"Lord, teach us to pray."* Cutting the parable out of that answer is
exactly the Full-Story Law violation the Bible warns about, and it is how this
story gets misread. The whole unit is:

1. **vv. 1-4** — the disciples ask him to teach them to pray; he gives them a
   prayer that opens by calling God **Father**.
2. **vv. 5-8** — the parable of the friend at midnight.
3. **vv. 9-10** — ask, seek, knock.
4. **vv. 11-13** — the father-and-son argument: if you, being evil, know how to
   give good gifts, **how much more** shall your heavenly Father.

> **1** And it came to pass, that, as he was praying in a certain place, when he
> ceased, one of his disciples said unto him, Lord, teach us to pray, as John also
> taught his disciples.
> **2** And he said unto them, When ye pray, say, Our Father which art in heaven,
> Hallowed be thy name. Thy kingdom come. Thy will be done, as in heaven, so in earth.
> **3** Give us day by day our daily bread.
> **4** And forgive us our sins; for we also forgive every one that is indebted to us.
> And lead us not into temptation; but deliver us from evil.
> **5** And he said unto them, Which of you shall have a friend, and shall go unto him
> at midnight, and say unto him, Friend, lend me three loaves;
> **6** For a friend of mine in his journey is come to me, and I have nothing to set
> before him?
> **7** And he from within shall answer and say, Trouble me not: the door is now shut,
> and my children are with me in bed; I cannot rise and give thee.
> **8** I say unto you, Though he will not rise and give him, because he is his friend,
> yet because of his importunity he will rise and give him as many as he needeth.
> **9** And I say unto you, Ask, and it shall be given you; seek, and ye shall find;
> knock, and it shall be opened unto you.
> **10** For every one that asketh receiveth; and he that seeketh findeth; and unto him
> that knocketh it shall be opened.
> **11** If a son shall ask bread of any of you that is a father, will he give him a
> stone? or if he shall ask a fish, will he for a fish give him a serpent?
> **12** Or if he shall ask an egg, will he offer him a scorpion?
> **13** If ye then, being evil, know how to give good gifts unto your children: how
> much more shall your heavenly Father give the Holy Spirit to them that ask him?

### MUST SHOW (the text states these)

- It is the disciples who ask him to teach them to pray (v1).
- The prayer he gives them opens by calling God **Father** (v2).
- **Midnight** — the parable is a NIGHT story (v5). Deep dark, no sun.
- The man goes to a **friend's** house, not a stranger's or a rich man's.
- He asks for **three loaves** — a small ask, one man's supper.
- **He is not asking for himself.** A travelling friend has arrived and he has
  nothing to feed him (v6). Every request in this story is made on behalf of
  somebody else.
- The neighbour **refuses** (v7): the door is barred, the children are in bed
  with him, he cannot get up. His reason is real, not lazy.
- He gets up anyway — **not because of friendship**, but because of the man's
  *importunity* (v8). The text says this in so many words.
- He gives him **as many as he needeth** — more than the three he asked for (v8).
- Ask / seek / knock, and the flat promise that everyone who does, receives (vv9-10).
- The father-and-son pairs: bread/stone, fish/serpent, egg/scorpion (vv11-12).
- The final gift is **not bread** — it is the **Holy Spirit**, God giving himself (v13).

### MUST NEVER SHOW

- **Jesus's face — never, at any angle, in any frame.** He appears in four shots
  (s1, s2, s12, s15) and in every one the camera is behind him. (The #1 Law.)
- **No divine figure anywhere inside the parable.** vv5-8 are a story Jesus is
  TELLING. The householder, the traveller and the sleeping neighbour are ordinary
  men with ordinary faces, fully shown.
- **No glow, halo, or rim-light** on Jesus. Ever.
- **No sunset, no sunrise, no golden-hour sky in the parable.** Luke 11:5 says
  *midnight.* The parable's only warm light is the flame of a clay oil lamp and a
  thin moon. (Time-of-Day Law — the storm-11 rejection.)
- **The neighbour is never a villain or a comic grump.** The text gives him a real
  reason. A cartoon grouch destroys the argument the parable is making.
- **God is never depicted as the sleeping neighbour.** That is the misreading this
  video exists to correct — it is never staged, never implied, never captioned.
- No serpent and no scorpion in any image. The narrator carries vv11-12; painting
  a snake or a scorpion into a tender father-and-son beat invites an ugly frame
  and an AI-weird one. Words only.
- No fake tears, no teardrop beads on anyone's cheek.
- No modern objects. No text baked into any image.

## 2. THE POINT (the Clarity / Why-Law check)

**A viewer with zero Bible background must be able to say the point in one sentence.**

The point of this video: *God is not the man behind the door — he is the opposite
of him, so ask without shame.*

This parable is misread more often than almost any other, and the misreading is
cruel: people hear *"pester God until he caves"* and come away believing God is
reluctant, irritated, and asleep. Jesus is arguing the other direction. The
neighbour is a **contrast**, not a portrait — a *lesser-to-greater* argument. If
even a man who has every good reason to say no will finally get up, **how much
more** the Father who was never asleep in the first place. The video must say this
out loud (n14a / n14b), or it will do harm.

## 3. STUDY GEMS (small, woven in, never a lecture)

- **People travelled at night** in that heat, so a guest genuinely could arrive at
  midnight, worn out and starving.
- **Hospitality was a village duty, not a personal favour.** If the guest went to
  bed hungry the shame landed on the whole town — which is why the neighbour's
  bread was, in a sense, already owed.
- **A peasant house was one room.** The whole family slept together on one raised
  platform under one blanket, animals below, a heavy wooden bar across the door.
  Getting up really did mean waking every child in the house. The refusal is honest.
- **"Importunity" (Greek *anaideia*) appears nowhere else in the New Testament.**
  It does not mean persistence. It means **shamelessness** — he simply was not too
  proud to look foolish. (Placed BEFORE Jesus's verse-8 line, so the KJV word lands
  already understood — and so the narrator never has to echo Jesus's sentence back.)
- **Ask / seek / knock are continuous verbs in the Greek** — keep asking, keep
  coming back. Not one polite try.
- **Each of the three pairs in vv11-12 is a LOOKALIKE.** A smooth pale river stone
  is the size and shape of a flat loaf; a scorpion curls up pale and round like an
  egg. Jesus is describing a father who hands his hungry child a **counterfeit** —
  and saying no father alive would do it.
- **The man at the door never asks for himself.** He is begging for bread he will
  not eat.

j0 = 11:2 · j1 = 11:5b-6 · j2 = 11:7 · j3 = **11:8 (verse card)** · j4 = 11:9 · j5 = 11:13.

Per the #38/#39 precedent, the characters quoted *inside* the parable (the man at

meaning and never re-quotes or echoes the KJV wording. This is why the
*shamelessness* gem is placed BEFORE j3, not after it — so the narrator explains
the idea in his own words and never has to repeat Jesus's sentence back.
