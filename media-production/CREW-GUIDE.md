# CREW GUIDE — How Anybody Runs the MBM Video Factory

> This guide is for the humans on the crew: **Cameron** and **Leighton**.
> It is written so an 11-year-old can run a session and so any AI session
> knows exactly how to work with whoever is at the keyboard.
> The PRODUCTION-BIBLE.md is still the law for HOW videos are made.
> This file is the law for HOW PEOPLE work the system.

---

## 1. Who does what

**The AI (Claude, in this project) does the heavy lifting:**
- Picks which story is next (from the queue in section 6)
- Reads the story's production pack and the scripture
- Writes every prompt — the crew NEVER has to write a prompt
- Drives Chrome to generate images and clips in Google Flow
- Checks every image and clip against the rules (face law, style, scripture)
- Assembles the finished video on this computer (free)
- Shows the finished video and explains, in plain words, what it did and why

**The crew (Cameron or Leighton) does the human part:**
- Says who is at the keyboard (section 2)
- Answers the AI's story questions — "which moment hits you hardest?",
  "does this picture FEEL like the story?" Your feelings are real data.
  The whole point is videos that feel human, not AI-made.
- Says yes/no on the look of images and clips as they come out
- Watches the finished video

**Cameron alone:**
- Gives the FINAL approval on every video. Nothing ships without his yes.
- Leighton's approval means "ready for Dad to review" — then she and the AI
  move on to the next story in the queue while Dad's list grows.

## 2. Starting and ending a shift

- Cameron opens the session. When Leighton takes over, he says something like
  **"Leighton is working on it for the day."** From then on the AI talks to
  Leighton — same rules, plain words, patient explanations.
- When Cameron is back he says **"this is cameron again."**
- The AI keeps a running note in the session of which videos reached
  "ready for Dad" status so Cameron can review them in one sitting.

## 3. The session script (what a shift looks like)

1. AI states which story is next and why, and reads its pack + scripture.
2. AI shows the storyboard: each beat, still or motion, and the narration.
   **Crew question round:** the AI asks 2–3 short questions about the story
   ("what part would make YOU stop scrolling?"). Answers can change the
   storyboard — that's the point.
3. AI generates stills in Flow (free-ish), showing them as they pass its own
   QC. Crew reacts: keep / redo. The AI explains each prompt in one line so
   the crew learns how prompting works.
4. AI generates the motion clips (10 credits each on Ultra) for money moments
   only, scrubs them fully, shows the keepers.
5. AI assembles, runs the full QC checklist, and presents the finished video.
6. Leighton on shift → mark it "READY FOR DAD," AI logs it, next story begins.
   Cameron on shift → he says yes (ship it) or no (AI fixes and re-presents).
7. End of shift: AI writes the session log entry, commits, pushes. Always.

## 4. The learning goal (working toward hands-off)

Cameron and Leighton are learning by watching. So every shift the AI:
- Explains each step in one or two plain sentences as it does it
- Shows the exact prompt text before submitting it, so the crew sees how
  the master style block + the story beat become a prompt
- When the crew wants to try driving: the AI switches to **prompt-only mode**
  — it writes the prompts and the QC checklist, the human pastes them into
  Flow and clicks the buttons. The AI still checks the results.

## 5. Crew safety rails (never break these, even to go faster)

- Jesus's face is NEVER visible. His hands are never visible. If you see
  either, the clip is dead no matter how pretty it is.
- Jesus speaks ONLY his exact KJV words. The narrator never puts words in
  his mouth.
- The painted storybook look is locked. Anything that looks like a photo or
  a 3D render gets rejected.
- Never rush the sacred moment — the music always dies to silence before
  Jesus's words.
- If you're not sure about something, don't approve it. Flag it for Dad.
  There is no prize for speed that's worth a wrong video about Jesus.

## 6. The queue (what gets made next, and in what order)

- **Next two (locked validation, LOW animation — mostly stills):**
  1. **#8 The Lost Coin** (Luke 15) — a quiet parable, perfect all-stills test.
  2. **#6 The Two Sons** (Matt 21) — short, two contrasting stills sequences.
- Then resume THE-200.md catalog order within Section I (packs 2–20 are
  written), applying the Story-Fit Rule for motion budget per story.
- The AI recommends, the crew can bump a story up if it means something to
  them — a story that moves the maker makes a better video.

## 7. Which parts of a story become video vs. spoken only

The AI decides using the Story-Fit Rule and explains its call in the
storyboard, in one line per beat:
- **Spoken over a still:** feelings, backstory, time passing, anything the
  narrator can carry ("She had spent everything she had...").
- **Motion clip:** the moment the story turns — the touch, the turn, the
  father running, the storm going still. If the beat would make you gasp
  in person, it earns motion.
- **Neither (cut):** anything that doesn't serve the Seed — the one question
  about God's goodness the video leaves behind.

## 8. Why we're doing it this way (read this once, remember it always)

We are not making content. We are introducing people to the kindness of
Jesus, one true story at a time, milk before meat. Every choice — the pacing,
the silence before his words, the question at the end — exists so a stranger
scrolling at 2am feels SEEN the way the woman in the crowd was seen.
If a video wouldn't make Jesus glad, it doesn't ship.
