# CONTENT-CARE — The Care Law for the Stories That Aren't Plain Milk

> **Why this file exists (Cameron, 2026-07-13).** THE-200 is the right catalog, but not
> every story in it is safe to hand a brand-new viewer the same way. Some contain
> violence, judgment, devils, sexual sin, dead children, or a knife over a boy on an
> altar. Scripture does not hide these things and neither do we — but Jesus never used
> fear, gore, or shame to teach, and neither does this app. This file is the law for
> HOW those stories get made and WHEN the app is allowed to serve them.
>
> **This file binds every video build.** Read it in step 4 of the production protocol,
> right alongside PRODUCTION-BIBLE.md. Before storyboarding any story, look it up in
> the flag table below. If it carries flags, every law for those flags binds the build.
> Unlisted story numbers are GREEN — plain milk, no special handling.
>
> **This file also feeds the app.** The flag table is the machine-readable source the
> Phase-2 feed engine imports for serving rules. Production and routing read the SAME
> table so a story is never made one way and served another.

---

## The one sentence that governs everything

**We tell the whole story true, but the camera has Jesus's restraint: the weight lands
on faces and mercy, never on wounds, flames, or fear.**

Scripture states hard things in a sentence and moves on — it never lingers. Our
pictures do the same.

---

## §1. The Care Laws (production — how flagged stories are MADE)

### R — THE RESTRAINT LAW (violence and suffering)
The violence in the text is never the picture. No gore, no wounds in close-up, no
blows landing, no blood pooling, no lingering on an instrument of pain.
- Show the **aftermath and the witnesses**, not the act: the healed ear, not the sword
  stroke; the fourth man IN the fire, not burning flesh; weapons going into the earth,
  not the killing.
- The **crucifixion arc (#120–141)**: the cross may be visible and real, but nails are
  never shown driving, the scourging is never depicted, wounds are never the focal
  point. The witnesses' faces — Mary, John, the centurion, the thief — carry the
  weight. Reverent restraint, exactly like the face law: what we withhold is what
  makes it worship instead of spectacle.
- QC question for every frame of an R story: **"Would a parent let a 10-year-old see
  this frame?"** If no, regenerate.

### A — THE ADVERSARY LAW (Satan and devils)
**The adversary is never given a face, body, or figure in any frame — the same logic
as the Jesus-face law from the other side.** We do not paint what we do not know, and
we never make evil watchable. No shadow-creature, no monster, no horror-genre imagery,
ever.
- His reality shows in the **person's condition and the deliverance**: the man among
  the tombs BEFORE (torn clothes, isolation, the villagers' fear at a distance) and
  AFTER (clothed, calm, sitting at Jesus's feet). The freedom is the picture.
- Self-harm in the text (the Gerasene cutting himself with stones) is **never
  depicted and never described in the narration beyond scripture's own single
  sentence.** Scars may not be shown.
- Convulsions/seizures (#71): one restrained beat at most — the father's desperate
  face is the shot, not the boy's suffering.
- In the temptations (#83) and the First Vision's dark moment (#200, if included):
  the adversary is presence, never portrait — Jesus alone in the wilderness, the
  darkness before the light. The temptation is heard, not seen.

### D — THE DIGNITY LAW (sexual sin in the text)
People in these stories are painted the way Jesus treated them: **as persons, never
as their sin.** Nothing sexualized, no suggestive framing, no shame-staging (no
painted "fallen woman" tropes — torn dress, smeared makeup, leering crowds in
close-up).
- The woman taken in adultery (#88): she is a frightened human being; the stones are
  in hands that LOWER them; no stone is ever in the air. The picture of the story is
  the stones on the ground and her face hearing "neither do I condemn thee."
- Hosea (#177): the redemption at the price paid, never the scandal. Narration keeps
  scripture's own economy of words about what she was.

### J — THE MERCY-IN-JUDGMENT LAW (hell, torment, exclusion, destruction)
These stories are in the catalog because Jesus told them — but told wrong, they
rebuild the exact cruel-God picture this whole project exists to correct. So:
- **The narration must find and carry the mercy that is IN the text** — it is always
  there: the tares spared till harvest for the wheat's sake, the fig tree given one
  more year, the door knocked on all night, "how often would I have gathered you."
  A judgment story that ships without its mercy spoken out loud is out of spec.
- **Hellfire and torment are never rendered literally.** The rich man and Lazarus
  (#37): heat, distance, and the great gulf as restrained visual metaphor — never a
  lake-of-fire torture scene. Outer darkness (#43) is darkness, not a pit of screams.
  The destruction of the cities (#165, #182) happens OFF-SCREEN: what we show is
  Abraham negotiating and the darkness with the voice in it — never people dying.
- **The closing question card is an INVITATION, never a threat.** Banned forever:
  "are you ready?", "which will you be?", "what if tonight...?" fear-questions.
  The Seed of a judgment story is always about God's patience or the open door —
  fear is not this app's tool, because it was not his. (The watching servants keep
  the door ready **with joy, not dread** — that line in THE-200 #50 is the standard
  for the whole section.)

### G — THE GRIEF-CARE LAW (dead children, infertility, despair)
These stories are gold for the grieving — IF the framing is honest. The danger is a
closing card that reads as a promise God has not made for today.
- **Never promise the same miracle.** The widow of Nain got her son back that hour;
  the viewer who buried a child last year did not. The Seed of a raising story is
  what it reveals about God — *he saw HER, he stopped, he felt it* — plus the
  resurrection as real HOPE ahead, never as an implied refund now.
- Despair in the text (Elijah's "take away my life," Hannah's silent weeping, the
  cry from the cross) is **honored, not rushed**: God's answer in each is presence
  and gentleness, and the narration lands there.
- A dead child is shown the way scripture shows it: the sleeping form, the parents'
  faces, the covered bier — never anything a bereaved parent could not bear to see.

### C — THE CHILDREN LAW (a child in peril)
**A child in danger is never the rendered image.** Isaac bound with the knife raised
(#166) is not painted — the pictures of that story are the walk up, the boy's
question, the RAM, the release, the embrace: "God himself provides the lamb" is the
story, and the imagery obeys it. Herod's massacre (#118) is never shown and never
described beyond scripture's sentence — the picture is the family on the refugee
road, the escape, God knowing displacement from inside.

## §2. Serving rules (app-side — WHEN a flagged story may appear)

These map the same flags to the Phase-2 feed/prescription engine. Production writes
the flags; the engine obeys them. (This is invisible emergent routing per the Jesus
Method — never a visible gate.)

- **L (LATE):** never among a user's **first 5 stories** and never a cold-open story
  for a new user. The engine serves L stories only after the profile shows engagement
  (answers given, stories completed) — the relationship earns the harder material,
  the same way Jesus saved Gethsemane teachings for the last night, not the first
  crowd.
- **ARC (#120–141, the Passion):** served as an **ordered journey the user chooses**
  ("Walk the last week with him") — never as a random single drop into a new user's
  feed. Individual ARC stories may still be prescribed out of order to a user whose
  profile shows they are ready (e.g. #131 to someone wrestling with forgiveness).
- **J stories:** never served while a user's recent language shows fresh church-hurt
  or fear-of-God trauma — those users get MILK about his gentleness first. J stories
  are for the curious and the steady, not the bleeding.
- **G stories:** MAY be prescribed to the grieving — that is who they are FOR — but
  the engine should prefer them when grief language is present-and-processing, not
  raw-first-week. The AI chat link after a G story is the most important one in the
  app; a grieving user who taps it must reach the gentlest version of the minister.
- **A / R / D stories:** no serving restriction beyond any L flag they carry — the
  care is in production. They are true milk once made under the laws above.
- **Section IX (#181–200):** already governed by the BOM law (post-signal only /
  member track from day one). Flags below add PRODUCTION care on top, not routing
  change.

## §4. Originality and the Church's materials (the copyright law)

The app must never take the Church's created works, and must never look official.

- **Scripture text is safe as we already use it:** the KJV is public domain in the
  United States, and the Book of Mormon / D&C / Pearl of Great Price ORIGINAL texts
  are public domain. **The Church's modern study apparatus is NOT** — chapter
  headings, footnotes, Bible Dictionary, Topical Guide, institute manuals: never
  quote or paraphrase those into narration or cards. Verse text only.
- **Never use Church-produced media of any kind:** no frames, audio, or style-copies
  of the Bible Videos / Book of Mormon Videos, no Church artwork (Friberg, Anderson,
  etc.), no hymn recordings, no General Conference clips, no Church logos or
  wordmarks. Our pipeline is 100% original paintings and original narration — that
  is already the rule; this line makes it law.
- **Never look official.** The app never uses the Church's name or trademarks in its
  own branding, and it states plainly (About screen / store listing) that it is an
  independent effort by a member and **not an official product of The Church of
  Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints.** When a person is ready for the real thing,
  we point them to the missionaries and ChurchofJesusChrist.org — that handoff is
  the Phase-3 design and it is also what keeps us honest.
- This aligns us with how the Church asks members to share: personal, honest,
  never pretending to speak FOR the Church. We are a member's testimony at scale,
  not an unauthorized channel of the institution.

## §5. The member track must FEED scripture time, not eat it

For MAINTENANCE-track users (self-identified members), the standing design rule:

- **Every member-track video points INTO the standard works, not around them.** The
  closing card for a member includes the reading ("Read all of Mark 5 — it takes
  four minutes") and the app treats opening actual scripture as the win, not watch
  time.
- The engine never optimizes a member's session length. If a member's usage pattern
  looks like doom-scroll replacement of study rather than an on-ramp to it, the app
  should gently point them to the chapter, not queue another video. Metrics serve
  the person (Gospel Principle: a person is not a data point) — for a member, the
  metric that matters is whether they went to the source.
