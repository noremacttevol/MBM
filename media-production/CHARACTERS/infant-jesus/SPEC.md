## What scripture tells us
- Luke 2:7 — "wrapped him in swaddling clothes, and laid him in a manger."
  Swaddling bands and the manger are the two named visual facts.
- Luke 2:12 — the sign given the shepherds is exactly that: "the babe wrapped
  in swaddling clothes, lying in a manger."
- Luke 2:16 — the shepherds find "the babe lying in a manger" — a newborn.
- Matt 2:11 — by the wise men's arrival he is "the young child" in a **house**,
  not the stable. Older than newborn (see the two sub-stages below).
- Isa 53:2 — "no beauty that we should desire him" — an ordinary infant, not an
  idealized cherub, never a glowing or haloed baby.

## Two sub-stages (do not mix them)
| Sub-stage | Used in | Look |
|---|---|---|
| **newborn** | #84, #85 | Hours-to-days old. Swaddling bands, in the manger. Eyes mostly closed or barely open. Fine dark newborn hair. |
| **young child** | #86 | Roughly one to two years old, per Matt 2:11 + 2:16. Sitting or held upright, alert, dark hair coming in, plain small tunic — NOT swaddled, NOT in a manger. |

## Continuity anchors (never change with age — the same person as the man)
Warm tan olive-brown skin; warm BROWN eyes; the same facial structure and nose
of JESUS-MASTER-REF rendered in infant proportions; dark brown-black hair.
Never caucasian, never pale, never blue-eyed, never blond.

## Standard garments
Newborn: undyed off-white CREAM linen swaddling bands (the cream signature is
his alone, even as a babe). Young child: a plain undyed CREAM wool child's
tunic. No halo, no glow, no radiance, ever — the light in the scene comes from
lamps, stars, or the sky, never from the child.
