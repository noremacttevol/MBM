## What scripture tells us
- Luke 6:15 and Acts 1:13 — "Simon called Zelotes."
- Matt 10:4 and Mark 3:18 — "Simon the Canaanite" (Aramaic *qanana*, the same
  word: zealous one).
- The name is the whole portrait scripture gives: a man who came out of the
  fiercest patriotic party in Israel — men who wanted Rome thrown out by
  force — and followed a Master who told him to love his enemies. He shares a
  table with Matthew, who collected taxes FOR Rome. That contained fire, now
  under discipline, is what his face carries: intense, watchful, controlled.
- Scripture gives no physical description; default is historically accurate
  first-century Judean.

## Written description
A wiry, intense man of about thirty-five: warm olive-brown Middle Eastern skin,
weathered and lean-cheeked, black hair pulled back tight from a high forehead
and tied at the nape, and a sharply trimmed narrow black beard. Dark, alert,
deep-set eyes that keep watch on the edges of a room. A lean, hard, restless
build — a fighter's frame at rest. The look is contained fire and discipline,
never rage: he is never scowling, never menacing.

## Standard garments
A DEEP OXBLOOD-MAROON wool tunic — a dark red with brown-purple depth, clearly
darker and redder than Judas Iscariot's orange-toned dark russet — belted with
a wide plain leather strap, a plaited leather cord worn at one wrist, a dark
brown mantle when a cloak is needed, worn leather sandals. No weapon of any
kind on the sheet. Never cream, never off-white.
