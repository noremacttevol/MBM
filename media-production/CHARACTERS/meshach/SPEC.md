## What scripture tells us (Daniel 1, 3)
- 1:6-7 — Mishael of the children of Judah, renamed Meshach by the prince of
  the eunuchs — a young Judean noble exile in Babylon, companion of Daniel.
- 1:4 — among the youths "in whom was no blemish, but well favoured, and
  skilful in all wisdom" — young, handsome, capable.
- 3:12 — "whom thou hast set over the affairs of the province of Babylon" —
  an appointed official; court dress, not laborer's dress.
- 3:16-18 — "our God whom we serve is able to deliver us... But if not, we
  will not serve thy gods" — the same quiet immovable courage as his two
  companions; they answer as one.
- 3:27 — "nor was an hair of their head singed, neither were their coats
  changed" — after the furnace he looks EXACTLY the same; the sheet holds
  for before and after.

## Written description
A young Judean noble exile serving as a Babylonian provincial official, in
his early thirties: warm Middle-Eastern skin, dark hair kept neat, a FULLER
DARK beard (his trio marker — visibly fuller than Shadrach's short black
beard; Abednego is clean-shaven), calm grave eyes, a steady thoughtful face
that meets a king's anger without flinching. He is one of THREE who always
read as distinct men: Meshach is the russet-brown one with the fuller dark
beard. He never resembles Shadrach or Abednego.

## Standard garments
A russet-brown ankle-length robe (his locked color, from #119) cut in the
Babylonian court manner, with a plain dark sash and simple leather sandals —
the modest court dress of an appointed exile official. No idol motifs, no
gold. Never white, cream, off-white or pale.
