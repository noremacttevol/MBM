## Why Elias is his own character (Latter-day Saint doctrine)
- **D&C 110:12** — at the Kirtland Temple, "Elias appeared, and committed the
  dispensation of the gospel of Abraham" — a distinct heavenly messenger, appearing
  in the SAME vision as, but SEPARATE from, Elijah (D&C 110:13-16, who restored the
  sealing keys). Two persons, two errands.
- **LDS Bible Dictionary, "Elias"** — Elias is both a title (forerunner) AND a specific
  man who "evidently lived in the days of Abraham" and holds the keys of the Abrahamic
  dispensation. He is NOT Elijah the Tishbite.
- Therefore Elias is depicted as a venerable PATRIARCH of the Abrahamic age — dignified
  and priestly — deliberately UNLIKE Elijah the wild wilderness prophet (2 Kgs 1:8,
  camel-hair, fierce). Distinct man, distinct bearing, distinct garments.

## Written description
A venerable Middle Eastern patriarch of great age, of the age of Abraham: warm tan
olive-brown skin lined with years, a LONG FLOWING WHITE beard and long white hair (an
ancient seer, not grey-streaked-dark like Elijah), calm luminous deep-set eyes, a
serene and priestly bearing — unhurried, dignified, a keeper of covenants rather than a
man of the wilderness. Upright and grave, quietly authoritative.

## Standard garments
A full, well-kept ankle-length undyed CREAM-FREE robe of pale oat/sand wool with a broad
draped mantle over one shoulder and a simple woven headwrap of the patriarchs; a plain
wide cloth girdle; a long smooth wooden staff. Modest and dignified — patriarchal, not
the rough camel-hair of Elijah. NEVER cream/off-white (that is reserved for Jesus), no
ornament, no halo, no glow.
