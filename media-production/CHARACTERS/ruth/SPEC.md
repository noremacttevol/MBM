## What scripture tells us (Ruth)
- 1:4 — a woman of Moab; a foreigner in Israel by choice and covenant.
- 1:16 — "whither thou goest, I will go... thy people shall be my people, and
  thy God my God" — resolve is her defining trait; her face carries it.
- 1:5 — her husband is dead; a young widow with nothing.
- 2:2 — "Let me now go to the field, and glean ears of corn" — a gleaner: the
  poorest work in the harvest, done without shame.
- 2:7 — she "continued even from the morning until now" — a tireless worker.
- 3:11 — "all the city of my people doth know that thou art a virtuous woman"
  — her reputation is her beauty; dignity in poverty.

## Written description
A Moabite woman of about twenty-five: a strong open face, warm olive skin
weathered a little by field work, dark eyes full of resolve, long dark hair
braided beneath her head-shawl. Young, but nothing fragile about her — a
widow who chose loyalty over safety and works from morning until evening
without complaint. Steady, direct, quietly courageous; her poverty is
carried with complete dignity, never pitiable. She never resembles Naomi.

## Standard garments
A dark rust-red wool dress (her locked color, from the #148 lock) with a
deep olive-green head-shawl over her braided dark hair. Plain cord belt,
simple worn leather sandals; in field scenes, a gleaner's small cloth bundle
of barley at her hip. Never pale, never cream.
