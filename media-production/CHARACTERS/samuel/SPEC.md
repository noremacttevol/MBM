## What scripture tells us
- 1 Sam 2:18 — "But Samuel ministered before the LORD, being a child, girded
  with a linen ephod" — a CHILD, in a linen ephod. This is his locked dress.
- 1 Sam 2:19 — "Moreover his mother made him a little coat, and brought it to
  him from year to year" — the little coat Hannah made is his signature outer
  garment.
- 1 Sam 3:1 — "the child Samuel ministered unto the LORD before Eli" — he
  serves in the sanctuary itself, sleeping near the lamp of God (3:3).
- 1 Sam 2:26 — "the child Samuel grew on, and was in favour both with the
  LORD, and also with men" — an open, earnest, likeable boy.

## Written description
An Israelite boy of about eleven: warm olive-tan skin, dark curly hair, a
bright open young face with large attentive dark eyes — earnest, eager to
serve, quick to run when called, wholly without guile. Small and slight, as
a boy is, but steady; a child at home in a holy place, solemn when it matters
and trusting always. No beard, obviously; nothing adult about him.

## Standard garments
The dress scripture gives him: a simple sleeveless oatmeal-linen tunic belted
at the waist — the boy's linen ephod (his locked color, from #104; muted
natural linen, never bright white, never cream) — worn with the little coat
his mother made: a small russet-brown wool robe, open at the front, plainly
homemade and lovingly kept. Bare feet or simple leather sandals.
