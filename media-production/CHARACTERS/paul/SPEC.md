## What scripture tells us
- Acts 22:3 — "born in Tarsus, a city in Cilicia... brought up in this city
  at the feet of Gamaliel" — a Pharisee of Tarsus, trained in the law.
- Phil 3:5 — "an Hebrew of the Hebrews; as touching the law, a Pharisee."
- Acts 18:3 — "by their occupation they were tentmakers" — a working man's
  hands, not a scholar's soft ones.
- 2 Cor 10:10 — "his bodily presence is weak, and his speech contemptible"
  — physically unimposing; his force is in his spirit, not his frame.
- 2 Cor 11:24-27 — beaten, stoned, shipwrecked, in weariness and painfulness
  — a travel-worn, weathered man.
- Scripture gives no portrait beyond this. Early tradition (Acts of Paul)
  remembers him small, balding and bandy-legged; the shipped lock below
  agrees: compact, receding hairline.

## Written description
(Adopts the [PAUL LOCK] shipped verbatim in builds #138, #184 and #186 —
the canonical repeated lock, must not drift.) A compact, energetic Middle
Eastern man of about fifty, a RECEDING hairline with short grey-streaked
dark hair, a full grey-streaked dark beard, keen deep-set eyes under strong
brows. Small and unimposing in frame, burning in expression — the weak
bodily presence of 2 Cor 10:10 carrying an unbreakable spirit; a
tentmaker's calloused, capable hands.
(Variance note: #155 aged him ~fifty-five in dun-and-brown with a pointed
beard; #166 said "balding on top" in generic dark earth tones. The
thrice-repeated #138/#184/#186 lock is canonical going forward.)

## Standard garments
A travel-worn DARK CHARCOAL-BROWN tunic and a faded DARK RUST mantle
(locked in #138/#184/#186), a plain leather belt, worn leather sandals —
never pale, never cream. The clothes of a man who walks the empire and
sews tents for his bread.
