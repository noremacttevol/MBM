## What scripture tells us
- John 12:6 — "he was a thief, and had the bag, and bare what was put
  therein" — the keeper of the company's money; trusted with it.
- John 13:29 — "some of them thought, because Judas had the bag, that Jesus
  had said unto him, Buy those things" — even at the last supper the others
  suspected nothing. He fooled everyone.
- Matt 26:15 — "they covenanted with him for thirty pieces of silver."
- Matt 26:48-49 — the betrayal comes by a kiss and "Hail, master" — closeness
  and courtesy, not menace.
- John 6:71 — "Judas Iscariot the son of Simon."
- Scripture never describes his face. The sheet keeps him a historically
  accurate first-century Judean — and deliberately ORDINARY. Nothing
  cartoonishly sinister: no sneer, no shadowed hood, no rat face. The whole
  weight of the story is that he looked as trustworthy as any of the twelve.

## Written description
A respectable-looking Middle Eastern Jewish man in his mid-thirties: neat
dark hair, a trimmed dark beard kept tidier than the fishermen's, warm olive
skin, an intelligent composed face with steady dark eyes — the reliable one,
the treasurer, the man you would hand your money to. Careful, well-kept
hands. Nothing in his face or bearing marks him out; he reads as ordinary
and dependable in every frame.

## Standard garments
A DARK RUSSET wool tunic (never cream; distinct from every other apostle's
locked color), a good leather belt with the small leather money bag of the
company hanging from it — his identifying prop — a deep charcoal-brown
mantle when a cloak is needed, leather sandals in better repair than a
fisherman's.
