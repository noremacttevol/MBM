## What scripture tells us
- Jonah 1:1 — "Jonah the son of Amittai" — a real prophet with a lineage.
- 2 Kings 14:25 — the same "Jonah, the son of Amittai, the prophet, which was
  of Gath-hepher" — a Galilean prophet in the days of Jeroboam II, eighth
  century BC.
- Jonah 1:3 — "But Jonah rose up to flee unto Tarshish from the presence of
  the LORD" — the runaway: a man in flight from his own calling, and it
  shows on him.
- Jonah 1:5 — asleep in the sides of the ship in the storm — worn out by
  running.
- Jonah 4:1 — "But it displeased Jonah exceedingly, and he was very angry" —
  a stubborn, sulking honesty; his face carries argument, not serenity.
- Jonah 4:5 — he sits east of Nineveh under a booth, waiting and watching —
  the weathered watcher.

## Written description
A weathered Israelite prophet in his fifties: warm Middle-Eastern skin
roughened by sea-spray and desert sun, greying dark hair and a greying
beard, deep-set eyes that are honest but troubled — a man who argues with
God and loses, and knows it. Sturdy but tired; his whole bearing is that of
a runaway who has been caught by mercy: rumpled, salt-worn, real. Never
serene like Elisha — Jonah's face always holds some weather in it.

## Standard garments
A dull olive-brown wool robe (his locked color, from #118) with a faded
ochre cloak thrown over one shoulder. Plain leather belt, worn leather
sandals, everything travel-stained and sea-worn. No prophet's grandeur, no
ornament. Never cream, never off-white.
