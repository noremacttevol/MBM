## What scripture tells us (Daniel 1, 3)
- 1:6-7 — Hananiah of the children of Judah, renamed Shadrach by the prince
  of the eunuchs — a young Judean noble exile in Babylon, like Daniel.
- 1:4 — among the youths "in whom was no blemish, but well favoured, and
  skilful in all wisdom" — young, handsome, capable.
- 3:12 — "whom thou hast set over the affairs of the province of Babylon" —
  an appointed official, so court dress, not laborer's dress.
- 3:16-18 — "our God whom we serve is able to deliver us... But if not, we
  will not serve thy gods" — quiet, immovable courage; his defining bearing.
- 3:27 — "upon whose bodies the fire had no power, nor was an hair of their
  head singed, neither were their coats changed" — after the furnace he
  looks EXACTLY the same; the sheet holds for before and after.

## Written description
A young Judean noble exile serving as a Babylonian provincial official,
about thirty: warm Middle-Eastern skin, dark hair kept neat, a SHORT BLACK
beard (his trio marker — shorter and darker-trimmed than Meshach's fuller
beard; Abednego is clean-shaven), steady dark eyes, a firm calm face set
with quiet dignity rather than defiance. He is one of THREE who always read
as distinct men: Shadrach is the indigo-blue one with the short black beard.
He never resembles Meshach or Abednego.

## Standard garments
A deep indigo-blue ankle-length robe (his locked color, from #119) cut in
the Babylonian court manner, with a plain dark sash and simple leather
sandals — the modest court dress of an appointed exile official. No idol
motifs, no gold. Never white, cream, off-white or pale.
