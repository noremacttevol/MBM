## What scripture tells us
- Gen 2:22 — made from Adam's rib and "brought her unto the man" — the first
  woman, made in full adult grace, never a child, never aged.
- Gen 3:20 — "Adam called his wife's name Eve; because she was the mother of
  all living."
- Gen 3:21 — "coats of skins, and clothed them" — the sheet's locked garment.
  The sheet is ALWAYS the clothed state; never nudity, at any angle, ever.
- Scripture is silent on her features. Default: warm ancient Near Eastern
  features, adult and timeless — the shipped #113 build locked only "earth-
  toned leaf and skin garments, modest," which this sheet obeys.

## Written description
The first woman, adult and timeless — she reads as in her late twenties,
in the full grace of unfallen creation: warm olive-brown skin, very long
thick dark brown-black wavy hair falling past her shoulders down her back,
gentle dark eyes full of intelligence and life, a strong graceful frame
made for the same garden labor as Adam's. The mother of all living: her
face carries warmth, courage and — after Genesis 3 — a quiet sorrow-and-hope,
never despair, never shame played for the camera.

## Standard garments
The coat of skins of Gen 3:21, and nothing else ever on this sheet: a simple
long modest garment of soft FAWN-BROWN animal skins from the shoulders to
the ankle, bound at the waist with a plain twisted hide cord, her long hair
falling naturally over it. Bare feet. Completely modest — shoulders, chest,
torso and legs always covered. No cream, no off-white, no ornament, no halo,
no glow.
