# STILL PROMPTS — Story Video #43: The Wedding Garment (matthew-22_wedding-garment)

Matthew 22:1-14. STILLS-ONLY (Law E) — fourteen painted stills, no motion clips.

CARE FLAGS: **J, L** (CONTENT-CARE.md). This is a JUDGMENT parable, and told wrong it
rebuilds the exact cruel-God picture this whole project exists to correct. So the
MERCY that is IN the text is carried out loud, all the way through: the invitation
goes to EVERYONE off the highways, "both bad and good"; the king CLOTHES every guest
himself at the door; the king calls the one man "Friend." The judgment beats are
rendered with restraint per the J-law: **outer darkness is DARKNESS ONLY — never a
pit of screams, never fire, never torment, never a monster.** The messengers being
killed (v6) and the city burned (v7) happen OFF-SCREEN and at a distance — no gore,
no bodies, no blows landing (the R-restraint principle). The picture of this story is
the FULL, LIT HALL and the free invitation, not the one man outside.

Jesus himself appears in only TWO shots — s1 and s13, the FRAME where he is telling
the parable in the temple. In both, the camera stands directly behind him, the back
of his head to us, and his face is never shown, never visible, at any angle. Verses
2-13 are the parable he is TELLING: inside it there is no divine figure of any kind —
the king, the servants, the guests and the man are ordinary people, drawn fully, their
faces shown. THE KING IS NOT JESUS and is not a depiction of God — he is a character
in a story, exactly like the father in the prodigal and the householder in the friend
at midnight, and his face is shown.

JESUS LOCK (identical wording in s1 and s13; the project-wide look standard): a
first-century Middle-Eastern man, warm olive-brown skin, LONG dark brown-black hair
falling well past the shoulders, one plain undyed off-white cream wool robe (a simple
Judean tunic with a plain mantle, the same garment in both shots), bare-headed with no
hood; no glow, no bright ring, no outlining light of any kind; an ordinary solid man. The camera is
always directly behind him and his face is never shown, never visible, at any angle.

LEADERS LOCK (s1, s13) — the chief priests, scribes and Pharisees he is answering in
the temple court. Eight to ten robed temple men of middle age and older, dignified and
prosperous, in FINE deep-dyed robes of INDIGO BLUE, DEEP MAROON and OCHRE with woven
borders, several wearing a striped prayer shawl with dark blue fringes; among them one
tall stern elder with a long iron-grey beard and one heavier man with a full black
beard. Their clothes are visibly richer and darker than the one plain cream robe of the
figure in the foreground; not one of them is dressed in off-white or cream, and no
second man in these pictures wears a cream robe. Their faces ARE shown, consistent in
both shots — guarded, proud, stiff, beginning to realize the story is about them.

ONLY JESUS WEARS CREAM (build law, PRODUCTION-BIBLE §1). In s1 and s13 the ONLY figure
in an off-white / cream robe is the man seen from behind. Every other man wears a
clearly darker, richer colour (indigo, maroon, ochre). Why: this rule exists so no
viewer ever mistakes an invented, painted likeness for him — a second cream-robed
bearded man reads as a second Jesus. One cream robe per frame, always the man from
behind.

KING LOCK (s2, s3, s4, s7, s10, s11, s12) — the king in the parable. A dignified older
man of about sixty, warm olive-brown skin, a neatly combed SILVER-GREY beard, wearing a
rich DEEP-PURPLE wool mantle with gold-embroidered borders over a long saffron-gold
tunic, and a simple thin gold circlet on his head. He is weighty and warm, a real king
and a good host, never a cartoon tyrant and never comic. His purple-and-gold royal
clothing is unmistakable and is never off-white or cream. His face IS shown, consistent
in every shot he appears in.

SERVANTS LOCK (s3, s4, s8, s9) — the king's household messengers. Ordinary working men
in clean belted tunics of RUSSET and warm BROWN, each with a narrow PURPLE sash at the
waist marking the king's household. Earnest, tired, faithful. Their faces ARE shown.

INVITED-GUESTS LOCK (s4, s5) — the men first invited, who will not come. Well-off
townsmen and merchants of middle age in finer clothes — striped cloaks, dyed blues and
browns, good sandals — turned away, dismissive, busy. Their faces ARE shown.

HIGHWAY-GUESTS LOCK (s8, s9, s10) — the crowd brought in off the roads. Ordinary poor
people of every age: day-labourers, beggars, a man leaning on a stick, a mother with a
child on her hip, old and young, their own clothes ragged and dust-grey and patched.
Weary, astonished, hopeful faces. At the door each of them is handed and helped into a
clean, bright wedding robe of pale honey-gold — fresh and festival, pulled on right
over the road-worn clothes. Their faces ARE shown.

THE-MAN LOCK (s10, s11, s12) — the one guest who keeps his own clothes. An ordinary
Middle-Eastern man of about forty, warm olive-brown skin, dark hair and a short dark
beard, dressed only in his OWN plain dust-brown road tunic and cloak — NO pale honey-gold
wedding robe over it, plainly the only un-robed man in a hall full of robed guests. He
is not a villain and not comic and not ragged-poorer than the rest — every person there
came in off the road. He simply refused the robe he was handed. His face IS shown,
ordinary and closed, consistent in every shot.

STILL STYLE BLOCK (prepended to every prompt, exactly):
Beautiful hand-painted 2D animation style, reverent and warm, like a classic
illustrated storybook of scripture brought to life. Soft painterly brushstroke
textures, glowing golden light, muted earth tones with warm gold highlights.
First-century Judea. Sacred, hushed tone. Not photorealistic. No text or captions
in the image. Historically modest clothing: rough-woven wool and linen in undyed
earth colors. No modern objects.

Every figure has two arms, two hands, two legs and one head (anatomy guard). Every
prompt ends with "One single continuous scene painted edge to edge." Skip any
comic-strip or multi-panel variant the model returns.

[STILL STYLE BLOCK] The great hall of a king's palace at golden evening, prepared for a wedding feast and empty of guests, glowing and expectant. Long low banquet tables run the length of the warm stone hall, spread with white linen and heaped with a feast — roasted meat on great platters, mounded bread, bowls of figs and pomegranates and grapes, jars of wine, oil lamps burning warm down the whole length of the tables. Garlands of green leaves and small flowers are hung along the walls. In the middle of it all stands the king: a dignified older man of about sixty with warm olive-brown skin and a neatly combed silver-grey beard, wearing a rich deep-purple wool mantle with gold-embroidered borders over a long saffron-gold tunic and a simple thin gold circlet on his head. His face is painted fully and clearly, glad and generous, looking out over the ready feast like a father on the morning of his son's wedding. Everything is ready and the hall is warm and full of light and there is no one yet to enjoy it. Every figure has two arms, two hands, two legs and one head. One single continuous scene painted edge to edge.
