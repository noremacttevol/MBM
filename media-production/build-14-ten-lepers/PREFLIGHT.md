### MUST SHOW (facts the text states)
- Journey to Jerusalem, passing between Samaria and Galilee (v11) —
  borderland, which is why Jews and a Samaritan share one miserable band.
- TEN men, lepers, who "stood afar off" (v12) — the GAP is mandated by law;
  exactly ten figures in every group frame until the fork.
- They LIFTED UP THEIR VOICES together: "Jesus, Master, have mercy on us"
  (v13) — the narrator may quote THEIR cry modernly (their words, not his).
- "When he saw them" (v14) — he responds to seeing; no touch, no approach.
- j1 = KJV v14: "Go shew yourselves unto the priests."
- Cleansed AS THEY WENT (v14) — on the road, mid-obedience, before any
  visible change. THE peak of the film; total silence lands here.
- ONE turned back when he SAW he was healed, praising God with a LOUD
  voice (v15) — the return is loud, public, running.
- Fell prostrate at Jesus's feet, giving thanks (v16).
- "And he was a Samaritan" (v16) — the outsider. v18 calls him "this
  stranger."
- j2 = KJV v17-18: "Were there not ten cleansed? but where are the nine?
  There are not found that returned to give glory to God, save this
  stranger."
- j3 = KJV v19: "Arise, go thy way: thy faith hath made thee whole."
- Cleansed (all ten, on the road) vs made WHOLE (the one, at his feet) —
  the two-word distinction is the Seed's hinge; the narration lands it.

### MUST NEVER SHOW
- Jesus rendered WHITE / Caucasian-pale / European (Correction #16b — the
  one hard racial rule, non-negotiable). His skin is warm MIDDLE-EASTERN
  tan / olive-brown. Audit every render at phone-scale AND high zoom: any
  pale-European skin tone on him is dead.
- ANY VIEW OF THE FRONT of the Jesus figure (Correction #18 — the #1 law).
  We do not know what he looked like; the camera NEVER renders the front of
  him. No portrait, no close-up, no front-on view, and nothing that names or
  builds his features. He is staged ONLY from behind, over-the-shoulder, or
  at a distance — the viewer sees his back, shoulder, and hair, never more.
  Hide by ANGLE only, never by glow. (This half of #16 was reversed by #18
  the same day; the warm Middle-Eastern-not-white half of #16 survives.)
- NO cloaked-figure gimmicks (dead under #16, but never revive them):
  black-void hood openings, solid-black human cutouts, or a
  robe-and-posture-only faceless figure. He is simply painted as a warm
  human being now.
- NO TOUCH OF THE LEPERS anywhere (STORY LOGIC, not a hiding rule). Luke
  17 states Jesus heals BY WORD, across the mandated distance — he never
  touches or approaches the ten. The pack's shot-6 "a hand enters frame
  and rests on his head" is DROPPED — not because hands are banned (they
  aren't anymore under #16) but because Luke states no touch. The gratitude
  beat is the healed man at Jesus's feet with Jesus present and warm, hands
  lowered/open but NOT laid on him as a healing touch.
- NO rim-light / halo / glow outlining his head (Correction #12, still in
  force). Natural warm daylight ON him is fine; a glow that traces the
  shape of his head is not.
- Jesus never the compositional star of the return scenes (#9): the
  picture is the healed man's joy and the watching disciples/villagers;
  gazes visibly converge on Jesus (#11) — he is present and warm but never
  center-staged as the hero, and never a small detached figure the crowd
  ignores.
- LEPROSY RESTRAINT (pack law): wrapped hands, half-covered faces,
  distance — NEVER gore, sores, or disfigurement. Dignity always
  (physicality-calibration law: afflicted, never grotesque).
- TIME-OF-DAY (#15a): the text states no time; staged as honest bright
  DAY end to end (the healing "sunlight everywhere" per the pack). No
  sunset/sunrise drama skies anywhere.
- ACTION LOGIC (#14b): unwrapping must read as unwrapping — pull, loosen,
  strip falls. The run back must read as running BACK the way they came;
  geometry law (#5): the returning man moves TOWARD the Jesus presence,
  and the Jesus presence is oriented TOWARD him (open posture), never
  turned away in rejection.
- CYCLICAL MOTION (#15c): walking strides cycle in Clip A; running
  strides cycle in Clip B; linen strips travel loosen-fall-blow, never a
  static pose with streaming particles.
- FIGURE COUNT (extension of the anatomy-count law): TEN figures exactly
  in v12-v14 group frames; NINE on the far road + ONE at the fork in s7;
  count every frame. Limbs counted on every figure in every frame —
  group/road scenes are this story's highest tangle risk.
- No painted tear beads (#3): the man's thanks lives in brows/eyes/mouth,
  wet shining eyes at most.
- No modern objects, no text in images, no photorealism; anti-panel +
  anti-texture sentences mandatory in every wide multi-figure still.

## WHY-LAW / STUDY-GEMS woven into narration
- WHY they stood far off: the Law of Moses required lepers to live
  outside the camp and warn everyone away — the distance was LAW, not
  shyness. Ten men no one had touched in years.
- WHY "go shew yourselves unto the priests": the priest was the only
  inspector who could certify a healed leper clean and readmit him to
  family, work, worship. Sending them was a promise in disguise — the
  instruction only made sense if they'd be clean on arrival.
- WHY a mixed group: the Samaria-Galilee borderland (v11) — and shared
  misery erases a border that religion built. GEM: disease ignored the
  divide; so did the healing.
- WHY Jesus asks about the nine: not wounded pride — the nine did
  nothing wrong; they obeyed and were cleansed. The question mourns what
  they missed: the giver himself. GEM: cleansed (v14) and made whole
  (v19) are different words — all ten got the first; only the one who
  returned heard the second.
- THE SEED (pack + PAIRING-LIST #14): healing met them walking; wholeness
  met him returning. Closing card verbatim from the pack: "The healing
  happened while they walked, before they could see it. Have you ever had
  to move forward on nothing but a word — before there was any proof?"

## Locks (written into EVERY prompt where the figure/prop appears)

**SAMARITAN LOCK (the one who returns):** a lean man in his forties with
a full dark beard streaked with gray, deep-set dark eyes, olive skin,
wearing a patched slate-gray wool tunic with a rope belt — the ONLY
slate-gray tunic in the group, so he is findable in every frame. Wrapped
phase (s2-s5): face half-covered by linen strips, gray-streaked beard
edge visible, hands bound. Healed phase (s6 on): wrappings gone, same
face, same tunic. Exactly two arms, two hands, two legs, every frame.

**TEN-LEPERS LOCK:** exactly TEN figures in ragged undyed wool and linen
robes of varied earth tones (the Samaritan's slate-gray among them),
faces half-wrapped in undyed linen strips, hands bound in linen, staged
at a distance in v12-v14 beats. Afflicted with restraint: wrappings and
distance tell the disease; no sores, no gore. After the fork: NINE on
the far road, ONE returning.

**JESUS LOCK (per Correction #16 — real Middle Eastern man, kept
non-specific):** a first-century Judean man with warm MIDDLE-EASTERN
olive-brown skin (NEVER white or European-pale), dark shoulder-length
hair and a short dark beard, in an undyed cream wool robe with a simple
mantle. His hands, skin, and hair are visible and natural. He is kept
OMNISCIENT / non-specific: rendered three-quarter, from a gentle
distance, slightly downcast, in soft shadow, or softly out of crisp
focus — NEVER a sharp photoreal portrait and NEVER a crisp side profile
that fixes exactly what he looked like. No halo, rim-light, or glow
outlining his head (#12). Never centered as the compositional star (#9).
In the roadside beats he stands warm and present at the head of a small
band of travelers (disciples). He heals BY WORD across the distance — he
never touches or approaches the ten (story logic). The softness of the
painted style itself is what keeps him non-specific; do not fight it
toward realism on his face.

**PROP LOCK — LINEN WRAPPINGS:** strips of undyed, road-dusted linen on
faces and hands of all ten; in the healing they loosen, fall, and blow
down the road like shed cocoons. Same linen everywhere.

**SETTING LOCK:** a dry limestone borderland — dusty pale road, rocky
ridges, scrub olive and terebinth trees, and a small walled village with
a single gate visible in the distance. Bright honest daylight, blue sky,
every scene. Same road, same village silhouette throughout.
