# Story Video #6 — The Two Sons (Matthew 21:28–32)

Runtime 60s. Prepend the STYLE BLOCK from 00-MASTER-PLAN.md. A parable: Jesus
is the off-screen storyteller (voice/narration); the father and sons in the
parable may be shown fully.

## Narration script (app's exact words)

> A father told his first son: Go work in the vineyard today. The son said,
> "I will not." Later he changed his mind and went.
>
> The father told his second son the same thing. That son said, "Yes, I will
> go." And didn't. *(beat)*
>
> Jesus asked the crowd: which one actually did the will of his father?
>
> The answer was the one who first said no. *(peak — music cuts to silence
> here)*
>
> "Tax collectors and prostitutes are entering the kingdom of God ahead of
> you," he told the religious leaders who thought they were the second son.

Closing card (hold 6s): *"Have you ever said no to something — maybe loudly —
and found yourself moving toward it anyway because part of you couldn't let
it go?"*

## Shot list

### Shot 1 — "I will not"
```
Morning outside a farmhouse: a weathered father gestures toward terraced
vineyard rows on a hillside. His first son, young and hard-jawed, shakes his
head sharply, says a visible flat refusal, and walks away. The father
doesn't argue. He just watches him go, steady-eyed.
```

### Shot 2 — "Yes, I will go"
```
Same doorway, moments later: the second son, smooth and pleasant, nods
eagerly to his father with an easy smile and a hand over his heart — all
agreeableness. The father nods and turns away; the instant his back is
turned, the son's smile goes slack and he wanders off in the other
direction.
```

### Shot 3 — The no that couldn't let go
```
The first son sits alone on a stone wall at midday, jaw tight, deliberately
not looking at the vineyard on the hill behind him. He keeps almost-looking.
Wind moves the vines. Finally his shoulders drop; he exhales, half-laughs at
himself, and stands.
```

### Shot 4 — He went (the money shot)
```
Golden hour in the vineyard rows: the first son works alone — sleeves
rolled, sweat on his face, pruning and tying vines with growing steadiness
and something like peace. Wide shot: one small figure keeping faith in a big
gold hillside. The second son is nowhere.
```

### Shot 5 — The empty row
```
The same golden vineyard light on a different row: an untouched line of
overgrown vines, tools still leaning clean and unused against a post where
they were left that morning. Nobody comes. The wind moves through the row
alone.
```

### Shot 6 — Which one?
```
Dusk: the father stands at the edge of the vineyard looking up at the worked
rows, then down at the single figure of his first son walking toward him,
dirty and spent. The father's face breaks into quiet pride. He claps a hand
on the back of the son who said no — and came.
```

### Closing card
Text on cream, serif. No narration.

## The Seed (what this video must leave behind)

Heaven reads the heart's actual direction, not the religious performance. The honest "no" that turns beats the pious "yes" that doesn't move. Quiet question: *which does the God you know reward — the appearance of faith, or the turning?* Carried by shots 3–5.
