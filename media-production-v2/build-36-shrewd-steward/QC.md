## Content care — the hardest parable in the gospels to stage honestly

The master commends the steward's **shrewdness, not his dishonesty**, and the narration
says so outright ("He was not praising the cheating. He was pointing at the urgency").

- **No frame celebrates the fraud.** A `_NO_HEIST` clause rides every parable beat:
  nobody winks, smirks, gloats, laughs slyly, exchanges a conspiratorial glance,
  handles money with delight, or is lit or framed as a hero pulling off a heist. The
  dealings are matter-of-fact, hurried and tense — a frightened man working fast.
- The commendation beat is the master's **rueful, complicated** look — head shaken,
  one brow up, mouth just short of a smile, the anger still underneath. No handshake,
  no embrace, no reward handed over, no celebration.
- The debtors are neither victims being robbed nor accomplices: ordinary men handed
  their own sheet, astonished.
- **Nothing of heaven, hell, throne, crown, gate, angel, judgement, death or afterlife
  is painted**, because the narration states none of it. "Everlasting habitations" is
  staged as what the word actually means in that world — **a house at dusk whose people
  take a traveller in** — never a sky-city or a gate of light. "Past the end of your
  life" is staged as Jesus speaking, never as a deathbed or a grave.
- **God is never depicted as any figure, face, form, light or presence.** This matters
  most at "Ye cannot serve God and mammon", which is staged on Jesus's own face and
  nowhere else. The two-masters illustration uses **two ordinary human householders** in
  a courtyard — exactly the saying's own image — and neither stands for God, neither is
  lit or robed to suggest it, and money is never personified. No idol, altar or scales.
- Nobody is seized, dragged or restrained anywhere in the cut.

## Who carries which red-letter line

Jesus is on screen only in the frames he speaks in **as himself**. Luke 16:3 ("What
shall I do? … I cannot dig; to beg I am ashamed") and 16:6 ("Take thy bill, and sit
down quickly, and write fifty") are red-letter but they are **the steward talking inside
the parable** — putting Jesus's face under a caption of a panicking man planning a
write-down would invert the line, so both are staged inside the parable. Luke 16:8
splits: the master commending is staged inside the parable; "for the children of this
world…" is Jesus's own comment and returns to him.

## Staging — six places, none repeating the realistic wave

Village **rooftop** in clear morning (used by no other row in the wave, and Luke 16:1

## Rerolls and the cures that stopped them

Three defects, each cured **preventively across every remaining beat in the same pass**
rather than paid for frame by frame:

1. **The steward anchor came back in a felted fleece.** Its charcoal mantle is the
   largest cloth surface in ~20 of his frames, so the defect would have propagated
   everywhere. Cured by stating the weave **positively inside the STEWARD lock itself**
   — flat, thin, matte, loom-woven, visible warp-and-weft grid, frayed selvedge, never
   napped, fleeced, brushed or blanket-like. Composition-level regeneration
   (delete + withheld REFS + fresh generation), **never `--redo`**. One image.
2. **Jesus looked into the lens on the first rooftop frame.** The geometry was the
   cause: on every "camera behind the listeners" beat, Jesus faces the listeners and
   therefore faces the camera. Prohibiting the gaze would not have fixed it, so the
   frame was **re-staged**: the man he addresses sits far out at one edge, his head is
   turned a quarter-turn off the lens axis and his eyeline runs laterally across the
   frame. Applied to **all seven** rooftop Jesus beats before any of them generated.
   One image paid, six saved.
3. **A hinged plank door appeared centre-frame** in the accounts room, which the ESTATE
   lock forbids — the clause was buried deep in a long block. Cured by **front-loading**
   the positive geometry: every opening in the estate is either a completely empty
   rectangular gap with nothing in it, or one dark goat-hair panel knotted back. Applied
   before the remaining thirteen estate beats generated.

**Per the row-35 lesson, the frames generated BEFORE each cure were re-inspected in the
same pass** rather than assumed safe. That re-inspection caught one more: `s02`, an
estate frame that predated the door cure and carried a timber gate leaf at the right
edge. It was regenerated. `s03`, `s04` and `s06` were checked against the same cure and
were clean.
