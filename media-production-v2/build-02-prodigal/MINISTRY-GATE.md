# MINISTRY GATE — row 2, build-02-prodigal (Luke 15:11-32) · V2

Answered per PRODUCTION-BIBLE §5, as the target viewer for rows 1–100: **a stranger
with no faith background**, who did not go looking for this and is deciding in the
first eight seconds whether to keep watching.

Watched once, start to finish, before writing anything below.

---

### 1. What does this video teach about Jesus, in one sentence?

**Jesus answers the accusation that he keeps bad company by telling a story in which
the father runs — meaning God is not waiting at the end of the road with folded arms
to see whether you grovel well enough, he is already sprinting toward you while you
are still rehearsing your apology.**

The sentence writes itself here, which is the test. The frame story is doing real
work: the video opens on the *complaint* (v1-2, religious men murmuring that he
receives sinners and eats with them) and the parable is his ANSWER to it. A viewer
who has ever been told they are the wrong kind of person is being answered too.

### 2. Does every claim come from the scripture on screen?

Yes, and this build is stricter than V1 was.

- Every red caption is **exact KJV** — v18-19, v24, v29, v31, v32 — verified against
  the text, not paraphrased. Jesus is the one telling the parable, so the quoted
  characters' lines are correctly in his voice and his colour.
- The narrator's lines add no doctrine. The one interpretive sentence — *"Old men in
  that world did not run. It was beneath their dignity. He ran anyway."* — is
  cultural background that makes v20 land, not theology smuggled in. It says nothing
  the text does not.
- **Nothing watered down.** The story keeps its offense: the son's request is
  presented as "as if to say he wished his father were already dead," and the parable
  is allowed to **end unresolved at the open door** — Jesus never says whether the
  elder brother went in, and neither does this video. The temptation to close that
  loop and send everyone home comfortable was refused, because closing it would be
  adding to the text.
- The BOM law is not in play — nothing beyond the Bible is referenced anywhere.

### 3. Did the story get its full weight?

Yes. **24 pictures over 158 seconds, against V1's 10 stills.** Nothing was compressed
to close a row — the runtime grew because the beats did.

Specifically, the beats V1 collapsed now each have their own frame:
- the father *seeing* him a great way off, the run, the scandal of the run, and the
  embrace are four separate pictures, not one
- the elder brother's whole arc — in the field, hearing the music, refusing to go in,
  the father coming out, the complaint, the answer, the open door — is eight pictures
- the pigsty is separated from the realization ("he came to himself" gets its own
  frame, because it is the hinge of the son's half of the story)

Honest note: the audio is the preserved V1 narration, so pacing is inherited, not
chosen. It does not feel rushed. The elder-brother section in particular gets its
natural length, which matters — that half of the parable is the half aimed straight
at the religious men who started the argument, and it is usually the half that gets
cut.

### 4. Would this cut, alone, make its target viewer want the next video?

Yes — and the reason is the closing question rather than the artwork.

It ends on *"Which one feels closest to where you are right now — the son who left,
the father who ran to meet him, or the brother who stayed, and felt unseen?"* That
question does not require belief to answer. It requires only honesty, and every one
of the three options is a real place a person can be, including the resentful one.
Nobody is cornered into confessing unbelief to participate — which is the trap the
answer-options law exists to prevent.

The one who has walked away sees the run. The one who never left and is quietly
bitter about it sees that Jesus gave *him* the last scene and the tenderest sentence
in the story. Both are met. Neither is pressured.

---

**GATE: PASS on all four.**

Reviewer note for the ledger: the picture that carries this video is s11 — the father
photographed from behind, running away from camera down the road toward a son who is
still a speck. In the first attempt the model composed that shot with the father
running *away* from his son, and it read as nothing at all. It is the icon of the
parable and it now reads correctly at a glance. That single fix is the difference
between this passing question 1 and not.
