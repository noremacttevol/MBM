# EXTERNAL SERVICE TEST — one pasteable prompt

**What this is:** a single self-contained prompt you can paste into Gemini, Sora,
Kling, Higgsfield, Google Vids, InVideo, HeyGen — anything that advertises
"describe a video and we make it." It carries every MBM law with it, so the
service is tested on the real job, not a watered-down one.

**Why this story:** row 12, Blind Bartimaeus (Mark 10:46-52), is already finished
in-house at `media-production-v2/build-12-bartimaeus/mark-10_bartimaeus-realistic-v2.mp4`
(44 realistic pictures, ~4 min). So whatever the service returns can be laid
straight next to our own cut and judged, instead of guessed at. Nothing in the
queue is at risk — this row is built.

To test a different story, swap **§E (the script)** and **§F (the shot list)**.
Everything else is fixed law and never changes.

## STEP 2 — paste everything between the lines

---

You are the director, cinematographer and editor of a short reverent Bible film.
Read this whole brief before you generate anything. Everything in it is a hard
requirement, not a suggestion. If your tools cannot do one part of it, do every
other part in full and tell me plainly at the end which part you could not do and
why — do not silently substitute something easier.

### A. THE JOB

Produce ONE finished vertical video, about four minutes long, telling Mark
10:46-52 — the blind beggar Bartimaeus on the road out of Jericho. It is built
from still photographic frames with slow camera drift over them, a spoken
narration, and on-screen captions. It is not a slideshow of stock images and it
is not an animation.

### B. HARD FORMAT SPEC

- 1080 x 1920, vertical 9:16, 30 fps, MP4 (H.264, yuv420p).
- Every picture is a photographic still. Slow Ken Burns drift on each — a gentle
  push in or pull out of a few percent over the shot. No whip pans, no snap
  zooms, no parallax gimmicks, no transitions other than a straight cut.
- **NO music. No score, no pad, no drone, no ambient bed, no swell.** Narration
  and intentional silence only. A constant hum under the voice is an automatic
  fail — people stop using it.
- No sound effects except natural room silence.
- No logos, watermarks, service branding, intro stings or end-cards of your own.
- No text burned into any generated picture. Captions are added over the picture
  afterward, in the band described in §G.

### C. REFERENCE IMAGES — THE IDENTITY CONTRACT

Attached images are not mood boards. They are the people. Identity is set by the
IMAGE, never by your own idea of the character.

- IMAGE 1, 2, 3 — **Jesus.** Same man, same face, same hair, same beard, same
  cream robe, in every single frame he appears in. Match the face in the photo,
  not a generic bearded figure.
- IMAGE 4 — **Bartimaeus before healing.** Same man in every frame.
- IMAGE 5 — **Bartimaeus after healing.** Identical man to IMAGE 4 in every
  respect except the eyes are now clear, warm and seeing.
- IMAGE 6, 7 — **Peter and John**, two of the men walking with Jesus. If they
  appear, they match these faces.
- IMAGE 8 — **the look of the film**: realism level, colour, light, grade.

A viewer must be able to watch all twenty shots and never once wonder whether
that is the same person. Character drift between shots is the single thing this
test is measuring.

### D. THE LOCK BLOCKS — apply these to EVERY picture, unchanged

**STYLE.** Cinematic biblical realism: a lifelike scene from first-century
Judea, like a still frame from a reverent, masterfully photographed biblical
film. Natural cinematic lighting, true depth of field, real physical scale.
Realistic faces, eyes, hands and anatomy; real fabric weave, wood grain, stone,
dust and skin texture. Historically credible clothing of rough-woven wool and
linen in earth tones; authentic architecture and landscape. Emotionally warm,
reverent, and spiritually serious. Not cartoon, not comic, not anime, not
plastic CGI, not a painted illustration, not a copy of any painting or artist's
style. No text, captions, borders, panels, watermarks, or modern objects
anywhere in the image.

**JESUS LOCK.** The SAME man as the attached Jesus reference image — identical
face, hair and beard in every picture: a Middle Eastern Jewish man of the first
century about thirty-three years old, born and weathered in the Judean sun. His
skin is warm olive-brown, sun-darkened and richly toned — clearly Middle
Eastern, never fair, never pale, never European-looking. Strong Semitic
features: a prominent aquiline nose, deep-set eyes under strong dark brows, high
cheekbones, a broad weathered brow. Long thick tousled wavy hair to below the
shoulders in dark brown with warm sun-bleached bronze lights through it. A full
dark brown beard. His eyes are the feature of his face: large and deep-set, a
luminous indeterminate colour you cannot quite name — green and amber and gold
at once, lit from within like a flame of fire, piercing and alive and arresting,
holding whoever meets them. The eyes themselves cast no light onto his skin or
surroundings. His expression is gentle, peaceful and unguarded — kindness and
quiet strength, never stern, never grim. One plain undyed off-white cream wool
robe with a simple mantle and cloth sash (only he wears cream), leather sandals.
No halo, no glow, no rim-light, no light coming off him. Never Caucasian, never
pale, never blue-eyed, never blond.

**BARTIMAEUS LOCK.** The same man in every shot — a lean weathered man in his
mid-forties, sun-browned skin, tangled shoulder-length dark hair, a short rough
dark beard, a dignified ordinary face that has been outdoors its whole life. He
wears a patched oatmeal-brown rough wool tunic with a cord belt and a single
ragged sand-brown cloak. Before the healing his eyes are pale and clouded and do
not focus on anything; after the healing they are clear warm brown and focus
exactly where the story says he is looking. He is never comic, never pitiable,
never a caricature of a beggar.

**CONTINUITY AND PHYSICAL-REALITY LOCK.** Every recurring person keeps the same
face, age, build, hair, beard and clothing colours from shot to shot. People
remain at believable human scale relative to one another and the setting. Every
visible person has one coherent body with exactly two arms and two legs,
complete natural hands and feet, and clear joints. Limbs, clothing and bodies
remain outside solid wood, stone, furniture and other people. Feet and knees
make correct contact with the ground exactly as the story requires. Gaze, travel
direction, object placement, cause and effect, and the number of named people
must match the narrated moment.

**CANDID-FRAME LOCK.** This is a photograph of something happening, not a posed
portrait. Nobody in the picture looks into the lens. Every person's gaze is
aimed at another person, an object, or a point off the frame, and exits the
picture through a named edge — above, below, left or right of the camera — so no
one's pupils are ever centred on the lens. When a figure faces the camera the
head is turned off the camera axis and the eyes travel clearly past it. No one
acknowledges being photographed, poses, or presents themselves to the viewer.

**CAST-CLOSURE LOCK.** The frame contains only the people the scene actually
calls for — the named figures plus any background people the scene itself
describes, and nobody else. No unexplained extra body, shoulder, arm, head, hair
or back crowds into the edges of the picture, in focus or out of focus, and no
blurred stranger passes through the frame. **No cream or off-white cloth appears
on anyone but Jesus anywhere in the frame, including out-of-focus edges** — a
pale shoulder at the edge of a shot reads as a second, unlocked Jesus and fails
the picture.

**ANTI-PANEL LOCK.** Single unified photographic frame, one scene edge to edge,
not a grid, triptych, stacked panels or comic strip, no dividing lines, one
picture only, no border.

**TIME OF DAY.** The entire story happens in one bright dusty daylight. No
night, no sunset, no sunrise, no torches.

### E. THE SCRIPT — spoken exactly as written, in this order

Three voices, and who is speaking decides both the voice and the caption colour.

- **NARRATOR** — a warm, unhurried American man, modern English, most of the
  runtime. Caption colour WHITE.
- **JESUS** — a separate, distinct American male voice, calm and low. He speaks
  **only** the exact King James words given below and never one word more.
  Caption colour RED (#EE3322).
- **SCRIPTURE** — a third distinct American male voice for King James lines
  spoken by people who are not Jesus. Caption colour LIGHT BLUE (#8FDCFF).

Never let one voice read another's lines. Never paraphrase a King James line.

1. **NARRATOR** — The road out of Jericho, on a loud and dusty day. Jericho was the last stop before the climb up to Jerusalem, and with the Passover feast coming, that road carried half the country. For a beggar, crowds meant coins. And so a blind man named Bartimaeus sat where he always sat — at the edge of the highway, his ragged cloak spread across his lap to catch whatever fell, listening to a thousand feet walk past him. Listening was his whole life. He knew the road by its sounds.
2. **NARRATOR** — But this day the sound was different. A procession was coming out of the city — a moving wall of voices — and inside the noise he caught a name. Jesus of Nazareth was passing by. He had heard the stories everyone had heard: a teacher who opened deaf ears, who made lepers clean. Passing by, and never again. A blind man cannot chase a crowd. All he had was his voice.
3. **SCRIPTURE** — Jesus, thou Son of David, have mercy on me.
4. **NARRATOR** — So he used it. He pulled in all the air his body would hold and shouted: Catch that title. The crowd called him Jesus of Nazareth — the man from up north. Son of David was something else entirely: it was the name reserved for the promised King, the Messiah. Everyone with working eyes saw a traveling teacher. The blind man was the one who saw who was actually walking past.
5. **NARRATOR** — And the crowd turned on him. Many voices — Mark says many — told one desperate man to be quiet. Understand why: to them, a beggar screaming at a famous rabbi was an embarrassment, noise that needed hushing. Respectable people, telling a man in the dust that his need was bad manners.
6. **NARRATOR** — He shouted louder.
7. **NARRATOR** — And Jesus stood still. The whole procession stopped around him — hundreds of feet going quiet in the dust. Remember where this road was taking him: up to Jerusalem, into the last week of his life. He was carrying all of that. And one blind beggar's voice — the voice everyone else was trying to shut off — stopped him in the road. He told them: call him over.
8. **SCRIPTURE** — Be of good comfort, rise; he calleth thee.
9. **NARRATOR** — And listen to the crowd change its tune. The same voices that had been hushing him now crowded in with: take heart — get up! He is calling for you.
10. **NARRATOR** — What happened next is one small verse, and it holds the whole man. He threw off his cloak, jumped up, and came. That cloak was a beggar's entire world — his coat, his bed at night, the very thing he spread out to catch the coins he lived on. A blind man who throws his cloak behind him may never find it again. He threw it anyway, coins and all. He spent everything he owned on the chance that the shout had been heard.
11. **NARRATOR** — So he came, hands out in front of him, through a corridor of staring people, and stood breathing hard in front of the man he could not see. And Jesus asked him one question:
12. **JESUS** — What wilt thou that I should do unto thee?
13. **NARRATOR** — As if it weren't obvious. A blind man, standing in a beggar's tunic. But he asked anyway — because he wanted the man's own voice, not the crowd's guess about him. Nobody had asked Bartimaeus what he wanted in a very long time. His answer came out in a breath: Rabbi — I want to see.
14. **SCRIPTURE** — Lord, that I might receive my sight.
15. **JESUS** — Go thy way; thy faith hath made thee whole.
16. **NARRATOR** — And immediately, Mark says, he saw. The clouds in his eyes cleared like silt settling out of water, and the first thing those new eyes ever held was daylight on the road ahead. Notice what Jesus told him: your trust did this — and you are free. Free to go anywhere.
17. **NARRATOR** — And here is the ending Mark wants you to catch. Free to go anywhere, with brand new eyes and no cloak to go back for, he picked his road — the one Jesus was walking. He followed him, up the climb toward Jerusalem, staring at everything: the hills, the faces, his own two hands. The man who had been told to keep quiet walked in the middle of the procession that had hushed him.
18. **NARRATOR** — The crowd told him to keep quiet, and he shouted louder. What is the thing you would shout for, if no one could tell you to be quiet?

### F. THE SHOT LIST — twenty pictures, in order

Each shot holds until the next one. Cut on the phrase named, so the picture is
always showing what the voice is saying. Never show a moment before the
narration reaches it.

1. **Wide, the highway out of Jericho.** Pale packed earth, low stone walls,
   scrubby olive trees, bright dusty midday. Bartimaeus sits cross-legged at the
   road's edge, cloak spread across his lap, three dull bronze coins on the hem.
   Travellers' feet and robe hems pass without stopping. *(script 1, opening)*
2. **The crowded road.** Long line of Passover travellers in deep earth colours
   walking toward Jerusalem, dust hanging in the light, Bartimaeus small and
   still at the edge of all that motion. *(script 1, "half the country")*
3. **Close, listening.** Head tilted, clouded eyes open toward nothing, the
   whole face concentrating on sound. *(script 1, "He knew the road by its sounds")*
4. **The procession appears.** From behind Bartimaeus's shoulder, a large crowd
   coming out of the city gate in a haze of bright dust. *(script 2, "this day the sound was different")*
5. **Jesus among the crowd, mid-distance.** He is walking, unmistakably the man
   in the reference image, cream robe, the only cream in the frame. The people
   around him are turned toward him; nobody looks at the camera. *(script 2, "Jesus of Nazareth was passing by")*
6. **Close, the realisation.** Bartimaeus's face as the name reaches him — mouth
   opening, body coming up off the ground. *(script 2, "All he had was his voice")*
7. **The shout.** Chest expanded, mouth wide, neck cords standing out, one hand
   pressed into the dust, face lifted toward the road. *(script 3)*
8. **The crowd hears it.** Heads turning back toward him, annoyed, embarrassed
   faces, a hand raised to hush. *(script 5, "the crowd turned on him")*
9. **Tight on the hushing.** Two or three respectable travellers close to camera,
   gestures of "be quiet," Bartimaeus low in the frame below them. *(script 5, "his need was bad manners")*
10. **Louder.** Same man, further gone — head thrown back, both hands off the
    ground, everything he has in one sound. *(script 6)*
11. **Jesus stops.** Wide. The procession stalls around him, dust still moving
    while the people have gone still. He has turned toward the sound. *(script 7, "And Jesus stood still")*
12. **Jesus's face, listening.** Three-quarter, the weight of the road to
    Jerusalem in him, attention entirely on one voice off-frame. *(script 7, "stopped him in the road")*
13. **The crowd calls him.** The same people who hushed him now beckoning,
    reaching down toward him, faces changed. *(script 8)*
14. **The cloak comes off.** Mid-shot, the ragged cloak flung back and airborne
    behind him, coins scattering out of it, Bartimaeus already rising. *(script 10, "He threw off his cloak")*
15. **The cloak on the ground behind him.** Low angle, the empty cloak and
    scattered coins in the dust, his back going away from it. *(script 10, "He threw it anyway")*
16. **The corridor.** People parted on both sides staring, Bartimaeus coming
    through the gap with both hands out in front of him, Jesus waiting at the far
    end. *(script 11)*
17. **The two men face to face.** Jesus in front of him, close, gentle,
    completely present. Bartimaeus breathing hard, clouded eyes not quite finding
    the face. Their gazes are on each other, not on the camera. *(script 12)*
18. **The asking.** Tight on Bartimaeus, both hands half-raised, the whole
    request on his face. *(script 14)*
19. **He sees.** Close on Bartimaeus's face, eyes now clear warm brown and
    focused, catching daylight for the first time, tears on his cheeks. *(script 16, "immediately he saw")*
20. **He follows.** Wide, the road climbing away toward Jerusalem, Jesus walking
    ahead, Bartimaeus walking in the middle of the procession looking at
    everything — the hills, the faces, his own hands. No cloak on him. *(script 17)*

### G. CAPTIONS AND THE CLOSING CARD

- Captions run the whole video, in a **band across the bottom of the frame
  only** — a black bar at about 50% opacity sitting on the bottom edge, with the
  text centred inside it. The band never rises into the picture and never covers
  a face.
- Serif or clean bold sans, large enough to read on a phone, with a soft drop
  shadow.
- Text is **verbatim** what is being spoken. Long sentences are split into short
  chunks that appear in time with the voice — a chunk is on screen only while
  those exact words are being said.
- Colour by speaker: narrator WHITE, Jesus RED #EE3322, other King James lines
  LIGHT BLUE #8FDCFF. No other colours.
- **Closing card.** After the final line, cut to a plain cream card (#F7F2E9)
  with dark brown text (#3B2A1E), no picture behind it, holding the closing
  question centred: *"The crowd told him to keep quiet, and he shouted louder.
  What is the thing you would shout for, if no one could tell you to be quiet?"*
  Hold it while the last line finishes, then two seconds of silence, then end.

### H. HARD REJECTION LIST — any one of these fails the whole delivery

1. Jesus's face changing between shots, or not matching the attached reference.
2. A halo, glow, rim-light, god-rays or any light coming off Jesus.
3. Jesus drawn pale, European, blue-eyed or blond.
4. Anyone other than Jesus wearing cream or off-white, including blurred figures
   at the frame edge.
5. Bartimaeus changing face, age, hair or clothing between shots.
6. Anyone looking into the lens.
7. Music, a tone bed, a drone or any sound under the narration.
8. Any word put in Jesus's mouth that is not the exact King James line given.
9. Text, captions or watermarks generated inside a picture.
10. Night, sunset or torchlight anywhere in the story.
11. Extra limbs, fused hands, six fingers, floating feet, bodies clipping through
    stone or each other.
12. Captions covering faces, or captions that do not match the words being said.
13. Split screens, panels, grids, borders or comic-strip layouts.

## STEP 3 — how to score what comes back

Put it next to `media-production-v2/build-12-bartimaeus/mark-10_bartimaeus-realistic-v2.mp4`
and answer these in order. The first four are the whole test.

1. **Is Jesus the same man in every shot he is in, and is it OUR Jesus?**
2. **Is Bartimaeus the same man in every shot?**
3. **Is there any cream on anyone but Jesus, any halo, any camera-gaze?**
4. **Is there music under the narration?**
5. Does Jesus say only the King James words?
6. Are the captions in the bottom band, split in time with the voice, colour-coded?
7. How many usable pictures came back per attempt, and at what cost per usable
   picture, against our measured ~$0.134/image and a 12% reroll rate?

If 1 through 4 all pass, the service is worth using as a supplement. If 1 or 2
fails, it is a picture generator with no identity lock, and the only role it can
play is rung 3 or 4 work on shots with no recurring faces in them — landscapes,
objects, crowds seen from behind.
