**Minister**: He is risen. Every bit of real peace or hope you've ever felt has a source — and you're welcome here exactly as you are.

I'm glad you're here. Before anything else — how are you, really? What's been sitting on your heart lately, or what brought you here today? There's no right answer, and you can share as much or as little as you want. I'm just here to listen first.

**Seeker**: Oh, that's a sweet way to start. He is risen indeed! 🙂

Honestly I'm doing okay, just... I've been feeling a little dry lately in my quiet times, you know? Like I'm going through the motions some mornings. Reading my Bible, praying, but it just feels a little distant. I know that happens to everyone in their walk, my pastor has talked about it. But it still weighs on me a little.

I don't know what brought me to try something like this exactly. Maybe just looking for a little encouragement, I guess.

**Minister**: I hear that. And honestly, the fact that it weighs on you — that dryness bothers you — that's not a small thing. It means you *want* real connection, not just habit. That matters.

A lot of people in that season just keep pushing through. The fact that you're noticing it and sitting with it says something about where your heart actually is.

Can I ask — when you think back to a time when your quiet times *weren't* dry, when it felt real and close — what was different then? What do you remember about how that felt?

**Seeker**: Mmm. Yeah, that's a good question.

I think back to when I first really gave my life to the Lord, I was maybe nineteen, and everything felt so *alive*. Like I was reading the same words I'd grown up hearing but they were suddenly lit up somehow. I'd sit with my coffee and just not want to move because it felt like He was right there.

I don't know exactly what was different. Maybe I was just more... open? Less distracted. Life gets busier and I think sometimes I'm just checking a box without realizing it, and then I feel a little guilty about that too.

**Minister**: That's a really honest picture. And listen — you're describing something real that happened. That aliveness you felt at nineteen wasn't fake, and the fact that you can still remember how it felt means something in you is still reaching for it.

Here's what I'm noticing though: you mentioned guilt about checking the box. I want to ask gently — do you think maybe part of what's making it feel distant now isn't that God *is* distant, but that you're approaching Him like you have to earn back that feeling? Like you're trying to *perform* your way back to that closeness?

Because that's actually the opposite of what opened it up at nineteen, isn't it? You said you were more open then. Less trying. Just... present.

What do you think — does that land at all, or am I off?

**Seeker**: *pauses*

Yeah. Yeah, that actually... that lands a little. I don't love admitting it but I think you might be right that there's some of that going on. Like I'm trying to manufacture something instead of just coming to Him.

It's funny because I *know* that. I've heard it a hundred times, grace not works, it's not about performing. But knowing it and living it are different things aren't they.

I guess I just worry that if I show up without having "done the work" so to speak, like read enough or prayed long enough, that I'm not being serious enough about my faith. Which when I say it out loud sounds a little off.

**Minister**: It does sound a little off — and I think you *know* that, which is why you said it that way. But here's what I'm hearing underneath: you're afraid that if you come to Him just as you are, without the performance, He might see you as... not serious. Not worthy enough. Not *enough*.

Can I ask you something? When you were nineteen and that aliveness happened — did you come to Him because you'd earned it? Because you'd done enough Bible reading or prayed the right amount of times?

Or did you come because something in you just... opened, and you wanted to be there?

And here's the real question: do you believe — actually believe — that the God who met you then, that Father who made Himself real to you in your kitchen with your coffee, do you think *He* changed? Or do you think maybe you're expecting something from yourself that He never asked for?

I'm asking because there's a difference between being *serious* about faith and being *anxious* about it. One draws you closer to Him. The other pushes Him away, even though you're trying to get closer.

What do you think?

**Seeker**: *quiet for a second*

No. No I didn't earn it at nineteen. I just... showed up. Honestly I was kind of a mess at nineteen and He met me right there in it.

And no, I don't think He changed. That's not who He is.

I think you're putting your finger on something real. There's definitely some anxiety in it. I've struggled with anxiety in general honestly, so it probably bleeds into my faith more than I realize.

I just... I want to make sure I'm taking my faith seriously, you know? It matters to me. He matters to me. I don't want to use grace as an excuse to be lazy about it.

But I hear what you're saying. Being anxious about it isn't the same as being serious about it. That's actually kind of convicting in a gentle way if that makes sense.

**Minister**: That makes complete sense. And I want to honor something: you *are* taking your faith seriously. A lazy person doesn't sit with the weight of whether they're doing it right. You care. That's already showing.

But here's what I'm wondering — and this is just a gentle mirror back at you:

What if the invitation right now isn't to *do* something different in your quiet times? What if it's to show up differently? Not with a checklist. Not trying to manufacture aliveness. Just... coming like you did at nineteen — a little messy maybe, distracted, anxious about life — and saying, "Here I am"?

Not performing faith. *Living* it. Which sometimes looks like sitting with coffee and just being present, even if nothing feels lit up yet.

The aliveness might come back. It might look different. But I think it comes back when you stop trying to earn the right to be there.

I'm also hearing that anxiety is something you carry more broadly — and that's real. That's not just a spiritual thing, that's a *you* thing. And I wonder if part of what would help isn't just a shift in how you approach God, but maybe also some real conversation with someone who knows you — a pastor, a counselor, someone who can walk with you in that anxiety piece specifically.

Does that feel like something worth exploring?
