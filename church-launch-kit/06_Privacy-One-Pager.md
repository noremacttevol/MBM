# What the App Collects and Protects — A Plain-Language One-Pager

*Short enough to hand to anyone who asks "what happens to what I say in there?" Written honestly — it names both what's protected and what's true about where data goes, because half-answers are what break trust.*

---

## The short version

You can use Milk Before Meat without giving your name, your email, or making any account at all. It is built to know as little about you as possible while still being able to help, and to let you delete what it does learn.

## What it collects

When you open the app, you're signed in **anonymously** — there's no login, and no name or email is required. If you choose to share things with the app (an answer to a story, a note about where you are with faith, a message to a real person), those are tied only to that anonymous ID, never to your real identity by default. The app also notices simple things from how you talk so it can respond more helpfully — and it shows you what it has noticed right on your profile screen.

## What you control

On the profile screen, the app shows you what it has learned about you and gives you a button to **remove** any of it. You decide what it keeps. There is no account to track you across devices, and there is a published privacy policy at **milkb4meat.org**.

## The honest part about where data goes

So you have the full picture: when you have a conversation, the words are processed by an outside AI service (the same kind of technology behind other well-known assistants), and if you message a real person, that message is stored in a standard secure cloud database so the person can read and reply. This is normal for an app like this — but it's true, and you deserve to know it rather than be told your words vanish into nowhere. Because of that, the app is deliberately built to collect little and let you delete what it has.

## What it will never do

It will never pretend to be God or answer your prayers — it tells you every time it opens to take those straight to Heavenly Father. It will never pressure you, shame you, or push you toward anything. And it will never hide what it is: if you ask who made it, it tells you immediately that members of The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints did, as an independent project that is not an official Church product.
