## The one big idea (read this first)

There are only **two states** an app can be in:

1. **Testing** — only people YOU invite can get it. The public cannot find it or search for it. It is private, on purpose.
2. **Published** (also called **Production** or **"going live"**) — anyone in the world can search the store and download it.

**Right now your app is in TESTING. That is correct. That is where it is supposed to be.** You are not trying to publish to the whole world yet. You are getting a small, trusted group to use it first. So when the stores don't show your app to the public — good. That's the plan working, not something broken.

---

## The words, decoded

The stores use insider words for simple things. Here is every word you'll see, in plain terms:

- **Internal testing** — your own tiny circle (just you and maybe one or two people). Instant, no waiting. *This is how the app is on YOUR Android phone right now.*
- **Closed testing** — a named list of people you invite by email. **This is the one your church rollout uses.** They get the app and keep it. This is what people mean by "the official testing phase."
- **Open testing** — anyone with a link can join. (You're not using this.)
- **Production / Public release / Go live** — the real, public store. The final step. (Later.)
- **TestFlight** — this is just *Apple's word* for testing. It was how iPhones got the app before Apple approved it; now the iPhone app is simply on the public App Store, so this word matters less every day.
- **"In review" / "Waiting for review"** — the company (Apple or Google) is looking it over before letting it go public. This is normal. It just means *waiting in line.* There is nothing for you to do while it says this.

That's the whole vocabulary. There isn't more.

## Why there's a "2-week" thing (the honest reason — and why it helps you)

Google has a rule for a personal developer account like yours: **before they will let you go public, at least 12 people you invited have to keep your app installed and opted-in for 14 days in a row.** It's a box Google makes you check to prove real people use the app.

This is the "2-week testing phase." It is a **real Google requirement**, not something you made up. Which is exactly why it's a great thing to tell people:

> *"You'd be one of the official testers Google needs actively using the app for two weeks, so it can launch to the public."*

Every word of that is true. You're not overselling — you're describing a real rule. That's what makes your invitation both honest and exciting.

## "Not a timed trial" — here's what your testers actually experience

You were right to care about this. Here is the truth:

**On Android: once someone installs from your invite link, the app just stays on their phone.** There is no countdown on their screen. No "your trial ends in X days." Nothing kicks them off. The 14 days is Google's behind-the-scenes counter, not a clock the user ever sees. They just use the app normally.

**On iPhone:** no countdown at all anymore — the app is live on the public App Store, a normal permanent install like any other app.

So you can promise people honestly: *install it and keep it — it's yours, it won't expire on you.*

## How to know "are we good, or is something wrong?"

You should never have to decode this alone — that's my job, and I check the pages and tell you in plain words. But here's how to read it yourself if you ever want to:

**The simple rule:** look for the color and the word.
- A **red banner**, or the words **"Rejected,"** **"Action required,"** or **"Issue"** = something needs doing. (Tell me — I'll handle it.)
- **Everything else** — "Waiting for review," "In review," "Ready to test," "Active," green checkmarks — = you're fine. It's just moving through the line.

**On the iPhone side** (App Store Connect): your app is in and processing correctly. "Waiting for Review" means Apple has it in line. "Ready to Test" means testers can install it. None of those are problems.

**On the Android side** (Play Console → "Test and release"): a build showing under Internal or Closed testing, marked "Active," with no red banners, means it's working. The setup checklist that says "10 of 11 complete" is just a to-do list, not an error.

**If you remember one sentence:** *no red, no "rejected" = we're just waiting, and waiting is normal.*
