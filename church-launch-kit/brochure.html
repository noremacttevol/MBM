An independent member-built ministry tool

Milk Before Meat

A faith app patterned on how the Savior ministered — one person at a time.

Milk Before Meat is a quiet companion app that meets a person exactly where they are, points them to the goodness of God revealed in Jesus Christ, and keeps a real human within reach the whole way. It is built on a simple conviction: that the Lord's way of reaching a heart was never a sales pitch, but presence, story, and patient love.

“I have fed you with milk, and not with meat: for hitherto ye were not able to bear it.” — 1 Corinthians 3:2

How it ministers

It meets people where they are. It listens to what a person is carrying and responds with the part of the gospel they can receive today — comfort to the grieving, honest answers to the skeptic.
It points to Christ, never to itself. The app speaks as a disciple would: “this is what Jesus said, this is how He treated people.” It never pretends to be Him, or to be a spiritual authority.
It never pressures. It asks more than it answers, welcomes honest doubt without shame, and lets anyone walk away free.
It keeps a real person one tap away. When someone is hurting or simply wants a human, the app says so and connects them.

What it is not

Not an official Church product. Every time it opens, it states plainly that it is “not officially affiliated with, endorsed by, or sponsored by any church.”
Not a replacement for God. At every open it reminds the user it is “a lantern, not the Light” — that it cannot answer a prayer, and that they should pray to Heavenly Father directly.
Not a substitute for missionaries or the ward. It supports the work of inviting; it never stands in for the real people and real covenants God uses.
Not hidden. Asked who made it, it answers truthfully and immediately: members of The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints.

How it honors the Church's counsel

The General Handbook's December 2025 guidance teaches that AI should be used in “positive, helpful, and uplifting ways,” that it “cannot replace the gift of divine inspiration,” and that it should “support and not supplant connection between God and His children.” This app was built to that exact standard — anchored in Elder Gerrit W. Gong's counsel that AI “can answer questions, but it cannot answer prayers… it is not God and cannot be God.” It uses none of the Church's protected names in its title, claims no endorsement, and lives the Handbook's measure of missionary work: that the effort is good “whether or not a person joins the Church.”

What I am asking

Not for the Church to endorse this — and not to call it official. Only for your blessing, as my priesthood leader, to invite a few trusted brethren to test it quietly for two weeks and tell me honestly what they see. I would be glad to put it on your own phone first, so you can hold it and judge it for yourself. I bring it to you before I take it anywhere, because I want it done under counsel and done right.

“A person who leaves unconverted but truly met, unpressured, and free is a success.” — from the app's own guiding instructions
